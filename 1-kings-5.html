Solomon rules every kingdom from the River to the border of Egypt; his daily provision, his stalls of horses, and his wisdom, greater than the wisdom of the sons of the east, are counted. Hiram of Tyre, who had always loved David, sends cedar and cypress by sea in rafts for wheat and oil; Solomon raises a forced labor of thirty thousand out of all Israel, with seventy thousand who carry burdens and eighty thousand who cut in the mountain, to prepare the timber and the stones of the house.
Before the building begins, which the next chapter dates to the fourth year of Solomon’s reign: the materials are being gathered, and the kingdom is at peace.

1
וּשְׁלֹמֹה, הָיָה מוֹשֵׁל בְּכָל-הַמַּמְלָכוֹת--מִן-הַנָּהָר אֶרֶץ פְּלִשְׁתִּים, וְעַד גְּבוּל מִצְרָיִם:  מַגִּשִׁים מִנְחָה וְעֹבְדִים אֶת-שְׁלֹמֹה, כָּל-יְמֵי חַיָּיו.  {פ}
And Solomon was ruler over all the kingdoms, from the River to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt; they brought tribute and served Solomon all the days of his life.note
Y Salomón era gobernante de todos los reinos, desde el Río hasta la tierra de los filisteos y hasta la frontera de Egipto; llevaban tributo y servían a Salomón todos los días de su vida.
2
וַיְהִי לֶחֶם-שְׁלֹמֹה, לְיוֹם אֶחָד:  שְׁלֹשִׁים כֹּר סֹלֶת, וְשִׁשִּׁים כֹּר קָמַח.
And Solomon’s bread for one day was thirty kor of fine flour and sixty kor of meal;note
Y el pan de Salomón para un día era treinta coros de flor de harina y sesenta coros de harina;
3
עֲשָׂרָה בָקָר בְּרִאִים, וְעֶשְׂרִים בָּקָר רְעִי--וּמֵאָה צֹאן; לְבַד מֵאַיָּל וּצְבִי, וְיַחְמוּר, וּבַרְבֻּרִים, אֲבוּסִים.
ten fattened oxen, and twenty pasture-fed oxen, and a hundred sheep — besides deer and gazelle and fallow deer and fattened fowl.note
diez bueyes cebados, y veinte bueyes de pasto, y cien ovejas — además de ciervos y gacelas y gamos y aves cebadas.
4
כִּי-הוּא רֹדֶה בְּכָל-עֵבֶר הַנָּהָר, מִתִּפְסַח וְעַד-עַזָּה--בְּכָל-מַלְכֵי, עֵבֶר הַנָּהָר; וְשָׁלוֹם, הָיָה לוֹ מִכָּל-עֲבָרָיו--מִסָּבִיב.
For he ruled over all the region beyond the River, from Tiphsah to Gaza, over all the kings beyond the River; and he had peace on all his sides round about.note
Porque dominaba sobre toda la región al otro lado del Río, desde Tifsa hasta Gaza, sobre todos los reyes al otro lado del Río; y tenía paz por todos sus lados alrededor.
5
וַיֵּשֶׁב יְהוּדָה וְיִשְׂרָאֵל לָבֶטַח, אִישׁ תַּחַת גַּפְנוֹ וְתַחַת תְּאֵנָתוֹ, מִדָּן, וְעַד-בְּאֵר שָׁבַע--כֹּל, יְמֵי שְׁלֹמֹה.  {ס}
And Judah and Israel dwelt securely, each man under his vine and under his fig tree, from Dan to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon.note
Y Judá e Israel habitaban seguros, cada uno bajo su vid y bajo su higuera, desde Dan hasta Beerseba, todos los días de Salomón.
6
וַיְהִי לִשְׁלֹמֹה, אַרְבָּעִים אֶלֶף אֻרְוֺת סוּסִים--לְמֶרְכָּבוֹ; וּשְׁנֵים-עָשָׂר אֶלֶף, פָּרָשִׁים.
And Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen.note
Y Salomón tenía cuarenta mil establos de caballos para sus carros, y doce mil jinetes.
7
וְכִלְכְּלוּ הַנִּצָּבִים הָאֵלֶּה אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ שְׁלֹמֹה, וְאֵת כָּל-הַקָּרֵב אֶל-שֻׁלְחַן הַמֶּלֶךְ-שְׁלֹמֹה--אִישׁ חָדְשׁוֹ:  לֹא יְעַדְּרוּ, דָּבָר.
And these officers sustained King Solomon, and all who came near to King Solomon’s table, each man in his month; they let nothing be lacking.note
Y estos oficiales sustentaban al rey Salomón, y a todos los que se acercaban a la mesa del rey Salomón, cada uno en su mes; no dejaban que faltara nada.
8
וְהַשְּׂעֹרִים וְהַתֶּבֶן, לַסּוּסִים וְלָרָכֶשׁ--יָבִאוּ, אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר יִהְיֶה-שָּׁם, אִישׁ, כְּמִשְׁפָּטוֹ.  {ס}
And the barley and the straw for the horses and the swift steeds they brought to the place where it was to be, each man according to his charge.note
Y la cebada y la paja para los caballos y los corceles las llevaban al lugar donde debían estar, cada uno conforme a su encargo.
9
וַיִּתֵּן אֱלֹהִים חָכְמָה לִשְׁלֹמֹה וּתְבוּנָה, הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד; וְרֹחַב לֵב--כַּחוֹל, אֲשֶׁר עַל-שְׂפַת הַיָּם.
And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding, very much, and breadth of heart like the sand that is on the shore of the sea.note
Y Dios dio a Salomón sabiduría y entendimiento, muchísimo, y anchura de corazón como la arena que está a la orilla del mar.
10
וַתֵּרֶב חָכְמַת שְׁלֹמֹה, מֵחָכְמַת כָּל-בְּנֵי-קֶדֶם, וּמִכֹּל, חָכְמַת מִצְרָיִם.
And Solomon’s wisdom was greater than the wisdom of all the sons of the east, and than all the wisdom of Egypt.note
Y la sabiduría de Salomón fue mayor que la sabiduría de todos los hijos del oriente, y que toda la sabiduría de Egipto.
11
וַיֶּחְכַּם, מִכָּל-הָאָדָם, מֵאֵיתָן הָאֶזְרָחִי וְהֵימָן וְכַלְכֹּל וְדַרְדַּע, בְּנֵי מָחוֹל; וַיְהִי-שְׁמוֹ בְכָל-הַגּוֹיִם, סָבִיב.
And he was wiser than every man — than Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, and Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol; and his name was in all the nations round about.note
Y fue más sabio que todo hombre — que Etán el ezraíta, y Hemán, y Calcol, y Darda, los hijos de Mahol; y su nombre estuvo en todas las naciones de alrededor.
12
וַיְדַבֵּר, שְׁלֹשֶׁת אֲלָפִים מָשָׁל; וַיְהִי שִׁירוֹ, חֲמִשָּׁה וָאָלֶף.
And he spoke three thousand proverbs; and his songs were a thousand and five.note
Y habló tres mil proverbios; y sus cantares fueron mil cinco.
13
וַיְדַבֵּר, עַל-הָעֵצִים, מִן-הָאֶרֶז אֲשֶׁר בַּלְּבָנוֹן, וְעַד הָאֵזוֹב אֲשֶׁר יֹצֵא בַּקִּיר; וַיְדַבֵּר עַל-הַבְּהֵמָה וְעַל-הָעוֹף, וְעַל-הָרֶמֶשׂ וְעַל-הַדָּגִים.
And he spoke of the trees, from the cedar that is in Lebanon to the hyssop that comes out of the wall; and he spoke of the beasts, and of the birds, and of the creeping things, and of the fish.note
Y habló de los árboles, desde el cedro que está en el Líbano hasta el hisopo que sale de la pared; y habló de los animales, y de las aves, y de los reptiles, y de los peces.
14
וַיָּבֹאוּ, מִכָּל-הָעַמִּים, לִשְׁמֹעַ, אֵת חָכְמַת שְׁלֹמֹה--מֵאֵת כָּל-מַלְכֵי הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר שָׁמְעוּ אֶת-חָכְמָתוֹ.  {ס}
And they came from all the peoples to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all the kings of the earth who had heard of his wisdom.note
Y venían de todos los pueblos a oír la sabiduría de Salomón, de todos los reyes de la tierra que habían oído de su sabiduría.
15
וַיִּשְׁלַח חִירָם מֶלֶךְ-צוֹר אֶת-עֲבָדָיו, אֶל-שְׁלֹמֹה, כִּי שָׁמַע, כִּי אֹתוֹ מָשְׁחוּ לְמֶלֶךְ תַּחַת אָבִיהוּ:  כִּי אֹהֵב, הָיָה חִירָם לְדָוִד--כָּל-הַיָּמִים.  {ס}
And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, for he had heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father; for Hiram had always loved David.note
Y Hiram rey de Tiro envió sus siervos a Salomón, porque había oído que lo habían ungido rey en lugar de su padre; porque Hiram siempre había amado a David.
16
וַיִּשְׁלַח שְׁלֹמֹה, אֶל-חִירָם לֵאמֹר.
And Solomon sent to Hiram, saying:note
Y Salomón envió a Hiram, diciendo:
17
אַתָּה יָדַעְתָּ אֶת-דָּוִד אָבִי, כִּי לֹא יָכֹל לִבְנוֹת בַּיִת לְשֵׁם יְהוָה אֱלֹהָיו, מִפְּנֵי הַמִּלְחָמָה, אֲשֶׁר סְבָבֻהוּ--עַד תֵּת-יְהוָה אֹתָם, תַּחַת כַּפּוֹת רגלו רַגְלָי.
‘You know that David my father was not able to build a house for the name of Jehovah his God, because of the war that surrounded him, until Jehovah put them under the soles of my feet.note
«Tú sabes que David mi padre no pudo edificar casa para el nombre de Jehová su Dios, a causa de la guerra que lo rodeaba, hasta que Jehová los puso bajo las plantas de mis pies.
18
וְעַתָּה, הֵנִיחַ יְהוָה אֱלֹהַי לִי מִסָּבִיב:  אֵין שָׂטָן, וְאֵין פֶּגַע רָע.
But now Jehovah my God has given me rest all around; there is no adversary, and no evil occurrence.note
Pero ahora Jehová mi Dios me ha dado reposo alrededor; no hay adversario, ni mal suceso.
19
וְהִנְנִי אֹמֵר--לִבְנוֹת בַּיִת, לְשֵׁם יְהוָה אֱלֹהָי:  כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה, אֶל-דָּוִד אָבִי לֵאמֹר, בִּנְךָ אֲשֶׁר אֶתֵּן תַּחְתֶּיךָ עַל-כִּסְאֶךָ, הוּא-יִבְנֶה הַבַּיִת לִשְׁמִי.
And look, I am saying to build a house for the name of Jehovah my God, as Jehovah spoke to David my father, saying: “Your son, whom I will set in your place on your throne, he shall build the house for my name.”note
Y mira, yo digo que edificaré casa para el nombre de Jehová mi Dios, como habló Jehová a David mi padre, diciendo: “Tu hijo, a quien pondré en tu lugar sobre tu trono, él edificará la casa para mi nombre”.
20
וְעַתָּה צַוֵּה וְיִכְרְתוּ-לִי אֲרָזִים מִן-הַלְּבָנוֹן, וַעֲבָדַי יִהְיוּ עִם-עֲבָדֶיךָ, וּשְׂכַר עֲבָדֶיךָ אֶתֵּן לְךָ, כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר תֹּאמֵר:  כִּי אַתָּה יָדַעְתָּ, כִּי אֵין בָּנוּ אִישׁ יֹדֵעַ לִכְרָת-עֵצִים--כַּצִּדֹנִים.
And now, command, and let them fell cedars for me from Lebanon; and my servants shall be with your servants, and the wages of your servants I will give you according to all that you say; for you know that there is not among us a man who knows how to fell timber like the Sidonians.’note
Y ahora, manda que corten para mí cedros del Líbano; y mis siervos estarán con tus siervos, y el salario de tus siervos te lo daré conforme a todo lo que digas; porque tú sabes que no hay entre nosotros hombre que sepa cortar madera como los sidonios».
21
וַיְהִי כִּשְׁמֹעַ חִירָם, אֶת-דִּבְרֵי שְׁלֹמֹה--וַיִּשְׂמַח מְאֹד; וַיֹּאמֶר, בָּרוּךְ יְהוָה הַיּוֹם, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן לְדָוִד בֵּן חָכָם, עַל-הָעָם הָרָב הַזֶּה.
And it was, when Hiram heard the words of Solomon, that he rejoiced greatly; and he said: ‘Blessed be Jehovah this day, who has given to David a wise son over this great people.’note
Y sucedió que, cuando Hiram oyó las palabras de Salomón, se alegró mucho; y dijo: «Bendito sea Jehová este día, que ha dado a David un hijo sabio sobre este pueblo grande».
22
וַיִּשְׁלַח חִירָם, אֶל-שְׁלֹמֹה לֵאמֹר, שָׁמַעְתִּי, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-שָׁלַחְתָּ אֵלָי; אֲנִי אֶעֱשֶׂה אֶת-כָּל-חֶפְצְךָ, בַּעֲצֵי אֲרָזִים וּבַעֲצֵי בְרוֹשִׁים.
And Hiram sent to Solomon, saying: ‘I have heard what you sent to me; I will do all your desire in the timbers of cedar and in the timbers of cypress.note
Y Hiram envió a Salomón, diciendo: «He oído lo que me enviaste; yo haré todo tu deseo en cuanto a las maderas de cedro y las maderas de ciprés.
23
עֲבָדַי יֹרִדוּ מִן-הַלְּבָנוֹן יָמָּה, וַאֲנִי אֲשִׂימֵם דֹּבְרוֹת בַּיָּם עַד-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר-תִּשְׁלַח אֵלַי וְנִפַּצְתִּים שָׁם--וְאַתָּה תִשָּׂא; וְאַתָּה תַּעֲשֶׂה אֶת-חֶפְצִי, לָתֵת לֶחֶם בֵּיתִי.
My servants shall bring them down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will make them into rafts in the sea to the place that you send me, and I will break them up there, and you shall carry them off; and you shall do my desire, to give bread to my household.’note
Mis siervos las bajarán del Líbano al mar, y yo las pondré en balsas en el mar hasta el lugar que me envíes, y allí las desharé, y tú las llevarás; y tú harás mi deseo, dando pan a mi casa».
24
וַיְהִי חִירוֹם נֹתֵן לִשְׁלֹמֹה, עֲצֵי אֲרָזִים וַעֲצֵי בְרוֹשִׁים--כָּל-חֶפְצוֹ.
And Hiram was giving Solomon timbers of cedar and timbers of cypress, all his desire.note
Y Hiram daba a Salomón maderas de cedro y maderas de ciprés, todo su deseo.
25
וּשְׁלֹמֹה נָתַן לְחִירָם עֶשְׂרִים אֶלֶף כֹּר חִטִּים, מַכֹּלֶת לְבֵיתוֹ, וְעֶשְׂרִים כֹּר, שֶׁמֶן כָּתִית:  כֹּה-יִתֵּן שְׁלֹמֹה לְחִירָם, שָׁנָה בְשָׁנָה.  {פ}
And Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand kor of wheat, food for his household, and twenty kor of beaten oil; so Solomon gave to Hiram year by year.note
Y Salomón dio a Hiram veinte mil coros de trigo, alimento para su casa, y veinte coros de aceite batido; así daba Salomón a Hiram año tras año.
26
וַיהוָה, נָתַן חָכְמָה לִשְׁלֹמֹה, כַּאֲשֶׁר, דִּבֶּר-לוֹ; וַיְהִי שָׁלֹם, בֵּין חִירָם וּבֵין שְׁלֹמֹה, וַיִּכְרְתוּ בְרִית, שְׁנֵיהֶם.
And Jehovah gave wisdom to Solomon, as he spoke to him; and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a covenant.note
Y Jehová dio sabiduría a Salomón, como le había hablado; y hubo paz entre Hiram y Salomón, y los dos hicieron pacto.
27
וַיַּעַל הַמֶּלֶךְ שְׁלֹמֹה מַס, מִכָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיְהִי הַמַּס, שְׁלֹשִׁים אֶלֶף אִישׁ.
And King Solomon raised forced labor out of all Israel; and the forced labor was thirty thousand men.note
Y el rey Salomón levantó trabajos forzados de todo Israel; y los trabajos forzados fueron treinta mil hombres.
28
וַיִּשְׁלָחֵם לְבָנוֹנָה, עֲשֶׂרֶת אֲלָפִים בַּחֹדֶשׁ חֲלִיפוֹת--חֹדֶשׁ יִהְיוּ בַלְּבָנוֹן, שְׁנַיִם חֳדָשִׁים בְּבֵיתוֹ; וַאֲדֹנִירָם, עַל-הַמַּס.  {ס}
And he sent them to Lebanon, ten thousand a month in shifts: a month they were in Lebanon, two months at home; and Adoniram was over the forced labor.note
Y los envió al Líbano, diez mil al mes por turnos: un mes estaban en el Líbano, dos meses en su casa; y Adoniram estaba sobre los trabajos forzados.
29
וַיְהִי לִשְׁלֹמֹה שִׁבְעִים אֶלֶף, נֹשֵׂא סַבָּל; וּשְׁמֹנִים אֶלֶף, חֹצֵב בָּהָר.
And Solomon had seventy thousand who carried burdens, and eighty thousand who cut in the mountain,note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · 1 Kings 5 Solomon’s dominion “from the River” — tribute, a day’s bread, peace, and every man under his vine and under his fig tree

“From the River.” “And Solomon was ruler over all the kingdoms, from the River to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt”: the Hebrew says only hannahar, “the River”, with no name, and the Euphrates is the great river of the promise to Abram, “from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates” (Genesis 15:18, now on these pages). The ASV has “from the River unto the land of the Philistines”, the NWT 1984 “from the River to the land of the Philistines”, the NIV “from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines”, and the TLB “from the Euphrates River to the land of the Philistines”. This chapter begins at what English calls 1 Kings 4:21; the Hebrew numbering starts it here, and everything in these notes cites the Hebrew.

Tribute. “They brought tribute and served Solomon all the days of his life”: “bringing minchah” stands only here, and minchah is the word of Moab’s servitude to David, “bearers of tribute” (2 Samuel 8:2, now on these pages). The ASV has “they brought tribute, and served Solomon”, the NIV “brought tribute”, the KJV “they brought presents”, the NWT 1984 “bringing gifts”, and the TLB “sent taxes”.

A day’s bread. “Thirty kor of fine flour and sixty kor of meal; ten fattened oxen, and twenty pasture-fed oxen, and a hundred sheep — besides deer and gazelle and fallow deer and fattened fowl”: the NIV has “thirty cors of the finest flour and sixty cors of meal”, the TLB “195 bushels of fine flour”, the ASV “thirty measures of fine flour, and threescore measures of meal”, and the NWT 1984 “thirty cor measures of fine flour”. “Deer and gazelle and fallow deer” stands in two verses, here and in the list of clean animals, “deer, gazelle, fallow deer” (Deuteronomy 14:5, now on these pages). The KJV has “harts, and roebucks, and fallowdeer”, the ASV “harts, and gazelles, and roebucks”, and the NIV “deer, gazelles, roebucks”. The last word, “fattened fowl”, barburim, stands only here, and it is uncertain. The ASV has “fatted fowl”, the NIV “choice fowl”, the TLB “plump fowl”, and the NWT 1984 “fattened cuckoos”.

Peace, and the vine and the fig tree. “For he ruled over all the region beyond the River, from Tiphsah to Gaza”: the verb is radah, “rule”, the verb of the human given dominion over the fish of the sea (Genesis 1:26, now on these pages), and Tiphsah stands in two verses, here and in a later king’s sack of it (2 Kings 15:16). The ASV has “on this side the River” and the NIV “west of the Euphrates River”; the Hebrew is “beyond”, and this page keeps it. “Each man under his vine and under his fig tree, from Dan to Beersheba”: the ASV has “dwelt safely”, the NWT 1984 “continued to dwell in security”, the NIV “lived in safety”, and the Douay “dwelt without any fear”; the Hebrew is lavetach, the word of “you shall dwell in the land in safety” (Leviticus 25:18, now on these pages). “From Dan to Beersheba” is the span of David’s count (2 Samuel 24:2, now on these pages), which ended in a pestilence from Dan to Beersheba (24:15).

Verses 6–8 · 1 Kings 5 Forty thousand stalls — horses, a king’s table in twelve months, and barley and straw

Forty thousand stalls. “And Solomon had forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen”: the ASV has “forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen”, the KJV the same, and the NWT 1984 “forty thousand stalls of horses for his chariots and twelve thousand horsemen”. The NIV has “four thousand stalls for chariot horses, and twelve thousand horses”, and the TLB “forty thousand chariot horses”. The Law says of a king, “he shall not multiply horses for himself” (Deuteronomy 17:16, now on these pages), and the page does not say how the two stand together.

A month each. “And these officers sustained King Solomon, and all who came near to King Solomon’s table, each man in his month”: the verb is kilkel, “sustained”, as of the twelve officers (1 Kings 4:7, now on these pages). The ASV has “provided victuals”, the NWT 1984 “supplied food”, the NIV “supplied provisions”, and the KJV “provided victual”.

“They let nothing be lacking.” “They let nothing be lacking” stands only here. The ASV has “they let nothing be lacking”, the KJV “they lacked nothing”, the NWT 1984 “they left nothing lacking”, and the NIV “They saw to it that nothing was lacking”.

Barley, straw, and a beast of the stable. “And the barley and the straw for the horses and the swift steeds they brought to the place where it was to be, each man according to his charge”: the word for the second animal is rekhesh, and the ASV has “swift steeds”, the KJV “dromedaries”, the NIV “the other horses”, and the NWT 1984 “teams of horses”. The word also stands where Lachish is told to harness the chariot to it (Micah 1:13, now on these pages).

Verses 9–14 · 1 Kings 5 “Breadth of heart like the sand” — wisdom, the sons of the east, three thousand proverbs, and cedar to hyssop

Breadth of heart. “And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding, very much, and breadth of heart like the sand that is on the shore of the sea”: “like the sand that is on the shore of the sea” stands in four verses, here, in the promise to Abraham (Genesis 22:17), in the Canaanite host (Joshua 11:4) and in the Philistine host (1 Samuel 13:5, all now on these pages); here it measures a heart. The ASV has “largeness of heart”, the NWT 1984 “a broadness of heart”, the NIV “a breadth of understanding as measureless as the sand”, and the TLB “a mind with broad interests”.

The sons of the east. “Solomon’s wisdom was greater than the wisdom of all the sons of the east, and than all the wisdom of Egypt”: the ASV has “all the children of the east”, the NWT 1984 “all the Orientals”, the NIV “all the people of the East”, and the KJV “the east country”. The sons of the east are where Jacob went (Genesis 29:1) and Job is the greatest of them (Job 1:3, both now on these pages). Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Darda are named as “the sons of Mahol”: “the sons of Mahol” stands only here; a Zerah has sons named Zimri, Ethan, Heman, Calcol and Dara (1 Chronicles 2:6), and an Ethan the Ezrahite has a psalm (Psalm 89:1). The ASV has “Ethan the Ezrahite, and Heman, and Calcol, and Darda, the sons of Mahol”, and the NWT 1984 “Eʹthan the Ezʹra·hite and Heʹman and Calʹcol and Darʹda the sons of Maʹhol”.

Proverbs and songs. “And he spoke three thousand proverbs; and his songs were a thousand and five”: the ASV has “three thousand proverbs; and his songs were a thousand and five”, the NIV “three thousand proverbs and his songs numbered a thousand and five”, the TLB “3,000 proverbs and wrote 1,005 songs”, and the Douay “three thousand parables”. The book that begins “the proverbs of Solomon” is the shelf of them that survives (Proverbs 1:1, now on these pages).

Cedar to hyssop, and to hear. “He spoke of the trees, from the cedar that is in Lebanon to the hyssop that comes out of the wall; and he spoke of the beasts, and of the birds, and of the creeping things, and of the fish”: the cedar and the hyssop are the two woods of the cleansing, “cedar wood, and scarlet, and hyssop” (Leviticus 14:4, now on these pages), and the four kinds run in the order of the dominion over the sea and the sky (Genesis 1:26). The ASV has “the hyssop that springeth out of the wall”, the NIV “the hyssop that grows out of walls”, the TLB “the tiny hyssop which grows in cracks in the wall”, and the NWT 1984 “the hyssop that is coming forth on the wall”. “And they came from all the peoples to hear the wisdom of Solomon”: the ASV has “to hear the wisdom of Solomon, from all kings of the earth”, and the NIV “people came to listen to Solomon’s wisdom, sent by all the kings of the world”.

Verses 15–20 · 1 Kings 5 Hiram, “lover of David” — a house for the name, rest on every side, and cedars from Lebanon

A lover of David. “And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon, for he had heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father; for Hiram had always loved David”: Hiram sent messengers and cedar to David himself (2 Samuel 5:11, now on these pages). The ASV has “Hiram was ever a lover of David”, the NWT 1984 “a lover of David Hiʹram had always proved to be”, the NIV “he had always been on friendly terms with David”, and the Douay “Hiram had always been David’s friend”.

No adversary, and no evil occurrence. “Jehovah my God has given me rest all around; there is no adversary, and no evil occurrence”: the ASV has “neither adversary, nor evil occurrence”, the Douay “no adversary nor evil occurrence”, the NWT 1984 “no resister, and there is nothing bad happening”, and the NIV “no adversary or disaster”. David could not build “because of the war that surrounded him, until Jehovah put them under the soles of my feet”: the ASV has “for the wars which were about him on every side”, and the NWT 1984 “because of the warfare with which they surrounded him”.

“The house for my name.” “Your son, whom I will set in your place on your throne, he shall build the house for my name”: Solomon quotes the promise that David’s son “shall build a house for my name” (2 Samuel 7:13, now on these pages), and he quotes it with the article. “He shall build the house” stands in three verses, here, in Solomon’s own recital (1 Kings 8:19) and in its retelling in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 6:9); the promise as Nathan gave it reads “a house”. The ASV has “he shall build the house for my name”, the KJV “he shall build an house unto my name”, the NWT 1984 “he is the one that will build the house”, and the NIV “will build the temple for my Name”.

Who knows how to fell timber. “Command, and let them fell cedars for me from Lebanon; and my servants shall be with your servants”: the ASV has “command thou that they cut me cedar-trees out of Lebanon”, the KJV “hew me cedar trees”, the NWT 1984 “command that they cut for me cedars from Lebanon”, and the NIV “give orders that cedars of Lebanon be cut for me”. “There is not among us a man who knows how to fell timber like the Sidonians”: the NIV has “skilled in felling timber as the Sidonians”, and the ASV “like unto the Sidonians”.

Verses 21–26 · 1 Kings 5 “Blessed be Jehovah this day” — cedar and cypress by rafts, wheat and oil, wisdom, and a covenant of peace

Hiram’s blessing. “Blessed be Jehovah this day, who has given to David a wise son over this great people”: the blessing is Jethro’s, “Blessed be Jehovah, who has delivered you” (Exodus 18:10, now on these pages), a foreigner’s praise of the God of Israel. The ASV has “Blessed be Jehovah this day, who hath given unto David a wise son over this great people”, the NWT 1984 “Blessed is Jehovah today in that he has given David a wise son”, and the NIV “Praise be to the Lord today”.

Cedar, cypress, and rafts. “I will do all your desire in the timbers of cedar and in the timbers of cypress”: the ASV has “timber of cedar, and concerning timber of fir”, the NIV “cedar and juniper logs”, the TLB “both cedar and cypress”, and the NWT 1984 “timbers of juniper trees”. “I will make them into rafts in the sea to the place that you send me, and I will break them up there, and you shall carry them off”: the ASV has “I will make them into rafts to go by sea”, the KJV “convey them by sea in floats”, and the NWT 1984 “log rafts”. The word chefets, “desire”, runs through the exchange: Hiram’s desire is wood, and Solomon’s to “do my desire, to give bread to my household”.

Wheat and oil. “Twenty thousand kor of wheat, food for his household, and twenty kor of beaten oil”: the ASV has “twenty thousand measures of wheat” and “twenty measures of pure oil”, the NWT 1984 “twenty cor measures of beaten-out oil”, and the TLB “96 gallons of pure olive oil”. The NIV has “twenty thousand baths of pressed olive oil”, a different number, and the Chronicler’s own account has twenty thousand baths of oil (2 Chronicles 2:9).

Wisdom, peace, and a covenant. “And Jehovah gave wisdom to Solomon, as he spoke to him; and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a covenant”: the name is Shelomoh and the peace shalom, and the two meet in the verse. The ASV has “they two made a league together”, the NWT 1984 “both of them proceeded to conclude a covenant”, the NIV “the two of them made a treaty”, and the TLB “a formal alliance of peace”.

Verses 27–32 · 1 Kings 5 The forced labor — thirty thousand, seventy thousand who carried burdens, eighty thousand who cut, and the Gebalites

Forced labor out of all Israel. “And King Solomon raised forced labor out of all Israel; and the forced labor was thirty thousand men”: the word is mas, “forced labor”, the one Adoniram is “over” (1 Kings 4:6, now on these pages), and the first Egyptian “chiefs of forced labor” set burdens on Israel (Exodus 1:11, now on these pages). The ASV has “raised a levy out of all Israel”, the NWT 1984 “those conscripted for forced labor”, the NIV “conscripted laborers from all Israel”, and the TLB “drafted thirty thousand laborers”.

A month, and two months. “Ten thousand a month in shifts: a month they were in Lebanon, two months at home”: the ASV has “ten thousand a month by courses”, the NIV “in shifts of ten thousand a month”, and the NWT 1984 “in shifts of ten thousand a month”.

Burden-bearers and cutters. “Seventy thousand who carried burdens, and eighty thousand who cut in the mountain”: the first are the “burdens” of the Egyptian chiefs (Exodus 1:11), the second are those who cut, as the Gibeonites “became cutters of wood” (Joshua 9:21, now on these pages), and “from the one who cuts your wood to the one who draws your water” (Deuteronomy 29:10, now on these pages). The ASV has “threescore and ten thousand that bare burdens, and fourscore thousand that were hewers in the mountains”, the NIV “seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hills”, and the NWT 1984 “eighty thousand cutters in the mountain”. “Besides the chiefs of the officers of Solomon who were over the work, three thousand three hundred, those who ruled over the people”: “the chiefs of the officers” stands in two verses, here and at the later list of them (1 Kings 9:23). The NIV has “thirty-three hundred foremen”, and the ASV “chief officers”.

Stones, and the Gebalites. “And the king commanded, and they quarried great stones, costly stones, to lay the foundation of the house — hewn stones”: the ASV has “hewed out great stones, costly stones”, the NIV “large blocks of high-grade stone”, and the NWT 1984 “quarry great stones, expensive stones”. “And Solomon’s builders and Hiram’s builders and the Gebalites cut them”: “the Gebalites” stands only here, from Gebal, and the land of the Gebalites is among the land still to be taken (Joshua 13:5, now on these pages). The ASV has “the Gebalites did fashion them”, the NWT 1984 “the Geʹbal·ites did the cutting”, the NIV “workers from Byblos”, and the Douay “the Giblians”. The verb “cut”, vayyifselu, stands only here in this form; its root is the one of “Cut for yourself two tablets of stone” (Exodus 34:1, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “ruler” and “rule”, as in Genesis 1:26; “tribute” for minchah, as in 2 Samuel 8:2; “deer, gazelle, fallow deer”, as in Deuteronomy 14:5; “sustained” for kilkel, as in 1 Kings 4:7; “they let nothing be lacking”; “breadth of heart”; “the sons of the east”; “forced labor”, as in 1 Kings 4:6 and Exodus 1:11; “who carried burdens” and “who cut”; “the Gebalites”; and “the house for my name”, with the article; with the formulas that differ in the notes.

Echoes paid. The promised land “from the river of Egypt to the great river” (Genesis 15:18); Moab’s tribute (2 Samuel 8:2); the clean animals (Deuteronomy 14:5); the king who is not to multiply horses (Deuteronomy 17:16); the officers who sustained the king (1 Kings 4:7); the dominion over the sea and sky (Genesis 1:26); the sand of the promise, of Canaan’s host and of the Philistines’ (Genesis 22:17; Joshua 11:4; 1 Samuel 13:5); the land in safety (Leviticus 25:18); David’s count from Dan to Beersheba (2 Samuel 24:2, 15); the sons of the east (Genesis 29:1; Job 1:3); the cedar and the hyssop of the cleansing (Leviticus 14:4); Hiram’s cedar to David (2 Samuel 5:11); the promise of a house for the name (2 Samuel 7:13); Jethro’s blessing (Exodus 18:10); the chiefs of forced labor and the burdens (Exodus 1:11); the Gibeonites who cut wood (Joshua 9:21); the land of the Gebalites (Joshua 13:5); and the tablets cut for Moses (Exodus 34:1).

Echoes planted. The house itself, the cedar and the foundation of hewn stones, which the next chapter begins to build; the “chiefs of the officers” (1 Kings 9:23); the Chronicler’s numbers for the horses and the oil (2 Chronicles 2:9); and the sons of Mahol (1 Chronicles 2:6; Psalm 89:1).

Next in this book: 1 Kings 6 — the house is built: its measures, its cedar, its gold and its cherubim, and the word of Jehovah that comes to Solomon in the middle of the building.
