Samson goes to a prostitute in Gaza; the Gazites wait for him at the gate, and at midnight he carries off the doors, posts and bar to the hill that faces Hebron. In the valley of Sorek he loves Delilah, and the five lords of the Philistines each promise her eleven hundred pieces of silver to learn where his strength lies. Three times he lies to her: fresh bowstrings, new ropes, his seven locks woven into the loom. Pressed day after day, he tells her all his heart; she has him shaved on her knees, Jehovah turns away from him, and the Philistines gouge out his eyes and set him to grind in the prison at Gaza. At a feast for Dagon they bring him out to make sport; he prays, 'Lord Jehovah, remember me', grasps the two middle pillars, and pulls the house down on the lords and some three thousand on the roof. His brothers bury him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah. He had judged Israel twenty years.
Within the period of the judges, with no date the chapter gives for itself; it closes Samson's twenty years, which fall inside the Philistine forty of Judges 13:1. The early/late question applies (c. 1200 BC on the early reckoning this project foregrounds, c. 1100 BC on the late).

1
וַיֵּלֶךְ שִׁמְשׁוֹן, עַזָּתָה; וַיַּרְא-שָׁם אִשָּׁה זוֹנָה, וַיָּבֹא אֵלֶיהָ.
Samson went to Gaza, and he saw a prostitute there, and went in to her.note
Sansón fue a Gaza, y vio allí a una prostituta, y entró a ella.
2
לַעַזָּתִים לֵאמֹר, בָּא שִׁמְשׁוֹן הֵנָּה, וַיָּסֹבּוּ וַיֶּאֶרְבוּ-לוֹ כָל-הַלַּיְלָה, בְּשַׁעַר הָעִיר; וַיִּתְחָרְשׁוּ כָל-הַלַּיְלָה לֵאמֹר, עַד-אוֹר הַבֹּקֶר וַהֲרַגְנֻהוּ.
[Word came] to the Gazites: ‘Samson has come here!’ And they surrounded the place and lay in wait for him all night at the city gate. They kept quiet all night, saying: ‘Until morning light — then we will kill him.’note
[Se avisó] a los de Gaza: «¡Sansón ha venido aquí!». Y rodearon el lugar y lo acecharon toda la noche a la puerta de la ciudad. Estuvieron callados toda la noche, diciendo: «Hasta la luz de la mañana; entonces lo mataremos».
3
וַיִּשְׁכַּב שִׁמְשׁוֹן, עַד-חֲצִי הַלַּיְלָה, וַיָּקָם בַּחֲצִי הַלַּיְלָה וַיֶּאֱחֹז בְּדַלְתוֹת שַׁעַר-הָעִיר וּבִשְׁתֵּי הַמְּזֻזוֹת, וַיִּסָּעֵם עִם-הַבְּרִיחַ וַיָּשֶׂם עַל-כְּתֵפָיו; וַיַּעֲלֵם אֶל-רֹאשׁ הָהָר, אֲשֶׁר עַל-פְּנֵי חֶבְרוֹן.  {פ}
Samson lay until midnight; at midnight he got up, took hold of the doors of the city gate and the two posts, pulled them up, bar and all, put them on his shoulders, and carried them up to the top of the hill that faces Hebron.note
Sansón se quedó acostado hasta la medianoche; a la medianoche se levantó, agarró las hojas de la puerta de la ciudad y los dos postes, los arrancó con cerrojo y todo, se los echó a los hombros y los subió a la cumbre del monte que está frente a Hebrón.
4
וַיְהִי, אַחֲרֵי-כֵן, וַיֶּאֱהַב אִשָּׁה, בְּנַחַל שֹׂרֵק; וּשְׁמָהּ, דְּלִילָה.
After this he loved a woman in the valley of Sorek, whose name was Delilah.note
Después de esto amó a una mujer en el valle de Sorec, que se llamaba Dalila.
5
וַיַּעֲלוּ אֵלֶיהָ סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, וַיֹּאמְרוּ לָהּ פַּתִּי אוֹתוֹ וּרְאִי בַּמֶּה כֹּחוֹ גָדוֹל, וּבַמֶּה נוּכַל לוֹ, וַאֲסַרְנוּהוּ לְעַנּוֹתוֹ; וַאֲנַחְנוּ, נִתַּן-לָךְ, אִישׁ, אֶלֶף וּמֵאָה כָּסֶף.
The lords of the Philistines came up to her and said to her: ‘Entice him, and see where his great strength lies, and how we can overpower him, so that we may bind him to afflict him; and we will each give you eleven hundred pieces of silver.’note
Los príncipes de los filisteos subieron a ella y le dijeron: «Sedúcelo, y mira en qué está su gran fuerza, y cómo podremos vencerlo, para atarlo y afligirlo; y cada uno de nosotros te dará mil cien piezas de plata».
6
וַתֹּאמֶר דְּלִילָה, אֶל-שִׁמְשׁוֹן, הַגִּידָה-נָּא לִי, בַּמֶּה כֹּחֲךָ גָדוֹל; וּבַמֶּה תֵאָסֵר, לְעַנּוֹתֶךָ.
Delilah said to Samson: ‘Tell me, please, where your great strength lies, and with what you could be bound to afflict you.’note
Dalila dijo a Sansón: «Declárame, por favor, en qué está tu gran fuerza, y con qué podrías ser atado para afligirte».
7
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלֶיהָ, שִׁמְשׁוֹן, אִם-יַאַסְרֻנִי בְּשִׁבְעָה יְתָרִים לַחִים, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-חֹרָבוּ--וְחָלִיתִי וְהָיִיתִי, כְּאַחַד הָאָדָם.
Samson said to her: ‘If they bind me with seven fresh bowstrings that have not been dried, I will become weak and be like any other man.’note
Sansón le dijo: «Si me atan con siete cuerdas de arco frescas, que no se hayan secado, me debilitaré y seré como cualquier otro hombre».
8
וַיַּעֲלוּ-לָהּ סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, שִׁבְעָה יְתָרִים לַחִים--אֲשֶׁר לֹא-חֹרָבוּ; וַתַּאַסְרֵהוּ, בָּהֶם.
The lords of the Philistines brought up to her seven fresh bowstrings that had not been dried, and she bound him with them.note
Los príncipes de los filisteos le subieron siete cuerdas de arco frescas, que no se habían secado, y ella lo ató con ellas.
9
וְהָאֹרֵב, יֹשֵׁב לָהּ בַּחֶדֶר, וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, פְּלִשְׁתִּים עָלֶיךָ שִׁמְשׁוֹן; וַיְנַתֵּק, אֶת-הַיְתָרִים, כַּאֲשֶׁר יִנָּתֵק פְּתִיל-הַנְּעֹרֶת בַּהֲרִיחוֹ אֵשׁ, וְלֹא נוֹדַע כֹּחוֹ.
Men lay in ambush, sitting in her inner room. And she said to him: ‘The Philistines are upon you, Samson!’ And he snapped the bowstrings as a strand of tow snaps when it smells the fire. So his strength was not known.note
Unos hombres estaban al acecho, sentados en la habitación interior de ella. Y ella le dijo: «¡Los filisteos sobre ti, Sansón!». Y él rompió las cuerdas como se rompe un hilo de estopa cuando huele el fuego. Así no se supo su fuerza.
10
וַתֹּאמֶר דְּלִילָה, אֶל-שִׁמְשׁוֹן, הִנֵּה הֵתַלְתָּ בִּי, וַתְּדַבֵּר אֵלַי כְּזָבִים; עַתָּה הַגִּידָה-נָּא לִי, בַּמֶּה תֵּאָסֵר.
Delilah said to Samson: ‘Look — you have mocked me and told me lies. Now tell me, please, with what you could be bound.’note
Dalila dijo a Sansón: «Mira —te has burlado de mí y me has dicho mentiras. Ahora declárame, por favor, con qué podrías ser atado».
11
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלֶיהָ--אִם-אָסוֹר יַאַסְרוּנִי בַּעֲבֹתִים חֲדָשִׁים, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-נַעֲשָׂה בָהֶם מְלָאכָה:  וְחָלִיתִי וְהָיִיתִי, כְּאַחַד הָאָדָם.
He said to her: ‘If they bind me fast with new ropes that have never been used for work, I will become weak and be like any other man.’note
Él le dijo: «Si me atan bien con cuerdas nuevas, que nunca se hayan usado para trabajo, me debilitaré y seré como cualquier otro hombre».
12
וַתִּקַּח דְּלִילָה עֲבֹתִים חֲדָשִׁים וַתַּאַסְרֵהוּ בָהֶם, וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו פְּלִשְׁתִּים עָלֶיךָ שִׁמְשׁוֹן, וְהָאֹרֵב, יֹשֵׁב בֶּחָדֶר; וַיְנַתְּקֵם מֵעַל זְרֹעֹתָיו, כַּחוּט.
So Delilah took new ropes and bound him with them, and said to him: ‘The Philistines are upon you, Samson!’ — and the men lay in ambush in the inner room. And he snapped them off his arms like a thread.note
Dalila tomó cuerdas nuevas y lo ató con ellas, y le dijo: «¡Los filisteos sobre ti, Sansón!» —y los hombres estaban al acecho en la habitación interior. Y él las rompió de sus brazos como un hilo.
13
וַתֹּאמֶר דְּלִילָה אֶל-שִׁמְשׁוֹן, עַד-הֵנָּה הֵתַלְתָּ בִּי וַתְּדַבֵּר אֵלַי כְּזָבִים--הַגִּידָה לִּי, בַּמֶּה תֵּאָסֵר; וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלֶיהָ--אִם-תַּאַרְגִי אֶת-שֶׁבַע מַחְלְפוֹת רֹאשִׁי, עִם-הַמַּסָּכֶת.
Delilah said to Samson: ‘Until now you have mocked me and told me lies. Tell me with what you could be bound.’ He said to her: ‘If you weave the seven locks of my head into the web.’note
Dalila dijo a Sansón: «Hasta ahora te has burlado de mí y me has dicho mentiras. Declárame con qué podrías ser atado». Él le dijo: «Si tejes las siete trenzas de mi cabeza con la tela».
14
וַתִּתְקַע, בַּיָּתֵד, וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, פְּלִשְׁתִּים עָלֶיךָ שִׁמְשׁוֹן; וַיִּיקַץ, מִשְּׁנָתוֹ, וַיִּסַּע אֶת-הַיְתַד הָאֶרֶג, וְאֶת-הַמַּסָּכֶת.
She drove in the peg and said to him: ‘The Philistines are upon you, Samson!’ He woke from his sleep and pulled out the peg of the loom, and the web.note
Ella clavó la estaca y le dijo: «¡Los filisteos sobre ti, Sansón!». Él despertó de su sueño y arrancó la estaca del telar, y la tela.
15
וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, אֵיךְ תֹּאמַר אֲהַבְתִּיךְ, וְלִבְּךָ, אֵין אִתִּי:  זֶה שָׁלֹשׁ פְּעָמִים, הֵתַלְתָּ בִּי, וְלֹא-הִגַּדְתָּ לִּי, בַּמֶּה כֹּחֲךָ גָדוֹל.
She said to him: ‘How can you say “I love you” when your heart is not with me? These three times you have mocked me and have not told me where your great strength lies.’note
Ella le dijo: «¿Cómo puedes decir “Te amo”, si tu corazón no está conmigo? Estas tres veces te has burlado de mí, y no me has declarado en qué está tu gran fuerza».
16
וַיְהִי כִּי-הֵצִיקָה לּוֹ בִדְבָרֶיהָ, כָּל-הַיָּמִים--וַתְּאַלְצֵהוּ; וַתִּקְצַר נַפְשׁוֹ, לָמוּת.
And as she pressed him with her words day after day, and urged him, his soul was cut short, to the point of death.note
Y como ella lo apremiaba con sus palabras día tras día, y lo acosaba, su alma se acortó hasta la muerte.
17
וַיַּגֶּד-לָהּ אֶת-כָּל-לִבּוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר לָהּ מוֹרָה לֹא-עָלָה עַל-רֹאשִׁי--כִּי-נְזִיר אֱלֹהִים אֲנִי, מִבֶּטֶן אִמִּי; אִם-גֻּלַּחְתִּי וְסָר מִמֶּנִּי כֹחִי, וְחָלִיתִי וְהָיִיתִי כְּכָל-הָאָדָם.
He told her all his heart and said to her: ‘No razor has ever touched my head, for I have been a Nazirite of God from my mother’s womb. If I am shaved, my strength will leave me, and I will become weak and be like any man.’note
Le declaró todo su corazón y le dijo: «Navaja nunca ha pasado sobre mi cabeza, porque soy nazareo de Dios desde el vientre de mi madre. Si me rapan, mi fuerza se apartará de mí, y me debilitaré y seré como cualquier hombre».
18
וַתֵּרֶא דְלִילָה, כִּי-הִגִּיד לָהּ אֶת-כָּל-לִבּוֹ, וַתִּשְׁלַח וַתִּקְרָא לְסַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים לֵאמֹר עֲלוּ הַפַּעַם, כִּי-הִגִּיד לה לִי אֶת-כָּל-לִבּוֹ; וְעָלוּ אֵלֶיהָ סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, וַיַּעֲלוּ הַכֶּסֶף בְּיָדָם.
When Delilah saw that he had told her all his heart, she sent and called the lords of the Philistines, saying: ‘Come up this once, for he has told me all his heart.’ And the lords of the Philistines came up to her and brought the silver in their hand.note
Cuando Dalila vio que le había declarado todo su corazón, mandó llamar a los príncipes de los filisteos, diciendo: «Suban esta vez, porque me ha declarado todo su corazón». Y los príncipes de los filisteos subieron a ella y trajeron la plata en la mano.
19
וַתְּיַשְּׁנֵהוּ, עַל-בִּרְכֶּיהָ, וַתִּקְרָא לָאִישׁ, וַתְּגַלַּח אֶת-שֶׁבַע מַחְלְפוֹת רֹאשׁוֹ; וַתָּחֶל, לְעַנּוֹתוֹ, וַיָּסַר כֹּחוֹ, מֵעָלָיו.
She made him sleep on her knees, called a man, and shaved off the seven locks of his head; and she began to afflict him, and his strength left him.note
Ella lo hizo dormir sobre sus rodillas, llamó a un hombre, y le rapó las siete trenzas de la cabeza; y comenzó a afligirlo, y su fuerza se apartó de él.
20
וַתֹּאמֶר, פְּלִשְׁתִּים עָלֶיךָ שִׁמְשׁוֹן; וַיִּקַץ מִשְּׁנָתוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר אֵצֵא כְּפַעַם בְּפַעַם וְאִנָּעֵר, וְהוּא לֹא יָדַע, כִּי יְהוָה סָר מֵעָלָיו.
She said: ‘The Philistines are upon you, Samson!’ He woke from his sleep and said: ‘I will go out as time after time, and shake myself free.’ But he did not know that Jehovah had turned away from him.note
Ella dijo: «¡Los filisteos sobre ti, Sansón!». Él despertó de su sueño y dijo: «Saldré como una y otra vez, y me sacudiré». Pero no sabía que Jehová se había apartado de él.
21
וַיֹּאחֲזוּהוּ פְלִשְׁתִּים, וַיְנַקְּרוּ אֶת-עֵינָיו; וַיּוֹרִידוּ אוֹתוֹ עַזָּתָה, וַיַּאַסְרוּהוּ בַּנְחֻשְׁתַּיִם, וַיְהִי טוֹחֵן, בְּבֵית האסירים הָאֲסוּרִים.
The Philistines seized him and gouged out his eyes; they brought him down to Gaza and bound him with bronze fetters, and he became a grinder in the prison house.note
Los filisteos lo agarraron y le sacaron los ojos; lo bajaron a Gaza y lo ataron con grilletes de bronce, y llegó a ser molendero en la cárcel.
22
וַיָּחֶל שְׂעַר-רֹאשׁוֹ לְצַמֵּחַ, כַּאֲשֶׁר גֻּלָּח.  {פ}
But the hair of his head began to grow again after it had been shaved.note
Pero el cabello de su cabeza comenzó a crecer otra vez, después de que lo raparon.
23
וְסַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, נֶאֶסְפוּ לִזְבֹּחַ זֶבַח-גָּדוֹל לְדָגוֹן אֱלֹהֵיהֶם--וּלְשִׂמְחָה; וַיֹּאמְרוּ--נָתַן אֱלֹהֵינוּ בְּיָדֵנוּ, אֵת שִׁמְשׁוֹן אוֹיְבֵנוּ.
The lords of the Philistines gathered to offer a great sacrifice to Dagon their god, and to rejoice; and they said: ‘Our god has given into our hand Samson our enemy.’note
Los príncipes de los filisteos se reunieron para ofrecer un gran sacrificio a Dagón su dios, y para alegrarse; y decían: «Nuestro dios ha entregado en nuestra mano a Sansón nuestro enemigo».
24
וַיִּרְאוּ אֹתוֹ הָעָם, וַיְהַלְלוּ אֶת-אֱלֹהֵיהֶם:  כִּי אָמְרוּ, נָתַן אֱלֹהֵינוּ בְיָדֵנוּ אֶת-אוֹיְבֵנוּ, וְאֵת מַחֲרִיב אַרְצֵנוּ, וַאֲשֶׁר הִרְבָּה אֶת-חֲלָלֵינוּ.
When the people saw him, they praised their god, for they said: ‘Our god has given into our hand our enemy, the ravager of our land, who multiplied our slain.’note
Cuando el pueblo lo vio, alabaron a su dios, porque decían: «Nuestro dios ha entregado en nuestra mano a nuestro enemigo, al que asolaba nuestra tierra, al que multiplicó nuestros muertos».
25
וַיְהִי, כי טוב כְּטוֹב לִבָּם, וַיֹּאמְרוּ, קִרְאוּ לְשִׁמְשׁוֹן וִישַׂחֶק-לָנוּ; וַיִּקְרְאוּ לְשִׁמְשׁוֹן מִבֵּית האסירים הָאֲסוּרִים, וַיְצַחֵק לִפְנֵיהֶם, וַיַּעֲמִידוּ אוֹתוֹ, בֵּין הָעַמּוּדִים.
When their hearts were merry, they said: ‘Call Samson, that he may make sport for us.’ They called Samson out of the prison house, and he made sport before them; and they stood him between the pillars.note
Cuando su corazón estaba alegre, dijeron: «Llamen a Sansón, para que nos divierta». Llamaron a Sansón de la cárcel, y él los divertía delante de ellos; y lo pusieron de pie entre las columnas.
26
וַיֹּאמֶר שִׁמְשׁוֹן אֶל-הַנַּעַר הַמַּחֲזִיק בְּיָדוֹ, הַנִּיחָה אוֹתִי, והימשני וַהֲמִישֵׁנִי אֶת-הָעַמֻּדִים, אֲשֶׁר הַבַּיִת נָכוֹן עֲלֵיהֶם; וְאֶשָּׁעֵן, עֲלֵיהֶם.
Samson said to the boy who held him by the hand: ‘Let me be, and let me feel the pillars on which the house rests, so that I may lean on them.’note
Sansón dijo al muchacho que lo llevaba de la mano: «Déjame, y hazme tocar las columnas sobre las que descansa la casa, para que me apoye en ellas».
27
וְהַבַּיִת, מָלֵא הָאֲנָשִׁים וְהַנָּשִׁים, וְשָׁמָּה, כֹּל סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; וְעַל-הַגָּג, כִּשְׁלֹשֶׁת אֲלָפִים אִישׁ וְאִשָּׁה, הָרֹאִים, בִּשְׂחוֹק שִׁמְשׁוֹן.
The house was full of men and women, and all the lords of the Philistines were there; and on the roof were about three thousand men and women, watching Samson make sport.note
La casa estaba llena de hombres y mujeres, y allí estaban todos los príncipes de los filisteos; y sobre el techo había unos tres mil hombres y mujeres, que miraban cómo Sansón los divertía.
28
וַיִּקְרָא שִׁמְשׁוֹן אֶל-יְהוָה, וַיֹּאמַר:  אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה זָכְרֵנִי נָא וְחַזְּקֵנִי נָא אַךְ הַפַּעַם הַזֶּה, הָאֱלֹהִים, וְאִנָּקְמָה נְקַם-אַחַת מִשְּׁתֵי עֵינַי, מִפְּלִשְׁתִּים.
Samson called to Jehovah and said: ‘Lord Jehovah, remember me, please, and strengthen me, please, only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines with one vengeance for my two eyes.’note
Sansón clamó a Jehová y dijo: «Señor Jehová, acuérdate de mí, por favor, y fortaléceme, por favor, solo esta vez, oh Dios, para que me vengue de los filisteos con una sola venganza por mis dos ojos».
29
וַיִּלְפֹּת שִׁמְשׁוֹן אֶת-שְׁנֵי עַמּוּדֵי הַתָּוֶךְ, אֲשֶׁר הַבַּיִת נָכוֹן עֲלֵיהֶם, וַיִּסָּמֵךְ, עֲלֵיהֶם--אֶחָד בִּימִינוֹ, וְאֶחָד בִּשְׂמֹאלוֹ.
Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested and braced himself against them, one with his right hand and the other with his left.note
Sansón se asió de las dos columnas del medio, sobre las que descansaba la casa, y se apoyó en ellas, una con su mano derecha y la otra con su izquierda.
30
וַיֹּאמֶר שִׁמְשׁוֹן, תָּמוֹת נַפְשִׁי עִם-פְּלִשְׁתִּים, וַיֵּט בְּכֹחַ, וַיִּפֹּל הַבַּיִת עַל-הַסְּרָנִים וְעַל-כָּל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר-בּוֹ; וַיִּהְיוּ הַמֵּתִים, אֲשֶׁר הֵמִית בְּמוֹתוֹ, רַבִּים, מֵאֲשֶׁר הֵמִית בְּחַיָּיו.
Samson said: ‘Let me die with the Philistines!’ And he pushed with all his strength, and the house fell on the lords and on all the people who were in it. So the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those he had killed in his life.note
Sansón dijo: «¡Muera yo con los filisteos!». Y empujó con toda su fuerza, y la casa cayó sobre los príncipes y sobre todo el pueblo que estaba en ella. Así, los muertos que mató al morir fueron más que los que había matado en su vida.
31
וַיֵּרְדוּ אֶחָיו וְכָל-בֵּית אָבִיהוּ, וַיִּשְׂאוּ אֹתוֹ, וַיַּעֲלוּ וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אוֹתוֹ בֵּין צָרְעָה וּבֵין אֶשְׁתָּאֹל, בְּקֶבֶר מָנוֹחַ אָבִיו; וְהוּא שָׁפַט אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, עֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה.  {פ}
His brothers and all his father’s house came down and took him; they brought him up and buried him between Zorah and Eshtaol in the tomb of Manoah his father. He had judged Israel twenty years.note
Sus hermanos y toda la casa de su padre bajaron y lo tomaron; lo subieron y lo sepultaron entre Zora y Estaol, en el sepulcro de Manoa su padre. Él había juzgado a Israel veinte años.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Samson at Gaza — a prostitute, an ambush that keeps quiet, and the city gates carried toward Hebron

A prostitute in Gaza. Ishah zonah, “a woman, a prostitute”, are the words for Rahab, at whose house the spies lodged (Joshua 2:1), and for Jephthah’s mother (Judges 11:1), both now on these pages. Gaza is one of the cities Judah took at the book’s start (Judges 1:18, now on these pages) and Philistine again here.

A verse with no verb. The Hebrew of verse 2 begins la-‘azzatim lemor, “to the Gazites, saying”, with no word for who told them; the translation brackets “[Word came]” rather than fill the gap silently. Both Greek texts have “it was told”, and so do the KJV and the ASV, “And it was told the Gazites”. The ambush “kept quiet all night”: vayitcharshu, from charash in the sense of silence, the same letters as the “plowing” of Judges 14:18 (now on these pages).

The gates. At midnight Samson takes the doors of the city gate, the two posts and the bar, and carries them “to the top of the hill that faces Hebron”: from the coast to the hill country of Judah, uphill all the way. The KJV has “the top of an hill that is before Hebron”, the TLB “the top of the mountain across from Hebron”. Josephus, who has him lodging at one of the inns of Gaza, says he carried them on his shoulders to the mountain above Hebron (Antiquities 5.304–305, numbered 5.8.10 in Whiston).

Verses 4–5 · Delilah in the valley of Sorek — he loved her, eleven hundred pieces of silver, and “to afflict him”

“He loved a woman.” It is the first time the story says Samson loved anyone; it never says she loved him. Delilah is the only woman in Samson’s story who is given a name — his mother, the bride at Timnah and the woman at Gaza are all nameless — and the chapter does not say what people she belonged to. Josephus calls her “a woman who was a courtesan among the Philistines”, and says Samson “was already transgressing the laws of his fathers” (Antiquities 5.306, numbered 5.8.11 in Whiston).

The valley of Sorek. Sorek is the word for a choice vine, the vine of Jacob’s blessing on Judah, “his donkey’s colt to the choice vine” (Genesis 49:11, now on these pages). The Nazirite who may eat nothing from the grapevine comes back, once more, to the vines.

The lords and the price. The seranim, “lords of the Philistines”, are the five of Judges 3:3 (now on these pages). They give her the word the wedding guests gave the Timnite bride, “Entice your husband” (Judges 14:15, now on these pages), and they want him bound “to afflict him”, le’annoto, the verb of Egypt’s chiefs of forced labour, set over Israel “to afflict them” (Exodus 1:11, now on these pages). Each promises eleven hundred pieces of silver, the sum Micah’s mother will lose at Judges 17:2 (now on these pages). The NWT 1984 calls them “the axis lords”, the TLB “The five heads of the Philistine nation”, and the TLB also turns the silver into “a thousand dollars”.

Verses 6–9 · Seven fresh bowstrings — the ambush in the inner room, and “The Philistines are upon you, Samson!”

Bowstrings, or vines. Yetarim lachim are cords still “fresh”, not yet dried; yeter is the string of a bow. The NIV has “seven fresh bowstrings”, the NWT 1984 “seven still-moist sinews”, the KJV “seven green withs”. Josephus has Delilah bind him, while drunk, with seven shoots of a vine (Antiquities 5.308–309, numbered 5.8.11 in Whiston), putting both wine and the vine into a story about a Nazirite.

The inner room. The ambush is “sitting in her inner room”, ba-cheder, the room Samson was refused at his wife’s house (Judges 15:1, now on these pages). She cries, “The Philistines are upon you, Samson!” — four times in the chapter (vv. 9, 12, 14, 20), and the fourth time it is true.

Tow that smells the fire. He snaps the cords “as a strand of tow snaps when it smells the fire”. Ne’oret, “tow”, is the word of “the strong one will become tow” (Isaiah 1:31, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 keeps the smell, “when it smells fire”; the KJV has “when it toucheth the fire”, and the Douay “a thread of tow twined with spittle, when it smelleth the fire”.

Verses 10–12 · “You have mocked me” — Laban’s verb, and the new ropes of Lehi

“You have mocked me.” Delilah’s hetalta bi stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all hers (vv. 10, 13, 15). The verb is the one Jacob used of Laban, “your father has cheated me” (Genesis 31:7, now on these pages). The KJV and the ASV have “thou hast mocked me”, the NWT 1984 “you have trifled with me”.

New ropes, again. Samson names the binding he already broke at Lehi, “new ropes” (Judges 15:13, now on these pages), and snaps them “like a thread”, ka-chut, the thread Abram would not take from the king of Sodom (Genesis 14:23, now on these pages).

Verses 13–14 · The loom and the peg — a shorter Hebrew, a longer Greek, and Jael’s tent peg

A sentence that stops. In the Hebrew Samson says only, “If you weave the seven locks of my head into the web”, and in the next verse “she drove in the peg”. Both Greek texts carry more: “and fasten it with the peg into the wall, and I will be weak like any man”, and in the next verse both tell how, while he slept, Delilah wove the seven locks into the web and fastened them to the wall with the peg. The NIV takes the extra words into its text, “and tighten it with the pin, I’ll become as weak as any other man”; the KJV and the ASV keep the Hebrew’s short “If thou weavest the seven locks of my head with the web”. This translation prints the Masoretic text and gives the Greek here.

The peg. “She drove in the peg”: vattitka bayated. The verb vattitka, “and she drove in”, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, both with a peg in a woman’s hand and a man asleep: here, and Jael, who “drove the peg into his temple” while Sisera slept (Judges 4:21, now on these pages). Sisera did not wake. Samson does, and pulls out “the peg of the loom, and the web”.

Verses 15–17 · “How can you say ‘I love you’?” — his soul cut short, and all his heart told

“Your heart is not with me.” The Timnite bride’s charge comes back in Delilah’s mouth: “You only hate me; you do not love me” (Judges 14:16, now on these pages) becomes “How can you say ‘I love you’ when your heart is not with me?” She “pressed him”, hetsiqah, the verb of the bride at 14:17.

“His soul was cut short.” The words vattiqtsar nafsho stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, where Samson’s soul is cut short “to the point of death”, and Judges 10:16 (now on these pages), where Jehovah’s soul “was cut short over the misery of Israel”. The KJV and the ASV have “his soul was vexed unto death”, the NIV “he was sick to death of it”.

All his heart. The phrase “all his heart”, et kol libbo, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, verses 17 and 18, three times over. What he tells her is the angel’s word to his mother, “no razor shall touch his head … a Nazirite of God from the womb” (Judges 13:5, now on these pages), in his own mouth. He names only the hair; the wine and the dead, the other two rules of Numbers 6 (now on these pages), are not mentioned.

Verses 18–22 · The razor, “Jehovah had turned away from him”, the eyes, and the mill

She began to afflict him. Delilah calls the lords “this once”, they come with the silver, and she puts him to sleep on her knees, calls a man, and shaves the seven locks. “And she began to afflict him”: vattachel, the verb of “he will begin to save Israel” and of the spirit that “began to stir him” (Judges 13:5 and 13:25, now on these pages). He was to begin a deliverance; she begins the affliction. The KJV and the ASV have “she began to afflict him”, the NIV “and so began to subdue him”.

“As time after time.” He wakes and says, “I will go out as time after time, and shake myself free”: kefa’am befa’am, the pa’am of the spirit that first stirred him. The phrase is Balaam’s too, who did not go “as time after time before” to look for omens (Numbers 24:1, now on these pages). “But he did not know that Jehovah had turned away from him.” The narrator does not say the strength was in the hair; he says Jehovah left. The ASV has “he knew not that Jehovah was departed from him”.

Eyes, bronze, and a mill. They gouge out his eyes, bring him down to Gaza, bind him in bronze fetters, and he becomes “a grinder”, the work of “the slave girl who is behind the millstones” (Exodus 11:5, now on these pages). Eyes put out and bronze fetters come again for the last king of Judah, Zedekiah (2 Kings 25:7). Then: “the hair of his head began to grow again” — vayyachel, the verb of beginning once more.

Verses 23–27 · Dagon’s feast — a victory song, Samson made to make sport, and three thousand on the roof

The song. The lords gather to sacrifice to Dagon, the god of Beth-dagon in Judah’s town list (Joshua 15:41, now on these pages), whose image will fall before the ark (1 Samuel 5:3–4, now on these pages). Their song rhymes on -enu, “our”: natan eloheinu beyadenu et Shimshon oyevenu, “Our god has given into our hand Samson our enemy”, and the people’s version adds machariv artsenu and chalaleinu, “the ravager of our land” and “our slain”.

“Make sport.” They call for Samson “that he may make sport for us”, visachek-lanu, and “he made sport before them”, vaytsacheq: one verb in its two spellings, with s and with ts, and the roof watches him “make sport” with the s again (v. 27). The ts spelling is the root of Isaac’s name: the letters of vaytsacheq stand in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, five of them the name, “and Isaac”, and one Abraham, who “laughed” at the promise of a son (Genesis 17:17, now on these pages). The same root gives Ishmael “laughing” (Genesis 21:9) and the people who “rose up to make merry” before the golden calf (Exodus 32:6), both now on these pages. The KJV and the ASV have “make us sport”; the NWT 1984 “offer us some amusement”.

The crowd. A boy leads the blind man by the hand to the pillars “on which the house rests”. The house is full, and on the roof are about three thousand men and women — as many as the men of Judah who came down to bind him at the rock of Etam (Judges 15:11, now on these pages).

Verses 28–31 · Samson’s last prayer — “remember me”, one vengeance for two eyes, “Let me die with the Philistines”

The second prayer. “Lord Jehovah, remember me, please”: Adonai YHWH, Gideon’s “Alas, Lord Jehovah!” (Judges 6:22, now on these pages), and zokhreni na, “remember me, please”, the verb of Hannah’s prayer that Jehovah would “remember me” (1 Samuel 1:11, now on these pages). “Only this once” takes back Delilah’s word to the lords, “Come up this once” (v. 18). The B Greek text keeps Adōnaie, the Hebrew word in Greek letters.

One vengeance for two eyes. Ve-innaqmah neqam-achat mishtei einai can be read “one vengeance for my two eyes” or “vengeance for one of my two eyes”, and the shelf takes both. The NWT 1984 has “vengeance for one of my two eyes”, and the TLB “for the loss of at least one of my eyes”; the KJV and the ASV have “that I may be at once avenged of the Philistines for my two eyes”. Both Greek texts read one act of vengeance for the two eyes, and so does this translation.

“Let me die with the Philistines.” Tamot nafshi, “let me die”, is Balaam’s wish, “Let me die the death of the upright” (Numbers 23:10, now on these pages); Samson asks to die with the uncircumcised. The narrator’s sum is cool: “the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those he had killed in his life.” Josephus closes with praise of his virtue and strength, and says that being caught by a woman must be put down to human nature (Antiquities 5.316–317, numbered 5.8.12 in Whiston).

Buried with his father. His brothers and his father’s house bring him up and bury him “between Zorah and Eshtaol”, where the spirit first stirred him (Judges 13:25, now on these pages), “in the tomb of Manoah his father”. The judge’s formula of Judges 15:20 (now on these pages) is said again, “He had judged Israel twenty years”, and the book gives no judge after him. The letter to the Hebrews names him among those of faith, with Gideon, Barak and Jephthah (Hebrews 11:32, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “[Word came]”, bracketed where the Hebrew has no verb; “Entice” for patti, as at Judges 14:15; “to afflict” for innah, as in Exodus; “the inner room” for cheder; “mocked” for hetel; “new ropes” for avotim chadashim, as at Lehi; “drove in the peg” for vattitka bayated, as with Jael; “his soul was cut short” for vattiqtsar nafsho, as at Judges 10:16; “began” for chalal in verses 19 and 22; “time after time” for kefa’am befa’am; “make sport” for tsachaq; “Lord Jehovah” for Adonai YHWH; and “one vengeance for my two eyes”.

Echoes paid. The Nazirite of Judges 13:5 tells his secret; the beginnings of 13:5 and 13:25 end in Delilah’s; Samson is buried between Zorah and Eshtaol, where the spirit first stirred him. The bride’s “you do not love me” and “she pressed him” at Judges 14:16–17 return with Delilah, and “Entice” from 14:15. The new ropes and the three thousand of Judges 15:11–13 return, and the twenty years of 15:20 are said again. Jael’s peg at Judges 4:21 is driven a second time, and the soul “cut short” at Judges 10:16 is now Samson’s.

Echoes planted. The eleven hundred pieces of silver come back with Micah’s mother at Judges 17:2, now on these pages. Dagon falls before the ark at 1 Samuel 5:3–4, now on these pages. Eyes put out and bronze fetters come back for Zedekiah at 2 Kings 25:7.

Next installment in this book: Judges 17 — no more judges: Micah of the hill country of Ephraim returns eleven hundred pieces of silver to his mother, she has an image made, and a wandering Levite from Bethlehem becomes his priest for ten pieces of silver a year; “In those days there was no king in Israel; each man did what was right in his own eyes.”
