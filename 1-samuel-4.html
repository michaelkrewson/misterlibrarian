Israel camps at Eben-ezer against the Philistines at Aphek and loses four thousand men. The elders fetch the ark of the covenant from Shiloh; Israel shouts so the earth rings, and the Philistines cry 'God has come into the camp' and fight. Thirty thousand fall, the ark is taken, and Hophni and Phinehas die on one day. A runner of Benjamin reaches Shiloh; Eli, ninety-eight and blind, falls backward from his seat at the mention of the ark and breaks his neck. Phinehas' wife dies in childbirth and names the boy Ichabod: 'the glory has gone into exile from Israel.'
The end of Eli's forty years as judge; the start of the Philistine dominance that 1 Samuel resolves under Samuel, Saul and David.

1
וַיְהִי דְבַר-שְׁמוּאֵל, לְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיֵּצֵא יִשְׂרָאֵל לִקְרַאת פְּלִשְׁתִּים לַמִּלְחָמָה, וַיַּחֲנוּ עַל-הָאֶבֶן הָעֵזֶר, וּפְלִשְׁתִּים, חָנוּ בַאֲפֵק.
And the word of Samuel came to all Israel. And Israel went out to meet the Philistines in battle, and they camped beside Eben-ezer, and the Philistines camped at Aphek.note
Y la palabra de Samuel llegó a todo Israel. E Israel salió al encuentro de los filisteos para la batalla, y acamparon junto a Eben-ezer, y los filisteos acamparon en Afec.
2
וַיַּעַרְכוּ פְלִשְׁתִּים לִקְרַאת יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַתִּטֹּשׁ הַמִּלְחָמָה, וַיִּנָּגֶף יִשְׂרָאֵל, לִפְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; וַיַּכּוּ בַמַּעֲרָכָה בַּשָּׂדֶה, כְּאַרְבַּעַת אֲלָפִים אִישׁ.
And the Philistines drew up in line against Israel, and the battle spread, and Israel was struck down before the Philistines; and they struck down about four thousand men in the battle line in the field.note
Y los filisteos se pusieron en orden de batalla contra Israel, y la batalla se extendió, e Israel fue derrotado delante de los filisteos; y mataron en la línea de batalla, en el campo, como a cuatro mil hombres.
3
וַיָּבֹא הָעָם, אֶל-הַמַּחֲנֶה, וַיֹּאמְרוּ זִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, לָמָּה נְגָפָנוּ יְהוָה הַיּוֹם לִפְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; נִקְחָה אֵלֵינוּ מִשִּׁלֹה, אֶת-אֲרוֹן בְּרִית יְהוָה, וְיָבֹא בְקִרְבֵּנוּ, וְיֹשִׁעֵנוּ מִכַּף אֹיְבֵינוּ.
And the people came to the camp, and the elders of Israel said: ‘Why has Jehovah struck us down today before the Philistines? Let us take to ourselves from Shiloh the ark of the covenant of Jehovah, that it may come among us and save us from the hand of our enemies.’note
Y el pueblo volvió al campamento, y los ancianos de Israel dijeron: «¿Por qué nos ha derrotado hoy Jehová delante de los filisteos? Traigamos a nosotros desde Silo el arca del pacto de Jehová, para que venga en medio de nosotros y nos salve de la mano de nuestros enemigos».
4
וַיִּשְׁלַח הָעָם, שִׁלֹה, וַיִּשְׂאוּ מִשָּׁם אֵת אֲרוֹן בְּרִית-יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, יֹשֵׁב הַכְּרֻבִים; וְשָׁם שְׁנֵי בְנֵי-עֵלִי, עִם-אֲרוֹן בְּרִית הָאֱלֹהִים--חָפְנִי, וּפִינְחָס.
So the people sent to Shiloh, and they carried from there the ark of the covenant of Jehovah of armies, who is enthroned on the cherubim; and the two sons of Eli were there with the ark of the covenant of God, Hophni and Phinehas.note
Y el pueblo envió a Silo, y trajeron de allí el arca del pacto de Jehová de los ejércitos, que está entronizado sobre los querubines; y los dos hijos de Elí estaban allí con el arca del pacto de Dios, Ofni y Finees.
5
וַיְהִי, כְּבוֹא אֲרוֹן בְּרִית-יְהוָה אֶל-הַמַּחֲנֶה, וַיָּרִעוּ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, תְּרוּעָה גְדוֹלָה; וַתֵּהֹם, הָאָרֶץ.
And when the ark of the covenant of Jehovah came into the camp, all Israel shouted a great shout, and the earth rang.note
Y cuando el arca del pacto de Jehová entró en el campamento, todo Israel gritó con gran júbilo, y la tierra retumbó.

And the Philistines heard the sound of the shout and said: ‘What is the sound of this great shout in the camp of the Hebrews?’ And they knew that the ark of Jehovah had come into the camp.note
Y los filisteos oyeron el sonido del júbilo y dijeron: «¿Qué es el sonido de este gran júbilo en el campamento de los hebreos?». Y supieron que el arca de Jehová había entrado en el campamento.
7

Y los filisteos tuvieron miedo, porque decían: «Dios ha entrado en el campamento». Y dijeron: «¡Ay de nosotros! Porque nada semejante ha sucedido ni ayer ni anteayer.
8

¡Ay de nosotros! ¿Quién nos librará de la mano de estos dioses poderosos? Estos son los dioses que hirieron a Egipto con toda clase de plagas en el desierto.

Pelearon, pues, los filisteos, e Israel fue derrotado, y huyeron cada uno a su tienda; y la matanza fue muy grande, y cayeron de Israel treinta mil hombres de a pie.

Y el arca de Dios fue tomada; y los dos hijos de Elí murieron, Ofni y Finees.

Y llegó, y mira —Elí estaba sentado en la silla junto al camino, vigilando, porque su corazón temblaba por el arca de Dios. Y el hombre entró para dar la noticia en la ciudad, y toda la ciudad clamó.

Y Elí oyó el sonido del clamor y dijo: «¿Qué es el sonido de este alboroto?». Y el hombre se dio prisa, y vino y se lo contó a Elí.

Y Elí tenía noventa y ocho años, y sus ojos se habían quedado fijos; no podía ver.

Y el hombre dijo a Elí: «Yo soy el que viene de la línea de batalla; hoy he huido de la línea de batalla». Y él dijo: «¿Qué ha pasado, hijo mío?».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · Ebenezer and Aphek — “the word of Samuel”, four thousand dead, and the ark of “Jehovah of armies, who is enthroned on the cherubim”

“The word of Samuel came to all Israel.” The Hebrew opens the chapter with the line that closes the last one’s thought: the word of Jehovah came to Samuel, and now “the word of Samuel came to all Israel”. Then, without a word from Samuel, “Israel went out to meet the Philistines in battle”. The Greek lacks the line, and opens instead “and it came to pass in those days that the Philistines gathered for war against Israel”. Israel camps at Eben-ezer, “stone of help”, a name the book will explain when Samuel sets up a stone of that name (1 Samuel 7:12, now on these pages); the Philistines camp at Aphek; an Aphek is among the kings Joshua struck (Joshua 12:18, now on these pages). The Douay, from the Latin, translates the name, “camped by the Stone of help”. Jehovah had given Israel into the hand of the Philistines before Samson was born (Judges 13:1, now on these pages).

“That it may come among us.” After four thousand fall, the elders ask the right question, “Why has Jehovah struck us down today?”, and give the wrong answer: fetch the ark from Shiloh, “that it may come among us and save us”. The Hebrew verb can be “it” or “he”; the elders speak as though the box and the God were the same. Moses’ words when the ark set out, “Rise up, Jehovah, and let your enemies be scattered” (Numbers 10:35), were a prayer; and when Israel once marched without it, “the ark of the covenant of Jehovah, and Moses, did not move from the middle of the camp” (Numbers 14:44, both now on these pages). The NIV has “so that he may go with us”; the Douay “let it come in the midst of us”.

“Enthroned on the cherubim.” They carry “the ark of the covenant of Jehovah of armies, who is enthroned on the cherubim”: the God who promised to speak “from between the two cherubim that are on the ark” (Exodus 25:22, now on these pages). The title “enthroned on the cherubim” stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, and this is the first of them. The full name of the ark here, “the ark of the covenant of Jehovah of armies”, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. With the ark come “the two sons of Eli … Hophni and Phinehas”. The ASV has “the ark of the covenant of Jehovah of hosts, who sitteth above the cherubim”.

Verses 5–9 · “A great shout” — the earth rings, the Philistines’ “these mighty gods”, “in the wilderness”, and “be men”

“A great shout.” “All Israel shouted a great shout, and the earth rang.” “A great shout”, teru’ah gedolah, stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the first two are Jericho, where the people “shouted with a great shout, and the wall fell down flat” (Joshua 6:20, now on these pages). This time the shout brings nothing down. The verb for the earth ringing is the one used when Naomi came home and “all the town was stirred” (Ruth 1:19, now on these pages). The ASV has “so that the earth rang again”; the NWT 1984 “so that the earth was in a stir”.

“These mighty gods.” The Philistines hear it and are afraid: “God has come into the camp … Who will deliver us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck Egypt with every kind of plague in the wilderness.” The word for plague is makkah, “a blow”. The Hebrew elohim takes plural adjectives and pronouns here, as it would for any people’s gods, and they place the plagues “in the wilderness”. “These mighty gods” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “these mighty gods”; the KJV capitalizes, “these mighty Gods”; the NWT 1984 makes it singular, “this majestic God”, which is the God the reader knows but not the words the Philistines say.

“Be men.” The answer to fear is a war cry: “Be strong and be men, Philistines, lest you serve the Hebrews as they have served you.” “Hebrews” is what outsiders call Israel. “And be men”, vihyu la’anashim, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “quit yourselves like men”; the NIV “Be men, and fight!”

Verses 10–11 · Thirty thousand, the ark taken, and Hophni and Phinehas dead “in one day”

The ark taken. “And the ark of God was taken; and the two sons of Eli died, Hophni and Phinehas.” The sign given to Eli, “in one day the two of them shall die” (1 Samuel 2:34), and the “thing in Israel at which both ears … will tingle” (1 Samuel 3:11, both now on these pages) come together in one sentence. Israel flees “each man to his tent”, and thirty thousand foot soldiers fall. Josephus adds that Eli had told his sons not to come back alive if the ark were taken, and that Phinehas was already serving as high priest because his father was old (Antiquities 5.354, numbered 5.11.2 in Whiston). The ASV has “the ark of God was taken; and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain”.

Verses 12–18 · The runner from Benjamin and the death of Eli — “earth on his head”, “his eyes were set”, the four blows of the news, and “old and heavy”

“Earth on his head.” A man of Benjamin runs some twenty miles to Shiloh “that same day, his clothes torn and earth on his head”, the dress of grief that Joshua and the elders put on after Ai (Joshua 7:6, now on these pages). “And earth on his head” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, the Amalekite who brings David the news of Saul’s death (2 Samuel 1:2, now on these pages), and Hushai on the Mount of Olives (2 Samuel 15:32).

Eli by the road. Eli sits “on the seat beside the road, watching”, the same kisse he sat on when Hannah prayed (1 Samuel 1:9, now on these pages); the written text has yakh and the margin yad, “beside”. “His heart trembled for the ark of God” — not for his sons. He is “ninety-eight years old, and his eyes were set”: the eyes that “had begun to grow dim” (1 Samuel 3:2, now on these pages) are now fixed and blind. The Greek makes him ninety; Josephus has ninety-eight (Antiquities 5.359, numbered 5.11.3 in Whiston). The ASV has “his eyes were set, so that he could not see”; the NWT 1984 “his eyes had set so that he was unable to see”; the KJV “his eyes were dim”.

Four blows. “How did the matter go, my son?” The messenger, ha-mevasser, the one who brings news (the root of besorah, “tidings”), answers in rising order: “Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great slaughter among the people, and your two sons also are dead, Hophni and Phinehas, and the ark of God has been taken.” The old man survives the first three.

“Old and heavy.” “When he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell backward off the seat beside the gate, and his neck was broken and he died, for the man was old and heavy.” Heavy is kaved, the root of kavod, “glory”, and of “honour”: Eli “honoured” his sons above God and fattened on the offerings (1 Samuel 2:29, now on these pages), and in two verses his daughter-in-law will say that the glory has gone. The word for his “neck”, mafreqet, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. “He had judged Israel forty years”; the Greek says twenty, Josephus forty. The ASV has “for he was an old man, and heavy”; the Douay, from the Latin, “for he was an old man, and far advanced in years”.

Verses 19–22 · Ichabod — “Do not fear, for you have borne a son”, “the glory has gone into exile from Israel”, and Isaiah’s “the glory … shall be revealed”

“Do not fear.” Phinehas’ wife hears the news and goes into labour, “for her pains came upon her”. As she dies the women say, “Do not fear, for you have borne a son”, the words of Rachel’s midwife, “Do not fear, for this too is a son for you” (Genesis 35:17, now on these pages); Rachel named her son with her last breath too. “But she did not answer, nor did she set her heart to it.” The ASV has “Fear not; for thou hast brought forth a son. But she answered not, neither did she regard it”; the NWT 1984 “did not set her heart on it”.

Ichabod — “No glory”. She names him I-khavod, “Where is the glory?” or “No glory”, “saying, ‘The glory has gone into exile from Israel’”. The name stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in 1 Samuel 14:3, where the priest Ahijah is named as the son of “Ahitub, Ichabod’s brother”. The verb is galah, the verb of a people carried off into exile, used here of the glory and the ark. Josephus calls the child Iachobes, says the name means “inglory”, and adds that he was born at seven months and lived (Antiquities 5.360, numbered 5.11.4 in Whiston). The Greek garbles the name as Ouai barchabōth. The NWT 1984 keeps the exile: “Glory has gone away from Israel into exile”; the ASV has “The glory is departed from Israel”.

Exiled or revealed. She says it twice, the second time with the reason: “for the ark of God has been taken”. The two words galah kavod stand together in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: twice here, and in Isaiah 40:5, where the same verb in its other sense means “revealed”: “the glory of Jehovah shall be revealed, and all flesh together shall see it” (Isaiah 40:5, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “it may come” for the elders’ yavo, with “he” in the note; “enthroned on the cherubim” for yoshev ha-keruvim; “a great shout” for teru’ah gedolah, as at Jericho; “these mighty gods”, keeping the Philistines’ plural; “the Hebrews”, the outsiders’ name; “earth on his head”, as in 2 Samuel 1:2; “the seat”, as in 1 Samuel 1:9; “set” for Eli’s eyes, after “grow dim” in 3:2; “heavy” for kaved; “yesterday or the day before”, as in Exodus 5:7; and “has gone into exile” for galah.

Echoes paid. “In one day the two of them shall die” (2:34) and the tingling ears (3:11) are fulfilled; Eli’s seat from 1:9 and his dimming eyes from 3:2 return; and the “heaviness” of a house that fattened itself (2:29–30) ends in “the glory has gone”.

Echoes planted. The ark in the house of Dagon at Ashdod in 1 Samuel 5, now on these pages; Eben-ezer named in 1 Samuel 7:12, now on these pages; and the line of Ichabod’s brother Ahitub in 1 Samuel 14:3.
