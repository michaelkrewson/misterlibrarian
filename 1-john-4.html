Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–3 · “Test the spirits” — false prophets, “Jesus Christ come in flesh,” and that of the antichrist

“Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.” “Spirit” is pneuma (dictionary), here the spirit that speaks through a prophet, so that to test the spirits is to test what the prophets say. “Test” is dokimazō, the verb of “But test everything; hold fast what is good” at 1 Thessalonians 5:21 (dictionary); in John’s Gospel and letters it stands only here. Pseudoprophētēs, “false prophet,” stands in eleven verses (dictionary). Among them are Jesus’s “Beware of the false prophets” (Matthew 7:15) and “many false prophets will rise up and lead many astray” (Matthew 24:11), and “false prophets also arose among the people” at 2 Peter 2:1. “Have gone out into the world” is the phrase of 2 John 1:7, “many deceivers have gone out into the world,” and it recalls “They went out from us” at 1 John 2:19. The KJV reads “try the spirits whether they are of God”; the ASV reads “prove the spirits”; the NWT 1984 reads “do not believe every inspired expression, but test the inspired expressions,” an interpretation of pneuma it keeps through verse 6; the TLB reads “don’t always believe everything you hear just because someone says it is a message from God.”

“By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in flesh is of God.” “You know” is ginōskete, which in Greek is also the form of the command, “know”; the Byzantine text reads “is known,” and the Douay reads “By this is the spirit of God known.” “Confesses” is homologeō, as in “whoever confesses the Son has the Father also” at 1 John 2:23 (dictionary). “Come” is the perfect participle elēlythota, “having come,” an arrival that stands; 2 John 1:7 has the present, “coming in flesh,” and the note there sets out the readings. The confession is the Gospel’s “the Word became flesh” (John 1:14), said against teachers who, the letter implies, would not say it (dictionary). The KJV reads “Every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God”; the NIV reads “acknowledges that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh”; the TLB asks, “Does it really agree that Jesus Christ, God’s Son, actually became man with a human body?”

⚠ “And every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not of God.” Westcott-Hort, Tregelles and the 28th Nestle-Aland edition read “does not confess Jesus.” The Byzantine text repeats the whole confession, “does not confess Jesus Christ come in flesh,” without the article before “Jesus”: the KJV reads “every spirit that confesseth not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is not of God”; the ASV reads “every spirit that confesseth not Jesus is not of God.” A third reading stands in none of these editions: “every spirit that looses Jesus,” that is, that undoes or divides him, Greek lyei, Latin solvit. The Douay, which translates the Latin, reads “every spirit that dissolveth Jesus.” Its witnesses are reported, not checked here: the Latin tradition, Latin translations of Irenaeus and Origen, a marginal note in one Greek manuscript, and the fifth-century historian Socrates, who says the old copies read it. Whether it is the original reading or an early comment on the verse, readings differ.

“And this is that of the antichrist, which you have heard is coming, and now it is in the world already.” “That of” stands for the Greek’s bare article, to tou antichristou, “the [thing] of the antichrist,” with no noun. Most versions supply “spirit”: the ASV reads “this is the spirit of the antichrist”; the NWT 1984 reads “this is the antichrist’s [inspired expression],” in brackets; the Douay reads “this is Antichrist.” This is the third of the four verses with antichristos (dictionary), after 1 John 2:18, “as you heard that antichrist is coming, even now many antichrists have come,” and 1 John 2:22, and before 2 John 1:7. What was heard of as coming is said to be here.

Verses 4–6 · “Greater is the one who is in you” — the world that listens, and “the spirit of truth and the spirit of error”

“You are of God, little children, and you have conquered them, because greater is the one who is in you than the one who is in the world.” “Them” are the false prophets of verse 1. “You have conquered” is nikaō in the perfect, as in “you have conquered the evil one” at 1 John 2:13, and Jesus’s “I have conquered the world” (John 16:33) (dictionary). “Greater” is meizōn, as in “God is greater than our heart” at 1 John 3:20. The letter does not name “the one who is in the world”; readers take him as the devil, “the ruler of this world” of John 12:31. The KJV reads “greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world”; the NWT 1984 reads “YOU have conquered those [persons]”; the Douay reads “have overcome him,” a singular the Greek editions named here do not give.

“They are of the world; for this reason they speak from the world, and the world listens to them.” “From the world” is ek tou kosmou: their speech has its source there, as “The one who is from the earth is from the earth, and from the earth he speaks” at John 3:31 (dictionary). The ASV reads “therefore speak they as of the world”; the NIV reads “speak from the viewpoint of the world”; the TLB reads “they are concerned about worldly affairs.”

“We are of God. Whoever knows God listens to us; whoever is not of God does not listen to us. From this we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error.” “We” are the witnesses of 1 John 1:3, “that which we have seen and heard we declare also to you.” The test is Jesus’s own: “Whoever belongs to God hears the words of God” (John 8:47), and “Everyone who belongs to the truth hears my voice” (John 18:37). “The spirit of truth” is Jesus’s name for the Spirit at John 14:17, John 15:26 and John 16:13, the only other verses where the phrase stands; “the spirit of error” stands nowhere else. “Error” is planē, wandering, being led astray (dictionary), the noun of “those who are leading you astray” at 1 John 2:26. This translation prints both in lower case, since the verse sets two spirits side by side as the sources of what people say. The KJV reads “Hereby know we the spirit of truth, and the spirit of error”; the NIV reads “the Spirit of truth and the spirit of falsehood,” with a capital for the first.

Verses 7–10 · ⚠ “God is love” — born of God, “his only Son,” and the atonement

“Beloved, let us love one another, because love is of God, and everyone who loves has been born of God and knows God.” “Love one another” is the new commandment of John 13:34 and the message of 1 John 3:11. “Has been born of God” is the phrase of 1 John 3:9. “Love” is agapē (dictionary), which the Douay prints “charity” throughout this chapter, from the Latin caritas: “for charity is of God.” The NWT 1984 reads “let us continue loving one another”; the KJV reads “every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God.”

⚠ “Whoever does not love has not known God, because God is love.” This is the second “God is” saying of the letter, after “God is light” (1 John 1:5), and with “God is spirit” (John 4:24) the third in John’s writings. “God is love” stands in two verses, this one and verse 16. In the Greek, ho theos agapē estin, “God” has the article and “love” does not, which marks “God” as the subject: the sentence says what God is, and does not turn round into “love is God.” “Has not known” is an aorist. The KJV reads “He that loveth not knoweth not God; for God is love”; the NWT 1984 reads “has not come to know God”; the Douay reads “for God is charity.”

“In this the love of God was made manifest in us: that God has sent his only Son into the world so that we might live through him.” “Made manifest” is phaneroō, as in “the life was made manifest” (1 John 1:2) and “the Son of God was made manifest” (1 John 3:8). “In us” is en hēmin, which can also mean “among us” or “in our case”: the KJV reads “toward us”; the NIV reads “among us”; the NWT 1984 reads “in our case.” “Only Son” is monogenēs (dictionary), “the only Son” of John 3:16 and John 3:18. Monogenēs stands in nine verses: three in Luke, four in John’s Gospel, Hebrews 11:17 and this one. The KJV reads “his only begotten Son,” from the Latin unigenitus; the NIV reads “his one and only Son.” “Has sent … into the world” is the sending of John 3:17, “God did not send the Son into the world to judge the world, but so that the world might be saved through him.”

“In this is love: not that we have loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as the atonement for our sins.” Love is defined from God’s side first, as in Paul’s “while we were still sinners, Christ died for us” (Romans 5:8). Hilasmos, “atonement,” stands in two verses, both in this letter (dictionary). The other is 1 John 2:2, “he himself is the atonement for our sins”; whether the word means turning away God’s anger or wiping away the sin, readings differ, and the note at Romans 3:25 sets them out. Westcott-Hort and the 28th Nestle-Aland edition read the perfect, “we have loved”; Tregelles and the Byzantine text read the aorist, “we loved.” The KJV reads “not that we loved God … and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins”; the NWT 1984 reads “as a propitiatory sacrifice”; the Geneva reads “to be a reconciliation for our sins”; the TLB reads “to satisfy God’s anger against our sins,” choosing one reading; the Douay reads “because he hath first loved us,” with the “first” of verse 19.

Verses 11–16 · “No one has ever looked upon God” — love made complete, “Saviour of the world,” and “God is love” again

“Beloved, if this is how God loved us, we also ought to love one another.” “This is how” is houtōs, as in “For this is how God loved the world” (John 3:16). “Ought” is the “ought” of “we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers” at 1 John 3:16. The NWT 1984 reads “if this is how God loved us, then we are ourselves under obligation to love one another”; the KJV reads “if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another.”

“No one has ever looked upon God.” The Gospel says “No one has ever seen God” (John 1:18) with heōraken; the letter has tetheatai, from theaomai, the “that which we looked upon” of 1 John 1:1 (dictionary). In 1 John it stands three times: 1:1, here, and verse 14, “we ourselves have looked upon it.” God himself has not been looked upon; the Son has, by the witnesses, and where there is love God remains.

“If we love one another, God remains in us, and his love has been made complete in us.” “Made complete” is teleioō, as in “in him the love of God has truly been made complete” at 1 John 2:5 (dictionary); in this letter it stands at 2:5 and in verses 12, 17 and 18. “His love” can be God’s love for us, our love for him, or the love that comes from him; readings differ. For the first sentence of the verse, the ASV reads “No man hath beheld God at any time” and the NWT 1984 reads “At no time has anyone beheld God”; for the second, the KJV reads “his love is perfected in us” and the NIV reads “his love is made complete in us.”

“By this we know that we remain in him and he in us: because he has given us of his Spirit. And we ourselves have looked upon it and testify that the Father has sent the Son as Saviour of the world.” “Of his Spirit” is ek tou pneumatos, a share of the Spirit; 1 John 3:24 said “by the Spirit he gave us.” The KJV reads “because he hath given us of his Spirit”; the NWT 1984 reads “because he has imparted his spirit to us,” without the “of” and in lower case, as at 3:24. “We ourselves” is the emphatic hēmeis of the witnesses; the Greek has no object after “looked upon,” and this translation supplies “it,” the sending that follows. “Testify” is martyreō, as in “we have seen it and testify to it” at 1 John 1:2 (dictionary). “Saviour of the world” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is the Samaritans’ “this is truly the Saviour of the world” (John 4:42). The ASV reads “we have beheld and bear witness”; the NWT 1984 reads “we ourselves have beheld and are bearing witness.”

“Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God, God remains in him, and he in God.” The confession of verse 2 in other words. Westcott-Hort reads “Jesus Christ”; Tregelles, the 28th Nestle-Aland edition and the Byzantine text read “Jesus.” The NWT 1984 follows Westcott-Hort: “Whoever makes the confession that Jesus Christ is the Son of God.” The KJV reads “Whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God, God dwelleth in him, and he in God.”

“And we have come to know and have believed the love that God has in us. God is love, and whoever remains in love remains in God, and God remains in him.” Peter said the same two verbs in the other order, “we have believed and have come to know that you are the Holy One of God” (John 6:69). “In us” is en hēmin again, as in verse 9: the ASV reads “the love which God hath in us”; the KJV reads “the love that God hath to us”; the NIV reads “the love God has for us.” “God is love” is said a second time, and now joined to “remain,” menō, the letter’s word for staying (dictionary). Tregelles leaves out the last “remains.” The NWT 1984 reads “he that remains in love remains in union with God and God remains in union with him.”

Verses 17–18 · ⚠ “There is no fear in love” — boldness in the day of judgment, complete love, and “fear has punishment”

“By this love has been made complete with us, so that we may have boldness in the day of judgment, because just as that one is, so also are we in this world.” “By this” can point back to the remaining of verse 16 or ahead to the “because” that follows; readings differ. “Boldness” is parrēsia (dictionary): at his coming in 1 John 2:28, toward God in 1 John 3:21, and here at the judgment. “The day of judgment” is the day of Matthew 12:36 and 2 Peter 3:7 (dictionary). “That one” is ekeinos, Jesus, as in “just as that one is pure” at 1 John 3:3. In what way “so also are we,” the letter does not say; readers have taken it of love, of being righteous as he is righteous (3:7), or of standing before the Father as the Son does. The NIV reads “In this world we are like Jesus,” supplying the name; the KJV reads “Herein is our love made perfect,” adding “our”; the NWT 1984 reads “that we may have freeness of speech in the day of judgment.”

⚠ “There is no fear in love, but complete love throws fear out, because fear has punishment; and whoever fears has not been made complete in love.” Phobos, “fear,” stands in John’s letters only in this verse (dictionary); the fear meant is the fear of the judgment just named. “Complete” is teleia, the adjective of teleioō, and the adjective stands nowhere else in John’s Gospel or letters. “Throws … out” is exō ballei, as in “he is thrown outside like the branch” (John 15:6). Kolasis, “punishment,” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is “these will go away into eternal punishment” (Matthew 25:46). Paul says the same of the Spirit: “you did not receive a spirit of slavery, back into fear” (Romans 8:15). The KJV reads “perfect love casteth out fear: because fear hath torment”; the ASV reads “because fear hath punishment”; the NIV reads “because fear has to do with punishment”; the NWT 1984 reads “perfect love throws fear outside, because fear exercises a restraint,” a rendering that leans on the older sense of the verb kolazō, to prune or lop; the Geneva reads “for fear hath painfulness.”

Verses 19–21 · “We love, because he first loved us” — the liar, the brother seen and God unseen, and one commandment

“We love, because he first loved us.” The critical text gives “love” no object; the Byzantine text adds “him.” The KJV reads “We love him, because he first loved us”; the ASV reads “We love, because he first loved us.” Agapōmen is also the form of “let us love” in verse 7, and the Douay reads it so: “Let us therefore love God, because God first hath loved us,” adding “therefore” and “God.” The verse sums up verse 10: “not that we have loved God, but that he loved us.” The NWT 1984 reads “As for us, we love.”

“If anyone says, ‘I love God,’ and hates his brother, he is a liar; for whoever does not love his brother, whom he has seen, cannot love God, whom he has not seen.” The letter has tested slogans before: “If we say that we have fellowship with him and yet walk in the darkness” (1 John 1:6), and “Whoever says he is in the light and hates his brother” (1 John 2:9). Pseustēs, “liar,” stands in ten verses (dictionary). Five are in this letter: 1:10, 2:4, 2:22, this one, and 5:10. “Whom he has seen” and “whom he has not seen” are heōraken, the verb the Gospel uses of God at John 1:18, “has ever seen God,” with verse 12 behind them. The Byzantine text makes the end a question, “how can he love God”: the KJV reads “how can he love God whom he hath not seen?”; the ASV reads “cannot love God whom he hath not seen”; the NWT 1984 reads “cannot be loving God, whom he has not seen.”

“And this commandment we have from him: that whoever loves God should also love his brother.” One commandment with two loves in it, as Jesus joined “you shall love Jehovah your God” and “You shall love your neighbour as yourself” (Mark 12:30–31); in John’s Gospel the commandment is “that you love one another” (John 13:34) (dictionary). “From him” can be God or Christ; readings differ. The Douay reads “this commandment we have from God”; the NIV reads “And he has given us this command”; the KJV reads “That he who loveth God love his brother also.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “test the spirits”; “false prophets,” as at Matthew 24:11; “come in flesh,” the perfect, beside 2 John 1:7’s “coming”; “that of the antichrist,” with no noun supplied; “conquered,” as at 2:13; “from the world”; “the spirit of truth and the spirit of error,” both in lower case; “God is love”; “in us” for en hēmin; “his only Son,” as at John 3:16; “atonement,” as at 2:2; “looked upon,” as at 1:1; “made complete,” as at 2:5; “Saviour of the world,” as at John 4:42; “boldness,” as at 2:28 and 3:21; “that one”; “complete love”; “throws fear out”; “punishment,” as at Matthew 25:46; “We love,” with no object; and “liar,” as at 2:4.

Echoes paid. “Test the spirits” and “God is love,” promised at the close of 1 John 3; the Spirit named at 1 John 3:24, now “he has given us of his Spirit”; the antichrist of 1 John 2:18 and 2:22; the atonement of 1 John 2:2; “looked upon” and “testify” from 1 John 1:1–2; “made complete” from 1 John 2:5; boldness from 1 John 2:28 and 3:21, now in the day of judgment; “born of God” from 1 John 3:9; the liar of 1 John 2:4; and the new commandment of John 13:34.

Echoes planted. “Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God” at 5:1; “this is the victory that has conquered the world, our faith” at 5:4; “the one who came through water and blood” at 5:6; God made “a liar” at 5:10; and “the true God and eternal life” at 5:20.

Next in this book: 1 John 5, already on these pages. “Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God”; “his commandments are not heavy”; “the victory that has conquered the world, our faith”; ⚠ the water and the blood, and the three that testify, with the Johannine Comma; “sin leading to death”; and “Little children, guard yourselves from idols.”
