📖 Dictionary

chesed חֶסֶד chesed

Steadfast love, covenant kindness — one of the Bible's great untranslatables, first here on Lot's lips (19:19). The word behind the Psalms' 'mercy,' and — paired with 'truth' (chesed ve-emet, see emet) — behind John's 'grace and truth' (see charis). It saturates Genesis 24, where the servant asks God to 'do chesed' with Abraham (24:12) and designs a sign that tests it — not the girl's beauty but her spontaneous kindness to a stranger and his ten thirsty camels. ⚠ Psalm 23:6 pairs it with tov and hands both to radaph, the hunting verb: goodness and chesed do not follow the psalmist, they pursue him. ⚠ Leviticus 20:17 (already on these pages) turns the very same word against brother-sister incest — 'it is a chesed,' rendered 'a disgrace' — in the identical book that opened with Lot begging for chesed at Sodom's gate, before his own daughters and their father became exactly this law's own case. Two real explanations exist and this translation does not choose: a second, homonymous root (cognate with Aramaic/Arabic words for shame) that only looks identical to the covenant-kindness word, or the same root turned bitterly ironic. KJV reads 'a wicked thing' here; ASV 'a shameful thing'; NIV 'a disgrace.' ⚠ It is the attribute Moses leans on hardest at Numbers 14:18-19, quoting the Sinai formula back to God and keeping only 'slow to anger and abundant in chesed' out of the whole merciful half — the two attributes that buy time and loyalty rather than acquittal. ⭐ Joshua 2:12 (now on these pages) puts the word in a Canaanite's mouth: Rahab names what she has done for the spies chesed and asks it back for her father's house, and the men answer with the full pair, chesed ve-emet, at 2:14 — the shape of the oath Abimelech once asked of Abraham, as I have done with you, so do with me (Genesis 21:23). ⭐ 1 Samuel 15:6 (now on these pages): Saul warns the Kenites away from Amalek “for you did kindness with all the sons of Israel when they came up from Egypt”. ⭐ 1 Samuel 20 (now on these pages) is a covenant asked both ways: David asks Jonathan to ‘do kindness with your servant’ (20:8), and Jonathan asks David for ‘the kindness of Jehovah’ while he lives and for his house forever (20:14–15); David keeps it by seeking out Jonathan’s son ‘for Jonathan’s sake’ (2 Samuel 9:1, now on these pages). ⭐ David blesses the men of Jabesh-gilead for ‘this kindness’ to Saul and prays ‘kindness and truth’ on them (2 Samuel 2:5–6, now on these pages), the word Jonathan had asked of him (1 Samuel 20:14). ⭐ Abner uses it of his own loyalty, ‘Today I do kindness with the house of Saul your father’ (2 Samuel 3:8, now on these pages). ⭐ Jehovah promises the son of David ‘my kindness shall not depart from him, as I removed it from Saul’ (2 Samuel 7:15, now on these pages). ⭐ David asks twice for someone to do kindness with, ‘for Jonathan’s sake’ and as ‘the kindness of God’ (2 Samuel 9:1, 3, 7, now on these pages). ‘The kindness of God’ stands in two verses. They are verse 3 of that chapter and Psalm 52:10. ⭐ David resolves ‘I will do kindness with Hanun son of Nahash, as his father did kindness with me’ (2 Samuel 10:2, now on these pages), the verb of 9:1 turned toward a king’s son, and Hanun’s princes read it as a spy’s cover. ⭐ 2 Samuel 15:20 (now on these pages): David tells Ittai the Gittite to go back ‘in kindness and truth’ (chesed ve’emet), with no verb in the Hebrew; Rahlfs’ Greek supplies one, ‘and the Lord will do with you mercy and truth’. ⭐ ‘He does kindness to his anointed, to David and to his seed, forever’ closes David’s song (2 Samuel 22:51, now on these pages). ⭐ ‘Show kindness to the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite’ (1 Kings 2:7, now on these pages). → first discussed at Gen 19:19
