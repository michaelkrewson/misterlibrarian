📖 Dictionary

yatsaq יָצַק vayatsiqum

TO POUR — oil on a stone or a head, metal into a mould, blood at the base of an altar; and, in a causative form, TO SET DOWN. Jacob poured oil on the top of the stone at Bethel (Genesis 28:18; 35:14), Moses poured the anointing oil on Aaron’s head (Exodus 29:7; Leviticus 8:12) and the blood at the altar’s base (8:15), and the tabernacle’s rings and sockets were cast with it (Exodus 25:12; 38:5, all already on these pages) — the same word for anointing a priest and casting bronze. ★ Vayatsiqum, ‘and they set them down,’ stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, Joshua 7:23 (now on these pages), of Achan’s silver, mantle and gold laid before Jehovah; its nearest kin is 2 Samuel 15:24 (not yet on these pages), where Zadok and the Levites set down the ark of God on the road out of Jerusalem — the verb of pouring used for putting a heavy thing on the ground. The shelf reads it four ways at Joshua 7:23: the NWT 1984 keeps the pouring, ‘poured them out’, the KJV and ASV ‘laid them out’ and ‘laid them down’, the NIV ‘spread them out’, the Douay-Rheims from the Vulgate ‘threw them down’; the TNM 1987 «los vertieron», the RV60 «lo pusieron». The Targum, Rashi reports, read it as melted them. → first discussed at Joshua 7:23
