📖 Dictionary

chazaq חָזַק chazaq

TO BE STRONG, firm, resolute — the verb of nerve and grip. It is the word behind Pharaoh’s hardened heart (the heart made strong, not softened), behind ‘his STRONG hand and outstretched arm’ (Deuteronomy 11:2, already on these pages), and above all behind the charge to a commander: chazaq ve-ematz, ‘be strong and courageous’. ⭐ Which is what makes Deuteronomy 12:23 worth stopping at. The exact phrase rak chazaq, ‘only be strong’, stands in three verses of the whole Hebrew Bible: that one, and Joshua 1:7 and 1:18, both spoken to Joshua about invading a country. In Deuteronomy it introduces a rule about not eating blood. The Hebrew treats abstinence as an act requiring the same quality as war. ⚠ Most of the English shelf translates the nerve out of it — KJV, ASV, Geneva and NIV all read ‘be sure’ — while the NWT keeps ‘firmly resolved’ and the Reina-Valera of 1909 «te esfuerces». The Geneva translators knew: their own margin at that verse reads, in full, ‘Hebrew, be strong or constant.’ ⭐ The charge itself lands at Deuteronomy 31 (now on these pages), which says it three times in eight verses and changes grammatical number each time: the plural chizqu ve-imtzu to the whole nation (31:6), the singular chazaq ve-ematz to Joshua in front of them (31:7), and the same singular a third time out of God’s own mouth (31:23). The singular pair stands in nine verses of the Hebrew Bible. The plural pair stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible. See ematz for the second verb on its own. ⭐ Joshua 4:24 (now on these pages) takes the Torah’s fixed yad chazaqah, the strong hand, apart into a clause — that all the peoples of the earth may know the hand of Jehovah, that it is STRONG — and ki chazaqah hi stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. With both articles, ha-yad ha-chazaqah stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, Deuteronomy 7:19 and 34:12, the Torah’s last verse. ⭐ In Isaiah 41:6–13 (now on these pages) the nations say ‘Be strong!’ and strengthen their idol with nails, and Jehovah answers with the same verb: ‘you whom I took hold of’, ‘I take hold of your right hand’. ⭐ At 1 Samuel 30:6 (now on these pages), with his men speaking of stoning him, ‘David strengthened himself in Jehovah his God’, in the reflexive form, where Jonathan had ‘strengthened his hand in God’ at Horesh (23:16). ⭐ 2 Samuel 2:7 (now on these pages) has David tell Jabesh ‘let your hands be strong’, and verse 16 uses the same root for the twelve men who ‘seized’ one another by the head. ⭐ 2 Samuel 3:1 (now on these pages) has David ‘stronger and stronger’ as the house of Saul grows weaker, and verse 6 has Abner ‘making himself strong’ in the house of Saul, in the reflexive form David used of himself at Ziklag (1 Samuel 30:6). ⭐ Joab says ‘Be strong, and let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our God’ (2 Samuel 10:12, now on these pages). ‘Be strong, and let us be strong’ stands in two verses. They are that verse and the Chronicles’ repetition of the speech (1 Chronicles 19:13). → first discussed at Deut 12:23
