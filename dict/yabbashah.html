📖 Dictionary

yabbashah יַבָּשָׁה ba-yabbashah

DRY GROUND — the noun of the third day of creation, let the dry land appear (Genesis 1:9, already on these pages, where that page prints ‘dry land’), and from Exodus 14 on the Red Sea’s word: this project prints it ‘dry ground’ from there, and keeps it apart from charavah, ‘dry land,’ as the Exodus pages do. ⭐ Read as this word, ba-yabbashah, ‘on dry ground,’ stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible (the consonants also spell Jabesh, the town, at 1 Samuel 31:13), and every one but one is the Red Sea — Exodus 14:16, 14:22, 14:29, 15:19 (all already on these pages), Nehemiah 9:11 retelling it (not yet on these pages) — and the one is the Jordan, Joshua 4:22 (now on these pages), where the fathers are to tell their sons on dry ground Israel crossed this Jordan, one verse before they name the Red Sea. Joshua 3:17 and 4:18 had said charavah; the catechism switches to the sea’s word. Jonah is vomited onto it (Jonah 2:11, English 2:10, not yet on these pages). ⚠ The English shelf keeps the two nouns apart too, but not the same way: the ASV gives ‘dry ground’ to charavah and ‘dry land’ to yabbashah, the reverse of this project, and the KJV prints ‘dry land’ for both at Joshua 4:18 and 22. → first discussed at Joshua 4:22
