📖 Dictionary

matzevah (pl. matzevot) מַצֵּבָה matzevah

A STANDING STONE — an upright slab set up to make something permanent, from natsav, to stand. The word is morally neutral and the Bible uses it both ways, which is why it needs its own entry rather than a gloss. ⭐ Read consonantally in the singular it stands in THIRTEEN verses of the Hebrew Bible (the raw match returns sixteen; three are a garrison, a place-name and a guard) — and EIGHT of the thirteen are Jacob. He sets one up at Bethel, the stone he had slept on, and pours oil on its top, which is the Bible’s first anointing of a sacred object (Genesis 28:18, 28:22); he raises one for the treaty with Laban (31:45, 31:51, 31:52), one at Bethel again (35:14) and one on Rachel’s grave (35:20) — and at 31:13 God identifies himself by one: I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar. ⭐ Moses set up twelve of them at the foot of Sinai, one for each tribe (Exodus 24:4). The patriarchs raise them freely and not one is condemned. ⚠ But a matzevah standing at a Canaanite shrine is the same object doing the same job for a different god, and the Law turns on it: Deuteronomy 7:5 and 12:3 order them smashed, Leviticus 26:1 forbids raising one, and Deuteronomy 16:22 is where the ban acquires a feeling: you shall not set up for yourself a standing stone, which Jehovah your God HATES. It is the clearest case in the Torah of a patriarchal practice becoming a forbidden one once Israel has the Law. ⭐ Afterwards the word keeps both charges: Hosea 3:4 counts the absence of a matzevah among the things Israel will sit without, and Isaiah 19:19 promises one to Jehovah inside Egypt. ⚠ The English shelf is not consistent: this translation and the NWT read ‘standing stones’/‘sacred pillars’, while KJV and ASV print ‘images’ and ‘pillars’ — and ‘image’ is misleading, since the point of a matzevah is that it is UNCARVED. It is a stone that means something, not a stone shaped like something; that is what distinguishes it from the pesel, the carved image, standing beside it in the same verse. ⚠ This project’s own English is not consistent either: the Genesis chapters, translated earlier, print ‘pillar’, and ‘standing stone’ is used from Deuteronomy 12 onward. One noun, two renderings. ⭐ Rows of them survive at Gezer and elsewhere, and their purpose is genuinely debated. → first discussed at Gen 28:18
