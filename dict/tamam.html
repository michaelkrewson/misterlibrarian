📖 Dictionary

tamam / tom תָּמַם tammu kol-ha-goy

TO BE FINISHED, complete, spent, at an end — the verb whose adjective is tamim, whole, blameless. It is the verb of things running out — the silver of Egypt in the famine, came to an end, is spent (Genesis 47:15, 18) — and of a generation running out: until your carcasses are finished in the wilderness (Numbers 14:33), until the whole generation … was finished (32:13), until the whole generation of the men of war had perished (Deuteronomy 2:14; all already on these pages). ⭐ Joshua makes it the verb of the crossing and its aftermath: tammu kol-ha-goy, ‘all the nation had finished,’ stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in that book — 3:17 and 4:1 of crossing, 5:8 of being circumcised (all now on these pages) — and ad-tom, ‘until … was finished,’ stands in two verses of Joshua, 4:10 of the priests’ errand in the river and 5:6 of the nation that walked forty years until it was. ⚠ The English shelf scatters it — ‘consumed’, ‘were dead’, ‘had died’, ‘clean passed over’, ‘wholly gone over’; this translation keeps finished throughout, so that the crossing and the dying can be heard as one word. ★ The related form ad-tumam, ‘until they were finished,’ stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible, and its frame — vayehi kekhalot X … ad tumam, ‘and it happened, when X finished … until it was finished’ — belongs to Moses first: Deuteronomy 31:24 (now on these pages), when Moses had finished writing the words of this law in a book, until they were finished. Joshua 8:24 puts the identical grammar to the opposite work — when Israel had finished killing all the inhabitants of Ai … until they were finished — and Joshua 10:20 (not yet on these pages) uses it a third time, of the five kings’ armies. The same two words that closed the writing of the Torah close the killing of a city. → first discussed at Joshua 5:6
