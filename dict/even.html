📖 Dictionary

even אֶבֶן even

A STONE — and, in the marketplace, a WEIGHT, because weights were stones. Proverbs 16:11 calls a merchant's set the stones of the bag, and Micah 6:11 a bag of deceitful stones (now on these pages); Leviticus 19:36 (already on these pages) demands stones of justice. ⭐ Deuteronomy 25:13–15 forbids a stone and a stone in the bag — Hebrew's idiom for two different kinds, a heavy one to buy with and a light one to sell with — and commands a whole stone, even shelemah. That phrase stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: the honest weight here, Proverbs 11:1's a whole stone is his delight, and 1 Kings 6:7's temple, built of whole stone from the quarry so that no iron tool was heard in it — an honest weight and Solomon's masonry sharing an adjective, and the altar of Deuteronomy 27:6 (now on these pages) to be made of the plural, whole stones that no iron has touched. ⚠ Elsewhere the word is simply a stone: the pillow at Bethel (Genesis 28:11), the one rolled from the well's mouth (29:3), the tablets (Exodus 24:12), all already on these pages. Only the NWT 2013 and TNM 2019 print the stone at Deuteronomy 25:13; everyone else translates it to 'weights'. ⭐ Joshua 4 (now on these pages) is a chapter of stones: twelve carried out of the Jordan on twelve shoulders and set up at Gilgal as a memorial (4:5–8, 20), and ‘twelve stones’ set up in the riverbed itself (4:9) — the Hebrew says neither OTHER nor THE, so whether that is a second set is the chapter’s standing question. Spelled this way, shteim esreh avanim stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Joshua 4:3, 4:9 and Elijah’s altar at 1 Kings 18:31. Even achat, ‘one stone,’ stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, from the one each man lifts at Joshua 4:5 to Samuel’s Ebenezer (1 Samuel 7:12, now on these pages) and the one stone with seven eyes before another Joshua (Zechariah 3:9, already on these pages). ⭐ 1 Samuel 6:14–18 (now on these pages) has “a great stone” in the field of Joshua at Beth-shemesh, on which the Levites set the ark; the Hebrew of verse 18 then reads “the great Abel”, with a feminine adjective that fits even, and the Greek reads “the great stone”. → first discussed at Deut 25:13
