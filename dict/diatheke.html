📖 Dictionary

diathēkē διαθήκη diathēkē

A COVENANT — and the word that named both halves of this Bible, by a route worth knowing. In ordinary Greek a diathēkē is a WILL, the document by which a man disposes of his estate; that is the sense Latin caught with testamentum, and through Latin English got TESTAMENT, and through that the titles Old Testament and New Testament. But in the Greek Old Testament diathēkē is the standing rendering of Hebrew berit, a sworn bond between parties, and that is unmistakably the sense at Matthew 26:28 — blood, a Passover meal, and an echo of Exodus 24:8. ⚠ So the two Testaments on every shelf in the world are named from the reading the shelf has since abandoned: KJV and Douay read 'testament' at 26:28 (Douay because it translates the Vulgate, which is the Latin route itself), while ASV, NIV and NWT all read 'covenant.' ⚠ A second dispute sits in the same verse: the earliest text reads 'my blood OF THE COVENANT' and the Byzantine tradition adds kainēs, 'of the NEW covenant' — which is where the KJV's famous phrasing comes from. Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25 read 'new covenant' with no dispute at all, so a copyist had two familiar parallels pulling the adjective in, and the next verse hands it to him outright. ⚠ Mark 14:24 has the same dispute and the same shorter reading, 'my blood of the covenant', with one more difference: where Matthew's blood is poured out peri pollōn, 'concerning many', Mark's is poured out hyper pollōn, 'on behalf of many' — a phrase found in no other verse of the New Testament — and Mark stops there, without Matthew's 'for the forgiveness of sins'. Moses's words at Exodus 24:8, 'the blood of the covenant', sit under both. ⭐⭐ Luke's own version of the supper (22:20) reads 'new covenant' with no dispute at all, and the phrase is not coined on the spot — it is the one place in the whole Hebrew Bible the words 'new covenant' occur, Jehovah's own promise through Jeremiah, 'the days are coming... when I will cut a NEW COVENANT with the house of Israel and the house of Judah' (Jeremiah 31:31, already on these pages), a covenant 'not like the covenant I cut with their fathers... though I was a husband to them' (31:32, same page). 1 Corinthians 11:25 and Hebrews 8:8–12 both go on to quote or echo the same Jeremiah text explicitly; none of the three Synoptic supper scenes cites it by name. ⚠ Luke's own supper also reuses the same root as a VERB two verses later: Jesus 'confers [diatithemai] a kingdom' on the Twelve 'just as my Father conferred [dietheto] one' on him (Luke 22:29) — the kingdom promised and the covenant just sealed over the cup, named in one Gospel's own Greek with the same word, once as noun and once as the verb it comes from. ⭐ Romans 11:27 has ‘the covenant from me for them’ without the adjective ‘new,’ the first clause of Isaiah 59:21 in the Greek, with ‘when I take away their sins’ from Isaiah 27:9. → first discussed at Matt 26:28
