📖 Dictionary

mas מַס la-mas

FORCED LABOUR, corvée — conscripted, unpaid work owed to a ruler or state; a levy paid in bodies rather than money. ⚠️ The word carries a bitter history in the Bible. It is what EGYPT imposed on Israel — 'they set taskmasters over them… to afflict them with their burdens' (Exodus 1:11 uses the cognate) — the very thing the exodus was a rescue FROM. So when Judges 1 says that, once Israel 'grew strong,' the tribes put the surviving Canaanites to MAS rather than driving them out (1:28, 30, 33, 35), the irony is deliberate and heavy: a people redeemed from forced labour imposing it, and doing so as a COMPROMISE with the command they were failing to keep. Solomon will later levy the same MAS on the remaining Canaanites (1 Kings 9:21) — and, disastrously, on Israel itself, which splits the kingdom. KJV 'tribute'; ESV/NIV 'forced labor.' ⭐ Deuteronomy 20:11 (already on these pages) sets the terms: a city very far off that answers with peace shall become forced labour for you, and shall serve you. Joshua 9 (now on these pages) gives the Gibeonites exactly that outcome — spared, and set to cutting wood and drawing water — without once using the word mas; the chapter says eved, servant, instead. The word itself arrives in Joshua at 16:10 and 17:13 (both now on these pages), on the Canaanites of Gezer and of Manasseh’s towns. ⭐ At 16:10 it arrives in its FULL two-word form, mas oved, 'a servant for forced labour' — the identical construction, letter for letter, this verse and only two others in the whole Hebrew Bible carry: Jacob's own blessing on Issachar, who 'became a servant for forced labour' by choice, not defeat (Genesis 49:15, already on these pages), and Solomon's census of Canaan's last remnants, levied 'to be a servant for forced labour, to this day' (1 Kings 9:21). Joshua 17:13 shortens the phrase to the bare one-word la-mas this entry already tracked — and its own opening clause, 'and it came about, when the sons of Israel grew strong,' is answered almost letter for letter at Judges 1:28, which then repeats the bare refrain three more times (1:30, 33, 35). Gezer's own failure is reported in the Bible's fuller, rarer phrase; every tribe's identical failure after it, in the refrain that phrase then contracts into. ⭐ ‘Adoram was over the forced labour’ stands in the list of 2 Samuel 20:24 (now on these pages); the first list (8:16–18) has no one over it. ⭐ ‘Adoniram son of Abda was over the forced labor’ in Solomon’s list (1 Kings 4:6, now on these pages); the ASV has ‘over the men subject to taskwork’, the KJV ‘over the tribute’. Solomon ‘raised forced labor out of all Israel’, thirty thousand men, with Adoniram over it (1 Kings 5:27–28, now on these pages). → first discussed at Judges 1:28
