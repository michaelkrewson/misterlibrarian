📖 Dictionary

matsav מַצָּב matsav raglei

A STANDING-PLACE — a post, a station, a position held; the noun of natsav, to stand, the root of matsevah, a standing stone, with which it shares its consonants. ⭐ Read as this noun it stands in ten verses of the Hebrew Bible, and in eight of them it is an enemy’s: the Philistine outpost Jonathan climbs up to at Michmash (1 Samuel 13:23; 14:1, 4, 6, 11, 15), the Philistine garrison at Bethlehem (2 Samuel 23:14), and the station Shebna is thrust from (Isaiah 22:19; none yet on these pages). The other two are Joshua 4:3 and 4:9 (now on these pages), the standing-place of the priests’ feet in the middle of the Jordan — the phrase matsav raglei stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, those two — from under which the twelve stones are taken and on which twelve more are set up. A matsav is a post that is held; the priests hold one in a riverbed. ⚠ The KJV and ASV read ‘the place where the priests’ feet stood firm’, the NIV ‘right where the priests are standing’, the NWT 1984 ‘the standing place of the feet of the priests’ at 4:9; in Spanish the RV60 «el lugar donde están firmes los pies de los sacerdotes», the NVI «el lugar donde los sacerdotes permanecieron de pie». → first discussed at Joshua 4:3
