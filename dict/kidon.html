📖 Dictionary

kidon כִּידוֹן kidon

A JAVELIN — a light, pointed weapon carried in the hand, and a rare enough word that its eight occurrences (seven written full, one short at Jeremiah 50:42) can all be read at once. Joshua 8:18 is the earliest in the Bible’s own story order: Jehovah tells Joshua to point it at Ai as a signal, not to throw it, and Joshua’s hand stays extended — per 8:26 — until the destruction is finished. Its other six appearances are all real battle gear: twice Goliath’s, once slung ‘between his shoulders’ and once named in David’s own list of the weapons he does not fear (1 Samuel 17:6, 45, now on these pages); three times a poetic image of war, an invading army that ‘grips bow and kidon’ (Jeremiah 6:23, and word for word against Babylon at 50:42) and the storm-blade Job’s war-horse charges toward (Job 39:23); and once Leviathan laughing at its rattle (Job 41:21). ⚠ The shelf cannot agree on the object. NWT, NIV, ASV and Spanish NVI read ‘javelin’/‘jabalina’; KJV, Geneva and both Reina-Valeras read ‘spear’/‘lanza’; and the Douay-Rheims alone reads ‘shield’ — following the Vulgate’s clipeus, a Latin-tradition divergence from the Hebrew rather than a genuine alternative reading of it. ⭐ In 1 Samuel 17 (now on these pages) the shelf splits differently than at Ai: the Greek reads a shield, aspis, at both verses, and the KJV (“a target of brass”, then “a shield”), the Geneva and the RV 1909 («escudo») follow it, while the ASV, NWT 1984 and RV60 keep “javelin”. → first discussed at Joshua 8:18
