📖 Dictionary

gal גַּל gal-avanim

A HEAP — of stones; and, from the same rolling root as galal and Gilgal, a WAVE. Jacob and Laban’s treaty is sworn over one, they took stones and made a heap, and they ate there by the heap (Genesis 31:46, already on these pages), and it is named twice, Galeed and Jegar-sahadutha, heap of witness. ★ Three men in the Bible are buried under one: gal-avanim gadol, ‘a great heap of stones,’ stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible — Achan in the Valley of Achor (Joshua 7:26, now on these pages), the king of Ai at his own gate (Joshua 8:29, now on these pages), and Absalom in the forest, a very great heap (2 Samuel 18:17; neither yet on these pages) — the thief of Jericho, the king of the Ruin, and the king’s son, each under a cairn that is a verdict. Ai itself becomes a tel, a different word for a mound, at 8:28. The waves are the psalms’ and Jonah’s: all your breakers and your waves have passed over me (Jonah 2:4; Psalm 42:8, neither yet on these pages). The KJV and ASV read ‘a great heap of stones’, the NWT 1984 ‘a big pile of stones’, the NIV ‘a large pile of rocks’; the RV60 «un gran montón de piedras». → first discussed at Joshua 7:26
