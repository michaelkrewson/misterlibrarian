Ziklag burned: David and his men come home on the third day to find the town burned by the Amalekites and the women and children carried off, among them Ahinoam and Abigail; the men weep until they have no strength and speak of stoning him, and David strengthens himself in Jehovah, asks for the ephod, and pursues; an Egyptian slave left behind leads him to the raiders, he rescues everything, and he rules that the share of the baggage equals the share of the battle, and sends gifts to the elders of Judah.
Undated: the same days as the battle of Gilboa, which David hears of at Ziklag after two days (2 Samuel 1:1); the chapter follows David’s dismissal by the Philistines in chapter 29.

1
וַיְהִי בְּבֹא דָוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו, צִקְלַג--בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁלִישִׁי; וַעֲמָלֵקִי פָשְׁטוּ, אֶל-נֶגֶב וְאֶל-צִקְלַג, וַיַּכּוּ אֶת-צִקְלַג, וַיִּשְׂרְפוּ אֹתָהּ בָּאֵשׁ.
And it happened, when David and his men came to Ziklag on the third day, that the Amalekites had raided the Negev and Ziklag; and they had struck Ziklag, and burned it with fire,note
Y sucedió que, cuando David y sus hombres llegaron a Siclag al tercer día, los amalecitas habían hecho una incursión contra el Néguev y contra Siclag; y habían herido a Siclag, y la habían quemado con fuego,
2
וַיִּשְׁבּוּ אֶת-הַנָּשִׁים אֲשֶׁר-בָּהּ מִקָּטֹן וְעַד-גָּדוֹל, לֹא הֵמִיתוּ אִישׁ; וַיִּנְהֲגוּ, וַיֵּלְכוּ לְדַרְכָּם.
and had taken captive the women who were in it, from the small to the great; they had put no one to death, but led them off and went on their way.note
y habían llevado cautivas a las mujeres que estaban en ella, desde el pequeño hasta el grande; no habían dado muerte a nadie, sino que las condujeron y se fueron por su camino.
3
וַיָּבֹא דָוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו אֶל-הָעִיר, וְהִנֵּה שְׂרוּפָה בָּאֵשׁ; וּנְשֵׁיהֶם וּבְנֵיהֶם וּבְנֹתֵיהֶם, נִשְׁבּוּ.
And David and his men came to the city, and look — it was burned with fire; and their wives and their sons and their daughters had been taken captive.note
Y David y sus hombres llegaron a la ciudad, y mira, estaba quemada con fuego; y sus mujeres y sus hijos y sus hijas habían sido llevados cautivos.
4
וַיִּשָּׂא דָוִד וְהָעָם אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ, אֶת-קוֹלָם--וַיִּבְכּוּ:  עַד אֲשֶׁר אֵין-בָּהֶם כֹּחַ, לִבְכּוֹת.
And David and the people who were with him lifted up their voice and wept, until there was no strength in them to weep.note
Y David y el pueblo que estaba con él alzaron su voz y lloraron, hasta que no hubo en ellos fuerza para llorar.
5
וּשְׁתֵּי נְשֵׁי-דָוִד, נִשְׁבּוּ--אֲחִינֹעַם, הַיִּזְרְעֵלִית, וַאֲבִיגַיִל, אֵשֶׁת נָבָל הַכַּרְמְלִי.
And David’s two wives had been taken captive: Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite.note
Y las dos mujeres de David habían sido llevadas cautivas: Ahinoam la jezreelita, y Abigail la mujer de Nabal el carmelita.
6
וַתֵּצֶר לְדָוִד מְאֹד, כִּי-אָמְרוּ הָעָם לְסָקְלוֹ--כִּי-מָרָה נֶפֶשׁ כָּל-הָעָם, אִישׁ עַל-בָּנָו וְעַל-בְּנֹתָיו; וַיִּתְחַזֵּק דָּוִד, בַּיהוָה אֱלֹהָיו.  {ס}
And David was greatly distressed, because the people spoke of stoning him; for the soul of all the people was bitter, each man for his sons and for his daughters. But David strengthened himself in Jehovah his God.note
Y David se angustió mucho, porque el pueblo hablaba de apedrearlo; pues el alma de todo el pueblo estaba amargada, cada uno por sus hijos y por sus hijas. Pero David se fortaleció en Jehová su Dios.
7
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, אֶל-אֶבְיָתָר הַכֹּהֵן בֶּן-אֲחִימֶלֶךְ, הַגִּישָׁה-נָּא לִי, הָאֵפוֹד; וַיַּגֵּשׁ אֶבְיָתָר אֶת-הָאֵפוֹד, אֶל-דָּוִד.
And David said to Abiathar the priest, the son of Ahimelech: ‘Bring the ephod here to me, please.’ And Abiathar brought the ephod to David.note
Y David dijo a Abiatar el sacerdote, hijo de Ahimelec: «Trae aquí el efod, por favor». Y Abiatar trajo el efod a David.
8
וַיִּשְׁאַל דָּוִד בַּיהוָה לֵאמֹר, אֶרְדֹּף אַחֲרֵי הַגְּדוּד-הַזֶּה הַאַשִּׂגֶנּוּ; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ רְדֹף, כִּי-הַשֵּׂג תַּשִּׂיג וְהַצֵּל תַּצִּיל.
And David inquired of Jehovah, saying: ‘Shall I pursue this troop? Will I overtake them?’ And he said to him: ‘Pursue; for you will surely overtake, and you will surely rescue.’note
Y David preguntó a Jehová, diciendo: «¿Perseguiré a esta tropa? ¿La alcanzaré?». Y él le dijo: «Persigue; porque de cierto alcanzarás, y de cierto rescatarás».
9
וַיֵּלֶךְ דָּוִד, הוּא וְשֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, וַיָּבֹאוּ, עַד-נַחַל הַבְּשׂוֹר; וְהַנּוֹתָרִים, עָמָדוּ.
And David went, he and the six hundred men who were with him, and they came to the brook Besor; and the rest stayed.note
Y David fue, él y los seiscientos hombres que estaban con él, y llegaron hasta el torrente de Besor; y los demás se quedaron.
10
וַיִּרְדֹּף דָּוִד, הוּא וְאַרְבַּע-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ; וַיַּעַמְדוּ, מָאתַיִם אִישׁ, אֲשֶׁר פִּגְּרוּ, מֵעֲבֹר אֶת-נַחַל הַבְּשׂוֹר.
And David pursued, he and four hundred men; but two hundred men stayed behind, who were too faint to cross the brook Besor.note
Y David persiguió, él y cuatrocientos hombres; porque doscientos hombres se quedaron atrás, que estaban demasiado cansados para cruzar el torrente de Besor.
11
וַיִּמְצְאוּ אִישׁ-מִצְרִי בַּשָּׂדֶה, וַיִּקְחוּ אֹתוֹ אֶל-דָּוִד; וַיִּתְּנוּ-לוֹ לֶחֶם וַיֹּאכַל, וַיַּשְׁקֻהוּ מָיִם.
And they found an Egyptian man in the field, and took him to David; and they gave him bread, and he ate, and they gave him water to drink,note
Y hallaron a un hombre egipcio en el campo, y lo llevaron a David; y le dieron pan, y comió, y le dieron a beber agua,
12
וַיִּתְּנוּ-לוֹ פֶלַח דְּבֵלָה וּשְׁנֵי צִמֻּקִים, וַיֹּאכַל, וַתָּשָׁב רוּחוֹ, אֵלָיו:  כִּי לֹא-אָכַל לֶחֶם, וְלֹא-שָׁתָה מַיִם, שְׁלֹשָׁה יָמִים, וּשְׁלֹשָׁה לֵילוֹת.  {ס}
and they gave him a slice of a cake of figs and two clusters of raisins; and he ate, and his spirit returned to him; for he had not eaten bread or drunk water for three days and three nights.note
y le dieron un pedazo de torta de higos y dos racimos de pasas; y comió, y su espíritu volvió a él; porque no había comido pan ni bebido agua por tres días y tres noches.
13
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ דָוִד לְמִי-אַתָּה, וְאֵי מִזֶּה אָתָּה; וַיֹּאמֶר נַעַר מִצְרִי אָנֹכִי, עֶבֶד לְאִישׁ עֲמָלֵקִי, וַיַּעַזְבֵנִי אֲדֹנִי כִּי חָלִיתִי, הַיּוֹם שְׁלֹשָׁה.
And David said to him: ‘To whom do you belong, and where are you from?’ And he said: ‘I am a young Egyptian, the slave of an Amalekite man; and my master left me, because I fell sick three days ago.note
Y David le dijo: «¿De quién eres tú, y de dónde eres?». Y él dijo: «Soy un joven egipcio, esclavo de un hombre amalecita; y mi amo me dejó, porque hace tres días caí enfermo.
14
אֲנַחְנוּ פָּשַׁטְנוּ נֶגֶב הַכְּרֵתִי, וְעַל-אֲשֶׁר לִיהוּדָה--וְעַל-נֶגֶב כָּלֵב; וְאֶת-צִקְלַג, שָׂרַפְנוּ בָאֵשׁ.
We raided the Negev of the Cherethites, and what belongs to Judah, and the Negev of Caleb; and Ziklag we burned with fire.’note
Nosotros hicimos una incursión contra el Néguev de los cereteos, y contra lo que pertenece a Judá, y contra el Néguev de Caleb; y a Siclag la quemamos con fuego».
15
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו דָּוִד, הֲתוֹרִדֵנִי אֶל-הַגְּדוּד הַזֶּה; וַיֹּאמֶר הִשָּׁבְעָה לִּי בֵאלֹהִים אִם-תְּמִיתֵנִי, וְאִם-תַּסְגִּרֵנִי בְּיַד-אֲדֹנִי, וְאוֹרִדְךָ, אֶל-הַגְּדוּד הַזֶּה.
And David said to him: ‘Will you take me down to this troop?’ And he said: ‘Swear to me by God that you will not put me to death, and will not hand me over into the hand of my master, and I will take you down to this troop.’note
Y David le dijo: «¿Me bajarás a esta tropa?». Y él dijo: «Júrame por Dios que no me darás muerte, ni me entregarás en mano de mi amo, y yo te bajaré a esta tropa».
16
וַיֹּרִדֵהוּ, וְהִנֵּה נְטֻשִׁים עַל-פְּנֵי כָל-הָאָרֶץ; אֹכְלִים וְשֹׁתִים, וְחֹגְגִים, בְּכֹל הַשָּׁלָל הַגָּדוֹל, אֲשֶׁר לָקְחוּ מֵאֶרֶץ פְּלִשְׁתִּים וּמֵאֶרֶץ יְהוּדָה.
And he took him down, and look — they were scattered over the whole ground, eating and drinking and celebrating, because of all the great spoil that they had taken from the land of the Philistines and from the land of Judah.note
Y lo bajó, y mira, estaban dispersos sobre toda la tierra, comiendo y bebiendo y festejando, por todo el gran botín que habían tomado de la tierra de los filisteos y de la tierra de Judá.
17
וַיַּכֵּם דָּוִד מֵהַנֶּשֶׁף וְעַד-הָעֶרֶב, לְמָחֳרָתָם; וְלֹא-נִמְלַט מֵהֶם אִישׁ, כִּי אִם-אַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת אִישׁ-נַעַר אֲשֶׁר-רָכְבוּ עַל-הַגְּמַלִּים וַיָּנֻסוּ.
And David struck them from the twilight until the evening of the next day; and not a man of them escaped, except four hundred young men who rode on camels and fled.note
Y los hirió David desde el crepúsculo hasta la tarde del día siguiente; y no escapó de ellos ningún hombre, sino cuatrocientos jóvenes que montaron en camellos y huyeron.
18
וַיַּצֵּל דָּוִד, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר לָקְחוּ עֲמָלֵק; וְאֶת-שְׁתֵּי נָשָׁיו, הִצִּיל דָּוִד.
And David rescued everything that Amalek had taken; and his two wives David rescued.note
Y David rescató todo lo que Amalec había tomado; y a sus dos mujeres las rescató David.
19
וְלֹא נֶעְדַּר-לָהֶם מִן-הַקָּטֹן וְעַד-הַגָּדוֹל וְעַד-בָּנִים וּבָנוֹת, וּמִשָּׁלָל, וְעַד כָּל-אֲשֶׁר לָקְחוּ, לָהֶם:  הַכֹּל, הֵשִׁיב דָּוִד.
And nothing was lacking to them, from the small to the great, to sons and daughters, to spoil, and to all that they had taken for themselves; David brought back everything.note
Y no les faltó nada, desde el pequeño hasta el grande, ni hijos ni hijas, ni del botín, ni de todo lo que habían tomado para sí; todo lo hizo volver David.
20
וַיִּקַּח דָּוִד, אֶת-כָּל-הַצֹּאן וְהַבָּקָר; נָהֲגוּ, לִפְנֵי הַמִּקְנֶה הַהוּא, וַיֹּאמְרוּ, זֶה שְׁלַל דָּוִד.
And David took all the flocks and the herds; they drove them before that other livestock, and said: ‘This is David’s spoil.’note
Y David tomó todas las ovejas y las vacas; las llevaron delante de aquel otro ganado, y dijeron: «Este es el botín de David».
21
וַיָּבֹא דָוִד, אֶל-מָאתַיִם הָאֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר-פִּגְּרוּ מִלֶּכֶת אַחֲרֵי דָוִד וַיֹּשִׁיבֻם בְּנַחַל הַבְּשׂוֹר, וַיֵּצְאוּ לִקְרַאת דָּוִד, וְלִקְרַאת הָעָם אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ; וַיִּגַּשׁ דָּוִד אֶת-הָעָם, וַיִּשְׁאַל לָהֶם לְשָׁלוֹם.  {ס}
And David came to the two hundred men who had been too faint to follow David, and whom they had made stay at the brook Besor; and they went out to meet David, and to meet the people who were with him; and David drew near to the people, and asked after their welfare.note
Y David llegó a los doscientos hombres que habían estado demasiado cansados para seguir a David, y a quienes habían hecho quedarse en el torrente de Besor; y salieron al encuentro de David, y al encuentro del pueblo que estaba con él; y David se acercó al pueblo, y preguntó por su bienestar.
22
וַיַּעַן כָּל-אִישׁ-רָע וּבְלִיַּעַל, מֵהָאֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר הָלְכוּ עִם-דָּוִד, וַיֹּאמְרוּ יַעַן אֲשֶׁר לֹא-הָלְכוּ עִמִּי, לֹא-נִתֵּן לָהֶם מֵהַשָּׁלָל אֲשֶׁר הִצַּלְנוּ:  כִּי-אִם-אִישׁ אֶת-אִשְׁתּוֹ וְאֶת-בָּנָיו, וְיִנְהֲגוּ וְיֵלֵכוּ.  {ס}
And every wicked man and man of worthlessness among the men who had gone with David answered and said: ‘Because they did not go with me, we will not give them any of the spoil that we rescued — except each man his wife and his sons; let them lead them away, and go.’note
Y respondió todo hombre malo y de la nada, de los hombres que habían ido con David, y dijeron: «Porque no fueron conmigo, no les daremos nada del botín que rescatamos; solamente a cada uno su mujer y sus hijos, que los lleven y se vayan».
23
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, לֹא-תַעֲשׂוּ כֵן אֶחָי:  אֵת אֲשֶׁר-נָתַן יְהוָה לָנוּ, וַיִּשְׁמֹר אֹתָנוּ, וַיִּתֵּן אֶת-הַגְּדוּד הַבָּא עָלֵינוּ, בְּיָדֵנוּ.
And David said: ‘You shall not do so, my brothers, with what Jehovah has given us; he has kept us, and has given the troop that came against us into our hand.note
Y David dijo: «No harán así, hermanos míos, con lo que Jehová nos ha dado; él nos ha guardado, y ha entregado en nuestra mano a la tropa que vino contra nosotros.
24
וּמִי יִשְׁמַע לָכֶם, לַדָּבָר הַזֶּה:  כִּי כְּחֵלֶק הַיֹּרֵד בַּמִּלְחָמָה, וּכְחֵלֶק הַיֹּשֵׁב עַל-הַכֵּלִים--יַחְדָּו יַחֲלֹקוּ.  {ס}
And who will listen to you in this matter? For as is the share of him who goes down to the battle, so is the share of him who stays by the baggage; together they shall divide.’note
¿Y quién los escuchará en este asunto? Porque como la parte del que baja a la batalla, así será la parte del que se queda junto al equipaje; juntos repartirán».
25
וַיְהִי, מֵהַיּוֹם הַהוּא וָמָעְלָה; וַיְשִׂמֶהָ לְחֹק וּלְמִשְׁפָּט, לְיִשְׂרָאֵל, עַד, הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.  {פ}
And it was so from that day forward; and he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel, to this day.note
Y fue así desde aquel día en adelante; y lo estableció como estatuto y ordenanza para Israel, hasta el día de hoy.
26
וַיָּבֹא דָוִד אֶל-צִקְלַג, וַיְשַׁלַּח מֵהַשָּׁלָל לְזִקְנֵי יְהוּדָה לְרֵעֵהוּ לֵאמֹר:  הִנֵּה לָכֶם  {ר}  בְּרָכָה, מִשְּׁלַל אֹיְבֵי יְהוָה.  {ס}
And David came to Ziklag, and sent some of the spoil to the elders of Judah, to his neighbours, saying: ‘Look — a blessing for you from the spoil of the enemies of Jehovah’:note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · Ziklag burned — the Amalekites raid the Negev, the women taken from small to great, “until there was no strength in them to weep”, and David’s two wives

On the third day. “When David and his men came to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had raided the Negev and Ziklag”: the verb is pashat, to raid, the verb of David’s own raids on “the Geshurite and the Gizrite and the Amalekite” (27:8, already on these pages) and of the Philistine raid on Keilah (23:27); the text does not call this a return blow. Ziklag is the town Achish gave him (27:6). The ASV has “the Amalekites had made a raid upon the South”; the KJV “had invaded the south”; the NWT 1984 “made a raid on the south”; the NIV “had raided the Negev”; the Douay “had made an invasion on the south side”.

Taken, and not killed. “And had taken captive the women who were in it, from the small to the great”, and “led them off and went on their way”: the Amalekites take captives and kill none. David’s raids were the opposite, “would not let man or woman live” (27:9, 27:11), and Saul had been told to put Amalek to death “from man to woman” (15:3, all already on these pages). The Greek says they put to death “neither man nor woman”. The ASV has “they slew not any”; the NWT 1984 “They did not put anyone to death”; the NIV “They killed none of them”; the Geneva “slew not a man”.

Weeping. “And look — it was burned with fire; and their wives and their sons and their daughters had been taken captive. And David and the people who were with him lifted up their voice and wept, until there was no strength in them to weep”: the people lifted up their voice and wept at Bochim (Judges 2:4), at Bethel (Judges 21:2) and at Gibeah when Jabesh’s messengers came (11:4, all already on these pages). “No strength in them” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. The ASV has “until they had no more power to weep”; the NIV “until they had no strength left to weep”; the NWT 1984 “until there was in them no power to weep”; the Douay “till they had no more tears”.

Ahinoam and Abigail. “And David’s two wives had been taken captive: Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite”: the two wives of 25:43 and 27:3 (already on these pages), Ahinoam still named for the Jezreel of Judah (Jezreel in Judah) and Abigail still for her first husband. At 27:3 the Carmelite is feminine, “the Carmelitess”, and here it is masculine, “Nabal the Carmelite”. The ASV and KJV have “Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite”; the NIV “Abigail, the widow of Nabal of Carmel”.

Verses 6–8 · “David strengthened himself in Jehovah his God” — the people speak of stoning him, the ephod again, and “you will surely rescue”

Bitter of soul. “And David was greatly distressed, because the people spoke of stoning him; for the soul of all the people was bitter, each man for his sons and for his daughters”: vatetser, from tsar, narrow, the verb of being hemmed in. The people are the men who “gathered themselves to him”, “every man bitter of soul” (22:2, already on these pages); the same root, mar, is here marah, and the bitterness that brought them to him now turns on him. “Stoning” is saqal. The ASV has “the people spake of stoning him, because the soul of all the people was grieved”; the NWT 1984 “the soul of all the people had become bitter”; the NIV “each one was bitter in spirit”; the Douay “the soul of every man was bitterly grieved”.

Strengthened himself. “But David strengthened himself in Jehovah his God”: va-yitchazzeq, the reflexive form of chazaq. “David strengthened himself” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. At Horesh it was Jonathan who “strengthened his hand in God” (23:16, already on these pages); here there is no Jonathan, and David does it for himself. The ASV has “David strengthened himself in Jehovah his God”; the KJV “David encouraged himself”; the NWT 1984 “took to strengthening himself by Jehovah his God”; the NIV “found strength in the Lord his God”; the Douay “took courage in the Lord his God”; the Geneva “comforted himself”.

The ephod, and the answer. “Bring the ephod here to me, please”: the request of 23:9 (already on these pages), to Abiathar son of Ahimelech, the one man to get away from Nob (22:20); the Greek has only “Bring the ephod” and not the clause that Abiathar brought it. “And David inquired of Jehovah”: Saul had “asked of Jehovah, and Jehovah did not answer him” (28:6, already on these pages), and David is answered at once, “Pursue; for you will surely overtake, and you will surely rescue.” “You will surely overtake” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. “Rescue” is natsal, and it is the verb of verse 18. The ASV has “thou shalt surely overtake them, and shalt without fail recover all”; the NWT 1984 “you will without fail make a deliverance”; the NIV “succeed in the rescue”; the Douay “recover the prey”. For “troop”, gedud, the ASV has “this troop”; the NWT 1984 “this marauder band”; the NIV “this raiding party”; the Douay “these robbers”. On the ephod, see the earlier entries.

Verses 9–10 · The brook Besor — six hundred, four hundred who pursue and two hundred who are too faint to cross

The brook Besor. “And David went, he and the six hundred men who were with him”: the six hundred of 23:13 and 27:2 (already on these pages). “They came to the brook Besor”: nachal ha-Besor, which stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in this chapter (verses 9, 10 and 21). It is usually taken for the wadi that runs to the sea near Gaza (reported, not verified here); Besor has its own entry. The ASV and KJV have “the brook Besor”; the NWT 1984 “the torrent valley of Besor”; the NIV “the Besor Valley”; the Douay “the torrent Besor”.

Too faint to cross. “But two hundred men stayed behind, who were too faint to cross the brook Besor”: pigru, which stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and verse 21. The six hundred divide here as they did at 25:13 (already on these pages), “about four hundred men”, and “two hundred stayed by the baggage”. The ASV has “who were so faint that they could not go over the brook Besor”; the NIV “too exhausted to cross the valley”; the NWT 1984 “too tired to pass over the torrent valley of Besor”; the Douay “who being weary could not go over the torrent Besor”; the Geneva “too weary to go over the river Besor”.

Verses 11–15 · An Egyptian in the field — bread, a cake of figs, “his spirit returned”, and “swear to me by God that you will not hand me over”

Figs and raisins. “And they found an Egyptian man in the field”: Amalek was the people “from Havilah as you come to Shur, which faces Egypt” (15:7, already on these pages). They give him bread and water, and “a slice of a cake of figs and two clusters of raisins”: the food of Abigail’s gift to David, “a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs” (25:18, already on these pages). The ASV has “a piece of a cake of figs, and two clusters of raisins”; the NWT 1984 “a slice of a cake of pressed figs and two cakes of raisins”; the NIV “part of a cake of pressed figs and two cakes of raisins”; the Douay “a piece of a cake of figs, and two bunches of raisins”; the Geneva “a few figs, and two clusters of raisins”.

His spirit returned. “And he ate, and his spirit returned to him; for he had not eaten bread or drunk water for three days and three nights”: “his spirit returned” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Samson at En-hakkore (Judges 15:19, already on these pages). “Three days and three nights” also stands in two verses, here and Jonah in the fish (Jonah 2:1 in the Hebrew numbering, which is 1:17 in English Bibles). The ASV has “his spirit came again to him”; the NWT 1984 “his spirit returned to him”; the Douay “his spirit returned, and he was refreshed”; the NIV “He ate and was revived”.

To whom do you belong? “To whom do you belong, and where are you from?”: the question asked of a slave. “I am a young Egyptian, the slave of an Amalekite man; and my master left me, because I fell sick three days ago”: ha-yom shloshah, “today three”. The raiders’ route is the Egyptian’s account: “the Negev of the Cherethites, and what belongs to Judah, and the Negev of Caleb”. The Cherethites are a people of the Philistine coast (Zephaniah 2:5), and the name returns for the guard of David’s own reign, “the Cherethites and the Pelethites” (2 Samuel 8:18, not yet); the Greek has “Cholthi”. The Negev of Caleb is the land of Nabal the Calebite (25:3) and of Achsah’s request, “you have given me the land of the Negev” (Judges 1:15, both already on these pages). The ASV has “To whom belongest thou?” and “I am a young man of Egypt, servant to an Amalekite”; the NIV “Who do you belong to?”; the NWT 1984 “To whom do you belong, and where are you from?” and “a slave of an Amalekite man”; the Douay “the south side of Cerethi”.

Swear to me by God. “Swear to me by God that you will not put me to death, and will not hand me over into the hand of my master”: tasgireni, “you will hand me over”, the verb of the law “You shall not hand over to his master a slave who has escaped to you” (Deuteronomy 23:16, already on these pages). The form stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. This slave was left behind, not fled, so the law fits him only by its spirit. He swears by Elohim, God, and not by the name. The Hebrew does not say that David swore; the Douay adds “And David swore to him”. The ASV has “Swear unto me by God, that thou wilt neither kill me, nor deliver me up into the hands of my master”; the NIV “Swear to me before God”; the NWT 1984 “you will not surrender me into the hand of my master”.

Verses 16–20 · David rescues all — a feast on the stolen spoil, “from the twilight” of which end of the night, and “nothing was lacking”

Celebrating. “They were scattered over the whole ground, eating and drinking and celebrating”: chogegim, from hagag, the verb of the pilgrim feast; the ASV and KJV have “dancing”, the NIV “reveling”, the NWT 1984 “having a feast”, the Douay “keeping a festival day”. “Because of all the great spoil that they had taken from the land of the Philistines and from the land of Judah”: the two lands together stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The Amalekites have raided both, and while the Philistines march north (29:11, already on these pages) their own country is stripped.

From the twilight. “And David struck them from the twilight until the evening of the next day”: nesheph is a twilight, and which one is not fixed. Proverbs 7:9 sets it “in the evening of the day”, and Psalm 119:147, “I rise before the dawn” (Psalm 119:147, already on these pages), is the one before morning. The ASV has “from the twilight even unto the evening of the next day”; the NIV “from dusk until the evening of the next day”; the Douay “from the evening unto the evening of the next day”; the NWT 1984 “from the morning darkness until the evening”, and it adds “that he might devote them to destruction”, which is its reading of the last word of the clause. “Not a man of them escaped, except four hundred young men who rode on camels and fled”: four hundred fled, as four hundred pursued.

Rescued. “And David rescued everything that Amalek had taken; and his two wives David rescued”: the verb is natsal twice, the verb of “you will surely rescue” in verse 8, and the ASV breaks it into “recovered” and “rescued”; the NWT 1984 keeps the one verb, “deliver … delivered”. “And nothing was lacking to them, from the small to the great”: the reversal of verse 2, where the women were taken “from the small to the great”. The ASV has “there was nothing lacking to them, neither small nor great”; the NWT 1984 “from the smallest to the greatest”; the NIV “Nothing was missing: young or old”.

David’s spoil. “And David took all the flocks and the herds; they drove them before that other livestock, and said: ‘This is David’s spoil’”: the livestock of the Amalekites themselves, driven ahead of what was given back. The Hebrew does not say who speaks. The ASV has “which they drove before those other cattle, and said, This is David’s spoil”; the NIV “his men drove them ahead of the other livestock”; the NWT 1984 “which they drove before that [other] livestock”; the Douay “made them go before him: and they said: This is the prey of David”.

Verses 21–25 · The share of the baggage — “as is the share of him who goes down to the battle”, worthless men, and a statute and an ordinance for Israel

Asked after their welfare. “And David drew near to the people, and asked after their welfare”: sha’al le-shalom, as Saul’s men were to ask after his (10:4) and David asked his brothers “how they were” (17:22, both already on these pages). The ASV has “he saluted them”; the NWT 1984 “began to ask them how they were”; the NIV “he asked them how they were”; the Douay “saluted them peaceably”.

Worthless men. “And every wicked man and man of worthlessness among the men who had gone with David answered”: ish ra u-veliyya’al, a phrase that stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Worthlessness, beliyya’al, is Eli’s sons’ (2:12) and Nabal’s (25:17, both already on these pages). “Because they did not go with me, we will not give them any of the spoil that we rescued”: each man speaks for himself, “with me”, and they use the verb of David’s rescue for their own. The ASV has “the wicked men and base fellows”; the KJV “wicked men and men of Belial”; the NWT 1984 “every bad and good-for-nothing man”; the NIV “all the evil men and troublemakers”; the Douay “the wicked and unjust men”.

The share of the baggage. “You shall not do so, my brothers, with what Jehovah has given us; he has kept us, and has given the troop that came against us into our hand”: the credit to Jehovah, who “kept” them. “For as is the share of him who goes down to the battle, so is the share of him who stays by the baggage; together they shall divide”: “together they shall divide” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The baggage, kelim, is where Saul hid at his choosing (10:22) and where David left his own before he ran to the battle line (17:22), and where two hundred stayed at 25:13 (all already on these pages). The law of the spoil had divided the prey between “those who took hold of the war” and “the whole congregation” (Numbers 31:27), and Joshua sent the Transjordan tribes home to “divide the spoil of your enemies with your brothers” (Joshua 22:8, both already on these pages). The Greek adds in verse 24, “for they are not less than you”. The ASV has “so shall his share be that tarrieth by the baggage: they shall share alike”; the KJV “tarrieth by the stuff”; the NWT 1984 “the one that sat by the baggage”; the NIV “The share of the man who stayed with the supplies is to be the same as that of him who went down to the battle”; the Douay “him that abode at the baggage”.

A statute and an ordinance. “And it was so from that day forward; and he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel, to this day”: le-choq u-le-mishpat. The pair stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: at the bitter water of Marah (Exodus 15:25), at Shechem (Joshua 24:25, both already on these pages), here, and Ezra’s heart (Ezra 7:10, not yet). The ASV and KJV have “a statute and an ordinance for Israel unto this day”; the NWT 1984 “a regulation and a judicial decision”; the NIV “a statute and ordinance for Israel”; the Douay “a statute, and an ordinance, and as a law”.

Verses 26–31 · “A blessing for you from the spoil of the enemies of Jehovah” — gifts to the elders of Judah, a list of thirteen places, and the Jerahmeelite and the Kenite

A blessing for his neighbours. “And sent some of the spoil to the elders of Judah, to his neighbours, saying: ‘Look — a blessing for you from the spoil of the enemies of Jehovah’”: berakhah, blessing, the word of Abigail’s gift (25:27, already on these pages), and le-re’ehu is from rea, neighbour or friend, the word of the “neighbour of yours who is better than you” who was to have Saul’s kingdom (15:28, already on these pages). “The enemies of Jehovah” are the Amalekites; “the battles of Jehovah” were Abigail’s phrase for David’s fighting (25:28, already on these pages). The ASV has “a present for you of the spoil of the enemies of Jehovah” and “even to his friends”; the NWT 1984 “a gift blessing”; the NIV “a gift for you from the plunder”; the Douay “Receive a blessing of the prey”.

The list. Thirteen entries follow, eleven towns and the cities of the Jerahmeelite and of the Kenite, all in the south of Judah. Jattir and Eshtemoa are in Judah’s hill country (Joshua 15:48, 15:50) and are Levitical towns (21:14); Hormah is the town of the old defeat and victory (Numbers 14:45, Judges 1:17); Hebron is the future capital (Hebron) (all already on these pages). This Beth-el is not the Bethel of the north; it is probably the southern Bethul, “Eltolad, and Bethul, and Hormah” (Joshua 19:4, already on these pages), which the Chronicler calls Bethuel (1 Chronicles 4:30, not yet). This Aroer is not the Aroer of the Arnon. Siphmoth stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and so does Bor-ashan; the KJV’s “Chorashan” follows another reading of the word, and the Greek has “Beersheba” in its place. Racal and Athach are not known elsewhere (reported, not verified here). The Greek names several of the places otherwise, among them Beth-zur for Beth-el and Carmel for Racal. The Jerahmeelite and the Kenite are the peoples David had told Achish he was raiding, “the Negev of the Jerahmeelite … the Negev of the Kenite” (27:10, already on these pages); he sends them gifts. The ASV has “Beth-el”, “Ramoth of the South”, “Jattir”, “Aroer”, “Siphmoth”, “Eshtemoa”, “Racal”, “Hormah”, “Bor-ashan” and “Athach”; the KJV “Chorashan”; the Douay “the lake Asan”; the NIV “Bethel, Ramoth Negev and Jattir”.

Where David walked about. “And to all the places where David walked about, he and his men”: hithalekh, the verb of “they went about wherever they went about” at Keilah (23:13, already on these pages; see hithalekh). The wandering of the fugitive years is paid back in gifts. The ASV has “to all the places where David himself and his men were wont to haunt”; the NWT 1984 “to all the places where David had walked about, he and his men”; the NIV “where he and his men had roamed”; the Douay “in which David had abode with his men”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “raided” for pashat, as in 27:8; “the Negev”; “from the small to the great” in verses 2 and 19 alike; “put no one to death”; “lifted up their voice and wept”, as in 11:4; “bitter”, as in 22:2; “stoning” for saqal; “strengthened himself”, as in 23:16; “Bring the ephod here”, as in 23:9; “inquired of Jehovah”, as in 23:2; “rescue” for natsal in verses 8 and 18 alike, and “rescued” in verse 22; “the troop” for gedud; “the brook Besor”; “too faint” for pigru; “cake of figs” and “clusters of raisins”, as in 25:18; “his spirit returned”, as in Judges 15:19; “hand me over” for tasgir, as in Deuteronomy 23:16; “asked after their welfare”, as in 10:4; “worthlessness” for beliyya’al, as in 2:12 and 25:17; “baggage” for kelim, as in 10:22 and 17:22; “a statute and an ordinance”, as in Exodus 15:25; “a blessing” for berakhah, as in 25:27; “neighbours” for rea, as in 15:28; and “walked about” for hithalekh, as in 23:13.

Echoes paid. David’s raids on the Amalekite and the other peoples, and his living remnant (27:8–11); Saul’s order to put Amalek to death (15:3); the weeping at Bochim, Bethel and Gibeah (Judges 2:4; 21:2; 1 Samuel 11:4); the two wives (25:43; 27:3); the six hundred and the split of four hundred and two hundred (23:13; 25:13; 27:2); every man bitter of soul (22:2); Jonathan at Horesh (23:16); the ephod and Abiathar (22:20; 23:9); Saul unanswered (28:6); Abigail’s figs and raisins and blessing and battles of Jehovah (25:18, 27, 28); the Amalek of Havilah and Shur (15:7); Samson’s returning spirit (Judges 15:19); Nabal the Calebite and Achsah’s Negev (25:3; Judges 1:15); the law of the escaped slave (Deuteronomy 23:16); the welfare question (10:4; 17:22); the sons of worthlessness (2:12; 25:17); the baggage (10:22; 17:22; 25:13); the spoil laws of Numbers 31:27 and Joshua 22:8; the statute and ordinance of Marah and Shechem (Exodus 15:25; Joshua 24:25); the neighbour of 15:28; the towns of Joshua 15, 19 and 21 and Hormah’s two accounts (Numbers 14:45; Judges 1:17); the Jerahmeelite and the Kenite of 27:10; and David’s walking about (23:13).

Echoes planted. The Cherethites and the Pelethites in David’s reign (2 Samuel 8:18) and the nation of the Cherethites (Zephaniah 2:5); “three days and three nights” in Jonah (2:1); the evening twilight of Proverbs 7:9; the statute and ordinance in Ezra 7:10; Bethuel and Hormah and Ziklag in 1 Chronicles 4:30; the Philistine battle at Gilboa and the death of Saul and his sons (31:1–6), which David hears of after he has stayed “in Ziklag two days” (2 Samuel 1:1); and the elders of Judah anointing David at Hebron (2 Samuel 2:1–4). None is translated here yet except 2 Samuel 1:1, which already is.

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 31 — Gilboa: the Philistines fight Israel and the men of Israel flee; Saul’s sons, Jonathan among them, are killed; Saul, wounded by the archers, asks his armour-bearer to run him through, and falls on his own sword; the Philistines fasten the bodies to the wall of Beth-shean, until the men of Jabesh-gilead come by night.
