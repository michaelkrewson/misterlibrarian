The shofar sounds in Zion and the day of Jehovah comes as an army of locusts, fire before and behind, Eden turned to wilderness; then “even now”, return with all your heart, tear your heart and not your garments, and a fast, an assembly and the priests weeping between the porch and the altar; Jehovah is jealous for his land and answers with grain, wine and oil; the early rain comes, the years the locust has eaten are restored, and afterward the spirit is poured out on all flesh, with wonders and the promise that everyone who calls on the name of Jehovah will escape.
Undatable, as in chapter 1: the heading names no king and nothing in the book supplies one, so this page files it under the monarchy by the same default. ⚠ English Bibles count verses 28–32 of this chapter as Joel 2, and the Hebrew Bible counts them as 3:1–5 of a four-chapter book; this page keeps the English count and says so in its last note.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Joel 2:1–2 a shofar in Zion — the day of Jehovah, a day of darkness

⭐ A shofar in Zion. “Blow a shofar in Zion” stands in two verses, here and verse 15 (shofar); the alarm is raised on “my holy mountain”, and the one who speaks is Jehovah. The KJV has “Blow ye the trumpet in Zion, and sound an alarm in my holy mountain”, the NIV “Blow the trumpet in Zion; sound the alarm on my holy hill”, the NWT 1984 “Blow a horn in Zion, O men, and shout a war cry in my holy mountain” and the TLB “Sound the alarm in Jerusalem!”. The day of Jehovah is “coming” and “near”; the last chapter ended with a drought and fire (Joel 1:15, now on these pages).

⭐ A day of darkness. “A day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and thick darkness” stands in two verses, here and Zephaniah 1:15 (now on these pages). Then the people: “a people great and mighty” (am rav ve’atsum), “like dawn spread over the mountains”, which is the locusts of chapter 1 read now as an army. The KJV has “A day of darkness and of gloominess, a day of clouds and of thick darkness, as the morning spread upon the mountains: a great people and a strong”, the NIV “Like dawn spreading across the mountains a large and mighty army comes”, the NWT 1984 “like light of dawn spread out upon the mountains” and the Douay “a day of clouds and whirlwinds”. ⚠ The Hebrew does not say who the people are; the versions say army, and the next verses give fire and horses.

Verses 3–5 · Joel 2:3–5 fire before it and a flame behind — Eden before, wilderness after — horses and chariots

⭐ Eden before, wilderness behind. “The land is like the garden of Eden before it” stands in this one verse, and behind it “a desolate wilderness”, with nothing left over (peletah). The garden is that of Genesis 2:8 (now on these pages), turned into the thing that is eaten. The KJV has “the land is as the garden of Eden before them, and behind them a desolate wilderness; yea, and nothing shall escape them”, the NIV “Before them the land is like the garden of Eden, behind them, a desert waste— nothing escapes them” and the NWT 1984 “Like the garden of Eʹden the land is ahead of it”.

⭐ Horses and chariots. The locust is drawn as cavalry: “its appearance is like the appearance of horses”, then ke-qol merkavot, “like the sound of chariots”, leaping over the mountaintops, “like the sound of a flame of fire eating stubble”, a strong people arranged for battle. The KJV has “Like the noise of chariots on the tops of mountains shall they leap”, the NIV “With a noise like that of chariots they leap over the mountaintops” and the NWT 1984 “As with the sound of chariots on the tops of the mountains they keep skipping about”.

Verses 6–11 · Joel 2:6–11 peoples writhe — they run like warriors — the earth quakes — who can endure the day?

⚠ “Gathers a glow.” “Gathers a glow” (qibbetsu pa’ur) stands in two verses, here and Nahum 2:11, and rests on a rare word, pa’ur, and the versions split three ways: the KJV has “all faces shall gather blackness”, the NIV “every face turns pale”, the NWT 1984 “they will certainly collect a glow [of excitement]”, the TLB “their faces grow pale with fright” and the Douay “all faces shall be made like a kettle”. This translation keeps the plain “glow” and says that the word is uncertain.

⭐ The army that does not break ranks. They run like warriors, climb the wall like men of war, each in his own way; “a man does not crowd his brother” stands in this one verse, and they “do not break off”. Then the city: “they rush upon the city; they run on the wall; they go up into the houses; through the windows they come in like a thief”. The KJV has “they shall not break their ranks” and “they shall enter in at the windows like a thief”, the NIV “They plunge through defenses without breaking ranks” and the NWT 1984 “Through the windows they go in like the thief”.

⭐ The sky, and the voice. “The earth quakes, the heavens tremble; the sun and moon are dark, and the stars gather in their shining”; the same sky as in verse 31 (the day of Jehovah). The KJV has “the sun and the moon shall be dark, and the stars shall withdraw their shining” and the NWT 1984 “the very stars have withdrawn their brightness”. Then “And Jehovah gives his voice before his army” stands in this one verse: the army is his. The KJV has “the Lord shall utter his voice before his army”, the NIV “The Lord thunders at the head of his army” and the NWT 1984 “Jehovah himself will certainly give forth his voice before his military force”. And the question that ends the passage: “and who can endure it?” stands in this one verse; the KJV has “and who can abide it?”, the NIV “Who can endure it?” and the NWT 1984 “who can hold up under it?”.

Verses 12–14 · Joel 2:12–14 “even now” — return with all your heart — tear your heart, not your garments — who knows?

⭐ “And even now.” “And even now, declares Jehovah” stands in this one verse, the turn of the whole book: “return to me with all your heart” stands in this one verse, with “with fasting and with weeping and with mourning” (tsom), which stands in this one verse, the three Esther names for the Jews in every province (Esther 4:3, now on these pages). The KJV has “turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning”, the NIV “return to me with all your heart, with fasting and weeping and mourning” and the NWT 1984 “come back to me with all YOUR hearts”.

⭐ Tear your heart. “And tear your heart and not your garments” stands in this one verse: the garment is what Mordecai tears in Esther 4:1 (now on these pages), and here it is not enough without the heart. The KJV has “rend your heart, and not your garments”, the NIV “Rend your heart and not your garments”, the NWT 1984 “rip apart YOUR hearts, and not YOUR garments” and the TLB “Let your remorse tear at your hearts and not your garments”.

⭐ Gracious and merciful. “Gracious and merciful, slow to anger and great in steadfast love” stands in this one verse, in this order: the name proclaimed to Moses at Exodus 34:6 (now on these pages) puts “merciful” before “gracious”, and Psalm 103:8 (now on these pages) has the same words with the first two turned. The last clause, “and he relents of the evil”, stands in two verses, here and Jonah 4:2. The KJV has “he is gracious and merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, and repenteth him of the evil”, the NIV “he is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and abounding in love, and he relents from sending calamity” and the NWT 1984 “he will certainly feel regret on account of the calamity”.

⭐ Who knows? “Who knows whether he will turn and relent” stands in two verses, here and Jonah 3:9; “and leave a blessing behind him” stands in this one verse, a grain offering and a drink offering, the very offerings that chapter 1 said were cut off. It is a hope and not a promise: the same question is David’s at 2 Samuel 12:22 and Mordecai’s at Esther 4:14 (both now on these pages). The KJV has “Who knoweth if he will return and repent, and leave a blessing behind him”, the NIV “Who knows? He may turn and relent and leave behind a blessing” and the NWT 1984 “Who is there knowing whether he will turn back”.

Verses 15–17 · Joel 2:15–17 a shofar again — a solemn assembly — between the porch and the altar — “Where is their God?”

⭐ Sanctify a fast. “Sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly” stands in two verses, here and Joel 1:14 (now on these pages); the second time it is a whole people, the old, the children and those nursing, and even the bridegroom out of his room and the bride out of her chamber. The KJV has “sanctify a fast, call a solemn assembly”, the NIV “declare a holy fast, call a sacred assembly” and the NWT 1984 “Sanctify a time of fasting. Call together a solemn assembly”.

⭐ Between the porch and the altar. “Between the porch and the altar” stands in this one verse, the place where the priests weep and say “Spare your people”. The KJV has “weep between the porch and the altar, and let them say, Spare thy people, O Lord, and give not thine heritage to reproach”, the NIV “weep between the portico and the altar” and the Douay “Between the porch and the altar the priests the Lord’s ministers shall weep”. And the taunt: “why should they say among the peoples, Where is their God?” stands in this one verse, the question of Psalm 42 asked of the psalmist (Psalm 42:4, now on these pages, “where is your God”).

Verses 18–20 · Joel 2:18–20 Jehovah was jealous for his land — the grain, the wine and the oil — the northerner removed

⭐ The turn. “Then Jehovah was jealous for his land” stands in this one verse, and the verbs shift to the past: he had compassion on his people. The KJV has “Then will the Lord be jealous for his land, and pity his people”, the NIV “Then the Lord was jealous for his land and took pity on his people” and the NWT 1984 “Jehovah will be zealous for his land and will show compassion upon his people”. ⚠ The versions split between past and future for the same Hebrew verbs.

⭐ Grain, wine and oil. “Look, I am sending you” stands in this one verse the three things that chapter 1 named as gone; “and the northerner I will remove far from you” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “I will send you corn, and wine, and oil, and ye shall be satisfied therewith” and “I will remove far off from you the northern army”, the NIV “I am sending you grain, new wine and olive oil, enough to satisfy you fully” and “I will drive the northern horde far from you” and the NWT 1984 “the northerner I shall put far away from upon YOU”. ⚠ The Hebrew has the one word hatsefoni, in the singular; the KJV makes it an army and the NIV a horde, and who it is the book does not say.

Verses 21–27 · Joel 2:21–27 do not fear, ground — the early rain — the years the swarmer has eaten — my people will not be put to shame

⭐ Do not fear, ground. “Do not fear, ground” stands in this one verse, addressed to the land itself (adamah), and then to the beasts of the field, for the pastures are green and the tree and the vine have given their strength. The KJV has “Fear not, O land; be glad and rejoice: for the Lord will do great things”, the NIV “Do not be afraid, land of Judah” and the NWT 1984 “Do not be fearful, O ground”.

⚠ The early rain, or a teacher. “The moreh in righteousness” (hamoreh litsedaqah) stands in this one verse, and moreh is both the early rain (yoreh, the first rain after the dry summer) and a teacher. The versions divide: the KJV has “the former rain moderately”, the NIV “the autumn rains because he is faithful”, the NWT 1984 “the autumn rain in right measure”, the Geneva “the rain of righteousness”, the TLB “the rains he sends are tokens of forgiveness” and the Douay “a teacher of justice”. This translation keeps “the early rain in right measure” and says that the Hebrew word is also the teacher of the Hill of Moreh in Judges 7:1 (now on these pages). “The early rain and the late rain” (moreh umalqosh) stands in this one verse in this form (malqosh).

⭐ The years the swarmer has eaten. “And I will restore to you the years that the swarmer has eaten” stands in this one verse: the verb is shillamti, I will make whole, pay back (shalam). “The swarmer, the licker, the finisher and the shearer” stands in this one verse, the same four words as Joel 1:4 (now on these pages) in the opposite order, the swarmer first (arbeh, gazam). The KJV has “I will restore to you the years that the locust hath eaten, the cankerworm, and the caterpiller, and the palmerworm”, the NIV “I will repay you for the years the locusts have eaten— the great locust and the young locust, the other locusts and the locust swarm”, the NWT 1984 “I will make compensation to YOU for the years that the locust, the creeping, unwinged locust, and the cockroach and the caterpillar have eaten”, the Geneva “I will render you the years” and the TLB “I will give you back the crops the locusts ate!”. ⚠ The Douay has “the ears which the locust, and the bruchus, and the mildew, and the palmerworm have eaten”, which reads “ears” where the Hebrew has “years”. And the locust is “my great army that I sent among you”, the same word as the army of verse 11.

⭐ Eat and be satisfied; never put to shame. “And you will eat, eating and being satisfied” stands in this one verse, and “and my people will not be put to shame forever” stands in two verses, 26 and 27, the same sentence said twice around “you will know that I am in the midst of Israel”. The KJV has “my people shall never be ashamed”, the NIV “never again will my people be shamed” and the NWT 1984 “my people will not be ashamed to time indefinite”.

Verses 28–32 · Joel 2:28–32 afterward — the spirit poured out on all flesh — wonders — everyone who calls on the name will escape

⚠ The numbering. These five verses are Hebrew Joel 3:1–5, and the Hebrew Bible then calls the next chapter Joel 4; English Bibles count them as 2:28–32 and the next chapter as Joel 3. This page numbers them as the English does, 28 to 32, because that is how Acts 2 and every other quotation of them is cited, and the Hebrew column is the Hebrew of 3:1–5.

⭐ “And afterward.” “And afterward it will be” stands in this one verse, after the restoration and not before it. “I will pour out my spirit on all flesh” stands in this one verse (ruach, bashar). Peter quotes the passage at Acts 2:17–21 (now on these pages), and it answers Moses at Numbers 11:29 (now on these pages): “would that all Jehovah’s people were prophets”. The KJV has “it shall come to pass afterward, that I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh”, the NIV “And afterward, I will pour out my Spirit on all people”, the NWT 1984 “And after that it must occur that I shall pour out my spirit on every sort of flesh” and the Douay “And it shall come to pass after this”. ⚠ The TLB has “After I have poured out my rains again, I will pour out my Spirit upon all of you!”, which adds the rains of verse 23 and is not in the Hebrew of the verse.

⭐ Sons, daughters, old men, young men. “And your sons and your daughters will prophesy” stands in this one verse, “your old men will dream dreams” stands in this one verse and “your young men will see visions” stands in this one verse; verse 29 adds the male servants and the female servants. The KJV has “your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, your young men shall see visions” and the NIV “Your sons and daughters will prophesy, your old men will dream dreams, your young men will see visions”.

⭐ Wonders, and the sun. “And I will give wonders in the heavens and on the earth” stands in this one verse, “blood and fire and pillars of smoke” stands in this one verse; “the sun will be turned to darkness, and the moon to blood” stands in this one verse, quoted in the sixth seal of Revelation 6:12 (now on these pages). “Before the coming of the day of Jehovah, the great and the terrible” stands in two verses, here and Malachi 3:23 in the Hebrew numbering, 4:5 in the English. The KJV has “I will shew wonders in the heavens and in the earth, blood, and fire, and pillars of smoke”, the NIV “blood and fire and billows of smoke” and the NWT 1984 “columns of smoke”.

⭐ Everyone who calls. “Everyone who calls on the name of Jehovah will escape” stands in this one verse, which Paul quotes at Romans 10:13 (now on these pages); and “for in Mount Zion and in Jerusalem there will be those who escape” stands in this one verse, with Obadiah 1:17 close beside it (now on these pages); and the end is “and among the survivors whom Jehovah calls”, which stands in this one verse. The KJV has “whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be delivered”, the NIV “And everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved”, the NWT 1984 “everyone who calls on the name of Jehovah will get away safe”, the Douay “shall be saved: for in mount Sion, and in Jerusalem shall be salvation” and the TLB “Everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the day of Jehovah” for yom YHWH; “shofar” for shofar; “return” for shuv in verses 12 and 13; “steadfast love” for chesed at verse 13; “relents” for nicham at verses 13 and 14; “the early rain” for moreh and “the late rain” for malqosh; “the swarmer, the licker, the finisher, the shearer” for the four locust words, as in chapter 1; “spirit” for ruach and “flesh” for basar at verse 28; “Jehovah” for the Name, written in verses 1, 11, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 21, 23, 26, 27, 31 and 32.

Echoes paid. Joel 1:4, 1:14 and 1:15; Zephaniah 1:15; Genesis 2:8; Esther 4:1, 4:3 and 4:14; Exodus 34:6; Psalm 103:8 and 42:4; 2 Samuel 12:22; Judges 7:1; Numbers 11:29; Acts 2:17–21; Revelation 6:12; Romans 10:13; Obadiah 1:17.

Echoes planted. Jonah 3:9 and 4:2 (the same questions and the same attributes); Malachi 3:23 (4:5 in English), the other “great and terrible day of Jehovah”. Not translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Joel stands on these pages at chapters 1 and 2, in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Joel 3, which is Hebrew chapter 4.
