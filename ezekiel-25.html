Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–5 · Ezekiel 25:1–5 against Ammon

⭐ A turn outward. Chapter 24 ended waiting for the fugitive. Here the words go to Israel’s neighbours: Ammon, Moab, Edom and the Philistines. Each is charged with ‘because’, answered with ‘therefore’, and told that someone will know. Ammon was named at 21:25 and 21:33. The chapter gives no date.

⭐ Set your face. As toward the south and toward Jerusalem (21:2, 21:7).

⭐ Aha. He’ach, a cry of delight at another’s fall. The enemy says it again at 36:2. Three things are gloated over: the sanctuary profaned (compare 24:21), the ground of Israel made desolate, and the house of Judah gone into exile. The KJV and NIV say ‘land’, the NWT 1984 “soil”.

⚠ Who is ‘you’. Verse 3 begins with a plural ‘Hear’; from ‘because you said’ to the end of verse 4 Ammon is addressed as one woman; at the end of verse 5 ‘you will know’ is plural; in verses 6 and 7 ‘you’ is one man. English does not show it.

⭐ Sons of the east. The words bene qedem stand in eleven verses of the Hebrew Bible, among them Judges 6:3, where they come up with Midian and Amalek. The NWT 1984 has “the Orientals”, the NIV “the people of the East”.

⭐ Possession. Morashah, the word of Exodus 6:8, where the land is promised to Israel; it is used again in verse 10.

⚠ Encampments. The word is used of the sons of Ishmael at Genesis 25:16. The KJV has “palaces”, the Douay “sheepcotes”, the NIV “camps”.

⭐ Camels and flocks. Rabbah, Ammon’s chief city, becomes a dwelling for camels. This ‘dwelling’ is naveh, a grazing ground; the ‘dwellings’ of verse 4 are another word, mishkenot, printed so at Numbers 24:5. Naveh and ‘lie down’ are words printed of Israel’s flock at 34:14; here they are used of what takes Ammon’s place.

Verses 6–7 · Ezekiel 25:6–7 the clapping and stamping

⭐ Hand and foot. At 6:11 the prophet was told to strike with his hand and stamp with his foot, saying ‘Alas’. Here the stamping verb returns, the clapping verb is a different one, and the mood is joy.

⭐ Scorn of soul. The words printed ‘scorn of soul’ at 36:5, of those who took the land. The Hebrew here is ‘all your scorn, in soul’; there and in verse 15, of the Philistines, it is ‘with scorn of soul’. A verb of the same root is printed ‘despise’ at 16:57. The KJV has “with all thy despite”, the NIV “with all the malice of your heart”.

⚠ One form written, another read. In verse 7 the text has lbg and the margin reads lbz, ‘as plunder’ (ketiv/qere); the translation follows what is read. ‘Plunder to the nations’ in these words stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, 26:5 (now on these pages) and 34:28, where God’s flock will be that no longer.

⚠ I have stretched out. The hand is stretched out three times in this chapter, in three forms of the verb: ‘I have stretched’ here, ‘I will stretch’ against Edom (verse 13), ‘I am stretching’ against the Philistines (verse 16). The ASV keeps “I have stretched out” here; the KJV and NIV have a future.

Verses 8–11 · Ezekiel 25:8–11 against Moab

⭐ Moab and Seir. Two are quoted. Seir is the hill country of Esau, who is Edom (Genesis 36:8); Edom has its own word from verse 12. Moab and Ammon are the two sons of Lot’s daughters (Genesis 19:37–38).

⭐ Like all the nations. The words stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: the king law (Deuteronomy 17:14), the elders asking for a king (1 Samuel 8:5 and 20), and here. There Israel is the one that wants to be like the nations; here others say that Judah already is.

⚠ The shoulder. Ketef, ‘shoulder’, as at 24:4; of land, a side or slope. The KJV has “the side of Moab”, the NIV “the flank”, the NWT 1984 “the slope”.

⭐ The glory of the land. Tzvi, the word printed ‘the glory of all lands’ of Israel’s land at 20:6, is here given to Moab’s towns: Beth-jeshimoth (Numbers 33:49), Baal-meon (Numbers 32:38) and Kiriathaim (Numbers 32:37). The last has one form written and another read.

⚠ Verse 10. The sentence runs on from verse 9: the shoulder is opened ‘to the sons of the east’. The words rendered ‘along with the sons of Ammon’ are al bene Ammon, which can also be taken as ‘against’ them: so the ASV, “to go against the children of Ammon”; the KJV has “with the Ammonites”. ‘I will give it’ has a feminine ‘it’, and the verse does not say which land is meant.

⭐ Acts of judgment. As against Jerusalem (5:10, 11:9; shephatim).

Verses 12–14 · Ezekiel 25:12–14 against Edom

⭐ Vengeance. The root is heard over and over from here to the end: Edom took it, the Philistines took it, and God calls what he will do ‘my vengeance’. ‘Execute my vengeance’ (verses 14 and 17) has the verb and noun of Numbers 31:3, where Moses sends armed men ‘to execute Jehovah’s vengeance on Midian’. There Israel carries it out, and Edom too is to receive it ‘by the hand of my people Israel’.

⭐ Deeply guilty. The verb is doubled, vayye’shemu ashom. Its letters vy’shmw are the ones met at 6:6. Edom is singular at the start of the verse and plural after. The verse does not say what was done; compare Obadiah (1:10, violence done to a brother) and Lamentations 4:22.

⭐ Man and beast. As at 14:13.

⚠ Teman and Dedan. The Hebrew has a ruin ‘from Teman’, then ‘and to Dedan’ they will fall. The ASV has “from Teman; even unto Dedan shall they fall”; the NIV puts both names with the falling, “from Teman to Dedan they will fall”; the KJV makes it “they of Dedan”. Teman can also mean ‘south’, and the Douay has “from the south”.

Verses 15–17 · Ezekiel 25:15–17 against the Philistines

⭐ An ancient enmity. Evat olam. Olam looks far back or far on: the Douay has “old enmities”, the ASV “perpetual enmity”. The same two words stand at 35:5, of Mount Seir.

⭐ A play on a name. ‘I will cut off the Cherethites’ is hikhratti et keretim: the verb and the name sound alike. Cherethites are named beside the Philistines here, and a Negev of theirs at 1 Samuel 30:14; men of that name serve David (2 Samuel 8:18). The Douay takes the name by a meaning instead: “will kill the killers”.

⭐ Great vengeances. Neqamot, plural. The form stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible. ‘Furious rebukes’ is the phrase of 5:15, where it is aimed at Jerusalem; there the Hebrew spells it with one letter fewer.

⭐ The close. Ammon is told twice that it will know, and Moab, Edom and the Philistines are each spoken of as knowing. Only of Edom is it said that they will know ‘my vengeance’, where the others will know ‘that I am Jehovah’.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “sons of Ammon” at 2, 3, 5 and 10; “possession” at 4 and 10; “scorn of soul” at 6 and 15; “stretch out my hand” at 7, 13 and 16; and “vengeance” at 12, 14, 15 and 17.

Echoes paid. The sword for Ammon of 21:33; the hand and foot of 6:11; the furious rebukes of 5:15.

Echoes planted. Tyre at 26:2 (now on these pages), also saying ‘Aha’; Mount Seir at 35:5.

Ezekiel 26, now on these pages: against Tyre.
