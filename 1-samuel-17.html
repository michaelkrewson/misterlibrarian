The Philistines camp between Socoh and Azekah, and Saul’s army faces them across the valley of Elah. For forty days Goliath of Gath, armoured in bronze, challenges Israel to single combat. Jesse sends David with food for his brothers; David hears the taunt, answers Eliab, and tells Saul of the lion and the bear. Refusing Saul’s armour, he goes out with his staff, five smooth stones and a sling in the name of Jehovah of armies; the stone sinks into Goliath’s forehead, David cuts off his head with his own sword, and Israel pursues the Philistines to Gath and Ekron. Saul asks Abner whose son the youth is.
Forty days of challenge, morning and evening (1 Samuel 17:16); the fight falls on the morning David arrives at the camp (17:20).

21

E Israel y los filisteos se pusieron en orden, línea de batalla frente a línea de batalla.

Y David dejó el equipaje de encima de sí en mano del guarda del equipaje, y corrió a la línea de batalla, y llegó y preguntó a sus hermanos cómo estaban.
23
וְהוּא מְדַבֵּר עִמָּם, וְהִנֵּה אִישׁ הַבֵּנַיִם עוֹלֶה גָּלְיָת הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי שְׁמוֹ מִגַּת ממערות מִמַּעַרְכוֹת פְּלִשְׁתִּים, וַיְדַבֵּר, כַּדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה; וַיִּשְׁמַע, דָּוִד.

Y mientras hablaba con ellos, mira, el campeón subía, llamado Goliat el filisteo, de Gat, de las líneas de batalla de los filisteos, y habló las mismas palabras; y David lo oyó.
24

And all the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from before him and were very afraid.note
Y todos los hombres de Israel, al ver al hombre, huyeron de delante de él y tuvieron mucho miedo.
25

And the men of Israel said: ‘Have you seen this man who is coming up? For it is to taunt Israel that he comes up. And the man who strikes him down, the king will enrich him with great riches, and will give him his daughter, and will make his father’s house free in Israel.’note
Y los hombres de Israel decían: «¿Han visto a este hombre que sube? Es para injuriar a Israel que sube. Y al hombre que lo derribe, el rey lo enriquecerá con grandes riquezas, y le dará su hija, y hará libre en Israel la casa de su padre».
26
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, אֶל-הָאֲנָשִׁים הָעֹמְדִים עִמּוֹ לֵאמֹר, מַה-יֵּעָשֶׂה לָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר יַכֶּה אֶת-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הַלָּז, וְהֵסִיר חֶרְפָּה מֵעַל יִשְׂרָאֵל: כִּי מִי, הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הֶעָרֵל הַזֶּה, כִּי חֵרֵף, מַעַרְכוֹת אֱלֹהִים חַיִּים.

Y David dijo a los hombres que estaban con él: «¿Qué se hará al hombre que derribe a ese filisteo de allí, y quite la afrenta de sobre Israel? Porque ¿quién es este filisteo incircunciso, para injuriar a las líneas de batalla del Dios viviente?».
27
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ הָעָם, כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה לֵאמֹר: כֹּה יֵעָשֶׂה, לָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר יַכֶּנּוּ.
And the people answered him in the same words, saying: ‘So it will be done for the man who strikes him down.’note
Y el pueblo le respondió con las mismas palabras, diciendo: «Así se hará al hombre que lo derribe».
28
וַיִּשְׁמַע אֱלִיאָב אָחִיו הַגָּדוֹל, בְּדַבְּרוֹ אֶל-הָאֲנָשִׁים; וַיִּחַר-אַף אֱלִיאָב בְּדָוִד וַיֹּאמֶר לָמָּה-זֶּה יָרַדְתָּ, וְעַל-מִי נָטַשְׁתָּ מְעַט הַצֹּאן הָהֵנָּה בַּמִּדְבָּר--אֲנִי יָדַעְתִּי אֶת-זְדֹנְךָ וְאֵת רֹעַ לְבָבֶךָ, כִּי לְמַעַן רְאוֹת הַמִּלְחָמָה יָרָדְתָּ.

And with whom have you left those few sheep in the wilderness? I know your presumption and the evil of your heart; for it is to see the battle that you have come down.’note
Y Eliab, su hermano mayor, oyó cuando hablaba con los hombres; y la ira de Eliab se encendió contra David, y dijo: «¿Para qué has bajado? ¿Y con quién has dejado aquellas pocas ovejas en el desierto? Yo conozco tu presunción y la maldad de tu corazón; porque es para ver la batalla que has bajado».
29
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, מֶה עָשִׂיתִי עָתָּה; הֲלוֹא, דָּבָר הוּא.

Y David dijo: «¿Qué he hecho ahora? ¿No fue solo una palabra?».
30
וַיִּסֹּב מֵאֶצְלוֹ אֶל-מוּל אַחֵר, וַיֹּאמֶר כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה; וַיְשִׁבֻהוּ הָעָם דָּבָר, כַּדָּבָר הָרִאשׁוֹן.
And he turned from him toward another, and spoke in the same words; and the people answered him word for word as before.note
Y se apartó de él hacia otro, y habló con las mismas palabras; y el pueblo le respondió palabra por palabra como antes.
31
וַיִּשָּׁמְעוּ, הַדְּבָרִים, אֲשֶׁר, דִּבֶּר דָּוִד; וַיַּגִּדוּ לִפְנֵי-שָׁאוּל, וַיִּקָּחֵהוּ.

Y se oyeron las palabras que David había hablado, y las contaron delante de Saúl; y él lo mandó llamar.
32
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-שָׁאוּל, אַל-יִפֹּל לֵב-אָדָם עָלָיו; עַבְדְּךָ יֵלֵךְ, וְנִלְחַם עִם-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הַזֶּה.

Y David dijo a Saúl: «Que no caiga el corazón de hombre alguno por causa de él; tu siervo irá y peleará con este filisteo».
33
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-דָּוִד, לֹא תוּכַל לָלֶכֶת אֶל-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הַזֶּה, לְהִלָּחֵם, עִמּוֹ: כִּי-נַעַר אַתָּה, וְהוּא אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה מִנְּעֻרָיו. {ס}

Y Saúl dijo a David: «No puedes ir contra este filisteo para pelear con él; porque tú eres un muchacho, y él es hombre de guerra desde su juventud».
34
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-שָׁאוּל, רֹעֶה הָיָה עַבְדְּךָ לְאָבִיו בַּצֹּאן; וּבָא הָאֲרִי וְאֶת-הַדּוֹב, וְנָשָׂא שֶׂה מֵהָעֵדֶר.
And David said to Saul: ‘Your servant was shepherding his father’s flock; and the lion came, and the bear, and carried off a lamb from the drove;note
Y David dijo a Saúl: «Tu siervo pastoreaba el rebaño de su padre; y venía el león, y el oso, y se llevaba un cordero de la manada;
35
וְיָצָאתִי אַחֲרָיו וְהִכִּתִיו, וְהִצַּלְתִּי מִפִּיו; וַיָּקָם עָלַי--וְהֶחֱזַקְתִּי בִּזְקָנוֹ, וְהִכִּתִיו וַהֲמִיתִּיו.
and I went out after it and struck it, and rescued it from its mouth; and when it rose against me, I seized it by its beard, and struck it and killed it.note

36
גַּם אֶת-הָאֲרִי גַּם-הַדֹּב, הִכָּה עַבְדֶּךָ; וְהָיָה הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הֶעָרֵל הַזֶּה, כְּאַחַד מֵהֶם, כִּי חֵרֵף, מַעַרְכֹת אֱלֹהִים חַיִּים. {ס}

Tanto al león como al oso ha derribado tu siervo; y este filisteo incircunciso será como uno de ellos, porque ha injuriado a las líneas de batalla del Dios viviente».
37
וַיֹּאמֶר, דָּוִד, יְהוָה אֲשֶׁר הִצִּלַנִי מִיַּד הָאֲרִי וּמִיַּד הַדֹּב, הוּא יַצִּילֵנִי מִיַּד הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי הַזֶּה; {ס} וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-דָּוִד לֵךְ, וַיהוָה יִהְיֶה עִמָּךְ.
And David said: ‘Jehovah, who rescued me from the hand of the lion and from the hand of the bear, he will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine.’ And Saul said to David: ‘Go, and Jehovah will be with you.’note
Y David dijo: «Jehová, que me libró de la mano del león y de la mano del oso, él me librará de la mano de este filisteo». Y Saúl dijo a David: «Ve, y Jehová estará contigo».
38
וַיַּלְבֵּשׁ שָׁאוּל אֶת-דָּוִד מַדָּיו, וְנָתַן קוֹבַע נְחֹשֶׁת עַל-רֹאשׁוֹ; וַיַּלְבֵּשׁ אֹתוֹ, שִׁרְיוֹן.
And Saul clothed David with his garments, and put a helmet of bronze on his head, and clothed him with a coat of mail.note
Y Saúl vistió a David con sus ropas, y le puso un casco de bronce sobre la cabeza, y lo vistió con una coraza.
39
וַיַּחְגֹּר דָּוִד אֶת-חַרְבּוֹ מֵעַל לְמַדָּיו וַיֹּאֶל לָלֶכֶת, כִּי לֹא-נִסָּה, וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-שָׁאוּל לֹא אוּכַל לָלֶכֶת בָּאֵלֶּה, כִּי לֹא נִסִּיתִי; וַיְסִרֵם דָּוִד, מֵעָלָיו.

Y David se ciñó la espada sobre sus ropas, e intentó andar, porque no las había probado. Y David dijo a Saúl: «No puedo andar con esto, porque no lo he probado». Y David se lo quitó de encima.
40
וַיִּקַּח מַקְלוֹ בְּיָדוֹ, וַיִּבְחַר-לוֹ חֲמִשָּׁה חַלֻּקֵי-אֲבָנִים מִן-הַנַּחַל וַיָּשֶׂם אֹתָם בִּכְלִי הָרֹעִים אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ וּבַיַּלְקוּט--וְקַלְעוֹ בְיָדוֹ; וַיִּגַּשׁ, אֶל-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי.
And he took his staff in his hand, and chose for himself five smooth stones from the brook, and put them in the shepherds’ bag that he had, in the pouch; and his sling was in his hand; and he drew near to the Philistine.note
Y tomó su cayado en la mano, y se escogió cinco piedras lisas del arroyo, y las puso en la bolsa de pastor que tenía, en el morral; y su honda estaba en su mano; y se acercó al filisteo.
41
וַיֵּלֶךְ, הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, הֹלֵךְ וְקָרֵב, אֶל-דָּוִד; וְהָאִישׁ נֹשֵׂא הַצִּנָּה, לְפָנָיו.
And the Philistine came on, coming nearer and nearer to David, and the man carrying the shield was before him.note
Y el filisteo avanzaba, acercándose más y más a David, y el hombre que llevaba el escudo iba delante de él.
42
וַיַּבֵּט הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי וַיִּרְאֶה אֶת-דָּוִד, וַיִּבְזֵהוּ: כִּי-הָיָה נַעַר, וְאַדְמֹנִי עִם-יְפֵה מַרְאֶה.
And the Philistine looked, and saw David, and despised him; for he was a youth, and ruddy, with a beautiful appearance.note
Y el filisteo miró, y vio a David, y lo despreció; porque era un muchacho, y rubicundo, de hermoso aspecto.
43
וַיֹּאמֶר הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, אֶל-דָּוִד, הֲכֶלֶב אָנֹכִי, כִּי-אַתָּה בָא-אֵלַי בַּמַּקְלוֹת; וַיְקַלֵּל הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי אֶת-דָּוִד, בֵּאלֹהָיו.
And the Philistine said to David: ‘Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?’ And the Philistine cursed David by his gods.note
Y el filisteo dijo a David: «¿Soy yo un perro, para que vengas a mí con palos?». Y el filisteo maldijo a David por sus dioses.
44
וַיֹּאמֶר הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, אֶל-דָּוִד: לְכָה אֵלַי--וְאֶתְּנָה אֶת-בְּשָׂרְךָ, לְעוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם וּלְבֶהֱמַת הַשָּׂדֶה. {ס}
And the Philistine said to David: ‘Come to me, and I will give your flesh to the birds of the heavens and to the beasts of the field.’note
Y el filisteo dijo a David: «Ven a mí, y daré tu carne a las aves de los cielos y a las bestias del campo».
45
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, אֶל-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, אַתָּה בָּא אֵלַי, בְּחֶרֶב וּבַחֲנִית וּבְכִידוֹן; וְאָנֹכִי בָא-אֵלֶיךָ, בְּשֵׁם יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, אֱלֹהֵי מַעַרְכוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲשֶׁר חֵרַפְתָּ.
And David said to the Philistine: ‘You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin; but I come to you in the name of Jehovah of armies, the God of the battle lines of Israel, whom you have taunted.note
Y David dijo al filisteo: «Tú vienes a mí con espada y con lanza y con jabalina; pero yo vengo a ti en el nombre de Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de las líneas de batalla de Israel, a quien tú has injuriado.
46
הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה יְסַגֶּרְךָ יְהוָה בְּיָדִי וְהִכִּיתִךָ, וַהֲסִרֹתִי אֶת-רֹאשְׁךָ מֵעָלֶיךָ, וְנָתַתִּי פֶּגֶר מַחֲנֵה פְלִשְׁתִּים הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, לְעוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם וּלְחַיַּת הָאָרֶץ; וְיֵדְעוּ, כָּל-הָאָרֶץ, כִּי יֵשׁ אֱלֹהִים, לְיִשְׂרָאֵל.

Este día Jehová te entregará en mi mano, y yo te derribaré y te quitaré la cabeza; y daré este día los cadáveres del campamento de los filisteos a las aves de los cielos y a las fieras de la tierra, para que sepa toda la tierra que hay Dios en Israel,
47
וְיֵדְעוּ כָּל-הַקָּהָל הַזֶּה, כִּי-לֹא בְּחֶרֶב וּבַחֲנִית יְהוֹשִׁיעַ יְהוָה: כִּי לַיהוָה הַמִּלְחָמָה, וְנָתַן אֶתְכֶם בְּיָדֵנוּ. {ס}

48
וְהָיָה כִּי-קָם הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, וַיֵּלֶךְ וַיִּקְרַב לִקְרַאת דָּוִד; וַיְמַהֵר דָּוִד, וַיָּרָץ הַמַּעֲרָכָה לִקְרַאת הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי.

Y sucedió que, cuando el filisteo se levantó y fue acercándose al encuentro de David, David se dio prisa y corrió hacia la línea de batalla al encuentro del filisteo.
49
וַיִּשְׁלַח דָּוִד אֶת-יָדוֹ אֶל-הַכֶּלִי, וַיִּקַּח מִשָּׁם אֶבֶן וַיְקַלַּע, וַיַּךְ אֶת-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, אֶל-מִצְחוֹ; וַתִּטְבַּע הָאֶבֶן בְּמִצְחוֹ, וַיִּפֹּל עַל-פָּנָיו אָרְצָה.

Y David metió la mano en la bolsa y tomó de allí una piedra, y la tiró con la honda, e hirió al filisteo en la frente; y la piedra se le hundió en la frente, y cayó sobre su rostro a tierra.
50
וַיֶּחֱזַק דָּוִד מִן-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי בַּקֶּלַע וּבָאֶבֶן, וַיַּךְ אֶת-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי וַיְמִתֵהוּ; וְחֶרֶב, אֵין בְּיַד-דָּוִד.
So David prevailed over the Philistine with the sling and with the stone, and struck the Philistine and killed him; and there was no sword in David’s hand.note
Así venció David al filisteo con la honda y con la piedra, e hirió al filisteo y lo mató; y no había espada en la mano de David.
51
וַיָּרָץ דָּוִד וַיַּעֲמֹד אֶל-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי וַיִּקַּח אֶת-חַרְבּוֹ וַיִּשְׁלְפָהּ מִתַּעְרָהּ, וַיְמֹתְתֵהוּ, וַיִּכְרָת-בָּהּ, אֶת-רֹאשׁוֹ; וַיִּרְאוּ הַפְּלִשְׁתִּים כִּי-מֵת גִּבּוֹרָם, וַיָּנֻסוּ.
And David ran and stood over the Philistine, and took his sword, and drew it out of its sheath, and killed him, and cut off his head with it. And the Philistines saw that their mighty man was dead, and they fled.note
Y David corrió y se puso sobre el filisteo, y tomó la espada de él, y la sacó de su vaina, y lo mató, y le cortó con ella la cabeza. Y los filisteos vieron que su valiente había muerto, y huyeron.
52
וַיָּקֻמוּ אַנְשֵׁי יִשְׂרָאֵל וִיהוּדָה וַיָּרִעוּ, וַיִּרְדְּפוּ אֶת-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּים, עַד-בּוֹאֲךָ גַיְא, וְעַד שַׁעֲרֵי עֶקְרוֹן; וַיִּפְּלוּ חַלְלֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, בְּדֶרֶךְ שַׁעֲרַיִם, וְעַד-גַּת, וְעַד-עֶקְרוֹן.
And the men of Israel and of Judah rose and shouted, and pursued the Philistines until you come to the ravine, and to the gates of Ekron; and the slain of the Philistines fell on the way of Shaaraim, as far as Gath and as far as Ekron.note
Y los hombres de Israel y de Judá se levantaron y gritaron, y persiguieron a los filisteos hasta llegar a la quebrada, y hasta las puertas de Ecrón; y los muertos de los filisteos cayeron por el camino de Saaraim, hasta Gat y hasta Ecrón.
53
וַיָּשֻׁבוּ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִדְּלֹק אַחֲרֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; וַיָּשֹׁסּוּ, אֶת-מַחֲנֵיהֶם.
And the sons of Israel returned from hotly pursuing the Philistines, and they plundered their camps.note
Y los hijos de Israel volvieron de perseguir con ardor a los filisteos, y saquearon sus campamentos.
54
וַיִּקַּח דָּוִד אֶת-רֹאשׁ הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, וַיְבִאֵהוּ יְרוּשָׁלִָם; וְאֶת-כֵּלָיו, שָׂם בְּאָהֳלוֹ. {ס}
And David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem; and his weapons he put in his tent.note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · The valley of Elah — Socoh, Azekah and Ephes-dammim, two armies on two mountains

Between Socoh and Azekah. “They were gathered at Socoh, which belongs to Judah, and they encamped between Socoh and Azekah, in Ephes-dammim”: Socoh and Azekah stand side by side in the list of Judah’s towns of the lowland, “Socoh, and Azekah” (Joshua 15:35), and Azekah is where Joshua’s pursuit of the five kings ended (Joshua 10:10, both now on these pages). The Philistines have come up from the coastal plain into Judah’s own valleys. Ephes-dammim is read as “the end of blood” or “the boundary of blood”; its site is not known. The ASV has “and encamped between Socoh and Azekah, in Ephes-dammim”; the Geneva “in the coast of Dammim”; the Douay “in the borders of Dommim”.

The valley of the terebinth. “In the valley of Elah”: elah is the terebinth, the great tree the angel sat under at Ophrah (Judges 6:11, now on these pages), and the Douay, from the Latin, translates the name instead of keeping it: “came to the valley of Terebinth”. The valley itself is the emeq; what lies between the two mountains in verse 3 is a different word, ha-gay, “the ravine”, the cut the stream has made down the valley floor. The NWT 1984 has “took up camping in the low plain of Elah”; the KJV “pitched by the valley of Elah”.

Verses 4–7 · Goliath of Gath — “the man of the in-between”, six cubits or four, and a spear like a weavers’ beam

The man of the in-between. “And the champion came out”: ish ha-benayim, “the man of the between”, the one who stands in the gap between two armies to settle the war alone. The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, both in this chapter (vv4, 23). The Geneva keeps the picture, “Then came a man between them both”; the Douay, from the Latin, has “a man baseborn”; the KJV “there went out a champion”. The Greek says “a mighty man”, and in verse 10 makes the challenge a monomachia, “single combat”.

Six cubits, or four. “His height was six cubits and a span”: the Masoretic text, followed by all thirteen versions on the two shelves, makes him roughly three metres tall; the TLB converts it, “measuring over nine feet tall”. The Greek reads “four cubits and a span”, about two metres, and so does Josephus: “he was of four cubits and a span in tallness” (Antiquities 6.9.1, in Whiston’s numbering). A Dead Sea Scroll copy of Samuel is reported to read four as well; that is reported here, not verified. The readings differ, and this translation follows the Hebrew it translates. The NWT 1984 has “his height being six cubits and a span”.

Bronze from head to foot. A helmet of bronze, “a coat of scale armour”, shiryon qasqassim, “a coat of scales”, the word for a fish’s scales in the food laws, “anything that has fins and scales” (Leviticus 11:9, now on these pages); its weight, “five thousand shekels”, is perhaps fifty-five to sixty kilograms. On his legs, mitschat nechoshet, “greaves of bronze”, and the word mitschat stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible; it is built on metsach, “forehead”, a “brow-piece” for the shin. Verse 49 will put the stone into the one metsach he left bare. The NWT 1984 has “a coat of mail, of overlapping scales”; the NIV “a coat of scale armor of bronze”; the KJV “a coat of mail”.

A javelin between his shoulders. “And a javelin of bronze between his shoulders”: kidon, the weapon Joshua stretched out toward Ai (Joshua 8:18, now on these pages). “Between his shoulders” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and Moses’ blessing of Benjamin, “he dwells between his shoulders” (Deuteronomy 33:12, now on these pages). The Greek reads a shield here, aspis, and the older shelf follows it: the KJV has “a target of brass between his shoulders”; the Geneva “a shield of brass upon his shoulders”; the Douay “a buckler of brass covered his shoulders”. The ASV and NWT 1984 have “a javelin”.

Like a weavers’ beam. “The shaft of his spear was like a weavers’ beam”: the written text has chets, “arrow”, read ets, “wood, shaft” (ketiv and qere). “Like a weavers’ beam” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; of the Egyptian whom Benaiah killed (1 Chronicles 11:23); and twice of another Philistine. 2 Samuel 21:19 says that “Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite struck Goliath the Gittite, and the shaft of his spear was like a weavers’ beam”, and 1 Chronicles 20:5 that Elhanan “struck Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite”. Readers have long noticed it. Some take the Chronicles verse as the original and Samuel’s as a copyist’s slip — oregim, “weavers”, stands twice in that one verse, and “the Bethlehemite”, beit ha-lachmi, looks like “Lahmi”; others think there were two men called Goliath; others that the Goliath story has gathered round David from another hero. These pages report the three verses and do not decide between them.

Verses 8–11 · The challenge — “choose yourselves a man”, servants of Saul, and “I taunt the battle lines of Israel”

Choose yourselves a man. “Am I not the Philistine, and you servants of Saul? Choose yourselves a man, and let him come down to me”: he is “the Philistine”, the whole nation in one body, and Israel is a king’s servants. The Greek reads “you Hebrews of Saul”. Beru-lakhem, “choose yourselves”, is a rare verb, and the shelf reaches for many words: the ASV has “choose you a man for you”; the Douay “Choose out a man of you, and let him come down and fight hand to hand”; the TLB makes the whole offer plain, “we will settle this in single combat!”.

I taunt. “I taunt the battle lines of Israel this day”: cheraf, the verb of the men of Succoth who “taunted” Gideon (Judges 8:15, now on these pages), and its noun, cherpah, “reproach”, is what Nahash wanted to lay on Israel (1 Samuel 11:2, now on these pages). The verb runs through the chapter: the Philistine says it (v10), the men of Israel say it of him (v25), David says it three times (vv26, 36, 45). The NWT 1984 has “I myself do taunt the battle lines of Israel this day”; the KJV “I defy the armies of Israel this day”.

Dismayed. “They were dismayed and very afraid”: vayyechattu, the verb of Moses’ last charge and Jehovah’s first to Joshua, “Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed” (Deuteronomy 31:8; Joshua 1:9, both now on these pages): Israel does here exactly what it was told not to do. The ASV has “they were dismayed, and greatly afraid”; the NWT 1984 “they became terrified and were greatly afraid”.

Verses 12–16 · David introduced again — “this Ephrathite”, eight sons, forty days, and the verses the Greek does not have

This Ephrathite. “Now David was the son of this Ephrathite of Bethlehem in Judah, whose name was Jesse”: the verse introduces David and his father as if for the first time, one chapter after Samuel anointed him in that same house. “Ephrathite” is the clan-name of Bethlehem’s families, Elimelech’s among them (Ruth 1:2, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “this Ephrathite from Bethlehem of Judah”; the Douay smooths the seam, “that Ephrathite of Bethlehem Juda before mentioned”.

Eight sons, and the verses the Greek does not have. “And he had eight sons”: as in 16:10–11 (now on these pages), and against the seven of 1 Chronicles 2:13–15. The Greek as Brenton prints it, from the Vatican manuscript, has nothing from verse 12 to verse 31, nor verse 41, nor verse 50, nor verses 55–58; in that Greek David goes straight from Saul’s court to the fight. The TLB turns the number round, “had seven older brothers”. Advanced in years among men, ba ba-anashim, literally “come among men”, is an idiom no one is sure of; the KJV has “the man went among men for an old man in the days of Saul”.

Back and forth, and forty days. “David was going back and forth from Saul to shepherd his father’s flock”: the verse joins this chapter to the last, where David became Saul’s armour-bearer (16:21, now on these pages). Meanwhile “the Philistine came near morning and evening, and took his stand forty days”: hashkem ve-ha’arev, “rising early and at evening”. Forty days is the length of Moses on the mountain and of the spies in the land (Exodus 24:18; Numbers 13:25, both now on these pages). The ASV has “Now David went to and fro from Saul to feed his father’s sheep”; the TLB “was on Saul’s staff on a part-time basis”.

Verses 17–22 · Jesse’s errand — roasted grain, ten cheeses, their pledge, and the ring of the camp

Roasted grain and ten cheeses. “This ephah of roasted grain and these ten loaves”: qali, the grain Boaz held out to Ruth at the meal in the fields of this same Bethlehem (Ruth 2:14, now on these pages). “These ten cheeses”: charitsei he-chalav, “cuts of the milk”, and the phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible; the NWT 1984 has “these ten portions of milk”; the KJV “these ten cheeses”; the Douay “these ten little cheeses”.

Take their pledge. “Look after your brothers’ welfare, and take their pledge”: arubbatam, a token, something to bring home that shows they are alive and well. The NIV has “bring back some assurance from them”; the TLB “bring us back a letter from them!”; the Douay, from the Latin, “learn with whom they are placed”.

The ring of the camp. “He came to the ring of the camp”: ha-ma’galah, from agol, round, the root of ma’gal, the worn wheel-track of Psalm 23:3 (now on these pages); it is the circle the camp is drawn up in, and at 26:5 Saul lies asleep “within the ring”. The shelf scatters: the NWT 1984 has “the camp enclosure”; the ASV “the place of the wagons”; the KJV “the trench”; and the Douay takes it for a place-name, “the place of Magala”.

The keeper of the baggage. “David left the baggage from upon him in the hand of the keeper of the baggage”: ha-kelim, the word Saul hid among on the day he was made king, “he has hidden himself among the baggage” (10:22, now on these pages). The same word does four jobs in this chapter: David’s baggage here, the shepherds’ bag and “the bag” he takes the stone from (vv40, 49), and the Philistine’s weapons that he puts in his tent (v54). The ASV has “left his baggage in the hand of the keeper of the baggage”; the KJV “left his carriage in the hand of the keeper of the carriage”.

Verses 23–27 · The reward — riches, the king’s daughter, a house made free, and “who is this uncircumcised Philistine?”

Out of the battle lines, or the caves. “Out of the battle lines of the Philistines”: the written text has mi-ma’arot, “out of the caves”, read mi-ma’arkhot, “out of the battle lines” (ketiv and qere). The KJV has “out of the armies of the Philistines”.

A house made free. “The king will enrich him with great riches, and will give him his daughter, and will make his father’s house free in Israel”: chofshi, the word for a slave’s release, “in the seventh he shall go out free” (Exodus 21:2, now on these pages); here it seems to mean free of the king’s demands, the levies Samuel warned of (8:15, now on these pages). The NIV has “will exempt his family from taxes in Israel”; the Douay “will make his father’s house free from tribute in Israel”; the ASV keeps the word, “make his father’s house free in Israel”.

The uncircumcised and the living God. “For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should taunt the battle lines of the living God?”: the word Samson’s parents used, “the uncircumcised Philistines” (Judges 14:3), and Jonathan’s at the pass, “these uncircumcised” (14:6, both now on these pages). “The living God” here is Elohim chayyim, with a plural adjective, and the phrase stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: twice in this chapter (vv26, 36), once in Moses’ “the voice of the living God speaking out of the midst of the fire” (Deuteronomy 5:26, 5:22 in the Hebrew numbering, now on these pages), and twice in Jeremiah (10:10; 23:36). Joshua’s “a living God is in your midst” is the singular, El chai (Joshua 3:10, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “the battle lines of the living God”; the KJV “the armies of the living God”.

Verses 28–30 · Eliab’s anger — “I know your presumption and the evil of your heart”, and “was it not only a word?”

I know the evil of your heart. “Eliab’s anger burned against David”: the eldest brother, the one Samuel took for the chosen and was told “Jehovah sees into the heart” (16:7, now on these pages), now claims to see into David’s: “I know your presumption and the evil of your heart”, zedon and roa levavkha. And “those few sheep in the wilderness” is a brother’s jab: the youngest left to mind the flock (16:11). The NWT 1984 has “I myself well know your presumptuousness and the badness of your heart”; the ASV “I know thy pride, and the naughtiness of thy heart”; the TLB “I know what a cocky brat you are”.

Was it not only a word? “What have I done now? Was it not only a word?”: halo davar hu, “is it not a word?”, which can mean “I was only talking” or “isn’t there a matter here?”. The shelf takes both roads: the NWT 1984 has “Was it not just a word?”; the NIV “Can’t I even speak?”; the KJV “Is there not a cause?”. Then “he turned from him toward another, and spoke in the same words”: the word davar comes four times in verses 29–30.

Verses 31–37 · David before Saul — “a youth” against “a man of war”, the lion and the bear, and “Jehovah will be with you”

A youth, and a man of war. “For you are a youth, and he has been a man of war from his youth”: na’ar twice, the boy against the man who has fought since he was a boy. The servant who brought David to court had already called him “a man of war” (16:18, now on these pages). “Let no one’s heart fall on account of him”: the NWT 1984 has “Do not let the heart of any man collapse within him”; the KJV “Let no man’s heart fail because of him”.

The lion and the bear. “The lion came, and the bear, and carried off a lamb from the drove; and I went out after it and struck it, and rescued it from its mouth; and when it rose against me, I seized it by its beard”: the shepherd’s own law already knew the case, a sheep “torn by wild beasts” (Exodus 22:13, now on these pages), but David does not bring the torn piece as evidence; he takes the lamb back. “By its beard” has troubled translators: the KJV keeps “I caught him by his beard”; the NIV “I seized it by its hair”; the TLB “I catch it by the jaw”; the Douay “I caught them by the throat”, which is the Greek’s word. Josephus has David catch the lion “by the tail” (Antiquities 6.9.3).

The hand of the lion, the hand of the Philistine. “Jehovah, who rescued me from the hand of the lion and from the hand of the bear, he will rescue me from the hand of this Philistine”: the Hebrew says yad, “hand”, three times, and this translation keeps it all three times, so that the Philistine is the third beast in the list. The ASV has “out of the paw of the lion, and out of the paw of the bear”. Saul’s answer is the line said of David at court, “Jehovah is with him” (16:18), turned into a wish: “Go, and Jehovah will be with you”. The Greek adds a line to verse 36, “Shall I not go and strike him, and take away reproach from Israel today?”, and the Douay has a form of it from the Latin: “I will go now, and take away the reproach of the people”.

Verses 38–40 · Saul’s armour and five smooth stones — the helmet of bronze, “I have not tested them”, the pouch and the sling

Dressed as the Philistine. “Saul clothed David with his garments, and put a helmet of bronze on his head, and clothed him with a coat of mail”: kova nechoshet and shiryon, the same two words as Goliath’s helmet and coat in verse 5. Saul dresses David in the Philistine’s own equipment, and David cannot walk in it. The NWT 1984 has “he put a copper helmet upon his head, after which he clothed him with a coat of mail”; the KJV “Saul armed David with his armour”.

I have not tested them. “And tried to walk, for he had not tested them”: lo nissah, the verb of “God tested Abraham” (Genesis 22:1, now on these pages), here of a new pair of boots. The first clause is short in the Hebrew, and some versions fill it out: the NWT 1984 brackets what it adds, “undertook to go [but could not], because he had not tried them out”; the ASV has “he assayed to go; for he had not proved it”.

Five smooth stones, the pouch and the sling. “Five smooth stones from the brook”: challuqei avanim, stones the water has worn smooth. He puts them “in the shepherds’ bag that he had, in the pouch”: yalqut, and the word stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The sling, qela, is the Benjamites’ weapon, “every one of these could sling a stone at a hair and not miss” (Judges 20:16, now on these pages). The ASV has “put them in the shepherd’s bag which he had, even in his wallet”; the KJV “even in a scrip”; the NWT 1984 “the five smoothest stones from the torrent valley”.

Verses 41–47 · “Am I a dog?” — the Philistine despises David, and “the battle is Jehovah’s”

Despised, ruddy, beautiful. “He despised him; for he was a youth, and ruddy, with a beautiful appearance”: vayyivzehu, the verb of “Esau despised his birthright” (Genesis 25:34, now on these pages), and admoni, “ruddy”, the word of Esau at his birth and of David at his anointing (16:12, now on these pages). What Samuel was told not to look at (16:7) is exactly what the Philistine looks at. The NWT 1984 has “he began despising him because he proved to be a boy and ruddy, of beautiful appearance”; the TLB “this nice little red-cheeked boy!”.

Am I a dog? “Am I a dog, that you come to me with sticks?”: ba-maqlot, plural, though David carries one staff (v40). The Greek has “with a rod and stones”, and gives David an answer, “No, but worse than a dog”; Josephus has the same reply, “No, not for a dog, but for a creature worse than a dog” (Antiquities 6.9.4). “And the Philistine cursed David by his gods”: be-elohav, which can also be read “by his god”. The ASV has “Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves?”; the NWT 1984 “called down evil upon David by his gods”.

The birds of the heavens. “I will give your flesh to the birds of the heavens and to the beasts of the field”, and David answers with the same curse turned round: “I will give the corpses of the camp of the Philistines this day to the birds of the heavens and to the wild beasts of the earth”. It is the curse of the covenant, “your carcass will be food for every bird of the heavens and for the beasts of the earth” (Deuteronomy 28:26, now on these pages). The ASV has “unto the birds of the heavens, and to the wild beasts of the earth”.

In the name of Jehovah of armies. “You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin; but I come to you in the name of Jehovah of armies, the God of the battle lines of Israel”: tseva’ot, armies, set against ma’arkhot, battle lines, the word the Philistine taunted. “So that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel, and all this assembly may know that not with sword and spear does Jehovah save; for the battle is Jehovah’s”: Jonathan said the same at the pass, “nothing holds Jehovah back from saving by many or by few” (14:6, now on these pages). Ha-qahal, “the assembly”, is the word for Israel gathered before God, used of an army about to watch a fight. The NWT 1984 has “to Jehovah belongs the battle”; the ASV “for the battle is Jehovah’s”; the TLB “he works without regard to human means!”.

Verses 48–54 · The stone in the forehead — “he fell on his face to the ground”, Goliath’s own sword, and the head brought to Jerusalem

The stone sank into his forehead. “And struck the Philistine on his forehead; and the stone sank into his forehead”: metsach, the bare place above the greaves named for it (v6), and vattitba, “sank”, the verb of Pharaoh’s captains, “sunk in the Red Sea” (Exodus 15:4, now on these pages). The Greek has the stone go “through the helmet into his forehead”. The ASV has “the stone sank into his forehead”; the Geneva “the stone sticked in his forehead”; the Douay “the stone was fixed in his forehead”.

He fell on his face to the ground. “And he fell on his face to the ground”: the Philistines’ god had done the same twice before the ark of Jehovah, “Dagon had fallen on his face to the ground” (5:3, 5:4, now on these pages), and the second time his head was cut off and lay on the threshold. Now the Philistines’ champion falls on his face, and his head is cut off. The words with this preposition, vayyipol al-panav artsah, are also those of Joshua before the ark after Ai (Joshua 7:6, now on these pages). The NIV has “he fell facedown on the ground”; the Geneva “he fell groveling to the earth”.

No sword in David’s hand. “So David prevailed over the Philistine with the sling and with the stone, and struck the Philistine and killed him; and there was no sword in David’s hand. And David ran and stood over the Philistine, and took his sword … and killed him, and cut off his head with it”: verse 50 has him already dead, and verse 51 kills him with his own sword; the Greek has no verse 50. The sword itself returns at Nob, “the sword of Goliath the Philistine, whom you struck in the valley of Elah” (21:10, 21:9 in English Bibles, now on these pages). “Their mighty man was dead”: gibboram, a different word from “the champion” of verse 4. The NWT 1984 has “their mighty one had died”; the ASV “their champion was dead”.

The pursuit. “Until you come to the ravine, and to the gates of Ekron; and the slain of the Philistines fell on the way of Shaaraim, as far as Gath and as far as Ekron”: gay, “ravine”, the word of verse 3; the Greek reads “Gath”, and the NIV follows it, “to the entrance of Gath and to the gates of Ekron”, while the ASV keeps the Hebrew as a name, “until thou comest to Gai”. Shaaraim, “two gates”, is in Judah’s list beside Socoh and Azekah (Joshua 15:36, now on these pages). “From hotly pursuing”, mi-dloq, is Jacob’s complaint to Laban, “that you have hotly pursued me” (Genesis 31:36, now on these pages).

To Jerusalem, and to his tent. “And David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem; and his weapons he put in his tent”: at this point in the story Jerusalem is still a Jebusite city, and David takes it only as king (2 Samuel 5:6–9), so the verse either looks ahead or tells what David did later; the TLB adds the word, “Later David took Goliath’s head to Jerusalem”. “His weapons” are the kelim again. The NIV has “he put the Philistine’s weapons in his own tent”; the NWT 1984 “his weapons he put in his tent”.

Verses 55–58 · “Whose son is this youth?” — Saul and Abner, and the seam between two chapters

Whose son is this youth? “Whose son is this youth, Abner?” … “By the life of your soul, O king, I do not know”: in the chapter before, Saul sent to Jesse by name to ask for David, loved him and made him his armour-bearer (16:19–22, now on these pages); here neither he nor Abner, his commander and his uncle’s son (14:50–51, now on these pages), knows whose son David is. Readers differ: many see two accounts of how David came to Saul set side by side; others harmonize, with a long interval between, or with Saul asking about the father, whose house he has just promised to make free (v25), rather than about the boy. The Greek as Brenton prints it has no verses 55–58. Some versions harmonize in the wording: the Douay has Saul ask “Of what family is this young man descended, Abner?”, the TLB “what sort of family does this young fellow come from?”, and the NVI puts the question in the past, «Anteriormente Saúl, al ver a David enfrentarse con el filisteo, había preguntado a Abner». The ASV has “Abner, whose son is this youth?”.

The young man. “You ask whose son the young man is”: ha-elem, “the young man”, a rarer word than na’ar; it stands again only at 20:22 (now on these pages), of the boy who fetches Jonathan’s arrows. Then David comes back “with the head of the Philistine in his hand”, and answers the question in four words, “the son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite”. The KJV has “Enquire thou whose son the stripling is”; the NWT 1984 “You inquire whose son the lad is”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the champion” for ish ha-benayim, and “their mighty man” for gibboram; “the valley” for emeq and “the ravine” for gay; “scale armour”; “greaves” for mitschat and “forehead” for metsach; “javelin” for kidon, as in Joshua 8:18; “battle line” for ma’arakhah, as in 4:2; “taunt” for cheraf, as in Judges 8:15, and “reproach” for cherpah, as in 11:2; “dismayed”, as in Joshua 1:9; “roasted grain” for qali, as in Ruth 2:14; “the ring of the camp”; “baggage”, “bag” and “weapons” for kelim; “free” for chofshi, as in Exodus 21:2; “the living God” for Elohim chayyim; “hand” three times in verse 37; “tested” for nasah, as in Genesis 22:1; “the pouch” for yalqut; “despised”, as in Genesis 25:34; “sank”, as in Exodus 15:4; “fell on his face to the ground”, as in 5:3; “hotly pursuing”, as in Genesis 31:36; and “the young man” for elem.

Echoes paid. The ruddy, beautiful youth of 16:12 is despised for it; the “man of war” of 16:18 is told he is only a youth; Eliab, rejected because Jehovah sees the heart (16:7), claims to see David’s; Saul who hid among the baggage (10:22) has a boy leave his baggage to fight; the javelin of Joshua 8:18 and the “between his shoulders” of Deuteronomy 33:12 come together on Goliath; the uncircumcised of Jonathan’s pass (14:6) and his “by many or by few” come back; Dagon’s fall on his face (5:3–4) is matched; and Joshua 11:22’s remnant of the Anakim in Gath sends out its giant.

Echoes planted. Saul’s ring of the camp at 26:5; Goliath’s sword at Nob (21:10, now on these pages); the boy and the arrows (20:22, now on these pages); the women’s song and Saul’s jealousy (18:6–9, now on these pages); Elhanan and Goliath the Gittite (2 Samuel 21:19; 1 Chronicles 20:5); the stronghold of Zion (2 Samuel 5:6–9); and “the battle is God’s” at 2 Chronicles 20:15. None of the others is translated here yet.
