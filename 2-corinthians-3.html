The same letter, written from Macedonia. Paul answers the question of recommendation: the Corinthians are his letter, and his competence is from God. He sets the ministry of the Spirit, which gives life, above the old covenant written on stone, and says the veil over Moses’ face is lifted in Christ.
Undated by the text: the chapter names no year. It follows the chapter 2 account of the visit that did not happen and of Titus in Macedonia.

Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need, as some do, letters of recommendation to you or from you?note
¿Empezamos otra vez a recomendarnos a nosotros mismos? ¿O necesitamos, como algunos, cartas de recomendación para ustedes o de parte de ustedes?
2
ἡ ἐπιστολὴ ἡμῶν ὑμεῖς ἐστε, ἐγγεγραμμένη ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν, γινωσκομένη καὶ ἀναγινωσκομένη ὑπὸ πάντων ἀνθρώπων·
You yourselves are our letter, written on our hearts, known and read by all people.note
Ustedes mismos son nuestra carta, escrita en nuestros corazones, conocida y leída por todas las personas.
3
φανερούμενοι ὅτι ἐστὲ ἐπιστολὴ Χριστοῦ διακονηθεῖσα ὑφʼ ἡμῶν, ἐγγεγραμμένη οὐ μέλανι ἀλλὰ πνεύματι θεοῦ ζῶντος, οὐκ ἐν πλαξὶν λιθίναις ἀλλʼ ἐν πλαξὶν καρδίαις σαρκίναις.
Clearly you are a letter of Christ, delivered through us, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on stone tablets but on tablets of human hearts.note
Es evidente que ustedes son una carta de Cristo, entregada por nosotros, escrita no con tinta sino con el Espíritu del Dios vivo; no en tablas de piedra sino en tablas de corazones de carne.

Πεποίθησιν δὲ τοιαύτην ἔχομεν διὰ τοῦ Χριστοῦ πρὸς τὸν θεόν.
Such is the confidence we have through Christ toward God.note
Esa es la confianza que tenemos ante Dios por medio de Cristo.
5
οὐχ ὅτι ἀφʼ ἑαυτῶν ἱκανοί ἐσμεν λογίσασθαί τι ὡς ἐξ ἑαυτῶν, ἀλλʼ ἡ ἱκανότης ἡμῶν ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ,
Not that we are competent in ourselves to claim anything as coming from us; our competence is from God,note
No es que nosotros, por nosotros mismos, seamos capaces de pensar algo como si viniera de nosotros; nuestra capacidad viene de Dios,

ὃς καὶ ἱκάνωσεν ἡμᾶς διακόνους καινῆς διαθήκης, οὐ γράμματος ἀλλὰ πνεύματος· τὸ γὰρ γράμμα ἀποκτέννει, τὸ δὲ πνεῦμα ζῳοποιεῖ.
who also made us competent as ministers of a new covenant, not of letter but of Spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life.note
el que nos hizo capaces como ministros de un nuevo pacto, no de una letra sino del Espíritu; porque la letra mata, pero el Espíritu da vida.

Εἰ δὲ ἡ διακονία τοῦ θανάτου ἐν γράμμασιν ἐντετυπωμένη λίθοις ἐγενήθη ἐν δόξῃ, ὥστε μὴ δύνασθαι ἀτενίσαι τοὺς υἱοὺς Ἰσραὴλ εἰς τὸ πρόσωπον Μωϋσέως διὰ τὴν δόξαν τοῦ προσώπου αὐτοῦ τὴν καταργουμένην,
Now if the ministry of death, engraved in letters on stones, came in glory, so that the sons of Israel could not gaze at the face of Moses because of the glory of his face, a glory that was passing away,note
Ahora bien, si el ministerio de muerte, grabado con letras en piedras, llegó con gloria, de modo que los hijos de Israel no podían fijar la vista en el rostro de Moisés por la gloria de su rostro, una gloria que iba a desaparecer,
8
πῶς οὐχὶ μᾶλλον ἡ διακονία τοῦ πνεύματος ἔσται ἐν δόξῃ;
how much more will the ministry of the Spirit be in glory?note
¿cómo no tendrá aún más gloria el ministerio del Espíritu?
9
εἰ γὰρ τῇ διακονίᾳ τῆς κατακρίσεως δόξα, πολλῷ μᾶλλον περισσεύει ἡ διακονία τῆς δικαιοσύνης δόξῃ.
For if the ministry of condemnation had glory, much more does the ministry of righteousness abound in glory.note
Porque si el ministerio de condenación tuvo gloria, mucho más abunda en gloria el ministerio de justicia.
10
καὶ γὰρ οὐ δεδόξασται τὸ δεδοξασμένον ἐν τούτῳ τῷ μέρει εἵνεκεν τῆς ὑπερβαλλούσης δόξης·
For what had been glorified has in this respect no glory, because of the surpassing glory.note
Pues lo que había sido glorificado no tiene gloria en esta parte, por causa de la gloria que lo supera.
11
εἰ γὰρ τὸ καταργούμενον διὰ δόξης, πολλῷ μᾶλλον τὸ μένον ἐν δόξῃ.
For if what was passing away came with glory, much more will what remains be in glory.note
Porque si lo que se iba a terminar llegó con gloria, mucho más tendrá gloria lo que permanece.
12
Ἔχοντες οὖν τοιαύτην ἐλπίδα πολλῇ παρρησίᾳ χρώμεθα,

Teniendo esa esperanza, hablamos con mucha franqueza,
13
καὶ οὐ καθάπερ Μωϋσῆς ἐτίθει κάλυμμα ἐπὶ τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ πρὸς τὸ μὴ ἀτενίσαι τοὺς υἱοὺς Ἰσραὴλ εἰς τὸ τέλος τοῦ καταργουμένου.
and not as Moses, who put a veil over his face so that the sons of Israel would not gaze at the end of what was passing away.note
y no como Moisés, que se ponía un velo sobre el rostro para que los hijos de Israel no fijaran la vista en el fin de lo que se iba a terminar.

Pero sus mentes se endurecieron. Hasta hoy, al leerse el antiguo pacto, el mismo velo sigue sin levantarse, porque solo en Cristo se quita.

But to this day, whenever Moses is read, a veil lies over their hearts.note
Pero hasta hoy, cada vez que se lee a Moisés, un velo yace sobre sus corazones.

ἡνίκα δὲ ἐὰν ἐπιστρέψῃ πρὸς κύριον, περιαιρεῖται τὸ κάλυμμα.

Pero cuando alguien se vuelve a Jehová, el velo se quita.

Ahora bien, el Señor es el Espíritu, y donde está el Espíritu del Señor hay libertad.
18

And all of us, with unveiled faces, reflecting like mirrors the glory of the Lord, are being transformed into the same image from glory to glory, just as from the Lord who is the Spirit.note
Y todos nosotros, con el rostro descubierto, reflejando como espejos la gloria del Señor, somos transformados en la misma imagen, de gloria en gloria, tal como viene del Señor que es el Espíritu.
Verses 1–3 · letters of recommendation, and the congregation as the letter of Christ

“Letters of recommendation.” synstatikōn epistolōn, letters of recommendation. The noun is the word for a letter that vouches for a person, and Paul asks whether he needs one, like others. The answer the sentence gives is the Corinthians themselves. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Are we starting to recommend ourselves again? Or do we need, like some men, letters of recommendation to you or from you?” The KJV “Do we begin again to commend ourselves? or need we, as some others, epistles of commendation to you, or letters of commendation from you?”

“A letter written on hearts.” epistolē hēmōn hymeis este, you are our letter. The Corinthians are the letter, known and read by all people, and the sentence makes their life the document of Paul’s ministry. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “You yourselves are our letter, inscribed on our hearts and known and being read by all mankind.” The TNM 2019 “Ustedes mismos son nuestra carta, inscrita en nuestros corazones y conocida y leída por toda la humanidad.”

“Not with ink.” ou melani alla pneumati theou zōntos, not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God. The letter is written by the Spirit, not in tablets of stone but on tablets of flesh. The shelf: the KJV “written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the heart.”

Verses 4–6 · competence from God, and the ministry of the Spirit that gives life

“Competence from God.” hikanoi, competent, and hikanotēs, competence. The word is the word of sufficiency, and Paul says it comes from God and not from himself. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Not that we of ourselves are adequately qualified to consider that anything comes from us, but our being adequately qualified comes from God.”

“Not of letter but of Spirit.” ou grammatos alla pneumatos, not of letter but of Spirit. The contrast is between the written code and the Spirit, and the sentence gives the reason: the letter kills, the Spirit gives life. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “not of a written code, but of spirit; for the written code condemns to death, but the spirit makes alive.” The KJV “not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life.”

Verses 7–11 · the glory of Moses’ face, the ministry of condemnation and of righteousness, and what passes away and what remains

“The ministry of death.” hē diakonia tou thanatou, the ministry of death, engraved in letters on stones. The sentence says the old ministry came with glory, so much that Israel could not gaze at Moses’ face, and that the glory was passing away. The argument is from the lesser to the greater: if the first had glory, the ministry of the Spirit will have more. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now if the code that administers death and that was engraved in letters on stones came with such glory that the sons of Israel could not gaze at the face of Moses because of the glory of his face, a glory that was to be done away with.” The KJV “But if the ministration of death, written and engraven in stones, was glorious, so that the children of Israel could not stedfastly behold the face of Moses for the glory of his countenance; which glory was to be done away.”

“What is passing away.” to katargoumenon, the thing being brought to an end. The verb katargeō is used of the old covenant and of the glory of the face, and it is the verb of ending or making inoperative. The translation keeps “passing away” so that the contrast with “what remains” in verse 11 stays visible. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “una gloria que iba a ser eliminada.”

Verses 12–15 · boldness, the veil that Moses put on, and the veil that remains over the reading of the old covenant

“Great boldness.” polle parrēsia, much freedom of speech. The noun is the word for speaking openly, and Paul says the hope lets him speak this way. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Since we have such a hope, we are using great freeness of speech.”

“The veil.” kalymma, a covering or veil. Paul says Moses put a veil on his face so that Israel would not look at the end of the passing glory, and that the same veil remains on the reading of the old covenant until it is taken away in Christ. The veil is the image of minds that cannot see. The shelf: the KJV “But their minds were blinded: for until this day remaineth the same vail untaken away in the reading of the old testament.” The TNM 2019 “Pero la mente de ellos estaba embotada.”

Verse 16 · turning to the Lord, and the veil taken away

“Turns to Jehovah.” epistrephō pros kyrion, turns to the Lord. The sentence echoes Exodus 34:34, where Moses turns back to the LORD and the veil is taken off. The referent is the God of Israel, and the project’s rule for an Old Testament referent of kyrios is Jehovah, so the translation prints the name. The note records the Greek word. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But when one turns to Jehovah, the veil is taken away.” The KJV “Nevertheless when it shall turn to the Lord, the vail shall be taken away.”

Verses 17–18 · the Lord is the Spirit, freedom, and the transformation from glory to glory

“The Lord is the Spirit.” ho de kyrios to pneuma estin, the Lord is the Spirit. The sentence does not say whether the Lord here is God or Christ. The translation keeps “the Lord” because the Greek leaves the referent open, and the note records that the NWT prints “Jehovah,” a reading that makes the referent God. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now Jehovah is the Spirit, and where the spirit of Jehovah is, there is freedom.” The KJV “Now the Lord is that Spirit: and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty.”

“Transformed from glory to glory.” metamorphoumetha apo doxēs eis doxan, we are transformed from glory to glory. The image is the reflection of a mirror, and the transformation is the change of the viewer into the image he sees. The translation keeps the mirror as the Greek has it. The shelf: the KJV “with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.” The TNM 2019 “somos transformados en esa misma imagen que va reflejando más y más gloria.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “letter” for epistolē and gramma at 3:1–7, kept apart so that the letter of Christ and the letter that kills are not confused; “ministry” for diakonia at 3:7–9; “passing away” for katargeō at 3:7, 3:11, 3:13 and 3:14; “veil” for kalymma at 3:13–16.

Echoes paid. The Spirit of 1 Corinthians 2:10–16 returns as the Spirit that gives life (3:6, 3:17); the hearts of 2 Corinthians 2:4 return as the letter written on hearts (3:2–3); the sufficiency of 2:16 returns as God’s competence (3:5–6).

Echoes planted. The face (3:7, 3:13, 3:18) returns in chapter 4, where Paul speaks of the light of the gospel of the glory of Christ (4:4–6).

2 Corinthians 4 is the next chapter; it is not yet on these pages. Paul turns from the veil to the treasure in clay jars.
