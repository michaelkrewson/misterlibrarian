Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · Ezekiel 19:1–4 a dirge — the lioness and her first cub

⭐ A dirge. Qinah, a lament for the dead, one of the words on the scroll of 2:10. ‘Take up a dirge’, the two words side by side, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, 28:12 and 32:2. The chapter opens ‘And you’, with no ‘son of man’. It is for the princes of Israel, literally ‘to’ them.

⭐ Your mother. ‘Your’ is singular, and the chapter does not say who the mother is.

⭐ A lioness. Levi’a. Genesis 49:9 calls Judah a lion’s whelp that lay down like a lion and a lioness; compare Numbers 24:9. The ‘whelp’ of Genesis is the word printed ‘cub’ here. The Douay reads the line as a question, “Why did thy mother the lioness lie down”.

⭐ Young lion. Kefir, as at Judges 14:5.

⭐ Humans he devoured. The words adam akhal stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, both here (verses 3 and 6). 36:13 uses the pair of a land.

⭐ In their pit. The word beshachtam stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and verse 8. ‘Caught’ is the verb of 12:13. Lamentations 4:20 speaks of the anointed one taken in pits, with a related noun and another verb.

⭐ To Egypt. 2 Kings 23:34 tells of King Jehoahaz taken to Egypt, where he died, and Jeremiah 22:11 says that Shallum son of Josiah, commonly taken to be the same king, will not return. Ezekiel names no king. The TLB writes “King Jehoahaz” into verse 3.

Verses 5–9 · Ezekiel 19:5–9 the second cub — the cage — Babylon

⭐ Hope lost. The words are those the bones say at 37:11. ‘She had waited’ is one rare form; the Douay has “seeing herself weakened”.

⚠ One of her cubs. The Hebrew says ‘one’ again, where several versions have ‘another’: the NIV “another of her cubs”.

⚠ Who is he? The second cub is not named, and readers differ. Later kings of Judah were taken or bound for Babylon: Jehoiakim (2 Chronicles 36:6), Jehoiachin (2 Kings 24:15) and Zedekiah (2 Kings 25:7), none of these passages translated here yet. The TLB writes in “King Jehoiachin”.

⚠ His widows. Verse 7 says ‘and he knew his widows’. ‘His’ is singular and ‘their cities’ plural. Many read a word for palaces or strongholds: the KJV has “desolate palaces”, the ASV “palaces”, the NWT 1984 “his dwelling towers”. The Douay keeps the widows: “He learned to make widows”. The same spelling at Isaiah 13:22 is read as ‘castles’.

⭐ Land and fullness. The verb and the noun are those of 12:19, where a land is to be ‘desolate of its fullness’. Here the land and its fullness are desolate together.

⭐ Nations set against him. The verb is literally ‘gave’, with no object. They spread a net, as Jehovah says he will at 12:13.

⚠ Cage and strongholds. The word for ‘cage’ stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Metsodot may be strongholds or nets: the NWT 1984 has “hunting nets”, the ASV “strongholds”. A related word is the ‘snare’ of 12:13 and 17:20.

⭐ The mountains of Israel. The phrase of 6:2 and 36:1.

Verses 10–11 · Ezekiel 19:10–11 the vine by the waters

⭐ A second picture. The mother is now a vine. Lion, sceptre and vine stand together in Genesis 49:9–11; this chapter does not cite it.

⚠ In your blood. The Hebrew is hard. Some read ‘in your vineyard’, a change of letters: the NIV has “in your vineyard”, the KJV “in thy blood”. Compare ‘in your blood’ at 16:6.

⭐ Many waters. As at 17:8, with the same word for boughs.

⚠ She, then it. Verse 11 begins with ‘she’ and then speaks of ‘its’ height and branches in the masculine.

⭐ Rods for sceptres. Matteh is a staff or branch and shevet a rod or sceptre, the word of Genesis 49:10.

⭐ Thick foliage. The word returns at 31:3. The Douay has “among the branches”.

Verses 12–14 · Ezekiel 19:12–14 uprooted — the wilderness — the fire

⭐ The east wind. It dries the vine of 17:10, and yavesh, ‘dry’, returns here.

⚠ Torn off. The verbs are plural though ‘her strong rod’ is singular. The form hitparqu stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here and Exodus 32:24, where gold is broken off.

⭐ Fire. The vine wood of 15:4 was given to the fire. ‘Consumed’ (verses 12 and 14) is the verb printed ‘devoured’ of the young lions in verses 3 and 6, and ‘eaten’ at 15:4.

⭐ In the wilderness. ‘Dry and thirsty’ in these words stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The chapter says the vine is planted there now.

⭐ No sceptre. The fire comes out of a rod of the vine itself. ‘For ruling’ is a form of mashal, ‘rule’, not the ‘proverb’ of the last chapter.

⚠ It is a dirge. ‘It is a dirge’ stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here and 32:16. The last verb is past, ‘has become’; the KJV has “shall be for a lamentation”, the NWT 1984 “will become a dirge”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “young lion” at 2, 3, 5 and 6; “pit” at 4 and 8; “strong rod” at 11, 12 and 14, apart from “sceptre”; and “dirge” at 1 and 14.

Echoes paid. The dirges of 2:10; the vine wood and fire of 15:4; and the vine and east wind of 17:10.

Echoes planted. A dirge over Pharaoh, who is likened to a young lion, at Ezekiel 32:2. Thick foliage again at Ezekiel 31:3.

Ezekiel 20, now on these pages: elders come to inquire, and Israel’s story is told again from Egypt.
