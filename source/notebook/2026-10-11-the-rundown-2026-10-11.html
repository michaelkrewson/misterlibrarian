The world

  • Houthi-claimed strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on October 8 killed three Saudi citizens and damaged a Saudia aircraft on the ground, Saudi aviation authorities said, prompting Lufthansa to suspend its Riyadh flights through October 16. ↗
  • President Trump announced October 9 that Russia will supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel immediately, 500,000 tons in November and several million more tons after that to ease U.S. fuel prices ahead of the midterms, a move Ukraine's Zelensky called "a weak decision by strong partners." ↗

Business / macro & markets

  • Bank earnings season opens Tuesday, October 13 with JPMorgan Chase's Q3 report, where analysts expect EPS near $5.86 to $5.91 on roughly $51 billion in revenue, following Q2's $21.2 billion profit. ↗
  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases September's Consumer Price Index on Wednesday, October 14 at 8:30 a.m. ET, with economists forecasting headline inflation ticking up to about 3.6-3.7% year-over-year from August's 3.4%. ↗

Technology

  • Cloudflare announced October 9 it is acquiring Deno, the JavaScript/TypeScript runtime Ryan Dahl co-created; the whole Deno team joins Cloudflare, Deno Deploy winds down over about six months, and the open-source runtime gets one more year of maintenance releases before development ends. ↗
  • Microsoft unveiled Decision-1 on October 9, a 9-billion-parameter model built on Alibaba's open-weight Qwen3.5 to score yes/no, multiple-choice and rating decisions rather than generate open-ended text, which Microsoft says runs 35 times faster than GPT-6 Sol at matching latency. ↗

Science

  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went October 5 to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for discovering and developing optogenetics, the light-gated ion channels researchers use to switch neurons on and off with light. ↗
  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics went October 6 to Francis Halzen for his work on the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a cubic kilometer of light detectors frozen into the Antarctic ice that led to the discovery of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos. ↗
  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went October 7 to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai, split equally, for their work on non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis, the chemistry behind why life builds its molecules in only one of two possible mirror-image forms. ↗

Arts & culture

  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature went October 8 to Canadian poet and classicist Anne Carson, best known for the verse novel "Autobiography of Red," with the Swedish Academy citing her inventive work's playful engagement with the classical tradition; the prize carries 12 million Swedish kronor and a December 10 Stockholm ceremony. ↗
