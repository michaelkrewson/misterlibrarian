The world

  • Russia told the WHO on October 6 that medical observation has ended for all roughly 200 contacts of lab worker Darya Shipilova, who died October 2 after reportedly breaking a test tube at the Irkutsk Anti-Plague Institute, with every contact testing negative — even as Russian media reported a second plague death that day, which federal health authorities deny. ↗
  • UK Maritime Trade Operations logged nine attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz in the first six days of October alone — already half of September's combined total for the strait and the wider Persian Gulf — including five incidents on October 6, four of them tanker strikes, plus an Iranian Revolutionary Guard boarding that forced one tanker to turn back. ↗

Science

  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Henri Kagan (France) and Kenso Soai (Japan), splitting the $1.2 million award for discoveries in asymmetric autocatalysis that solved a century-old puzzle about how chemistry produces molecules of a single handedness, or "homochirality." ↗

Space

  • South Korea launched the fifth Nuri rocket today from the Naro Space Center carrying a record 15 satellites — five KAIST-built cluster satellites plus ten university and research CubeSats — the country's first deployment of a satellite constellation in a single mission. ↗

Health & medicine

  • The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth (Stanford), Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel (both Germany) for discovering light-gated ion channels and turning them into optogenetics, the technique researchers now use to switch individual brain cells on and off with light. ↗
