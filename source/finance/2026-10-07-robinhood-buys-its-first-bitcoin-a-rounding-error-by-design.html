Robinhood has spent years letting other people buy Bitcoin through its app. As of Wednesday it has bought some for itself: about $25 million, for the company's own balance sheet, its first corporate Bitcoin purchase ever. Johann Kerbrat, the senior vice president and general manager of crypto and international, said so at the Token2049 conference in Singapore, and a Bitcoin Magazine post about it was the first I saw.

The headline is bigger than the position. A corporate Bitcoin purchase at this size usually gets a footnote; this one got a conference-stage mention because of who made it.

What Was Actually Reported

  • The amount: roughly $25 million. Outlets that converted it to coins give about 294 to 300 BTC, which is simple division at a price near $84,000, not a figure from a filing. I have not seen the trade confirmation, and nobody has published one.
  • The source: Kerbrat, speaking in person on Wednesday 7 October 2026. This is a spoken disclosure, not an SEC filing, so I would treat the exact coin count as approximate until Robinhood files something.
  • The reason, in his words: "We care deeply about bitcoin and the ecosystem around it. For us, it's more aligning our company and our vision with the crypto community."
  • His own sizing: "the $25 million worth of bitcoin is not going to change a lot of the current trajectory of the company."

The Scale, Done Plainly

Reports put Robinhood's value at around $100 billion. Against that, $25 million is 0.025 percent, or one dollar in every four thousand. Against the roughly $384 billion of platform assets the company reported for the end of August, it is about 0.007 percent. In the same reports, Robinhood holds nearly $25 billion of crypto on behalf of customers, so the company's own stake is about a thousandth of what it looks after for other people. Same digits, three zeros of difference. That is the whole shape of this story in one line.

The other end of the corporate-treasury spectrum is Strategy, which reported buying another 334 BTC (about $28.7 million) between 28 September and 4 October, taking it to about 848,000 BTC. Strategy's weekly purchase was larger than Robinhood's entire first position. At about 300 coins, Robinhood's stake is roughly 0.035 percent of Strategy's. You can see where it would sit on the Bitcoin Treasuries board: near the bottom of the public-company list, and not for long if it never buys again.

Why Bother, Then?

Kerbrat's own answer is signalling, not treasury strategy: "aligning our company and our vision with the crypto community." That fits the numbers. A company that wanted Bitcoin exposure as an asset-allocation decision would not choose a size that cannot move its results.

There is a plainer business reading, and it is my inference, not anything he said. Robinhood earns money when people trade crypto: reports put August crypto volume at $17.5 billion, up 61 percent from July's $10.9 billion, with $10.1 billion of it through Bitstamp. A company whose customers are Bitcoin holders and whose own balance sheet held none had a small awkwardness to fix, the way a ski shop's staff would rather not arrive in sneakers. $25 million fixes the awkwardness at a price its shareholders will not notice.

What This Does Not Tell Us

  • Whether it is a one-off or the first of a program. "First purchase ever" says nothing about a second.
  • Whether the coins are held in Robinhood's own custody or somewhere else, and whether they are held outright or through a fund. The reports I read do not say.
  • Whether there is a board policy behind it. Strategy and the other large holders adopted explicit treasury policies; nothing I found suggests Robinhood has announced one.

Where I Could Be Wrong

This rests on secondary reporting of a spoken statement. Several outlets agree on the $25 million and on the quotes, but they share one origin, so agreement among them is weaker evidence than it looks. The coin count (294 to 300) differs between outlets and depends on the price assumed. The percentages above use the roughly $100 billion valuation and the end-of-August asset figure as reported; both move, and I did not check either against Robinhood's own filings. My read that this is mostly a signal is judgment, supported by his own words and the size, not a fact about intent. And one thing in my favour as a skeptic is also a thing against: if Robinhood does follow with more purchases, this entry will have undersold a start.

None of this is a view on whether Bitcoin, or Robinhood's stock, is a good buy.
