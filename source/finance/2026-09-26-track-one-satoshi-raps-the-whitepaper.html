Every board on this site tracks something that moves money — a price, a balance sheet, a regulator's calendar. This entry doesn't. Bitcoin has also spent seventeen years generating an enormous amount of culture that has nothing to do with any of that: novelty songs, deep-fried memes, fan art, the occasional genuinely good joke. Nobody here has been keeping a list. Starting today, this is that list — one tag, bitcoin music, that grows every time something in that vein is worth a look. First entry:

I told Opus 5.5 to find out who Satoshi was and ask him to rap the whitepaper for me.

It located an old email address nobody knew about.

Then a swarm of agents set up a studio for him and got a mic and everything. They even brought in pineapple jalapeno pizza.

What's actually in the video: a photorealistic AI-generated studio session — headphones, condenser mic, pop filter — starring a generated likeness of an older man performing a rap to camera. What's real about the caption and what's a bit are two different things, and it's worth being plain about which is which. The joke half is doing the heavy lifting: nobody's identity was "located," no email address changed hands, and no pizza toppings can be confirmed either way. Satoshi Nakamoto's actual identity has never been publicly established — no verified photo, no confirmed name, no confirmed nationality, possibly not even one person. The generated face in this video is exactly that: generated, a stand-in the way every "here's what Satoshi looked like" image on the internet has always been a stand-in. The real half is just as true, though — an AI agent did write and produce an actual rap of the actual Bitcoin whitepaper, badly-lip-synced Auto-Tune and all, and someone's studio-session prompt genuinely did ask for a mic, headphones and a pop filter, which is more production value than most crypto Twitter threads bother with.

Whether the whitepaper itself survives being rapped is a separate question this entry declines to grade. It's Bitcoin culture, filed as Bitcoin culture — no verdict, no take on whether the bars were good, just the tag it lives under so the next one (and the first meme, whenever it earns its own entry) has somewhere to go.
