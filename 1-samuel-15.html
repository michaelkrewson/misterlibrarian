Samuel sends Saul to devote Amalek to destruction. Saul musters the army at Telaim, warns the Kenites away, and strikes Amalek from Havilah toward Shur, but spares King Agag and the best of the flocks. Jehovah tells Samuel he regrets making Saul king; Samuel confronts Saul at Gilgal (“to listen is better than sacrifice”), rejects his plea, and the robe tears as he turns to go. Samuel hews Agag in pieces at Gilgal, goes home to Ramah, and does not see Saul again.
Some time after the wars of 1 Samuel 14; the confrontation at Gilgal falls on the morning after Samuel’s night of prayer (1 Samuel 15:11–12).

And Samuel said to Saul: ‘It was I whom Jehovah sent to anoint you as king over his people, over Israel; and now, listen to the voice of the words of Jehovah.note
Y Samuel dijo a Saúl: «A mí me envió Jehová para ungirte por rey sobre su pueblo, sobre Israel; ahora, pues, escucha la voz de las palabras de Jehová.
2
כֹּה אָמַר, יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, פָּקַדְתִּי, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה עֲמָלֵק לְיִשְׂרָאֵל--אֲשֶׁר-שָׂם לוֹ בַּדֶּרֶךְ, בַּעֲלֹתוֹ מִמִּצְרָיִם.
Thus says Jehovah of armies: I have marked what Amalek did to Israel, how he set himself against him on the road when he came up from Egypt.note
Así dice Jehová de los ejércitos: He tomado en cuenta lo que Amalec hizo a Israel, cómo se le puso en contra en el camino cuando subía de Egipto.
3
עַתָּה לֵךְ וְהִכִּיתָה אֶת-עֲמָלֵק, וְהַחֲרַמְתֶּם אֶת-כָּל-אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ, וְלֹא תַחְמֹל, עָלָיו; וְהֵמַתָּה מֵאִישׁ עַד-אִשָּׁה, מֵעֹלֵל וְעַד-יוֹנֵק, מִשּׁוֹר וְעַד-שֶׂה, מִגָּמָל וְעַד-חֲמוֹר.  {ס}
Now go, and strike Amalek, and devote to destruction all that he has, and do not spare him; but put to death from man to woman, from child to suckling, from ox to sheep, from camel to donkey.’note
Ahora ve, y hiere a Amalec, y consagren a la destrucción todo lo que tiene, y no lo perdones; sino da muerte desde el hombre hasta la mujer, desde el niño hasta el de pecho, desde el buey hasta la oveja, desde el camello hasta el asno».
4
וַיְשַׁמַּע שָׁאוּל, אֶת-הָעָם, וַיִּפְקְדֵם בַּטְּלָאִים, מָאתַיִם אֶלֶף רַגְלִי; וַעֲשֶׂרֶת אֲלָפִים, אֶת-אִישׁ יְהוּדָה.
And Saul summoned the people, and mustered them at Telaim: two hundred thousand on foot, and ten thousand, the men of Judah.note
Y Saúl convocó al pueblo, y les pasó revista en Telaim: doscientos mil de a pie, y diez mil, los hombres de Judá.
5
וַיָּבֹא שָׁאוּל, עַד-עִיר עֲמָלֵק; וַיָּרֶב, בַּנָּחַל.
And Saul came as far as the city of Amalek, and lay in wait in the wadi.note
Y Saúl llegó hasta la ciudad de Amalec, y puso emboscada en el torrente.
6
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-הַקֵּינִי לְכוּ סֻּרוּ רְדוּ מִתּוֹךְ עֲמָלֵקִי, פֶּן-אֹסִפְךָ עִמּוֹ, וְאַתָּה עָשִׂיתָה חֶסֶד עִם-כָּל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, בַּעֲלוֹתָם מִמִּצְרָיִם; וַיָּסַר קֵינִי, מִתּוֹךְ עֲמָלֵק.
And Saul said to the Kenite: ‘Go, turn aside, go down from among the Amalekite, lest I gather you up with him; for you did kindness with all the sons of Israel when they came up from Egypt.’ So the Kenite turned aside from among Amalek.note
Y Saúl dijo al ceneo: «Váyanse, apártense, desciendan de en medio del amalecita, no sea que te recoja junto con él; porque tú hiciste bondad con todos los hijos de Israel cuando subían de Egipto». Y el ceneo se apartó de en medio de Amalec.
7
וַיַּךְ שָׁאוּל, אֶת-עֲמָלֵק, מֵחֲוִילָה בּוֹאֲךָ שׁוּר, אֲשֶׁר עַל-פְּנֵי מִצְרָיִם.
And Saul struck Amalek from Havilah as you come to Shur, which faces Egypt.note
Y Saúl hirió a Amalec desde Havila según se va a Shur, que está frente a Egipto.
8
וַיִּתְפֹּשׂ אֶת-אֲגַג מֶלֶךְ-עֲמָלֵק, חָי; וְאֶת-כָּל-הָעָם, הֶחֱרִים לְפִי-חָרֶב.
And he seized Agag king of Amalek alive, and all the people he devoted to destruction with the edge of the sword.note
Y apresó vivo a Agag rey de Amalec, y a todo el pueblo lo consagró a la destrucción a filo de espada.
9
וַיַּחְמֹל שָׁאוּל וְהָעָם עַל-אֲגָג, וְעַל-מֵיטַב הַצֹּאן וְהַבָּקָר וְהַמִּשְׁנִים וְעַל-הַכָּרִים וְעַל-כָּל-הַטּוֹב, וְלֹא אָבוּ, הַחֲרִימָם; וְכָל-הַמְּלָאכָה נְמִבְזָה וְנָמֵס, אֹתָהּ הֶחֱרִימוּ.  {פ}
But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the flock and the herd, and the second-born, and the lambs, and all that was good, and they were not willing to devote them to destruction; but all the goods that were despised and worthless, those they devoted to destruction.note
Pero Saúl y el pueblo perdonaron a Agag, y a lo mejor de las ovejas y de las vacas, y a los de segunda cría, y a los corderos, y a todo lo bueno, y no quisieron consagrarlos a la destrucción; pero todos los bienes que eran despreciables y sin valor, esos los consagraron a la destrucción.
10
וַיְהִי, דְּבַר-יְהוָה, אֶל-שְׁמוּאֵל, לֵאמֹר.
And the word of Jehovah came to Samuel, saying:note
Y vino la palabra de Jehová a Samuel, diciendo:
11
נִחַמְתִּי, כִּי-הִמְלַכְתִּי אֶת-שָׁאוּל לְמֶלֶךְ--כִּי-שָׁב מֵאַחֲרַי, וְאֶת-דְּבָרַי לֹא הֵקִים; וַיִּחַר, לִשְׁמוּאֵל, וַיִּזְעַק אֶל-יְהוָה, כָּל-הַלָּיְלָה.
‘I regret that I made Saul king, for he has turned back from following me, and has not carried out my words.’ And Samuel was angry, and he cried out to Jehovah all night.note
«Me arrepiento de haber hecho rey a Saúl, porque se ha vuelto de en pos de mí, y no ha cumplido mis palabras». Y Samuel se enojó, y clamó a Jehová toda la noche.
12
וַיַּשְׁכֵּם שְׁמוּאֵל לִקְרַאת שָׁאוּל, בַּבֹּקֶר; וַיֻּגַּד לִשְׁמוּאֵל לֵאמֹר, בָּא-שָׁאוּל הַכַּרְמֶלָה וְהִנֵּה מַצִּיב לוֹ יָד, וַיִּסֹּב וַיַּעֲבֹר, וַיֵּרֶד הַגִּלְגָּל.
And Samuel rose early to meet Saul in the morning; and it was told Samuel, saying: ‘Saul came to Carmel, and look — he is setting up a monument for himself; and he turned and passed on and went down to Gilgal.’note
Y Samuel madrugó para ir al encuentro de Saúl por la mañana; y se le avisó a Samuel, diciendo: «Saúl vino a Carmel, y mira, se está levantando un monumento; y dio la vuelta y pasó, y descendió a Gilgal».
13
וַיָּבֹא שְׁמוּאֵל, אֶל-שָׁאוּל; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ שָׁאוּל, בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה לַיהוָה--הֲקִימֹתִי, אֶת-דְּבַר יְהוָה.
And Samuel came to Saul; and Saul said to him: ‘Blessed are you of Jehovah! I have carried out the word of Jehovah.’note
Y Samuel llegó a Saúl; y Saúl le dijo: «¡Bendito seas tú de Jehová! He cumplido la palabra de Jehová».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל, וּמֶה קוֹל-הַצֹּאן הַזֶּה בְּאָזְנָי, וְקוֹל הַבָּקָר, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי שֹׁמֵעַ.
And Samuel said: ‘Then what is this voice of the flock in my ears, and the voice of the herd that I hear?’note
Y Samuel dijo: «¿Qué es, pues, esta voz de las ovejas en mis oídos, y la voz de las vacas que yo oigo?».
15
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל מֵעֲמָלֵקִי הֱבִיאוּם, אֲשֶׁר חָמַל הָעָם עַל-מֵיטַב הַצֹּאן וְהַבָּקָר, לְמַעַן זְבֹחַ, לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ; וְאֶת-הַיּוֹתֵר, הֶחֱרַמְנוּ.  {פ}
And Saul said: ‘From the Amalekite they have brought them; for the people spared the best of the flock and the herd, to sacrifice to Jehovah your God; and the rest we have devoted to destruction.’note
Y Saúl dijo: «Del amalecita los han traído; porque el pueblo perdonó lo mejor de las ovejas y de las vacas, para sacrificarlo a Jehová tu Dios; y lo demás lo hemos consagrado a la destrucción».
16
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל, אֶל-שָׁאוּל, הֶרֶף וְאַגִּידָה לְּךָ, אֵת אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֵלַי הַלָּיְלָה; ויאמרו וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, דַּבֵּר.  {ס}
And Samuel said to Saul: ‘Stop, and let me tell you what Jehovah spoke to me this night.’ And he said to him: ‘Speak.’note
Y Samuel dijo a Saúl: «Detente, y te declararé lo que Jehová me ha dicho esta noche». Y él le dijo: «Habla».
17
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל--הֲלוֹא אִם-קָטֹן אַתָּה בְּעֵינֶיךָ, רֹאשׁ שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל אָתָּה; וַיִּמְשָׁחֲךָ יְהוָה לְמֶלֶךְ, עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And Samuel said: ‘Though you are small in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel? And Jehovah anointed you king over Israel,note
Y Samuel dijo: «Aunque seas pequeño a tus propios ojos, ¿no eres tú cabeza de las tribus de Israel? Y Jehová te ungió por rey sobre Israel,
18
וַיִּשְׁלָחֲךָ יְהוָה, בְּדָרֶךְ; וַיֹּאמֶר, לֵךְ וְהַחֲרַמְתָּה אֶת-הַחַטָּאִים אֶת-עֲמָלֵק, וְנִלְחַמְתָּ בוֹ, עַד כַּלּוֹתָם אֹתָם.
and Jehovah sent you on a journey, and said, Go, and devote to destruction the sinners, Amalek, and fight against them until you have made an end of them.note
y Jehová te envió en camino, y dijo: Ve, y consagra a la destrucción a los pecadores, a Amalec, y pelea contra ellos hasta que acabes con ellos.
19
וְלָמָּה לֹא-שָׁמַעְתָּ, בְּקוֹל יְהוָה; וַתַּעַט, אֶל-הַשָּׁלָל, וַתַּעַשׂ הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.  {ס}
Why then did you not listen to the voice of Jehovah, but swooped on the spoil, and did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah?’note
¿Por qué, pues, no escuchaste la voz de Jehová, sino que te lanzaste sobre el botín, e hiciste lo malo a los ojos de Jehová?».
20
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-שְׁמוּאֵל, אֲשֶׁר שָׁמַעְתִּי בְּקוֹל יְהוָה, וָאֵלֵךְ, בַּדֶּרֶךְ אֲשֶׁר-שְׁלָחַנִי יְהוָה; וָאָבִיא, אֶת-אֲגַג מֶלֶךְ עֲמָלֵק, וְאֶת-עֲמָלֵק, הֶחֱרַמְתִּי.
And Saul said to Samuel: ‘But I did listen to the voice of Jehovah, and I went on the journey on which Jehovah sent me, and I have brought Agag king of Amalek, and Amalek I have devoted to destruction.note
Y Saúl dijo a Samuel: «¡Pero sí escuché la voz de Jehová, y fui por el camino por el que Jehová me envió, y he traído a Agag rey de Amalec, y a Amalec lo he consagrado a la destrucción!
21
וַיִּקַּח הָעָם מֵהַשָּׁלָל צֹאן וּבָקָר, רֵאשִׁית הַחֵרֶם, לִזְבֹּחַ לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, בַּגִּלְגָּל.  {ס}
But the people took from the spoil flock and herd, the first of the devoted things, to sacrifice to Jehovah your God at Gilgal.’note
Pero el pueblo tomó del botín ovejas y vacas, lo primero de lo consagrado, para sacrificarlo a Jehová tu Dios en Gilgal».
22
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל, הַחֵפֶץ לַיהוָה בְּעֹלוֹת וּזְבָחִים, כִּשְׁמֹעַ, בְּקוֹל יְהוָה:  הִנֵּה שְׁמֹעַ מִזֶּבַח טוֹב, לְהַקְשִׁיב מֵחֵלֶב אֵילִים.
And Samuel said: ‘Has Jehovah as much delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as in listening to the voice of Jehovah? Look — to listen is better than sacrifice, to pay heed than the fat of rams.note
Y Samuel dijo: «¿Se complace Jehová en holocaustos y sacrificios tanto como en que se escuche la voz de Jehová? Mira: escuchar es mejor que el sacrificio, y prestar atención, que la grasa de los carneros.
23
כִּי חַטַּאת-קֶסֶם מֶרִי, וְאָוֶן וּתְרָפִים הַפְצַר:  יַעַן, מָאַסְתָּ אֶת-דְּבַר יְהוָה, וַיִּמְאָסְךָ, מִמֶּלֶךְ.  {ס}
For rebellion is the sin of divination, and presumption is iniquity and teraphim. Because you have rejected the word of Jehovah, he has rejected you from being king.’note
Porque la rebelión es pecado de adivinación, y la porfía es iniquidad y terafim. Por cuanto has rechazado la palabra de Jehová, él te ha rechazado para que no seas rey».
24
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-שְׁמוּאֵל חָטָאתִי, כִּי-עָבַרְתִּי אֶת-פִּי-יְהוָה וְאֶת-דְּבָרֶיךָ:  כִּי יָרֵאתִי אֶת-הָעָם, וָאֶשְׁמַע בְּקוֹלָם.
And Saul said to Samuel: ‘I have sinned; for I have crossed the mouth of Jehovah and your words, because I feared the people and listened to their voice.note
Y Saúl dijo a Samuel: «He pecado; porque he traspasado la boca de Jehová y tus palabras, porque temí al pueblo y escuché su voz.
25
וְעַתָּה, שָׂא נָא אֶת-חַטָּאתִי; וְשׁוּב עִמִּי, וְאֶשְׁתַּחֲוֶה לַיהוָה.
And now, please, lift up my sin, and return with me, and I will worship Jehovah.’note
Y ahora, por favor, perdona mi pecado, y vuelve conmigo, y adoraré a Jehová».
26
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל אֶל-שָׁאוּל, לֹא אָשׁוּב עִמָּךְ:  כִּי מָאַסְתָּה, אֶת-דְּבַר יְהוָה, וַיִּמְאָסְךָ יְהוָה, מִהְיוֹת מֶלֶךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {ס}
And Samuel said to Saul: ‘I will not return with you; for you have rejected the word of Jehovah, and Jehovah has rejected you from being king over Israel.’note
Y Samuel dijo a Saúl: «No volveré contigo; porque has rechazado la palabra de Jehová, y Jehová te ha rechazado para que no seas rey sobre Israel».
27
וַיִּסֹּב שְׁמוּאֵל, לָלֶכֶת; וַיַּחֲזֵק בִּכְנַף-מְעִילוֹ, וַיִּקָּרַע.  {ס}
And Samuel turned to go, and he seized the corner of his robe, and it tore.note
Y Samuel se volvió para irse, y él se asió del borde de su manto, y se rasgó.
28
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, שְׁמוּאֵל, קָרַע יְהוָה אֶת-מַמְלְכוּת יִשְׂרָאֵל מֵעָלֶיךָ, הַיּוֹם; וּנְתָנָהּ, לְרֵעֲךָ הַטּוֹב מִמֶּךָּ.  {ס}
And Samuel said to him: ‘Jehovah has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbour of yours who is better than you.note
Y Samuel le dijo: «Jehová ha rasgado hoy de ti el reino de Israel, y lo ha dado a un prójimo tuyo, mejor que tú.
29
וְגַם נֵצַח יִשְׂרָאֵל, לֹא יְשַׁקֵּר וְלֹא יִנָּחֵם:  כִּי לֹא אָדָם הוּא, לְהִנָּחֵם.
And also the Everlasting One of Israel will not lie and will not regret; for he is not a human, that he should regret.’note
Y además, el Eterno de Israel no mentirá ni se arrepentirá; porque no es un ser humano para arrepentirse».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · The ban on Amalek — “I have marked what Amalek did”, devoting to destruction, and “do not spare him”

Listen to the voice. Samuel opens with the verb the whole chapter turns on: “listen to the voice of the words of Jehovah”. Shama with “the voice of” is the Hebrew way of saying obey, and it comes back at verses 19, 20, 22 and 24, where Saul says whose voice he did listen to. Samuel had been told to “listen to the voice of the people” when they asked for a king (8:7, now on these pages); Saul is told to listen to another voice. The NWT 1984 keeps it: “listen to the voice of the words of Jehovah”; the ASV “hearken thou unto the voice of the words of Jehovah”; the NIV “listen now to the message from the Lord”.

I have marked what Amalek did. “I have marked what Amalek did to Israel, how he set himself against him on the road when he came up from Egypt”: paqadti, the verb of visiting and reckoning, the same that musters the army in verse 4. It is the old account of Exodus 17:8, when “Amalek came and fought with Israel at Rephidim”, and of the oath that followed, “Jehovah will have war with Amalek from generation to generation” (Exodus 17:16). Deuteronomy 25:17–19 had told Israel to remember it and, once given rest from its enemies, to “blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven” (all now on these pages). Josephus has Samuel say that Amalek did Israel much harm in the wilderness (Antiquities 6.132, numbered 6.7.1 in Whiston). The ASV has “I have marked that which Amalek did to Israel”; the NWT 1984 “I must call to account what Amalek did”; the KJV “I remember that which Amalek did”.

Devote to destruction. “Strike Amalek, and devote to destruction all that he has”: the cherem verb, the ban by which a thing is given to God by being placed beyond human use, so that in war it is destroyed and not plundered (Leviticus 27:28–29; Joshua 6:21, both now on these pages). The singular commands to Saul (“go”, “strike”, “do not spare”) break once into the plural, ve-hacharamtem, “and you shall devote”; this form of the verb stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Rahab’s report of what Israel did to Sihon and Og (Joshua 2:10, now on these pages). The list “from man to woman, from child to suckling, from ox to sheep, from camel to donkey” is the list of Jericho, “both man and woman, young and old, ox and sheep and donkey” (Joshua 6:21), with the camel of a desert people added. The NWT 1984 has “devote him to destruction with all that he has”; the ASV “utterly destroy all that they have”; the NIV “totally destroy all that belongs to them”.

Do not spare him. “And do not spare him”: chamal, to spare or to pity. The chapter turns on it, for in verse 9 “Saul and the people spared Agag” and in verse 15 “the people spared the best of the flock”. Josephus says the order was to spare neither women nor infants nor beasts, but to give everything to God (Antiquities 6.133). This command has troubled readers in every age, and the chapter records it without softening; Josephus himself pauses to say Saul thought nothing he did cruel, since the dead were enemies and the order was God’s (Antiquities 6.136, numbered 6.7.2 in Whiston). The ASV has “and spare them not”; the NWT 1984 “you must not have compassion upon him”; the Douay adds from the Latin “nor covet any thing that is his”.

Verses 4–9 · Telaim, the Kenites and Agag — the muster, Havilah to Shur, and the best of the flock spared

Summoned and mustered at Telaim. “Saul summoned the people”: vayshamma, “made the people hear”, from the same verb as “listen” in verse 1. He “mustered them”, vayyifqedem, the verb of “I have marked” in verse 2. Telaim means “lambs”, the word of Isaiah’s shepherd who “will gather the lambs in his arm” (Isaiah 40:11, now on these pages), and the Douay, following the Latin, reads it so: “numbered them as lambs”. Many identify it with Telem among Judah’s southern towns (Joshua 15:24, now on these pages); that is a proposal, not a finding. The Greek has “Galgala”, Gilgal, and four hundred thousand with thirty thousand of Judah for the Hebrew’s two hundred thousand and ten thousand; Josephus has the same Gilgal and the same larger numbers (Antiquities 6.134). The ASV has “numbered them in Telaim, two hundred thousand footmen”; the NWT 1984 “took the count of them in Telaim”.

The Kenite and the kindness. “Go, turn aside, go down from among the Amalekite, lest I gather you up with him”: the commands are plural and the rest is singular, the Kenite as one people. The Kenites are the family of Moses’ father-in-law, who went up “with the sons of Judah” (Judges 1:16, now on these pages). “For you did kindness with all the sons of Israel”: chesed. The text does not say which kindness; the nearest are Jethro’s counsel (Exodus 18:19) and Hobab’s guiding, “you shall be for us as eyes” (Numbers 10:31, both now on these pages). Josephus spares them as kin of Raguel, Moses’ father-in-law, and places them in the middle of Midian (Antiquities 6.140, numbered 6.7.3 in Whiston). The ASV has “for ye showed kindness to all the children of Israel”; the NWT 1984 “you exercised loving-kindness with all the sons of Israel”.

From Havilah to Shur. “From Havilah as you come to Shur, which faces Egypt” is the range of Ishmael’s sons, “from Havilah to Shur, which faces Egypt” (Genesis 25:18, now on these pages), a desert span and not a single battlefield. The chapter itself does not claim the end of Amalek: Amalekites raid again in David’s day (1 Samuel 27:8, now on these pages; 30:1), and an Amalekite brings David the news of Saul’s death (2 Samuel 1:8, now on these pages). Josephus makes it “from Pelusium of Egypt to the Red Sea” (Antiquities 6.140). The ASV has “from Havilah as thou goest to Shur, that is before Egypt”; the NWT 1984 “from Havilah as far as Shur, which is in front of Egypt”.

The second-born and the despised. They spared “Agag, and the best of the flock and the herd, and the second-born, and the lambs”. Ha-mishnim is “the second ones”; the old Jewish reading takes them as animals of a second birth, reckoned the better; many modern readers move two letters and read ha-shmenim, “the fat ones”, which is where “fatlings” comes from. The word stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, and in the other, 1 Chronicles 15:18, it names Levites “of the second rank”. What they destroyed was “all the goods that were despised and worthless”: nemibzah, a form found nowhere else, and names, “melting away”. Josephus says Saul spared Agag for his beauty and his size (Antiquities 6.137). The ASV has “the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs”; the NWT 1984 “the fat ones and upon the rams”; the NIV “the fat calves and lambs”.

Verses 10–16 · “I regret that I made Saul king” — Samuel’s night, the monument at Carmel, and the voice of the flock

I regret. “I regret that I made Saul king”: nichamti, the verb of Genesis 6:6, “Jehovah regretted that he had made humankind on the earth”, in the same shape, nacham followed by “that I made” (now on these pages). “He has turned back from following me, and has not carried out my words”: lo heqim, the verb Saul will use of himself in verse 13. The verb returns at verses 29 and 35, and the note at verse 29 takes the three together. The NWT 1984 has “I do regret that I have caused Saul to reign as king”; the ASV “It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king”; the NIV “I regret that I have made Saul king”.

Samuel was angry. “And Samuel was angry, and he cried out to Jehovah all night”: vayyichar li-Shmuel, “it burned to Samuel”, the idiom of Cain, who “became very angry” (Genesis 4:5, now on these pages). The Hebrew does not say at whom; the shelf divides between anger and grief. Josephus says Samuel was deeply troubled and spent the night begging God to be reconciled to Saul (Antiquities 6.143, numbered 6.7.4 in Whiston). The ASV has “Samuel was wroth”; the KJV “it grieved Samuel”; the NWT 1984 “it was distressing to Samuel”.

A monument at Carmel. “Saul came to Carmel, and look — he is setting up a monument for himself”: the Hebrew says yad, “a hand”. Absalom later “set up for himself” a pillar, “and it is called Absalom’s hand to this day” (2 Samuel 18:18). This Carmel is the town of Judah’s hill country listed with Maon and Ziph (Joshua 15:55, now on these pages), not the mountain by the sea. The Greek adds that Samuel came to Gilgal and found Saul offering a burnt offering from the first of the spoils, and the Douay keeps the addition from the Latin: “Saul was offering a holocaust to the Lord out of the choicest of the spoils”. The ASV has “he set him up a monument”; the KJV “he set him up a place”; the NIV “a monument in his own honor”.

The voice of the flock. “Blessed are you of Jehovah! I have carried out the word of Jehovah.” “Then what is this voice of the flock in my ears, and the voice of the herd that I hear?” The word for the bleating is qol, the “voice” Saul was told to listen to in verse 1, and Samuel hears the wrong one. “Stop, and let me tell you”: the written text has “and they said”, read “and he said” (ketiv vayyomeru, qere vayyomer). Josephus has Saul run to Samuel, embrace him and give thanks for the victory (Antiquities 6.145). The NWT 1984 has “what does this sound of the flock in my ears mean”; the ASV “What meaneth then this bleating of the sheep in mine ears”; the TLB “what was all the bleating of sheep”.

Verses 17–23 · “To listen is better than sacrifice” — small in your own eyes, the first of the devoted things, and rebellion as divination

Small in your own eyes. “Though you are small in your own eyes, are you not the head of the tribes of Israel?”: Saul had said “Am I not a Benjamite, from the smallest of the tribes of Israel?” (9:21) and had hidden among the baggage (10:22, both now on these pages). “Go, and devote to destruction the sinners, Amalek”: ha-chatta’im, the word Saul will use of himself twice, “I have sinned” (verses 24, 30). The Greek has “those who sin against me”. The ASV has “Go, and utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites”; the NIV “those wicked people, the Amalekites”; the NWT 1984 “devote the sinners, the Amalekites, to destruction”.

Swooped on the spoil. “Why then did you not listen to the voice of Jehovah, but swooped on the spoil”: vatta’at, from ‘ayit, a bird of prey. It is what the hungry army did after Michmash, “the people swooped on the spoil” (14:32, now on these pages). Saul answers “But I did listen to the voice of Jehovah”, and lays the sheep on the people: “the first of the devoted things”, reshit ha-cherem, the word for firstfruits joined to the word for what may not be used. In the Greek, verse 20 begins the other way, “because I listened to the voice of the people”. The ASV has “didst fly upon the spoil” and “the chief of the devoted things”; the NWT 1984 “went darting greedily at the spoil”; the KJV “the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed”.

To listen is better than sacrifice. “Has Jehovah as much delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices as in listening to the voice of Jehovah? Look — to listen is better than sacrifice, to pay heed than the fat of rams.” “Obey” in the familiar wording is the same shama, “listen”, that has run through the chapter. “To listen is better than sacrifice” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, but the thought returns: “you do not want sacrifice, or I would give it” (Psalm 51:18), “I have had my fill of burnt offerings of rams” (Isaiah 1:11), and the scribe who tells Jesus that to love God and neighbour “is more than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices” (Mark 12:33); Jesus twice quotes “I desire mercy, and not sacrifice” (Matthew 9:13; 12:7, all now on these pages), from Hosea 6:6. Josephus turns it into a speech: God is pleased not with sacrifices but with the good and the just (Antiquities 6.147). The ASV has “Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams”; the NWT 1984 “To obey is better than a sacrifice, to pay attention than the fat of rams”.

Rebellion and divination. “For rebellion is the sin of divination, and presumption is iniquity and teraphim”: the Hebrew has no “as”, only the words set side by side. Meri, rebellion, is the root of “rebel against the mouth of Jehovah” (12:14–15); qesem is divination; teraphim are the household images of Rachel and of Micah (Genesis 31:19; Judges 17:5, all now on these pages). Haftsar, presumption, is from patsar, to press or urge, the verb of Lot pressing the strangers to come in (Genesis 19:3, now on these pages); this causative form stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. “Because you have rejected the word of Jehovah, he has rejected you from being king”: ma’as, the verb of 8:7, “me they have rejected from being king over them”. The Greek reads quite otherwise, “for sin is divination; teraphim bring pain and toil”. The ASV has “stubbornness is as idolatry and teraphim”; the NWT 1984 “pushing ahead presumptuously the same as [using] uncanny power and teraphim”; the TLB “because you have rejected the word of Jehovah”.

Verses 24–31 · “I have sinned” — lift up my sin, the torn robe, and the Everlasting One of Israel who will not regret

Crossed the mouth of Jehovah. “I have sinned; for I have crossed the mouth of Jehovah and your words, because I feared the people and listened to their voice”: avarti et-pi YHVH, the phrase Moses used to the people who went up against his word, “why are you crossing the mouth of Jehovah?” (Numbers 14:41, now on these pages). Saul names the voice he listened to. Josephus has him say he did not stop the soldiers from plundering out of fear of them (Antiquities 6.151). The ASV has “I have transgressed the commandment of Jehovah, and thy words”; the NWT 1984 “I have overstepped the order of Jehovah and your words”.

Lift up my sin. “And now, please, lift up my sin”: sa na et-chattati, to forgive is to lift and carry away. Pharaoh had asked Moses the same, “And now, lift up my sin just this once” (Exodus 10:17, now on these pages), with the same three words and no et. Pharaoh’s request was granted; Saul’s is refused: “I will not return with you”. The ASV has “I pray thee, pardon my sin, and turn again with me”; the Douay “bear, I beseech thee, my sin”; the NWT 1984 “please, pardon my sin and return with me”.

The torn robe. “And Samuel turned to go, and he seized the corner of his robe, and it tore”: the Hebrew does not name who seized it. The grammar allows Saul seizing Samuel’s robe to hold him back, or Samuel seizing Saul’s. The Greek names Saul, and so do Josephus (Antiquities 6.152, numbered 6.7.5 in Whiston) and the Latin caption of the picture on this page. The robe, me’il, is the garment Hannah made for the boy year by year (2:19, now on these pages). “Jehovah has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbour of yours who is better than you”: the neighbour is unnamed, the “man after his own heart” of 13:14. The ASV supplies the name: “Saul laid hold upon the skirt of his robe”; the KJV keeps the Hebrew’s “he laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle”; the NWT 1984 “he immediately grabbed hold of the skirt of his sleeveless coat”.

The Everlasting One will not regret. “And also the Everlasting One of Israel will not lie and will not regret; for he is not a human, that he should regret.” Netsach is the word of la-netsach, “for ever”, and as a title of God it stands only here: “the netsach of Israel” is in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The argument is Balaam’s, “God is not a man, that he should lie, nor a son of man, that he should relent” (Numbers 23:19, now on these pages), with a different verb for “lie” and the same verb nacham. And the same chapter says twice, at verse 11 and verse 35, that Jehovah did regret making Saul king. The text does not resolve this, and the translation keeps one English word through all three verses so that the reader sees it. “Regret” is the word because verses 11 and 35 have the construction of Genesis 6:6, “regretted that he had made”; at Numbers 23:19, where nothing has been done to regret, the same verb is “relent”. Josephus explains it as God holding to his decisions, since changing one’s mind is a human feeling and not divine strength (Antiquities 6.153). The Greek reads “and Israel will be divided in two”. The ASV has “the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent”; the NIV “He who is the Glory of Israel does not lie or change his mind”; the NWT 1984 “the Excellency of Israel will not prove false, and He will not feel regrets”.

Honour me before the elders. “I have sinned; now honour me, please, before the elders of my people and before Israel, and return with me, and I will worship Jehovah your God”: three times Saul says “your God” to Samuel (verses 15, 21, 30). Samuel returns, and “Saul worshiped Jehovah”, the verb of Elkanah at Shiloh (1:3, now on these pages). The ASV has “honor me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people”; the NIV “please honor me before the elders of my people”; the TLB “at least honor me before the leaders”.

Verses 32–33 · Agag in fetters — “the bitterness of death has turned aside”, and Samuel at Gilgal

In fetters, or cheerfully. “And Agag came to him in fetters”: ma’adannot. The same letters, spelled in full, stand in one other verse of the Hebrew Bible, Job 38:31, “Can you bind the fetters of the Pleiades?”, and the translation reads Agag’s word so. The same consonants also make “delights”, as in Asher’s “royal delicacies” (Genesis 49:20, now on these pages), and so the older shelf has Agag come “delicately” or “cheerfully”. The Greek has “trembling”; the picture on this page shows him with his ankles chained. The NIV has “Agag came to him in chains”; the ASV “Agag came unto him cheerfully”; the KJV “delicately”; the NWT 1984 “reluctantly”; the Douay “very fat, and trembling”.

The bitterness of death. “Surely the bitterness of death has turned aside”: sar, has turned away, which a man in fetters could say either in hope or in bitter irony. The Greek makes it a question, “Is death thus bitter?”, and Josephus follows the question (Antiquities 6.155). The ASV has “Surely the bitterness of death is past”; the NWT 1984 “Truly the bitter experience of death has departed”; the Douay “Doth bitter death separate in this manner?”.

Childless among women. “As your sword has made women childless, so shall your mother be childless among women”: shakal, the verb of Jacob’s “if I am bereaved, I am bereaved” (Genesis 43:14, now on these pages). “And Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before Jehovah at Gilgal”: vayshassef, and the verb stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, so its exact sense is known only from this scene; the Greek has “slaughtered”. “Before Jehovah at Gilgal” is the place where the kingship was renewed (11:15, now on these pages). Josephus has Samuel order Agag put to death at once at Gilgal (Antiquities 6.155). The ASV has “Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before Jehovah in Gilgal”; the NWT 1984 “Samuel went hacking Agag to pieces before Jehovah”; the NIV “Samuel put Agag to death”.

Verses 34–35 · Ramah and Gibeah of Saul — Samuel mourns, and Jehovah regretted

Two houses. “And Samuel went to Ramah; and Saul went up to his house at Gibeah of Saul”: Samuel to the town where his house was (7:17), Saul to the town the messengers came to from Jabesh (11:4, both now on these pages). Josephus says Gibeah means “hill” (Antiquities 6.156, numbered 6.8.1 in Whiston). The ASV has “Saul went up to his house to Gibeah of Saul”; the NIV “to his home in Gibeah of Saul”.

Samuel mourned, and Jehovah regretted. “And Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death, for Samuel mourned for Saul; and Jehovah regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel.” The verse closes the chapter on the verb it opened with at verse 11. The two men do meet once more, when Saul prophesies before Samuel at Naioth (1 Samuel 19:24, now on these pages); “see” here may mean seek out. The ASV has “for Samuel mourned for Saul: and Jehovah repented”; the KJV “nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul”; the NWT 1984 “As for Jehovah, he regretted that he had made Saul king over Israel”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “listen to the voice” for shama be-qol through the whole chapter, and “to listen is better than sacrifice”; “I have marked” for paqad, as quoted in Deuteronomy 25; “devote to destruction” for the cherem verb, as in Joshua 2:10; “spare” for chamal; “kindness” for chesed; “the second-born” for mishnim; “regret” for nacham in verses 11, 29 and 35 alike, as in Genesis 6:6, where Numbers 23:19 has “relent”; “a monument” for yad; “the voice of the flock”; “swooped on the spoil”, as in 14:32; “crossed the mouth of Jehovah”, as in Numbers 14:41; “lift up my sin”, as in Exodus 10:17; “robe” for me’il, as in 2:19; “the Everlasting One of Israel” for netsach; and “in fetters” for ma’adannot.

Echoes paid. The war with Amalek of Exodus 17 and the charge of Deuteronomy 25 come due; Agag of Numbers 24:7 appears; the Kenites of Judges 1:16 are spared; Saul who was small in his own eyes (9:21) is head of the tribes; the people who swooped on the spoil at 14:32 do it again; “they have rejected me from being king” (8:7) is answered; and the kingdom lost at 13:14 is torn away.

Echoes planted. “How long will you mourn for Saul?” (1 Samuel 16:1, now on these pages); Michal’s teraphim (1 Samuel 19:13); Saul before Samuel at Naioth (1 Samuel 19:24, both now on these pages); David cutting the corner of Saul’s robe (1 Samuel 24, now on these pages); the Amalekites again (1 Samuel 27, now on these pages, and 30); Samuel known by his robe and Amalek named at En-dor (1 Samuel 28:14, 18, now on these pages); and Haman the Agagite (Esther 3:1). None of the others is translated here yet.

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 16 — David anointed at Bethlehem: Samuel sent to Jesse’s sons, “Jehovah looks on the heart”, the youngest brought in from the sheep, the spirit rushing on David and leaving Saul, and David’s lyre in Saul’s court.
