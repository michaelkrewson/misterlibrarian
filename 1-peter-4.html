Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · “Arm yourselves” — the same way of thinking, and “ceased from sin”

“Since Christ, then, suffered in the flesh, you too arm yourselves with the same way of thinking.” The sentence picks up 1 Peter 3:18, “Christ also suffered once for sins … put to death in the flesh,” and the “Christ also suffered” of 1 Peter 2:21. The Byzantine text and the Received Text add “for us”; the KJV reads “Christ hath suffered for us in the flesh.” Hoplizō, “to arm,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary); its noun hopla is the “armor of light” of Romans 13:12. Ennoia, “way of thinking,” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is Hebrews 4:12, “the thoughts and intentions of the heart,” where this translation reads “intentions”; here the word is the thought Christ acted on, and the readers are to take it up as a weapon. The KJV and the ASV read “the same mind”; the NWT 1984 reads “the same mental disposition”; the NIV reads “the same attitude”; the Douay reads “the same thought.”

⚠ “Because the one who has suffered in the flesh has ceased from sin.” Who “the one who has suffered” is, the Greek does not say, and readings differ. On one reading it is Christ, whose dealing with sin is finished, as the “once” of 3:18 says. On another it is the believer: whoever has suffered in the flesh, or has shared Christ’s death, has broken with sin, as Romans 6:7 says, “the one who has died has been justified from sin.” On a third, suffering itself checks the one who suffers from going on in sin. This translation does not choose. Westcott and Hort print the plural “sins”; the NWT 1984 reads “has desisted from sins” and the Douay “hath ceased from sins”; the KJV reads “hath ceased from sin”; the NIV reads “is done with sin.”

“So as to live the rest of the time in the flesh no longer for human desires but for the will of God.” Bioō, “to live,” stands in one verse, and its noun biōsis is the “manner of life” of Acts 26:4 (dictionary). Epiloipos, “the rest,” also stands in one verse. “Desires” are the “desires you had before, in your ignorance” of 1 Peter 1:14 and the “fleshly desires” of 1 Peter 2:11 (dictionary); “the will of God” is the phrase of 1 Peter 2:15 and 3:17, and comes again at verse 19. The KJV reads “to the lusts of men”; the NWT 1984 reads “no more for the desires of men, but for God’s will”; the NIV reads “for evil human desires,” where “evil” is not in the Greek.

Verses 3–5 · Sensuality, revels, drinking parties — “surprised” at the change, and “the judge of the living and the dead”

“For the time that has gone by is enough for having done what the nations intend.” “The nations” are the Gentiles, and the readers’ own past is in this list: they were “ransomed from the futile way of life handed down from your fathers” (1 Peter 1:18). “What the nations intend” is boulēma, a plan or settled intention (dictionary). Later texts read thelēma, “will,” and add “of life” with a pronoun, which the Received Text has as “us”: the KJV reads “the time past of our life may suffice us to have wrought the will of the Gentiles.”

“Walking as you did in sensuality, desires, bouts of heavy drinking, revels, drinking parties, and unlawful idolatries.” “Sensuality” is aselgeia, as in Mark 7:22 and Galatians 5:19 (dictionary). Oinophlygia, “heavy drinking,” from oinos, “wine,” stands in one verse (dictionary). Potos, “drinking party,” also stands in one verse (dictionary). Kōmos, “revel,” stands in three verses (dictionary). The other two are Paul’s lists, “not in revels and drunkenness” at Romans 13:13 and “envy, drunkenness, revels” at Galatians 5:21. “Unlawful” is athemitos, the word Peter uses in Cornelius’s house at Acts 10:28, “how unlawful it is for a Jewish man to join himself to … one of another tribe” (dictionary). The KJV reads “lasciviousness, lusts, excess of wine, revellings, banquetings, and abominable idolatries”; the ASV reads “winebibbings, revellings, carousings”; the NWT 1984 reads “excesses with wine, revelries, drinking matches, and illegal idolatries”; the Douay reads “unlawful worshipping of idols.”

“In this they are surprised that you do not run with them into the same flood of debauchery, and they speak abusively.” “Are surprised” is xenizō, which stands in ten verses (dictionary). Most of the others are about a guest, as Peter “is a guest with a certain Simon” at Acts 10:6 and some “have entertained angels” at Hebrews 13:2; at Acts 17:20 it is “some strange things.” The old neighbors find the readers strange, and verse 12 turns the same verb round. Syntrechō, “run with,” stands in three verses, with Mark 6:33 and Acts 3:11. Anachysis, “flood,” literally a pouring out, stands in one verse (dictionary). Asōtia, “debauchery,” stands in three verses (dictionary), with “do not get drunk with wine, in which is debauchery” at Ephesians 5:18 and Titus 1:6. “They speak abusively” is blasphēmeō, the verb of “blaspheme,” with no object (dictionary): whether the abuse is aimed at the readers or at God, the Greek does not say. The shelf supplies the readers: the KJV reads “speaking evil of you,” the NWT 1984 “go on speaking abusively of YOU,” and the NIV “they heap abuse on you.”

“They will give account to the one who stands ready to judge the living and the dead.” “Give account” is the phrase of Matthew 12:36, “they will give an account of it on the day of judgment.” “The living and the dead” is said of Christ at Acts 10:42, “the one determined by God as judge of living and dead,” and at 2 Timothy 4:1; in this letter the one who judges is the Father at 1 Peter 1:17 and “the one who judges justly” at 1 Peter 2:23. Which one is meant here, the verse does not say. Westcott and Hort read “the one who judges.” The KJV reads “to judge the quick and the dead”; the ASV reads “to him that is ready to judge the living and the dead.”

Verse 6 · ⚠ “The good news was proclaimed also to the dead” — which dead, and when?

“For to this end the good news was proclaimed also to the dead, so that, though they are judged in the flesh according to human beings, they may live in the spirit according to God.” The verb is euangelizō, “to proclaim good news,” the verb of “those who preached the good news to you” at 1 Peter 1:12 (dictionary), not the kēryssō, “made proclamation,” of 3:19. It is passive, with no one named as the one who proclaimed. “For” ties the verse to verse 5: the one who judges is judge of the dead as well as of the living. The pairing “in the flesh … in the spirit” is the pairing of 3:18, “put to death in the flesh but made alive in the spirit,” and the same question, the human spirit or the Spirit of God, is open here.

⚠ Who “the dead” are, readings differ, and this translation does not choose. On one reading they are believers who heard the good news while they lived and have since died; their death was a judgment “in the flesh,” as all human beings die, and they live “in the spirit,” as God lives. The NIV prints this reading by adding a word, “to those who are now dead.” On another reading the verse goes with the spirits in prison of 3:19, and the dead heard the good news after death; the TLB reads “to those who were dead—killed by the flood.” On a third, “the dead” are the spiritually dead, as in “dead in your trespasses and your sins” at Ephesians 2:1. “According to human beings … according to God” can mean “in human eyes … in God’s eyes” or “as humans are … as God is.” The KJV reads “judged according to men in the flesh, but live according to God in the spirit”; the NWT 1984 reads “judged as to the flesh from the standpoint of men but might live as to the spirit from the standpoint of God.”

Verses 7–11 · “The end of all things has drawn near” — earnest love, “love covers a multitude of sins,” and stewards of God’s varied grace

“The end of all things has drawn near. So be sensible and be sober for prayers.” “Has drawn near” is the perfect of engizō, as in “the kingdom of God has drawn near” at Mark 1:15, “the day has drawn near” at Romans 13:12, and “the presence of the Lord has drawn near” at James 5:8. “End” is telos (dictionary), and it comes again at verse 17, “what will be the end of those who disobey.” “Be sensible” is sōphroneō, the healed man “in his right mind” at Mark 5:15 (dictionary); “be sober” is nēphō, as in “being fully sober” at 1 Peter 1:13 (dictionary). The KJV reads “the end of all things is at hand: be ye therefore sober, and watch unto prayer”; the NWT 1984 reads “has drawn close. Be sound in mind, therefore, and be vigilant with a view to prayers”; the NIV reads “be alert and of sober mind so that you may pray.”

“Above all, keep your love for one another earnest, because love covers a multitude of sins.” “Above all” is the “But above all” of James 5:12. The adjective ektenēs, “earnest,” stands in one verse (dictionary). Its adverb is the “love one another earnestly” of 1 Peter 1:22 and the prayer made “earnestly” for Peter in prison at Acts 12:5. “Love covers a multitude of sins” is the closing phrase of James 5:20, “will cover a multitude of sins,” and the Byzantine text has the same future here. Both recall the Hebrew of Proverbs 10:12, ve’al kol-pesha’im tekasseh ahavah, “love covers all transgressions”; the Greek of Proverbs reads “friendship covers all who are not quarrelsome” (both checked; Proverbs 10 yet to come in this translation). ⚠ Whose sins love covers, the sins of the one loved or of the one who loves, the sentence does not say, and this translation does not choose. The KJV reads “have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins”; the NWT 1984 reads “have intense love for one another”; the Douay reads “a constant mutual charity,” from the Latin continuam.

“Be hospitable to one another without grumbling. As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of the varied grace of God.” “Hospitable” is philoxenos, “a lover of strangers,” asked of the overseer at 1 Timothy 3:2 and Titus 1:8 (dictionary). “Without grumbling” is the “Do everything without grumblings” of Philippians 2:14, and gongysmos is the “grumbling” of the Hellenists at Acts 6:1 (dictionary); the Byzantine text has the plural. Charisma, “gift,” stands in seventeen verses (dictionary). Every other one is in the letters that carry Paul’s name, as “varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit” at 1 Corinthians 12:4. “Stewards” is oikonomos, the manager of a household, as in “stewards of God’s mysteries” at 1 Corinthians 4:1, where “what is looked for in stewards is that someone be found faithful” (dictionary). “Varied” is poikilos, the word of the “various trials” at 1 Peter 1:6: the grace comes in as many kinds as the trials. The KJV and the ASV read “the manifold grace of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “God’s undeserved kindness expressed in various ways”; the NIV reads “God’s grace in its various forms.”

“If anyone speaks, let it be as oracles of God; if anyone serves, let it be as from the strength that God supplies.” “Oracles” is logia, “the oracles of God” entrusted to the Jews at Romans 3:2 and the “first elements of the oracles of God” at Hebrews 5:12 (dictionary). Chorēgeō, “to supply,” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is 2 Corinthians 9:10, “the one who supplies seed to the sower … will supply.” The KJV reads “let him speak as the oracles of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “as it were [the] sacred pronouncements of God”; the NIV reads “as one who speaks the very words of God.”

⚠ “So that in all things God may be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom belong the glory and the might forever and ever. Amen.” “To whom” can go back to Jesus Christ, the nearest name, or to God, the subject of the sentence; readings differ, and this translation keeps the Greek order and does not choose. The same question stands at Hebrews 13:21, “through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory forever.” The Greek has a verb, estin, “is,” so the line says the glory belongs to him rather than asking that it be given. “Might” is kratos, as in “to him be honor and eternal might” at 1 Timothy 6:16 (dictionary). The KJV reads “to whom be praise and dominion”; the ASV reads “whose is the glory and the dominion”; the NWT 1984 begins a new sentence, “The glory and the might are his forever and ever”; the Douay reads “to whom is glory and empire.”

Verses 12–14 · “Do not be surprised at the burning among you” — sharing Christ’s sufferings, and “insulted for the name of Christ”

“Beloved, do not be surprised at the burning among you, which is coming upon you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you.” “Be surprised” is xenizō again, from verse 4, and “strange” is xenos, from the same root (dictionary): the neighbors find the readers strange, and the readers are not to find their trouble strange. Pyrōsis, “burning,” stands in three verses (dictionary). The other two are “the smoke of her burning” at Revelation 18:9 and 18:18. In the Greek of Proverbs 27:21 the word is the testing of metal, dokimion argyriō kai chrysō pyrōsis, “for silver and gold the test is burning,” with the dokimion of 1 Peter 1:7, “the tested genuineness of your faith — more precious than gold, which perishes though tested by fire”; the Hebrew of Proverbs 27:21 reads “the crucible is for silver, and the furnace for gold” (both checked; Proverbs 27 yet to come in this translation). “To test you” is peirasmos, the “trials” of 1:6 (dictionary). The KJV and the ASV read “the fiery trial”; the NWT 1984 reads “the burning among YOU”; the NIV reads “the fiery ordeal”; the Douay reads “the burning heat which is to try you.”

What the burning is, the letter does not name. What it does name is speech: the readers are spoken against as evildoers (1 Peter 2:12, 3:16), abused (verse 4), and insulted (verse 14). No court, governor or emperor is named here, and a reading that ties “the burning” to a particular persecution brings the occasion from outside the text.

“But insofar as you share in the sufferings of Christ, rejoice, so that at the revelation of his glory you may also rejoice and exult.” “Share” is koinōneō, the “sharing with the holy ones” of Romans 12:13; “the sufferings of Christ” are those “destined for Christ” at 1 Peter 1:11 (dictionary), and Paul speaks of “the partnership of his sufferings” at Philippians 3:10 and of those who “suffer with him, so that we may also be glorified together with him” at Romans 8:17. “Rejoice and exult” is chairō with agalliaō, the pair of Matthew 5:12, “Rejoice and be glad”; agalliaō is the “rejoice greatly” of 1 Peter 1:6 and 1:8 (dictionary). “The revelation” is apokalypsis, as in “at the revelation of Jesus Christ” at 1 Peter 1:7 and 1:13 (dictionary). The KJV reads “ye may be glad also with exceeding joy”; the NWT 1984 reads “rejoice and be overjoyed also during the revelation of his glory.”

“If you are insulted for the name of Christ, you are happy, because the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you.” “Insulted” is oneidizō, as in “Happy are you when they insult you” at Matthew 5:11 and Luke 6:22 (dictionary); “happy” is makarios, as in “you are happy” at 1 Peter 3:14 (dictionary). The apostles went away “rejoicing that they had been counted worthy to be dishonored for the Name” at Acts 5:41. “Rests on” is anapauomai, the verb of the Greek of Isaiah 11:2, “the Spirit of God will rest on him,” where the Hebrew has “the Spirit of Jehovah” (both checked; Isaiah 11 yet to come in this translation). ⚠ The Greek reads, word for word, “the of glory and the of God Spirit”: either one Spirit called “of glory and of God,” or “the glory” and “the Spirit of God” as two things that rest on the readers. The NIV reads “the Spirit of glory and of God”; the ASV reads “the Spirit of glory and the Spirit of God”; the NWT 1984 supplies a word, “the [spirit] of glory, even the spirit of God.” The Douay’s “that which is of the honour, glory, and power of God” follows the Latin. The Byzantine text adds “and of power,” and it and the Received Text add a sentence that the KJV prints as “on their part he is evil spoken of, but on your part he is glorified”; the ASV and the NWT 1984 do not have it.

Verses 15–16 · Allotriepiskopos, “a meddler” — and suffering “as a Christian”

“For let none of you suffer as a murderer or a thief or an evildoer, or as a meddler in other people’s affairs.” “Evildoer” is kakopoios, the word of 1 Peter 2:12, “they speak against you as evildoers,” and of the governors sent “to punish evildoers” at 1 Peter 2:14. The Douay reads “a railer,” from the Latin maledicus. ⚠ Allotriepiskopos stands in one verse (dictionary), and no earlier Greek use of it is known (reported, not checked here), so the sentence has to carry its sense. It is built from allotrios, “belonging to another,” and episkopos, “overseer,” the word of 1 Peter 2:25, “the shepherd and overseer of your souls” (dictionary): an overseer of what is not his. The KJV reads “a busybody in other men’s matters,” the ASV “a meddler in other men’s matters,” and the NWT 1984 “a busybody in other people’s matters”; the Douay reads “a coveter of other men’s things,” from the Latin alienorum appetitor, someone who wants what belongs to others. The second “as” sets this last word apart from the three crimes before it.

“But if as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but let him glorify God in this name.” Christianos stands in three verses (dictionary): the disciples “called Christians” first at Antioch at Acts 11:26, and Agrippa’s “you are persuading me to make a Christian” at Acts 26:28. Here the name stands in a list with murderer and thief, as a charge someone suffers under. “Let him not be ashamed” is the verb of “I am not ashamed of the gospel” at Romans 1:16. “In this name” is the reading of Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and the 27th Nestle-Aland edition; the 28th edition and the Byzantine text read en tō merei toutō, “in this matter.” The ASV and the NWT 1984 read “in this name”; the KJV reads “on this behalf”; the Douay reads “in that name”; the NIV reads “praise God that you bear that name.”

Verses 17–19 · “Judgment to begin from the house of God” — Proverbs 11:31 in Greek, and “a faithful Creator”

“For it is the time for the judgment to begin from the house of God; and if it begins first from us, what will be the end of those who disobey the gospel of God?” “The time” is kairos, the set time (dictionary). “The house of God” is the “spiritual house” of 1 Peter 2:5, the “household of God, which is the congregation of the living God” of 1 Timothy 3:15, and the house of Hebrews 3:6, “we are that house.” Judgment beginning with God’s own place is in the prophets: in Jeremiah 25:29, “I am beginning to bring disaster on the city that is called by my name,” and in Ezekiel 9:6, “begin at my sanctuary,” where the Greek has apo tōn hagiōn mou arxasthe, with the verb and the preposition Peter uses (checked; Ezekiel 9 yet to come in this translation). Peter quotes neither. “Disobey” is apeitheō, as in “they disobey the word” at 1 Peter 2:8, 3:1 and “who had once been disobedient” at 3:20 (dictionary); “the gospel of God” is Paul’s phrase at Romans 1:1, and 2 Thessalonians 1:8 has “those who do not obey the gospel.” The KJV reads “the time is come that judgment must begin at the house of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “it is the appointed time for the judgment to start with the house of God”; the NIV reads “to begin with God’s household”; the Douay reads “them that believe not the gospel of God,” from the Latin qui non credunt.

And “if the righteous one is scarcely saved, where will the ungodly and the sinner appear?” The line is the Greek of Proverbs 11:31, ei ho men dikaios molis sōzetai, ho asebēs kai hamartōlos pou phaneitai, word for word except that Peter’s text has no men (checked). The Hebrew says something else: hen tsaddiq ba’arets yeshullam, af ki-rasha’ vechote’, “Look, the righteous is repaid on the earth; how much more the wicked and the sinner” (checked; Proverbs 11 yet to come in this translation). Peter does not say “it is written,” and this translation prints the line in quotation marks, as the NWT 1984 and the NIV do. Molis, “scarcely,” stands in six verses, as in “scarcely will anyone die for a righteous person” at Romans 5:7 and “they scarcely made the crowds stop sacrificing” at Acts 14:18. “Ungodly” is asebēs (dictionary). The KJV reads “if the righteous scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly and the sinner appear?”; the NWT 1984 reads “is being saved with difficulty, where will the ungodly [man] and the sinner make a showing?”; the NIV reads “what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?”

“So then, let those also who suffer according to the will of God entrust their souls to a faithful Creator, in doing good.” “According to the will of God” takes up 3:17, “if the will of God should will it.” “Entrust” is paratithēmi, Jesus’s word on the cross at Luke 23:46, “Father, into your hands I entrust my spirit” (dictionary); at 1 Peter 2:23 Christ “kept entrusting himself to the one who judges justly” with another verb, paradidōmi. Ktistēs, “Creator,” stands in one verse (dictionary); “faithful” is as in “God is faithful” at 1 Corinthians 10:13. Agathopoiia, “doing good,” also stands in one verse (dictionary). Its verb is the “doing good” of 1 Peter 2:15, 2:20, 3:6 and 3:17, and the letter’s run of doing good while suffering ends on it. The Byzantine and Received texts add “as”: the KJV reads “commit the keeping of their souls to him in well doing, as unto a faithful Creator.” The ASV reads “commit their souls in well-doing unto a faithful Creator”; the NIV reads “commit themselves to their faithful Creator and continue to do good.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “arm yourselves”; “ceased from sin”; “the nations”; “sensuality,” as at Galatians 5:19; “revels,” as at Romans 13:13; “unlawful,” as at Acts 10:28; “debauchery,” as at Ephesians 5:18; “surprised,” in verses 4 and 12; “the good news was proclaimed,” not “made proclamation”; “has drawn near,” as at James 5:8; “earnest,” as at 1:22; “covers a multitude of sins,” as at James 5:20; “grumbling,” as at Acts 6:1; “stewards”; “varied,” the word of the various trials of 1:6; “oracles,” as at Romans 3:2; “the burning”; “insulted” and “happy,” as at Matthew 5:11; “Christian”; “disobey,” as at 2:8; “scarcely,” as at Romans 5:7; and “entrust,” as at Luke 23:46.

Echoes paid. “Christ suffered in the flesh” from 1 Peter 3:18; the good news “proclaimed also to the dead,” set beside the proclamation to the spirits in prison of 1 Peter 3:19; “love covers a multitude of sins” from James 5:20; the various trials and the gold tested by fire of 1 Peter 1:6 and 1:7, in the varied grace and the burning; being sober from 1 Peter 1:13; earnest love from 1 Peter 1:22; the spiritual house of 1 Peter 2:5; evildoers from 1 Peter 2:12; doing good from 1 Peter 2:15; the overseer of 1 Peter 2:25 inside “meddler”; “you are happy” from 1 Peter 3:14; and the will of God from 1 Peter 3:17.

Echoes planted. “A sharer in the glory that is about to be revealed” at 5:1; “the chief shepherd” at 5:4; “Be sober; stay awake” at 5:8; “To him be the might forever and ever” at 5:11; and “the smoke of her burning” at Revelation 18:9.

Next in this book: 1 Peter 5, already on these pages. “Shepherd the flock of God”; the chief shepherd and the unfading crown of glory; “God opposes the proud” once more; “casting all your anxiety on him”; the adversary who walks around “like a roaring lion”; and greetings from “she who is in Babylon” and from Mark, “my son.”
