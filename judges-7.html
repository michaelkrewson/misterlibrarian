Gideon sends the fearful home until three hundred remain, chosen by the way they drink. At night, with horns, torches and broken jars, the Midianite camp panics and turns its swords on itself. Israel pursues to the Jordan fords, and two Midianite princes, Oreb and Zeeb, are killed and their heads brought to Gideon.
Undated within the chapter. It follows directly on from Judges 6, so it takes the same undated window the library gives the judges period.

1
וַיַּשְׁכֵּם יְרֻבַּעַל הוּא גִדְעוֹן, וְכָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, וַיַּחֲנוּ, עַל-עֵין חֲרֹד; וּמַחֲנֵה מִדְיָן הָיָה-לוֹ מִצָּפוֹן, מִגִּבְעַת הַמּוֹרֶה בָּעֵמֶק.
Then Jerubbaal, who is Gideon, and all the people with him rose early and camped beside the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was north of them, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.note
Entonces Jerobaal, es decir Gedeón, y todo el pueblo que estaba con él, madrugaron y acamparon junto al manantial de Harod. El campamento de Madián quedaba al norte de ellos, junto a la colina de Moré, en el valle.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, אֶל-גִּדְעוֹן, רַב הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתָּךְ, מִתִּתִּי אֶת-מִדְיָן בְּיָדָם: פֶּן-יִתְפָּאֵר עָלַי יִשְׂרָאֵל לֵאמֹר, יָדִי הוֹשִׁיעָה לִּי.
Jehovah said to Gideon: “There are too many people with you for me to give Midian into their hand. Otherwise Israel would boast against me, saying, ‘My own hand has saved me.’note
Jehová dijo a Gedeón: «Hay demasiada gente contigo para que yo entregue a Madián en sus manos; si no, Israel se jactaría contra mí, diciendo: “Mi propia mano me salvó”».
3
וְעַתָּה, קְרָא נָא בְּאָזְנֵי הָעָם לֵאמֹר, מִי-יָרֵא וְחָרֵד, יָשֹׁב וְיִצְפֹּר מֵהַר הַגִּלְעָד; וַיָּשָׁב מִן-הָעָם, עֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁנַיִם אֶלֶף, וַעֲשֶׂרֶת אֲלָפִים, נִשְׁאָרוּ.
Now then, call out in the ears of the people: ‘Whoever is fearful and trembling, let him turn back and take wing early from Mount Gilead.’ So twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand remained.note
Ahora, pues, pregona a oídos del pueblo: «Quien tenga miedo y tiemble, que se vuelva y levante vuelo de madrugada desde el monte de Galaad». Y regresaron del pueblo veintidós mil, y quedaron diez mil.
4
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-גִּדְעוֹן, עוֹד הָעָם רָב, הוֹרֵד אוֹתָם אֶל-הַמַּיִם, וְאֶצְרְפֶנּוּ לְךָ שָׁם; וְהָיָה אֲשֶׁר אֹמַר אֵלֶיךָ זֶה יֵלֵךְ אִתָּךְ, הוּא יֵלֵךְ אִתָּךְ, וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר-אֹמַר אֵלֶיךָ זֶה לֹא-יֵלֵךְ עִמָּךְ, הוּא לֹא יֵלֵךְ.
Jehovah said to Gideon: “The people are still too many. Bring them down to the water, and I will refine them for you there. The one of whom I say to you, ‘This one shall go with you,’ shall go with you; and the one of whom I say to you, ‘This one shall not go with you,’ shall not go.”note
Jehová dijo a Gedeón: «Todavía hay demasiada gente; llévalos al agua, y allí te los refinaré. Aquel de quien yo te diga: “Este irá contigo”, irá contigo; y aquel de quien yo te diga: “Este no irá contigo”, no irá».
5
וַיּוֹרֶד אֶת-הָעָם, אֶל-הַמָּיִם; וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-גִּדְעוֹן, כֹּל אֲשֶׁר-יָלֹק בִּלְשׁוֹנוֹ מִן-הַמַּיִם כַּאֲשֶׁר יָלֹק הַכֶּלֶב תַּצִּיג אוֹתוֹ לְבָד, וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר-יִכְרַע עַל-בִּרְכָּיו, לִשְׁתּוֹת.
So he brought the people down to the water. Jehovah said to Gideon: “Everyone who laps up the water with his tongue, as a dog laps, you shall set apart; and everyone who kneels down on his knees to drink.”note
Bajó, pues, al pueblo al agua. Jehová dijo a Gedeón: «Todo el que lama el agua con la lengua, como lame el perro, apártalo; y todo el que se arrodille para beber».
6
וַיְהִי, מִסְפַּר הַמְלַקְקִים בְּיָדָם אֶל-פִּיהֶם--שְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת, אִישׁ; וְכֹל יֶתֶר הָעָם, כָּרְעוּ עַל-בִּרְכֵיהֶם לִשְׁתּוֹת מָיִם.
The number of those who lapped, bringing their hand to their mouth, was three hundred men. All the rest of the people knelt down on their knees to drink water.note
El número de los que lamieron llevándose la mano a la boca fue de trescientos hombres. Todo el resto del pueblo se arrodilló para beber agua.
7
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-גִּדְעוֹן, בִּשְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת הָאִישׁ הַמְלַקְקִים אוֹשִׁיעַ אֶתְכֶם, וְנָתַתִּי אֶת-מִדְיָן, בְּיָדֶךָ; וְכָל-הָעָם--יֵלְכוּ, אִישׁ לִמְקֹמוֹ.
Jehovah said to Gideon: “By the three hundred men who lapped I will save you, and I will give Midian into your hand. Let all the other people go, each to his place.”note
Jehová dijo a Gedeón: «Con los trescientos hombres que lamieron los salvaré, y entregaré a Madián en tu mano. Que se vayan todos los demás, cada uno a su lugar».
8
וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת-צֵדָה הָעָם בְּיָדָם וְאֵת שׁוֹפְרֹתֵיהֶם, וְאֵת כָּל-אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל שִׁלַּח אִישׁ לְאֹהָלָיו, וּבִשְׁלֹשׁ-מֵאוֹת הָאִישׁ, הֶחֱזִיק; וּמַחֲנֵה מִדְיָן, הָיָה לוֹ מִתַּחַת בָּעֵמֶק.
So they took the people’s provisions in their hands, and their horns. He sent every other man of Israel back to his tent, and kept the three hundred. The camp of Midian was below him in the valley.note
Tomaron, pues, en sus manos las provisiones del pueblo y sus cuernos. Y a todos los demás hombres de Israel los mandó cada uno a su tienda, y retuvo a los trescientos. El campamento de Madián estaba debajo de él, en el valle.
9
וַיְהִי, בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא, וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו יְהוָה, קוּם רֵד בַּמַּחֲנֶה: כִּי נְתַתִּיו, בְּיָדֶךָ.
That same night Jehovah said to him: “Get up, go down into the camp, for I have given it into your hand.”note
Aquella misma noche Jehová le dijo: «Levántate, desciende al campamento, porque lo he entregado en tu mano».
10
וְאִם-יָרֵא אַתָּה, לָרֶדֶת--רֵד אַתָּה וּפֻרָה נַעַרְךָ, אֶל-הַמַּחֲנֶה.
But if you are afraid to go down, go down with Purah your attendant to the camp.note
Pero si temes descender, desciende tú con Purá tu criado al campamento.
11
וְשָׁמַעְתָּ, מַה-יְדַבֵּרוּ, וְאַחַר תֶּחֱזַקְנָה יָדֶיךָ, וְיָרַדְתָּ בַּמַּחֲנֶה; וַיֵּרֶד הוּא וּפֻרָה נַעֲרוֹ, אֶל-קְצֵה הַחֲמֻשִׁים אֲשֶׁר בַּמַּחֲנֶה.
You will hear what they say, and afterward your hands will be strong, and you will go down into the camp.” So he went down with Purah his attendant to the edge of the armed men who were in the camp.note
Oirás lo que dicen, y después se fortalecerán tus manos, y descenderás al campamento». Y descendió con Purá su criado hasta el borde de los armados que estaban en el campamento.
12
וּמִדְיָן וַעֲמָלֵק וְכָל-בְּנֵי-קֶדֶם נֹפְלִים בָּעֵמֶק, כָּאַרְבֶּה לָרֹב; וְלִגְמַלֵּיהֶם אֵין מִסְפָּר, כַּחוֹל שֶׁעַל-שְׂפַת הַיָּם לָרֹב.
Midian and Amalek and all the sons of the east lay in the valley like locusts for multitude, and their camels were without number, like the sand on the seashore for multitude.note
Madián, Amalec y todos los hijos del oriente yacían en el valle tan numerosos como las langostas; y sus camellos eran sin número, como la arena que está a la orilla del mar en multitud.
13
וַיָּבֹא גִדְעוֹן--וְהִנֵּה-אִישׁ, מְסַפֵּר לְרֵעֵהוּ חֲלוֹם; וַיֹּאמֶר הִנֵּה חֲלוֹם חָלַמְתִּי, וְהִנֵּה צְלִיל לֶחֶם שְׂעֹרִים מִתְהַפֵּךְ בְּמַחֲנֵה מִדְיָן, וַיָּבֹא עַד-הָאֹהֶל וַיַּכֵּהוּ וַיִּפֹּל וַיַּהַפְכֵהוּ לְמַעְלָה, וְנָפַל הָאֹהֶל.
When Gideon came, look! A man was telling his companion a dream. He said: “I dreamed a dream: a cake of barley bread came rolling into the camp of Midian, came to a tent and struck it so that it fell, and turned it over, and the tent lay flat.”note
Cuando llegó Gedeón, he aquí que un hombre contaba a su compañero un sueño. Decía: «He soñado un sueño: una torta de pan de cebada rodaba hacia el campamento de Madián; llegó a una tienda, la golpeó y la derribó, la volcó, y la tienda cayó».
14
וַיַּעַן רֵעֵהוּ וַיֹּאמֶר, אֵין זֹאת, בִּלְתִּי אִם-חֶרֶב גִּדְעוֹן בֶּן-יוֹאָשׁ, אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל: נָתַן הָאֱלֹהִים בְּיָדוֹ, אֶת-מִדְיָן וְאֶת-כָּל-הַמַּחֲנֶה.
His companion answered: “This is nothing but the sword of Gideon son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has given Midian and all the camp into his hand.”note
Respondió su compañero: «Esto no es sino la espada de Gedeón hijo de Joás, varón de Israel. Dios ha entregado en su mano a Madián y a todo el campamento».
15
וַיְהִי כִשְׁמֹעַ גִּדְעוֹן אֶת-מִסְפַּר הַחֲלוֹם, וְאֶת-שִׁבְרוֹ--וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ; וַיָּשָׁב, אֶל-מַחֲנֵה יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיֹּאמֶר קוּמוּ, כִּי-נָתַן יְהוָה בְּיֶדְכֶם אֶת-מַחֲנֵה מִדְיָן.
When Gideon heard the telling of the dream and its interpretation, he bowed down in worship. He returned to the camp of Israel and said: “Get up, for Jehovah has given the camp of Midian into your hand.”note
Cuando Gedeón oyó la narración del sueño y su interpretación, se inclinó para adorar. Volvió al campamento de Israel y dijo: «Levántense, que Jehová ha entregado en sus manos el campamento de Madián».
16
וַיַּחַץ אֶת-שְׁלֹשׁ-מֵאוֹת הָאִישׁ, שְׁלֹשָׁה רָאשִׁים; וַיִּתֵּן שׁוֹפָרוֹת בְּיַד-כֻּלָּם וְכַדִּים רֵיקִים, וְלַפִּדִים בְּתוֹךְ הַכַּדִּים.
He divided the three hundred men into three companies, and put horns in every man’s hand, and empty jars, with torches inside the jars.note
Dividió a los trescientos hombres en tres compañías, y puso en las manos de todos ellos cuernos y cántaros vacíos, con antorchas dentro de los cántaros.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵיהֶם, מִמֶּנִּי תִרְאוּ וְכֵן תַּעֲשׂוּ; וְהִנֵּה אָנֹכִי בָא בִּקְצֵה הַמַּחֲנֶה, וְהָיָה כַאֲשֶׁר-אֶעֱשֶׂה כֵּן תַּעֲשׂוּן.
He said to them: “Look at me and do the same. When I come to the edge of the camp, as I do, so you shall do.”note
Les dijo: «Miren lo que hago yo y hagan lo mismo. Cuando yo llegue al borde del campamento, como yo haga, así harán ustedes».
18
וְתָקַעְתִּי, בַּשּׁוֹפָר--אָנֹכִי, וְכָל-אֲשֶׁר אִתִּי; וּתְקַעְתֶּם בַּשּׁוֹפָרוֹת גַּם-אַתֶּם, סְבִיבוֹת כָּל-הַמַּחֲנֶה, וַאֲמַרְתֶּם, לַיהוָה וּלְגִדְעוֹן.
When I blow the horn, I and all who are with me, then you also blow the horns all around the whole camp, and say: ‘For Jehovah and for Gideon!’”note
«Cuando yo toque el cuerno, yo y todos los que están conmigo, entonces también ustedes tocarán los cuernos alrededor de todo el campamento, y dirán: “¡Por Jehová y por Gedeón!”».
19
וַיָּבֹא גִדְעוֹן וּמֵאָה-אִישׁ אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ בִּקְצֵה הַמַּחֲנֶה, רֹאשׁ הָאַשְׁמֹרֶת הַתִּיכוֹנָה--אַךְ הָקֵם הֵקִימוּ, אֶת-הַשֹּׁמְרִים; וַיִּתְקְעוּ, בַּשּׁוֹפָרוֹת, וְנָפוֹץ הַכַּדִּים, אֲשֶׁר בְּיָדָם.
Gideon and the hundred men with him came to the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, just after they had posted the watch; and they blew the horns and broke the jars that were in their hands.note
Gedeón y los cien hombres que estaban con él llegaron al borde del campamento al comienzo de la vigilia intermedia, justo cuando acababan de apostar a los centinelas. Tocaron los cuernos y rompieron los cántaros que tenían en las manos.
20
וַיִּתְקְעוּ שְׁלֹשֶׁת הָרָאשִׁים בַּשּׁוֹפָרוֹת, וַיִּשְׁבְּרוּ הַכַּדִּים, וַיַּחֲזִיקוּ בְיַד-שְׂמֹאולָם בַּלַּפִּדִים, וּבְיַד-יְמִינָם הַשּׁוֹפָרוֹת לִתְקוֹעַ; וַיִּקְרְאוּ, חֶרֶב לַיהוָה וּלְגִדְעוֹן.
The three companies blew the horns and broke the jars. They held the torches in their left hands and the horns in their right hands to blow, and they cried out: “The sword of Jehovah and of Gideon!”note
Las tres compañías tocaron los cuernos y rompieron los cántaros; sostuvieron las antorchas en la mano izquierda y en la derecha los cuernos para tocar; y gritaron: «¡La espada de Jehová y de Gedeón!».
21
וַיַּעַמְדוּ אִישׁ תַּחְתָּיו, סָבִיב לַמַּחֲנֶה; וַיָּרָץ כָּל-הַמַּחֲנֶה וַיָּרִיעוּ, ויניסו וַיָּנוּסוּ.
Each man stood in his place all around the camp, and the whole camp ran; they shouted and fled.note
Cada uno se quedó en su puesto alrededor del campamento; y todo el campamento corrió, gritó y huyó.
22
וַיִּתְקְעוּ, שְׁלֹשׁ-מֵאוֹת הַשּׁוֹפָרוֹת, וַיָּשֶׂם יְהוָה אֵת חֶרֶב אִישׁ בְּרֵעֵהוּ, וּבְכָל-הַמַּחֲנֶה; וַיָּנָס הַמַּחֲנֶה עַד-בֵּית הַשִּׁטָּה, צְרֵרָתָה--עַד שְׂפַת-אָבֵל מְחוֹלָה, עַל-טַבָּת.
The three hundred blew the horns, and Jehovah set each man’s sword against his fellow throughout the camp. The camp fled as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, to the border of Abel-meholah, by Tabbath.note
Los trescientos seguían tocando los cuernos, y Jehová puso la espada de cada uno contra su compañero en todo el campamento. El campamento huyó hasta Bet-sitá, hacia Zererá, hasta el límite de Abel-meholá, junto a Tabat.
23
וַיִּצָּעֵק אִישׁ-יִשְׂרָאֵל מִנַּפְתָּלִי וּמִן-אָשֵׁר, וּמִן-כָּל-מְנַשֶּׁה; וַיִּרְדְּפוּ, אַחֲרֵי מִדְיָן.
The men of Israel were called out from Naphtali and from Asher and from all of Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.note
Y se convocó a los hombres de Israel de Neftalí, de Aser y de todo Manasés, y persiguieron a Madián.
24

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verse 1 · the spring of Harod and the hill of Moreh

Jerubbaal, who is Gideon. The name the book gave him at Judges 6:32 (now on these pages) comes first, so the reader knows which man is camped at the spring. The Hebrew sets the two names side by side, and the translation keeps both.

‘Ein Harod. Ein is a spring, and the phrase ‘ein Harod’ stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The place is named with charod, trembling, the word verse 3 will use for the men who are afraid, so the reader meets the spring first and the trembling second. The KJV has ‘the well of Harod’ and the ASV ‘the spring of Harod’; the translation keeps ‘spring’, which is what ein is.

The hill of Moreh. The hill of Moreh stands in one verse. Moreh is the word for a teacher, and also the word for the early rain (Joel 2:23, not yet on these pages). The translation keeps ‘Moreh’ rather than choosing, because the Hebrew leaves both open.

Verses 2–3 · a boast to prevent, and the fearful sent home

‘Lest Israel boast.’ Verse 2 gives the reason for the cut: pen yitpa’er, lest Israel boast, a phrase that stands in one verse, this one. The boast is quoted in full: my own hand has saved me. The ASV and the KJV both read ‘Mine own hand hath saved me’, and the NWT 1984 reads ‘My hand it was that saved me.’ The translation takes the plainer English of the first two.

‘Fearful and trembling.’ Yare ve-chared, the afraid and the trembling, stands in one verse, verse 3. The officers’ speech in Deuteronomy 20:8 (already on these pages) sends home a man who is afraid, but in different words, and this is the verse that sends the trembling men home from the army at the spring. The ASV reads ‘Whosoever is fearful and trembling’, the KJV ‘Whosoever is fearful and afraid’, the NWT 1984 ‘Who is there afraid and trembling?’

‘Take wing from Mount Gilead.’ The verb yitzpor is the verb for a bird’s flight, from the same root as tsippor, a bird. The men are told to fly home early. The translation says ‘take wing’, which is strange enough to send a reader to this note. The KJV has ‘depart early’ and the ASV ‘depart’, and both lose the bird. Twenty-two thousand go, and ten thousand remain.

Verses 4–7 · the water test and the dog’s way of drinking

‘I will refine them.’ The verb in verse 4 is tsaraf, to refine metal in the fire, and it is not the verb of ‘test’ in Judges 6:39 (now on these pages). The water is the furnace, and the men are assayed by how they drink. The KJV and ASV both say ‘try them’, and the NWT 1984 says ‘put them to the proof’. The translation keeps ‘refine’, because the word is about metal and the reader should hear it.

‘As a dog laps.’ Yalaq is the verb for a dog’s lapping, and it is the verb of 1 Kings 21:19 (not yet on these pages), where dogs lick up Naboth’s blood. The KJV and ASV both read ‘as a dog lappeth’, and the NWT 1984 reads ‘just as a dog laps’. The translation keeps the animal in the sentence.

Three hundred who lap, and the rest who kneel. The men who bring the hand to the mouth in verse 6 are kept, and the rest kneel to drink. The text gives the test and not the reason, so the reader is not told why the lappers are chosen. Commentators have offered several reasons, and the translation does not choose among them. Verse 7 repeats the word ‘lapped’, and the translation keeps the same word both times.

Verses 8–11 · provisions, horns, and the armed men at the edge

‘The people’s provisions.’ Verse 8 has the men give up their food and horns to the three hundred, and the men of Israel go home. The word for the provisions is the food a man carries for a journey, and the translation says ‘provisions’ because the reader needs to know the army is going out with what it can carry.

‘The armed men.’ The ‘armed men’ of verse 11 are the word chamushim, which stands in Exodus 13:18 and Joshua 1:14 (both now on these pages), and in Joshua 4:12 (now on these pages). The Joshua 4 note already called this verse the place where Gideon stands at the edge of the Midianite camp, and this note is where the word is read in its last sense. The dictionary entry lists all four verses.

Purah the attendant. The man who goes down with Gideon is called his attendant, naar, the same word that names a young servant. The translation keeps ‘attendant’ because the word covers a servant who serves a commander in war, not a boy.

Verse 12 · locusts for number, and sand for number

‘Like locusts for multitude.’ The enemy is the one Judges 6:5 (now on these pages) compared to locusts, and the camels are the same. The ASV keeps the wording exactly, ‘like locusts for multitude’; the KJV has ‘like grasshoppers for multitude’, and the NWT 1984 ‘as numerous as locusts’. The translation keeps the locust.

‘Like the sand on the seashore.’ The camels are counted by the sand on the seashore, the same words God gives Abraham for his seed in Genesis 22:17 (already on these pages). The enemy’s camels are measured by the promise made to Abraham, and the reader can hear the comparison without its being pressed.

Verses 13–14 · a barley cake, and a dream read at once

‘A cake of barley bread.’ The word tsalil, a cake or loaf, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The NWT 1984 has ‘a round cake of barley bread turning over and over’; the KJV and ASV both have ‘a cake of barley bread’. The translation lets the verb do the rolling. Barley is the poorer grain, and 2 Kings 4:42 (not yet on these pages) has barley loaves as the food brought to a prophet.

A dream read by a companion. The dreamer is a man telling his companion, and the phrase ‘a dream to his companion’ stands in one verse. The companion gives the reading at once, that the dream is the sword of Gideon. Both men are in the Midianite camp, so the speaker names God, ha-Elohim, and not Jehovah. The translation keeps ‘God’ because the speaker is not Israel.

Verses 15–17 · worship in the enemy’s camp, and three companies

‘He bowed down in worship.’ Gideon hears the dream and its reading and bows down before he gives any order. The verb is the verb for bowing in worship, and the translation says ‘bowed down in worship’ so that the act is seen. The KJV has ‘worshipped’ and the ASV ‘worshipped’; the reader is not told to what.

‘Three companies.’ Verse 16 has the three hundred divided into three companies, and the phrase ‘the three heads’ stands in one verse, verse 20. The word for head is also the word for the leader of a body, and the translation says ‘companies’ in verse 16 and keeps the same word in verse 20 so the two verses match. The NWT 1984 has ‘three bands’.

Torches inside the jars. The weapons are horns, empty jars and torches. The jar hides the light until it is broken, and verse 19 breaks them. The translation says ‘torches inside the jars’ and leaves the jar in the picture.

Verses 18–20 · the cry without a sword, and the cry with one

‘For Jehovah and for Gideon.’ The shout Gideon orders in verse 18 has no sword in it: le-Jehovah ul-Gideon. Verse 20 adds one, cherev le-Jehovah ul-Gideon, a sword for Jehovah and for Gideon. The shout stands in two verses, 18 and 20, and the sword is in verse 20 alone. The ASV reads ‘For Jehovah and for Gideon’ in verse 18 and ‘The sword of Jehovah and of Gideon’ in verse 20, and the translation keeps that difference.

‘The middle watch.’ Ashmoret ha-tikhonah, the middle watch, stands in one verse. The Hebrew reckons the night in three watches, where the Roman reckoning in Matthew 14:25 (already on these pages) has four. Verse 19 has Gideon arrive just after the sentries are posted, and the translation says ‘just after they had posted the watch’.

Horns blown, jars broken. The three companies blow the horns and break the jars in verse 20, and the torches are held in the left hand and the horns in the right. The translation keeps the hands, because the scene depends on them.

Verses 21–22 · each man’s sword against his fellow

‘Each man’s sword against his fellow.’ Cherev ish be-re’ehu, the sword of each man against his fellow, the phrase ‘sword of each man’ stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one, 1 Samuel 14:20 (not yet on these pages), where the panic in Saul’s army takes the same form, and Ezekiel 38:21 (not yet on these pages), where the sword of the invader turns on his own companies. The first words are the same in all three, and the translation keeps them the same.

‘As far as Beth-shittah.’ Beth-shittah stands in one verse. Zererah stands in one verse beside it. Abel-meholah stands in three verses: here, 1 Kings 4:12 (not yet on these pages), and 1 Kings 19:16 (not yet on these pages), where Elisha son of Shaphat is anointed as prophet from Abel-meholah. The KJV has ‘to Bethshittah in Zererath, and to the border of Abelmeholah, unto Tabbath’ and the ASV ‘as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah’. The flight runs east, toward the Jordan.

Verses 23–25 · the pursuit, and two princes named for birds and beasts

Oreb and Zeeb. The two princes are named for the raven and the wolf. ‘The rock of Oreb’ stands in two verses: here, and Isaiah 10:26 (not yet on these pages), where the scourge of Jehovah of hosts is raised as at the striking of Midian at the rock of Oreb. The rock is remembered as the place Midian was struck. ‘The winepress of Zeeb’ stands in one verse. The same two names come together in Psalm 83:11 (not yet on these pages; the Hebrew numbers it 83:12), where the enemies are asked to be made like Oreb and like Zeeb. The ASV reads ‘slew Oreb at the rock of Oreb’, and the KJV ‘slew Oreb upon the rock Oreb’.

‘From beyond the Jordan.’ The heads are brought to Gideon from beyond the Jordan, me-ever la-yarden. The phrase does not say which bank Gideon stands on, and the translation keeps that open. The ASV has ‘beyond the Jordan’ and the KJV ‘on the other side Jordan’.

The heads of the princes. The pursuit ends with the heads brought in, and the book does not describe Gideon’s response here. Judges 8:3 (not yet on these pages) returns to the two names when the men of Ephraim complain, and the story of the heads continues there.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “spring” for ein in verse 1, and “take wing” for yitzpor in verse 3, where the bird is kept in the verb; “refine” for tsaraf in verse 4, kept separate from the “test” of Judges 6:39; “lapped” for the same word both times in verse 7; “companies” for the three heads of verses 16 and 20; “the middle watch” for ashmoret ha-tikhonah; and “For Jehovah and for Gideon” without the sword in verse 18, with “The sword of Jehovah and of Gideon” in verse 20.

Echoes paid. The locusts and camels of Judges 6:5 return in verse 12, and the Amalekites and the sons of the east of 6:3 with them. The call to Naphtali, Asher and Manasseh in 6:35 is answered in verse 23. The chamushim that Joshua 4 left for this chapter is read here at verse 11. The four watches of Matthew 14 are set against the three of verse 19. The ‘fearful and trembling’ of Deuteronomy 20:8 is answered by the same fear in verse 3.

Echoes planted. Oreb and Zeeb return at Judges 8:3, and the rock of Oreb at Isaiah 10:26. The sword of each man against his fellow comes back at 1 Samuel 14:20 and Ezekiel 38:21. Abel-meholah, the place the army fled toward, is where Elisha is from at 1 Kings 19:16. The dogs who lick at 1 Kings 21:19 are the ones the lapping of this chapter already calls to mind.
