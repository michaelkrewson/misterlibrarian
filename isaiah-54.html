A barren woman is told to sing and to enlarge her tent; her shame of youth and widowhood forgotten, and her Maker is her husband; forsaken for a small moment and gathered with great mercies, with the oath of the waters of Noah and the covenant of peace that outlasts the mountains; the afflicted city to be built with sapphires and fiery stones, its sons taught of Jehovah; and the smith and the destroyer both created, so that no weapon formed against her succeeds.
Undated. The chapter follows the Servant of Isaiah 53 and sits in the second half of Isaiah, which this site files with the exile; it names no king and no city but speaks to a woman, the afflicted one, whose desolate cities are to be settled. ⚠ Nothing in it fixes a year, and who the woman is, a city, a people or the wife of Jehovah, is said by the images and not named.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Isaiah 54:1–3 “Sing, barren one” — enlarge the place of your tent

⭐ Sing, barren one. “Sing, barren one who did not bear” stands in this one verse, and “break into singing” stands in this one verse. The barren woman (aqarah) is told to sing before she has a child: Sarah (Genesis 11:30) and Hannah (1 Samuel 2:5, where the barren has borne seven) are both on these pages, and Paul quotes this verse at Galatians 4:27 (now on these pages). “More are the children of the desolate one” stands in this one verse; the married wife is the one with a baal, a husband-owner. The KJV has “Sing, O barren, thou that didst not bear”, the NIV “Sing, barren woman, you who never bore a child”, the NWT 1984 “Cry out joyfully, you barren woman that did not give birth!”, the TLB “Sing, O childless woman!” and the Douay “Give praise, O thou barren, that bearest not”. For the second half the KJV has “more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife” and the NWT 1984 “the sons of the desolated one are more numerous than the sons of the woman with a husbandly owner”.

⭐ Enlarge the tent. “Enlarge the place of your tent” stands in this one verse, and the rest is a tent being let out: “the curtains of your dwellings” (yeriah), “lengthen your cords” stands in this one verse, “and strengthen your pegs” stands in this one verse (yated). The KJV has “Enlarge the place of thy tent”, the NIV “Enlarge the place of your tent, stretch your tent curtains wide”, the NWT 1984 “Make the place of your tent more spacious”, the Douay “stretch out the skins of thy tabernacles” and the TLB “Enlarge your house; build on additions; spread out your home!”.

⭐ Right and left. “To the right and to the left you will break out” stands in this one verse, the verb being paratz (perets), to burst through, as at Genesis 28:14 (now on these pages); and “your seed will take possession of nations” stands in this one verse (zera, goy), with “they will settle the desolate cities” (1). The KJV has “thy seed shall inherit the Gentiles”, the NIV “your descendants will dispossess nations”, the NWT 1984 “your own offspring will take possession even of nations” and the TLB “you will soon be bursting at the seams!”.

Verses 4–6 · Isaiah 54:4–6 do not fear — the shame of your youth — “your Maker is your husband” — a wife forsaken

⭐ The shame of your youth. “The shame of your youth” stands in this one verse and “the reproach of your widowhood” stands in this one verse (cherpah, almanah). Both are to be forgotten, and the command is the one of “do not fear”, which stands in eleven verses. The KJV has “thou shalt forget the shame of thy youth, and shalt not remember the reproach of thy widowhood any more”, the NIV “You will forget the shame of your youth and remember no more the reproach of your widowhood”, the NWT 1984 “the reproach of your continuous widowhood you will remember no more” and the TLB “The shame of your youth and the sorrows of widowhood will be remembered no more”.

⭐ “Your Maker is your husband.” “Your husband, your Maker” stands in this one verse, and both words are written in the plural, bo’alayikh osayikh, the plural of majesty, though the sense is one husband and one Maker. The husband is the baal, the one who owns and rules, the word God uses of himself at Jeremiah 31:31 (now on these pages, in the Hebrew numbering): “though I was a husband to them”. The KJV has “For thy Maker is thine husband”, the NIV “For your Maker is your husband”, the NWT 1984 “your Grand Maker is your husbandly owner”, the TLB “your Creator will be your “husband.”” and the Douay “For he that made thee shall rule over thee”. ⚠ The Douay and the NWT 1984 keep the sense of rule or ownership in the word; the others say husband.

⭐ Redeemer, Holy One, God of all the earth. “And your redeemer the Holy One of Israel” stands in two verses, here and Isaiah 41:14 (now on these pages) (goel, qadosh); “Jehovah of hosts is his name” stands in twelve verses, the name of tzevaot; and “the God of all the earth he will be called” stands in this one verse. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah of armies being his name; and the Holy One of Israel is your Repurchaser” and the NIV “the Holy One of Israel is your Redeemer; he is called the God of all the earth”.

⭐ A wife forsaken. “Forsaken and grieved in spirit” stands in this one verse (azav), and “a wife of youth” stands in this one verse, the first wife, rejected. The KJV has “as a woman forsaken and grieved in spirit, and a wife of youth, when thou wast refused”, the NIV “as if you were a wife deserted and distressed in spirit—a wife who married young, only to be rejected”, the NWT 1984 “as if you were a wife left entirely and hurt in spirit”, the TLB “a young wife abandoned by her husband” and the Douay “as woman forsaken and mourning in spirit, and as a wife cast off from her youth”.

Verses 7–10 · Isaiah 54:7–10 a small moment — an overflow of anger — the waters of Noah — the mountains may depart

⭐ A small moment. “For a small moment I forsook you” stands in this one verse (rega, azav), and the gathering is with great mercies. The KJV has “For a small moment have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee”, the NIV “For a brief moment I abandoned you, but with deep compassion I will bring you back”, the NWT 1984 “For a little moment I left you entirely, but with great mercies I shall collect you together” and the TLB “For a brief moment I abandoned you. But with great compassion I will take you back”.

⭐ An overflow of anger. “In an overflow of anger” (beshetsef qetsef) stands in this one verse; shetsef is a rare word chosen to sound like qetsef, anger. “I hid my face for a moment” stands in this one verse, and “with steadfast love everlasting I have had mercy on you” stands in this one verse (chesed, rekhem). The KJV has “In a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a moment; but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee”, the NIV “In a surge of anger I hid my face from you for a moment, but with everlasting kindness I will have compassion on you”, the NWT 1984 “With a flood of indignation I concealed my face from you for but a moment”, the TLB “In a moment of anger I turned my face a little while” and the Douay “In a moment of indignation have I hid my face a little while from thee”.

⭐ The waters of Noah. “For this is as the waters of Noah to me” stands in this one verse, me Noach, and the oath is that of Genesis 9:11 (now on these pages): the waters will not pass over the earth again. ⚠ Four of the shelf versions read “days” where the Hebrew has “waters”: the NIV has “To me this is like the days of Noah, when I swore that the waters of Noah would never again cover the earth”, the NWT 1984 “This is just as the days of Noah to me”, the Douay “This thing is to me as in the days of Noe” and the TLB “Just as in the time of Noah”. The KJV and the Geneva keep “as the waters of Noah”, and so does this translation.

⭐ The mountains may depart. “The mountains may depart” stands in this one verse, and the same verb (mush) is turned on his love: “my covenant of peace” stands in this one verse (brit, shalom); the next chapter has the “everlasting covenant” of Isaiah 55:3 (now on these pages). “The one who has compassion on you” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “For the mountains shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed”, the NIV “Though the mountains be shaken and the hills be removed, yet my unfailing love for you will not be shaken nor my covenant of peace be removed”, the NWT 1984 “my loving-kindness itself will not be removed from you” and the TLB “my promise of peace for you will never be broken”.

Verses 11–14 · Isaiah 54:11–14 stones laid in antimony — sapphires and fiery stones — taught of Jehovah — established in righteousness

⭐ Afflicted, storm-tossed. “Afflicted one, storm-tossed” stands in this one verse (oni, nacham), and the stones are laid “laying your stones in kohl”, the black eye-paint used as mortar, stands in this one verse (pukh); the foundations are “sapphires”, a word found in two verses, here and Song of Solomon 5:14 (sappir). The KJV has “I will lay thy stones with fair colours, and lay thy foundations with sapphires”, the NIV “I will rebuild you with stones of turquoise, your foundations with lapis lazuli”, the NWT 1984 “here I am laying with hard mortar your stones”, the Douay “I will lay thy stones in order, and will lay thy foundations with sapphires”, the Geneva “I will lay thy stones with the carbuncle” and the TLB “I will rebuild you on a foundation of sapphires”.

⭐ Rubies and fiery stones. “Your pinnacles” stands in this one verse, made of kadkod, a stone, which stands in two verses, here and Ezekiel 27:16 (kadkod); “fiery stones” (eqdach) stands in this one verse (eqdach); and “stones of delight” stands in this one verse (even, chefets). ⚠ The names of the stones are not settled, and the versions give agates, carbuncles, rubies, jasper and emeralds for the same words. The KJV has “I will make thy windows of agates, and thy gates of carbuncles”, the NIV “I will make your battlements of rubies, your gates of sparkling jewels”, the NWT 1984 “I will make your battlements of rubies, and your gates of fiery glowing stones”, the Douay “I will make thy bulwarks of jasper” and the Geneva “I will make thy windows of emeralds”. The foundation stones and gates of Revelation 21:19–21 (now on these pages) are built of the same kind of jewels.

⭐ Taught of Jehovah. “Taught of Jehovah” stands in this one verse (limmud), and Jesus quotes it at John 6:45 (now on these pages); “and great will be the peace of your sons” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “all thy children shall be taught of the Lord; and great shall be the peace of thy children”, the NIV “All your children will be taught by the Lord, and great will be their peace”, the NWT 1984 “all your sons will be persons taught by Jehovah” and the TLB “all your citizens shall be taught by me”.

⭐ Established in righteousness. “In righteousness you will be established” stands in this one verse (tzedaqah), “be far from oppression” stands in this one verse (esek), and “from terror”, mechittah, stands in this one verse (machtah). The KJV has “In righteousness shalt thou be established: thou shalt be far from oppression”, the NWT 1984 “You will prove to be firmly established in righteousness itself” and the Douay “And thou shalt be founded in justice: depart far from oppression”.

Verses 15–17 · Isaiah 54:15–17 if anyone stirs up strife — the smith and the destroyer — “no weapon formed against you”

⚠ “Stirs up strife.” “If anyone stirs up strife, it is nothing” stands in this one verse: the verb gur (gur) is spelled like the verb for sojourning, and the versions take it both ways. The KJV has “Behold, they shall surely gather together, but not by me: whosoever shall gather together against thee shall fall for thy sake”, the NIV “If anyone does attack you, it will not be my doing; whoever attacks you will surrender to you”, the NWT 1984 “If anyone should at all make an attack, it will not be at my orders”, the TLB “If any nation comes to fight you, it will not be sent by me to punish you”, the Geneva “the enemies shall gather himself, but without me” and the Douay “an inhabitant shall come, who was not with me”, which reads the verb as sojourning. This translation keeps the strife and says so.

⭐ The smith and the destroyer. “I have created the smith” stands in this one verse (charash), “who blows on the fire of coals” stands in this one verse, and “I have created the destroyer” stands in this one verse (mashchit). The same verb, bara, creates both the maker of the tool and the one who ruins, as it creates Jacob at Isaiah 43:1 (now on these pages). The KJV has “I have created the smith that bloweth the coals in the fire, and that bringeth forth an instrument for his work; and I have created the waster to destroy”, the NIV “it is I who created the blacksmith who fans the coals into flame”, the NWT 1984 “the craftsman, the one blowing upon the fire of charcoal” and the Douay “I have created the killer to destroy”.

⭐ “No weapon formed against you.” “Every weapon formed against you will not succeed” stands in this one verse, and “and every tongue that rises with you in judgment you will condemn” stands in this one verse. ⚠ The word for weapon, keli, is the word for any tool or vessel, the same word as the smith’s “tool for his work” in verse 16; and the verb is yutsar, “formed”, from yatsar, the potter’s and the maker’s verb. The KJV has “No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper; and every tongue that shall rise against thee in judgment thou shalt condemn”, the NIV “no weapon forged against you will prevail, and you will refute every tongue that accuses you”, the NWT 1984 “Any weapon whatever that will be formed against you will have no success”, the TLB “no weapon turned against you shall succeed”, the Geneva “all the weapons that are made against thee, shall not prosper” and the Douay “No weapon that is formed against thee shall prosper”.

⭐ The inheritance of the servants. “The inheritance of the servants of Jehovah” stands in this one verse (nachalah), “servants of Jehovah” standing in six verses, and “and their righteousness is from me” stands in this one verse. The servant of chapter 53 is one (Isaiah 53:11, now on these pages), and here the servants are many. ⚠ The word for righteousness is the one of verse 14, and the versions divide: the KJV has “their righteousness is of me”, the NIV “this is their vindication from me”, the Douay “their justice with me” and the TLB “This is the blessing I have given you”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “barren” for aqarah; “husband” for baal and “Maker” for oseh; “redeemer” for goel; “Jehovah of hosts” for YHWH tsevaot; “steadfast love” for chesed at verses 8 and 10; “covenant of peace” for berit shalom; “the waters of Noah”, as written; “weapon” for keli at verse 17 and “tool” for the same word at verse 16; “righteousness” for tsedaqah in verses 14 and 17; “Jehovah” for the Name, written eight times in the chapter.

Echoes paid. Genesis 11:30, 28:14 and 9:11; 1 Samuel 2:5; Galatians 4:27; Jeremiah 31:31; Isaiah 41:14, 43:1, 53:11 and 55:3; Revelation 21:19–21; John 6:45.

Echoes planted. Song of Solomon 5:14 and Ezekiel 27:16, the other places for sapphires and the pinnacles. Not translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Isaiah stands on these pages at chapters 1, 6, 7, 9, 11, 26, 40, 41, 43, 53, 54, 55, 58 and 61, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Isaiah 2.
