A father's teaching in one long sentence: if the son receives his sayings and seeks wisdom like silver and hidden treasures, he will find the fear of Jehovah, who gives wisdom and stores up practical wisdom for the upright; wisdom will rescue him from the man who speaks perversities and from the strange woman whose house sinks down to the shades, so that he walks in the way of the good; the upright will dwell in the land and the treacherous be torn up from it.
Attributed to Solomon, son of David (reigned c. 970–931 BC), like the rest of the book's opening collection; wisdom literature keeps no calendar of its own.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · “If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures” — sayings stored up, a voice lifted, and wisdom dug for like silver

If, if, if. The first eleven verses are built as one long condition: three “if”s (vv1, 3, 4), two “then”s (vv5, 9), and a “for” behind each (vv6, 10). After it come two clauses that begin “to rescue you” (vv12, 16), and the chapter ends with “so you will walk” (v20). It is a single argument from the first verse to the last. It also has twenty-two verses, the number of letters in the Hebrew alphabet, and its key words fall on two letters: im, “if”, and az, “then”, begin with alef, the first letter; lehatsilkha, “to rescue you”, and lema’an, “so”, begin with lamed, the letter that opens the second half of the alphabet. Some readers see an alphabet poem in that shape, though the chapter is not an acrostic. The ASV has “My son, if thou wilt receive my words, And lay up my commandments with thee”; the NWT 1984 “My son, if you will receive my sayings”.

Store up. “If you receive my sayings, and store up my commandments with you”: titspon, “store up”, is from tsafan, to hide a thing away to keep it safe. The verb comes back in v7, where Jehovah “stores up practical wisdom for the upright”: the son stores up his father’s commandments, and God stores up wisdom for the son. Amarai, “my sayings”, is the word of “receive my sayings” (Proverbs 4:10). The NWT 1984 has “treasure up my own commandments with yourself”; the KJV “and hide my commandments with thee”.

Lift your voice. “Making your ear attentive to wisdom, inclining your heart to discernment — yes, if you call out to understanding, and lift your voice for discernment”: binah is “understanding” and tevunah “discernment”, two words from one root, kept apart here as in chapter 3. Titten qolekha, “lift your voice”, is what Wisdom herself does in chapter 1, where “in the squares she lifts her voice” (Proverbs 1:20). In chapter 8 the call comes back the other way: “Does not wisdom call out, and discernment lift her voice?” (Proverbs 8:1). Here the son calls, and there Wisdom calls. The ASV has “Yea, if thou cry after discernment, And lift up thy voice for understanding”, with the two words the other way round; the NWT 1984 “if, moreover, you call out for understanding itself and you give forth your voice for discernment itself”. The Greek adds a line to each verse: “and you will apply it to the admonition of your son”, and “and seek perception with a loud voice”.

Silver and hidden treasures. “If you seek her like silver, and search for her like hidden treasures”: matmonim are things hidden in the ground, the word of Joseph’s steward, “Your God and the God of your father gave you treasure in your bags” (Genesis 43:23). Tachpesennah, “search for her”, is the verb of a search for something hidden: Laban “searched but did not find the teraphim” (Genesis 31:35), and the steward “searched” the brothers’ bags for the cup (Genesis 44:12). Job uses the same treasure-word of the wretched “who dig for it more than for hidden treasures”, and what they dig for is death (Job 3:21). The Greek has hōs thēsaurous, “as treasures”, the word of “in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden away” (Colossians 2:3). The KJV has “If thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures”; the Douay “and shalt dig for her as for a treasure”; the NIV “and search for it as for hidden treasure”, where the Hebrew has “her”.

Verses 5–8 · “For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding” — the search ends in God, practical wisdom stored up, a shield, and his loyal ones

Then. “Then you will understand the fear of Jehovah, and find the knowledge of God”: the search for wisdom ends in more than wisdom. “The fear of Jehovah” is the motto of the book, “the beginning of knowledge” (Proverbs 1:7; see yir’at YHVH), and da’at elohim, “the knowledge of God”, is da’at, knowing by acquaintance. The ASV has “Then shalt thou understand the fear of Jehovah, And find the knowledge of God”; the NWT 1984 “and you will find the very knowledge of God”.

From his mouth. “For Jehovah gives wisdom; from his mouth come knowledge and discernment”: the son searches, and Jehovah gives; the chapter does not choose between the two. Wisdom comes as speech, out of a mouth, as the father’s sayings do in v1. The Greek has “from his face”. The ASV has “For Jehovah giveth wisdom; Out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding”; the NWT 1984 “out of his mouth there are knowledge and discernment”.

Practical wisdom stored up. “He stores up practical wisdom for the upright, a shield to those who walk in integrity”: tushiyyah is “practical wisdom”, the wisdom that gets a thing done, as in Proverbs 3:21. The written text has ve-tsafan, “and he has stored up”; the text as read has yitspon, “he stores up”, the verb of v1. The Greek has “and he treasures up salvation for those who do right”, and the Douay, from the Latin, “He will keep the salvation of the righteous”. Magen, “a shield”, is God’s word to Abram, “I am a shield to you” (Genesis 15:1), and holkhei tom, “those who walk in integrity”, has the tom of Abimelech’s plea, “In the integrity of my heart” (Genesis 20:5). The ASV has “He layeth up sound wisdom for the upright; He is a shield to them that walk in integrity”; the NIV “He holds success in store for the upright”.

His loyal ones. “Guarding the paths of justice; and he keeps the way of his loyal ones”: the two verbs are the pair of Proverbs 4:6, natsar, “guard”, and shamar, “keep”. Chasidav, “his loyal ones”, are those who hold to chesed, loyal love; the psalm says Jehovah “will not forsake his loyal ones” (Psalm 37:28). The word is written without the letter that marks the plural, so the letters alone could read “his loyal one”, and the reading tradition gives the plural. That written spelling stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, both with the verb shamar: this one, and Hannah’s song, “The feet of his faithful ones he guards” (1 Samuel 2:9). The NWT 1984 has “and he will guard the very way of his loyal ones”; the ASV “And preserve the way of his saints”.

Verses 9–11 · “Then shalt thou understand righteousness, and judgment, and equity; yea, every good path” — the three words of the prologue, wisdom coming in, and a guard on the door

The three words of 1:3. “Then you will understand righteousness and justice and fairness, every good track”: tsedeq u-mishpat u-meisharim are the three words the book’s prologue promises, “righteousness, justice, and fairness” (Proverbs 1:3). Together they stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, that one and this one. Ma’gal is a track, the rut a wheel cuts in a road, as in “tracks of uprightness” (Proverbs 4:11). The Greek hears the wheel too, and has “you will set straight all good axles”, axonas; it uses the same word for the woman’s tracks in v18. The ASV has “Then shalt thou understand righteousness and justice, And equity, yea, every good path”; the NWT 1984 “and uprightness, the entire course of what is good”.

Wisdom comes in. “For wisdom will come into your heart, and knowledge will be pleasant to your soul”: yin’am, “will be pleasant”, is the verb of sweetness and delight. The son went out searching in v4; here wisdom comes in to him. Nefesh, “soul”, is the living self. The NIV has “For wisdom will enter your heart, and knowledge will be pleasant to your soul”; the KJV reads ki as “when”, “When wisdom entereth into thine heart”, making a new condition where this translation reads a reason, “for”.

A guard on the door. “Discretion will keep watch over you, discernment will guard you”: mezimmah is “discretion”, as in Proverbs 1:4, and the verbs are shamar and natsar again. In v8 Jehovah guarded and kept; here the son’s own discretion and discernment do it. The ASV has “Discretion shall watch over thee; Understanding shall keep thee”; the NWT 1984 “thinking ability itself will keep guard over you, discernment itself will safeguard you”. The Greek has “good counsel will guard you, and holy thought will keep you”.

Verses 12–15 · “To deliver thee from the way of the evil man” — perversities, paths forsaken, and the crooked and the devious

To rescue you. “To rescue you from the way of evil, from the man who speaks perversities”: lehatsilkha, “to rescue you”, is from natsal, to snatch out, and it begins two verses, this one and v16: there are two dangers, a man and a woman. Tahpukhot, “perversities”, is from hafakh, to turn over: speech turned upside down. It is “a man of perversities” in Proverbs 16:28 and “a generation of perversity” in Moses’ song (Deuteronomy 32:20). The ASV has “From the men that speak perverse things”; the KJV “from the man that speaketh froward things”. The Greek has “from a man who speaks nothing trustworthy”.

Paths forsaken. “From those who forsake the paths of uprightness to walk in the ways of darkness”: ha-ozvim, “who forsake”, is the verb that returns in v17 for the woman “who forsakes the close friend of her youth”. The men forsake a road, and she forsakes a person. The ways of darkness are the way of the wicked in chapter 4, “like deep darkness” (Proverbs 4:19). The ASV has “Who forsake the paths of uprightness, To walk in the ways of darkness”; the NWT 1984 “from those leaving the paths of uprightness”.

Joy in evil. “Who rejoice in doing evil, and delight in the perversities of evil”: yagilu, “delight”, is the verb the psalms use for exulting in God and in his king, turned here to exulting in harm. The NWT 1984 has “who are joyful in the perverse things of badness”; the KJV “and delight in the frowardness of the wicked”.

Crooked and devious. “Whose paths are crooked, and who are devious in their tracks”: iqqeshim, “crooked”, is the root of “the crooked” in Proverbs 22:5 and of “crookedness of mouth” in 4:24; nelozim, “devious”, is from luz, to turn aside, the root of “let them not slip from your eyes” (3:21) and of “the devious is an abomination to Jehovah” (3:32). The Greek has “their wheel-tracks are bent”. The NWT 1984 has “those whose paths are crooked and who are devious in their general course”; the ASV “Who are crooked in their ways, And wayward in their paths”.

Verses 16–19 · “To deliver thee from the strange woman, even from the stranger which flattereth with her words” — who she is, the close friend of her youth, and a house that sinks to the shades

The strange woman. “To rescue you from the strange woman, from the foreign woman who makes her sayings smooth”: ishah zarah is a woman who belongs elsewhere, “strange” in its old sense, the word of the “strange fire” of Leviticus 10:1; nokhriyyah is the feminine of nokhri, a foreigner. Who she is, readings differ: a foreign woman in the plain sense; a woman outside a man’s own marriage, like the wife in chapter 7 whose husband “is not at home” (Proverbs 7:19); or a figure, the opposite of Wisdom. The Greek reads her as an idea and has no woman here at all: “to make you far from the straight way and a stranger to righteous judgment. Son, let not bad counsel overtake you”. Amareha, “her sayings”, is the noun of “my sayings” in v1, so two voices offer sayings, the father’s and hers; hechelikah, “makes smooth”, is to make slippery. The line stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, and word for word before the story of chapter 7 (Proverbs 7:5). The KJV has “To deliver thee from the strange woman, even from the stranger which flattereth with her words”; the NWT 1984 “from the foreign woman who has made her own sayings smooth”; the NIV chooses one reading, “the adulterous woman”, and the TLB another, “prostitutes”.

The close friend of her youth. “Who forsakes the close friend of her youth, and has forgotten the covenant of her God”: alluf is the clan-chief of Edom in Genesis 36:15 and the “close friends” a whisperer separates in Proverbs 16:28; here it is the man she married young. With “youth” it stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Jeremiah, where Israel calls to God, “My father, you are the close friend of my youth” (Jeremiah 3:4). Joel has “the husband of her youth” (Joel 1:8). “The covenant of her God” is read two ways: the marriage itself, made before God as a covenant, as in Malachi’s “the wife of your covenant” (Malachi 2:14); or Israel’s covenant with God, whose commandments forbid adultery. The KJV has “Which forsaketh the guide of her youth”; the ASV “That forsaketh the friend of her youth”; the NWT 1984 “who is leaving the confidential friend of her youth”; the NIV “and ignored the covenant she made before God”. The Greek has “bad counsel, which forsakes the teaching of youth and has forgotten the divine covenant”.

Down to the shades. “For her house sinks down to death, and her tracks to the shades”: shachah, “sinks down”, is a feminine verb, and “house” is masculine in Hebrew, so readings differ: some take her house as the subject, as this translation does, and some read “she sinks down to death, which is her house”. Rephaim are the shades, the faint dead, as in “the shades do not rise” (Isaiah 26:14). The Greek has “for she has set her house beside death, and her axles beside Hades with the earth-born”: gēgeneis, “earth-born”, is the word Sophocles uses for “the earth-born host of the Giants”, and rephaim is also the name of the giant clans of old (see the encyclopedia’s Rephaim). The NWT 1984 has “For down to death her house does sink and down to those impotent in death her tracks”; the NIV “and her paths to the spirits of the dead”; the Douay “and her paths to hell”.

None come back. “None who go in to her come back, nor do they reach the paths of life”: ba’eha, “those who go in to her”, is the idiom for sleeping with a woman, as in the law of the brother-in-law, who “shall go in to her” (Deuteronomy 25:5). Yasigu, “reach”, is to overtake, the verb of the blessings that “shall come upon you and overtake you” (Deuteronomy 28:2). The Greek adds “for they are not taken hold of by the years of life”. The NWT 1984 has “None of those having relations with her will come back”; the ASV “None that go unto her return again, Neither do they attain unto the paths of life”.

Verses 20–22 · “For the upright shall dwell in the land, and the perfect shall remain in it” — the way of the good, the land, and the treacherous torn up

So you will walk. “So you will walk in the way of the good, and keep the paths of the righteous”: lema’an, “so”, closes the argument that began with “if” in v1. The son now does the keeping, tishmor, that Jehovah did for him in v8 and discretion did in v11. The ASV has “That thou mayest walk in the way of good men, And keep the paths of the righteous”; the NWT 1984 “The purpose is that you may walk in the way of good people”.

Dwell in the land. “For the upright will dwell in the land, and the blameless will be left in it”: yishkenu, “will dwell”, is the verb of “The righteous will possess the land and dwell upon it forever” (Psalm 37:29); temimim, “the blameless”, are the whole, as Noah was “blameless in his generation” (Genesis 6:9); and yivvateru, “will be left”, is from yatar, to be left over, a survivors’ word. Erets is both “land” and “earth”; on that word and Jesus’ “they shall inherit the earth”, see Psalm 37:11. Later in the book it is turned around: “the wicked will not dwell in the land” (Proverbs 10:30). The ASV has “For the upright shall dwell in the land, And the perfect shall remain in it”; the NWT 1984 “For the upright are the ones that will reside in the earth, and the blameless are the ones that will be left over in it”. The Greek says it twice: “the kind will be inhabitants of the land, and the innocent will be left in it; for the upright will dwell in the land, and the holy will be left in it”.

Cut off and torn up. “But the wicked will be cut off from the land, and the treacherous will be torn up from it”: yikkaretu, “will be cut off”, is the refrain of the psalm, “evildoers will be cut off” (Psalm 37:9); yissechu, “will be torn up”, is from nasach, the verb of the covenant curse, “you will be torn up from off the ground” (Deuteronomy 28:63). Bogdim, “the treacherous”, are those who break faith, and the verb is Malachi’s for a man who “dealt treacherously” with the wife of his youth (Malachi 2:14), the other side of v17. The ASV has “But the wicked shall be cut off from the land, And the treacherous shall be rooted out of it”; the KJV “and the transgressors shall be rooted out of it”; the NWT 1984 “and as for the treacherous, they will be torn away from it”. The Greek has “the ways of the ungodly will perish from the land, and the lawless will be driven out of it”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “my sayings” for amarai, as in 4:10, and “her sayings” for the same noun in v16; “store up” for tsafan, both for the son (v1) and for Jehovah (v7); “understanding” for binah and “discernment” for tevunah; “hidden treasures” for matmonim; “practical wisdom” for tushiyyah, as in 3:21; “guard” for natsar and “keep” for shamar, as in 4:6; “loyal ones” for chasidim, as in Psalm 37:28; “righteousness and justice and fairness”, the three words of 1:3; “track” for ma’gal, “path” for orach and “way” for derekh; “discretion” for mezimmah; “perversities” for tahpukhot, as in 16:28; “devious” for the naloz of 3:32; “the strange woman” and “the foreign woman”; “the close friend of her youth” for alluf ne’ureha; “the shades” for rephaim, as in Isaiah 26:14; and “torn up” for nasach, as in Deuteronomy 28:63.

Echoes paid. Wisdom lifting her voice (1:20); the fear of Jehovah of 1:7, the discretion of 1:4 and the three words of 1:3; the sayings received in 4:10, the keep-and-guard of 4:6, the tracks of 4:11, the darkness of 4:19 and the crookedness of 4:24; practical wisdom, the slipping eyes and the devious of 3:21 and 3:32; the treasure in the bags of Genesis 43:23 and the searches of Genesis 31:35 and 44:12; the shield of Genesis 15:1, the integrity of Genesis 20:5 and the blameless Noah of Genesis 6:9; the loyal ones of Psalm 37:28 and Hannah’s faithful ones (1 Samuel 2:9); the man of perversities of 16:28 and the generation of perversity of Deuteronomy 32:20; the crooked of 22:5; the chiefs of Edom (Genesis 36:15), the close friends of 16:28 and the husband of her youth of Joel 1:8; the strange fire of Leviticus 10:1; the shades of Isaiah 26:14; the brother-in-law’s law of Deuteronomy 25:5 and the blessings that overtake in Deuteronomy 28:2; dwelling and being cut off in Psalm 37:9, 11 and 29; the torn up of Deuteronomy 28:63; and the treasures of Colossians 2:3.

Echoes planted. Wisdom calling with her voice raised (Proverbs 8:1); the hidden treasures of Job 3:21; the strange woman’s smooth sayings again, and the husband not at home (Proverbs 7:5 and 7:19); the close friend of my youth (Jeremiah 3:4); the wife of your covenant, dealt with treacherously (Malachi 2:14); and the wicked who will not dwell in the land (Proverbs 10:30). None is translated here yet.
