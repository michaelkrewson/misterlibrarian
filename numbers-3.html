🕰
The Levites' own census and assignment. Aaron's line is narrowed to two sons — Nadab and Abihu already dead for offering strange fire, leaving Eleazar and Ithamar to carry the priesthood forward. The tribe of Levi is formally taken in place of Israel's firstborn, then counted by clan from a single month old: Gershon (7,500, camped west, charged with the tabernacle's coverings and curtains), Kohath (8,600, camped south, charged with the ark, table, lampstand, and altars — the holiest furniture, under Eleazar's own direct oversight), and Merari (6,200, camped north, charged with the structural frames, bars, and pillars), with Moses, Aaron, and his sons camped east before the entrance itself. The chapter closes on an exact transaction: 22,273 firstborn Israelites against 22,000 Levites, the 273-person shortfall redeemed at five shekels a head, 1,365 shekels paid to Aaron's sons.
⚠ Undated within the chapter, like the census and camp arrangement around it. Still inside the same Sinai window as Numbers 1 and 2 (already on these pages).

Numbers 3 — The Mister Translation

1
וְאֵלֶּה תּוֹלְדֹת אַהֲרֹן, וּמֹשֶׁה: בְּיוֹם, דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֶת-מֹשֶׁה--בְּהַר סִינָי.
And these are the generations of Aaron and Moses, in the day that Jehovah spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai.note
Estas son las generaciones de Aarón y de Moisés, en el día en que Jehová habló con Moisés en el monte Sinaí.
2
וְאֵלֶּה שְׁמוֹת בְּנֵי-אַהֲרֹן, הַבְּכֹר נָדָב, וַאֲבִיהוּא, אֶלְעָזָר וְאִיתָמָר.
And these are the names of the sons of Aaron: the firstborn Nadab, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar.note
Y estos son los nombres de los hijos de Aarón: el primogénito Nadab, y Abíu, Eleazar e Itamar.
3
אֵלֶּה, שְׁמוֹת בְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן, הַכֹּהֲנִים, הַמְּשֻׁחִים--אֲשֶׁר-מִלֵּא יָדָם, לְכַהֵן.
These are the names of the sons of Aaron, the anointed priests, whom he ordained to minister as priests.note
Estos son los nombres de los hijos de Aarón, los sacerdotes ungidos, a quienes él consagró para ministrar como sacerdotes.
4
וַיָּמָת נָדָב וַאֲבִיהוּא לִפְנֵי יְהוָה בְּהַקְרִבָם אֵשׁ זָרָה לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, בְּמִדְבַּר סִינַי, וּבָנִים, לֹא-הָיוּ לָהֶם; וַיְכַהֵן אֶלְעָזָר וְאִיתָמָר, עַל-פְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן אֲבִיהֶם. {פ}
But Nadab and Abihu died before Jehovah, when they offered strange fire before Jehovah in the wilderness of Sinai, and they had no children; and Eleazar and Ithamar ministered as priests in the presence of Aaron their father. {פ}note
Pero Nadab y Abíu murieron delante de Jehová, cuando ofrecieron fuego extraño delante de Jehová en el desierto de Sinaí, y no tuvieron hijos; y Eleazar e Itamar ministraron como sacerdotes en presencia de Aarón su padre.

Y guardarán su encargo, y el encargo de toda la congregación, delante del tabernáculo de reunión, para hacer el servicio del tabernáculo.

And you shall give the Levites to Aaron and to his sons: they are wholly given to him from among the children of Israel.note
Y darás los levitas a Aarón y a sus hijos: enteramente dados le son de entre los hijos de Israel.

And you shall appoint Aaron and his sons, and they shall keep their priesthood; and the outsider who draws near shall be put to death. {פ}note
Y designarás a Aarón y a sus hijos, y guardarán su sacerdocio; y el extraño que se acerque morirá.
11
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה, אֶל-מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר.
And Jehovah spoke to Moses, saying:note
Jehová habló a Moisés, diciendo:
12

And I, behold, I have taken the Levites from among the children of Israel, in place of every firstborn that opens the womb among the children of Israel; and the Levites shall be mine.note
Y yo, he aquí, he tomado a los levitas de entre los hijos de Israel, en lugar de todo primogénito que abre matriz entre los hijos de Israel; y los levitas serán míos.

13

Porque mío es todo primogénito: el día en que yo herí a todo primogénito en la tierra de Egipto, consagré para mí a todo primogénito en Israel, sea hombre o animal; míos serán: yo soy Jehová.
14
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, בְּמִדְבַּר סִינַי לֵאמֹר.
And Jehovah spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai, saying:note
Jehová habló a Moisés en el desierto de Sinaí, diciendo:

Y Moisés los recluta conforme a la palabra de Jehová, como se le mandó.

And the sons of Kohath, by their clans: Amram, and Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel.note
Y los hijos de Coat, por sus clanes: Amram e Izhar, Hebrón y Uziel.

Of Gershon was the clan of the Libnites, and the clan of the Shimeites: these are the clans of the Gershonites.note
De Gersón procedía el clan de los libnitas, y el clan de los simeitas: estos son los clanes de los gersonitas.
22
פְּקֻדֵיהֶם בְּמִסְפַּר כָּל-זָכָר, מִבֶּן-חֹדֶשׁ וָמָעְלָה; פְּקֻדֵיהֶם, שִׁבְעַת אֲלָפִים וַחֲמֵשׁ מֵאוֹת.

Los reclutados de ellos, en el número de todo varón de un mes para arriba, los reclutados de ellos eran siete mil quinientos.
23

Y el jefe de la casa paterna de los clanes de los coatitas será Elizafán hijo de Uziel.

Y su encargo será el arca, y la mesa, y el candelabro, y los altares, y los utensilios del santuario con que ministran, y la cortina, y todo su servicio.
32
וּנְשִׂיא נְשִׂיאֵי הַלֵּוִי, אֶלְעָזָר בֶּן-אַהֲרֹן הַכֹּהֵן, פְּקֻדַּת, שֹׁמְרֵי מִשְׁמֶרֶת הַקֹּדֶשׁ.
And Eleazar son of Aaron the priest shall be chieftain over the chieftains of Levi, appointed over those who keep the charge of the sanctuary.note
Y Eleazar hijo de Aarón el sacerdote será jefe sobre los jefes de Leví, encargado de los que guardan el encargo del santuario.
33
לִמְרָרִי--מִשְׁפַּחַת הַמַּחְלִי, וּמִשְׁפַּחַת הַמּוּשִׁי; אֵלֶּה הֵם, מִשְׁפְּחֹת מְרָרִי.

De Merari procedía el clan de los mahlitas, y el clan de los musitas: estos son los clanes de Merari.

Numbers 3 meaning — two dead sons, and the reason stated in one clause

⚠ V4 disposes of Nadab and Abihu in a single relative clause — ‘they had no children’ — before moving straight on to Eleazar and Ithamar. This project's own note on Leviticus 10:1-2 (already on these pages) already told the story this verse only summarizes: the same fire that consumed the altar's first offering consumed the two priests who offered it wrong. Nothing here softens or expands that account; it simply confirms, one chapter of the Torah later, that the consequence was permanent — no line continues from Aaron's oldest sons, and the priesthood runs through the two who are left.

Given, given — a doubled word for a total gift

⚠ V9's ‘wholly given’ (netunim netunim) doubles the same participle back to back — not ‘given’ once but ‘given, given,’ the same intensifying repetition this project has already flagged in phrases like Genesis 1's own ‘swarm with swarms’ (already on these pages). KJV and ASV both render it ‘wholly given,’ folding the doubling into a single adverb; NWT keeps the repetition visible instead, ‘given ones, given.’ The Levites are not lent to Aaron for a task; they are transferred, entirely, the doubling itself doing the work an English adverb would otherwise have to supply.

The trade explained — a tribe for the firstborn of a nation

This is the transaction Numbers 2's own note on Ephraim and Manasseh (already on these pages) already assumed and Leviticus 27's own note on the firstborn (already on these pages) already flagged as unrepeatable: this project's own note on Exodus 13:1-2 (already on these pages) first consecrated every Israelite firstborn to Jehovah on the night the plague passed over their houses. That claim was never rescinded; here it is finally settled by substitution — one whole tribe, taken as a body, standing in for every firstborn son in every other tribe. The Levites do not earn this status; they are simply designated to carry it.

Three sons, three clans, one family tree spelled out twice

Levi's own three sons — Gershon, Kohath, Merari — each get a clan named for their own sons in turn, the identical genealogical structure this project has already tracked through Genesis's own toledot formula. Unlike the fighting tribes of Numbers 1 (already on these pages), counted only from twenty years old, the Levites are counted from a single month old — every male infant already belongs to the census, because Levite status is inherited at birth, not earned at fighting age.

Gershon: the fabric of the tent, camped behind it

Gershon's own charge is the tabernacle's soft architecture — its coverings, curtains, and screens, the fabric that gives the tent its actual shape. Fittingly, Gershon's own clan camps on the tabernacle's own west side, ‘behind’ it relative to the entrance — the same ‘seaward’ direction this project's own note on Numbers 2:18 (already on these pages) already named for Ephraim's standard, here repurposed for the Levite clan whose own work covers the building's own skin.

Kohath: the holiest objects, and a chief over chiefs

⚠ Kohath's own charge is the single most dangerous assignment in the whole camp: the ark, the table, the lampstand, the altars — the actual furniture that sat closest to the presence itself. V32 gives their own supervisor a title that appears nowhere else among the chapters translated so far, ‘chieftain over the chieftains of Levi’ (nesi nesi'ei ha-Levi) — Eleazar, Aaron's own surviving elder son, ranked above the other two clan-chiefs specifically because his own clan's cargo carries the greatest risk. Numbers 4 (already on these pages) spells out exactly how carefully that cargo has to be wrapped before Kohath's own sons may even look at it.

Merari: the heavy structure, and the tent's own inner ring closed

Merari's own charge is the tabernacle's skeleton — frames, bars, pillars, sockets, the structural weight nobody sees once the fabric goes up. With Merari camped north and Moses, Aaron, and his sons camped east directly before the entrance, the geometry Numbers 2's own note (already on these pages) already described in words is now complete in every direction: Gershon west, Kohath south, Merari north, the priesthood itself east, and at the center, still, the tent. ⚠ V39's total, 22,000, is rounder than it looks: Gershon's 7,500 plus Kohath's 8,600 plus Merari's 6,200 actually sum to 22,300 — a 300-person gap the text itself never explains. Later interpreters (the tradition is old, though outside this project's own source text) suggested the 300 were themselves firstborn Levites, ineligible to redeem anyone else since they still needed redemption of their own; this translation prints the numbers exactly as the text gives them and flags the gap rather than closing it, the same posture already taken toward the eleph ambiguity in Numbers 1 (already on these pages).

22,273 against 22,000 — the math that ends the chapter

The whole chapter has been building to a single subtraction: 22,273 firstborn Israelite males against 22,000 Levites, a shortfall of exactly 273 the Levites cannot cover one-for-one. Those 273 are redeemed in cash instead of in person — five shekels a head, the identical gulgolet, ‘skull,’ this project's own note on Numbers 1 (already on these pages) already tied to Golgotha — for a total of 1,365 shekels, paid to Aaron's own sons. The arithmetic is exact and stated twice, once as a count and once as a total, leaving nothing for the reader to calculate independently: a nation's worth of firstborn sons, priced to the shekel.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English Numbers 3 both run 1–51 — verified against the Masoretic text, no versification shift. Seven Masoretic paragraphs divide it: open after v4, v10, v13; closed after v26, v39; open after v43, v51. One new dictionary entry: mishmeret, ‘charge,’ the word this chapter repeats more than any other. Established renderings continued from Numbers 1–2 (already on these pages): paqad as ‘muster,’ matteh as ‘tribe,’ nasi as ‘chieftain,’ eda as ‘congregation,’ gulgolet for the ‘head by head’ count.

Echoes paid. Leviticus 10:1-2's account of Nadab and Abihu's death (already on these pages), here summarized in one clause rather than retold; Exodus 13:1-2's consecration of the firstborn (already on these pages), finally settled by substitution; Genesis 1's own doubling construction (already on these pages), reused here for ‘given, given’; Numbers 1's own eleph ambiguity and gulgolet/Golgotha note (both already on these pages), both reused for this chapter's own numbers; and Numbers 2's own camp geometry and ‘seaward’ direction (both already on these pages), completed here with the Levites filling in the ring the outer tribes already surrounded. Numbers 4 (already on these pages) also now details exactly how the Kohathites must wrap the sanctuary's holiest objects before carrying them, on pain of death for looking too soon.
