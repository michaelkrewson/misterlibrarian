“Bring up Samuel for me”: the Philistines gather at Shunem, and Achish tells David he will march with him. Samuel is dead and Saul has removed the mediums from the land, but camped on Gilboa and answered by no dream, Urim or prophet, he goes in disguise by night to a woman at En-dor who is mistress of a ghost. She sees Samuel come up, an old man wrapped in a robe, who tells Saul that Jehovah has torn the kingdom from him and given it to David, and that tomorrow he and his sons will be with him. The woman feeds the fallen king, and they go out into the night.
The night before the battle of Gilboa (31), while David is with the Philistines at Aphek (29).

And it happened in those days that the Philistines gathered their camps for war, to fight against Israel. And Achish said to David: ‘You must surely know that you will go out with me in the camp, you and your men.’note
Y sucedió en aquellos días que los filisteos juntaron sus campamentos para la guerra, para pelear contra Israel. Y Aquis dijo a David: «De cierto has de saber que saldrás conmigo en el campamento, tú y tus hombres».

וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, אֶל-אָכִישׁ, לָכֵן אַתָּה תֵדַע, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-יַעֲשֶׂה עַבְדֶּךָ; וַיֹּאמֶר אָכִישׁ, אֶל-דָּוִד, לָכֵן שֹׁמֵר לְרֹאשִׁי אֲשִׂימְךָ, כָּל-הַיָּמִים. {פ}
And David said to Achish: ‘Therefore you will know what your servant will do.’ And Achish said to David: ‘Therefore I will make you keeper of my head all the days.’note
Y David dijo a Aquis: «Por eso tú sabrás lo que hará tu siervo». Y Aquis dijo a David: «Por eso te pondré por guarda de mi cabeza todos los días».
3
וּשְׁמוּאֵל מֵת--וַיִּסְפְּדוּ-לוֹ כָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיִּקְבְּרֻהוּ בָרָמָה וּבְעִירוֹ; וְשָׁאוּל, הֵסִיר הָאֹבוֹת וְאֶת-הַיִּדְּעֹנִים--מֵהָאָרֶץ.

Y Samuel había muerto, y todo Israel lo había lamentado y lo había sepultado en Ramá, en su propia ciudad. Y Saúl había quitado de la tierra a los médiums y a los espiritistas.

Y los filisteos se juntaron, y vinieron y acamparon en Sunem; y Saúl juntó a todo Israel, y acamparon en Gilboa.

Y Saúl vio el campamento de los filisteos, y tuvo miedo, y su corazón tembló en gran manera.

וַיִּשְׁאַל שָׁאוּל בַּיהוָה, וְלֹא עָנָהוּ יְהוָה--גַּם בַּחֲלֹמוֹת גַּם בָּאוּרִים, גַּם בַּנְּבִיאִם.

Y Saúl preguntó a Jehová, y Jehová no le respondió, ni por sueños, ni por el Urim, ni por profetas.

וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל לַעֲבָדָיו, בַּקְּשׁוּ-לִי אֵשֶׁת בַּעֲלַת-אוֹב, וְאֵלְכָה אֵלֶיהָ, וְאֶדְרְשָׁה-בָּהּ; וַיֹּאמְרוּ עֲבָדָיו אֵלָיו, הִנֵּה אֵשֶׁת בַּעֲלַת-אוֹב בְּעֵין דּוֹר.

Y Saúl dijo a sus siervos: «Búsquenme una mujer que sea dueña de un espíritu de muerto, y que yo vaya a ella y consulte por medio de ella». Y sus siervos le dijeron: «Mira, hay una mujer dueña de un espíritu de muerto en En-dor».
8
וַיִּתְחַפֵּשׂ שָׁאוּל, וַיִּלְבַּשׁ בְּגָדִים אֲחֵרִים, וַיֵּלֶךְ הוּא וּשְׁנֵי אֲנָשִׁים עִמּוֹ, וַיָּבֹאוּ אֶל-הָאִשָּׁה לָיְלָה; וַיֹּאמֶר, קסומי- קָסֳמִי- נָא לִי בָּאוֹב, וְהַעֲלִי לִי, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-אֹמַר אֵלָיִךְ.
And Saul disguised himself, and put on other garments, and went, he and two men with him, and they came to the woman by night. And he said: ‘Divine for me, please, by the ghost, and bring up for me the one I name to you.’note
Y Saúl se disfrazó, y se puso otras ropas, y fue él y dos hombres con él, y llegaron a la mujer de noche. Y él dijo: «Adivíname, te ruego, por el espíritu de muerto, y hazme subir al que yo te nombre».
9
וַתֹּאמֶר הָאִשָּׁה אֵלָיו, הִנֵּה אַתָּה יָדַעְתָּ אֵת אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה שָׁאוּל, אֲשֶׁר הִכְרִית אֶת-הָאֹבוֹת וְאֶת-הַיִּדְּעֹנִי, מִן-הָאָרֶץ; וְלָמָה אַתָּה מִתְנַקֵּשׁ בְּנַפְשִׁי, לַהֲמִיתֵנִי.
And the woman said to him: ‘Look — you know what Saul has done, how he has cut off the mediums and the spiritists from the land. Why then are you laying a snare for my life, to have me put to death?’note
Y la mujer le dijo: «Mira, tú sabes lo que ha hecho Saúl, cómo ha cortado de la tierra a los médiums y a los espiritistas. ¿Por qué, pues, tiendes un lazo a mi vida, para hacerme morir?».
10
וַיִּשָּׁבַע לָהּ שָׁאוּל, בַּיהוָה לֵאמֹר: חַי-יְהוָה, אִם-יִקְּרֵךְ עָוֺן בַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה.
And Saul swore to her by Jehovah, saying: ‘As Jehovah lives, no guilt shall befall you in this matter.’note
Y Saúl le juró por Jehová, diciendo: «Vive Jehová, que ninguna culpa te sobrevendrá por este asunto».

וַתֹּאמֶר, הָאִשָּׁה, אֶת-מִי, אַעֲלֶה-לָּךְ; וַיֹּאמֶר, אֶת-שְׁמוּאֵל הַעֲלִי-לִי.

Y la mujer dijo: «¿A quién te haré subir?». Y él dijo: «Hazme subir a Samuel».

Y la mujer vio a Samuel, y clamó a gran voz; y la mujer dijo a Saúl: «¿Por qué me has engañado? ¡Tú eres Saúl!».

Y el rey le dijo: «No temas; pero ¿qué ves?». Y la mujer dijo a Saúl: «Veo dioses que suben de la tierra».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר לָהּ, מַה-תָּאֳרוֹ, וַתֹּאמֶר אִישׁ זָקֵן עֹלֶה, וְהוּא עֹטֶה מְעִיל; וַיֵּדַע שָׁאוּל כִּי-שְׁמוּאֵל הוּא, וַיִּקֹּד אַפַּיִם אַרְצָה וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ. {ס}
And he said to her: ‘What is his form?’ And she said: ‘An old man is coming up, and he is wrapped in a robe.’ And Saul knew that it was Samuel, and he bowed with his face to the ground and prostrated himself.note
Y él le dijo: «¿Cuál es su forma?». Y ella dijo: «Sube un hombre anciano, y va envuelto en un manto». Y Saúl supo que era Samuel, y se inclinó rostro a tierra y se postró.
15
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל אֶל-שָׁאוּל, לָמָּה הִרְגַּזְתַּנִי לְהַעֲלוֹת אֹתִי; וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל צַר-לִי מְאֹד וּפְלִשְׁתִּים נִלְחָמִים בִּי, וֵאלֹהִים סָר מֵעָלַי וְלֹא-עָנָנִי עוֹד גַּם בְּיַד-הַנְּבִיאִים גַּם-בַּחֲלֹמוֹת, וָאֶקְרָאֶה לְךָ, לְהוֹדִיעֵנִי מָה אֶעֱשֶׂה. {ס}
And Samuel said to Saul: ‘Why have you disturbed me, by bringing me up?’ And Saul said: ‘I am in great distress; for the Philistines are fighting against me, and God has turned away from me and does not answer me any more, either by the hand of the prophets or by dreams. So I have called you, to let me know what I shall do.’note
Y Samuel dijo a Saúl: «¿Por qué me has perturbado, haciéndome subir?». Y Saúl dijo: «Estoy en gran angustia; porque los filisteos pelean contra mí, y Dios se ha apartado de mí y ya no me responde, ni por mano de los profetas ni por sueños. Por eso te he llamado, para que me hagas saber lo que he de hacer».
16
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל, וְלָמָּה תִּשְׁאָלֵנִי: וַיהוָה סָר מֵעָלֶיךָ, וַיְהִי עָרֶךָ.
And Samuel said: ‘Why then do you ask me, when Jehovah has turned away from you and has become your adversary?note
Y Samuel dijo: «¿Y por qué me preguntas a mí, si Jehová se ha apartado de ti y se ha vuelto tu adversario?
17
וַיַּעַשׂ יְהוָה לוֹ, כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר בְּיָדִי; וַיִּקְרַע יְהוָה אֶת-הַמַּמְלָכָה מִיָּדֶךָ, וַיִּתְּנָהּ לְרֵעֲךָ לְדָוִד.
And Jehovah has done for himself as he spoke by my hand: Jehovah has torn the kingdom out of your hand, and has given it to your neighbour, to David.note
Y Jehová ha hecho para sí como habló por mi mano: Jehová ha rasgado el reino de tu mano, y lo ha dado a tu prójimo, a David.
18
כַּאֲשֶׁר לֹא-שָׁמַעְתָּ בְּקוֹל יְהוָה, וְלֹא-עָשִׂיתָ חֲרוֹן-אַפּוֹ בַּעֲמָלֵק; עַל-כֵּן הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, עָשָׂה-לְךָ יְהוָה הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
Because you did not listen to the voice of Jehovah, and did not carry out his burning anger against Amalek, therefore Jehovah has done this thing to you this day.note
Porque no escuchaste la voz de Jehová, ni ejecutaste el ardor de su ira contra Amalec, por eso Jehová te ha hecho esto hoy.
19
וְיִתֵּן יְהוָה גַּם אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל עִמְּךָ, בְּיַד-פְּלִשְׁתִּים, וּמָחָר, אַתָּה וּבָנֶיךָ עִמִּי; גַּם אֶת-מַחֲנֵה יִשְׂרָאֵל, יִתֵּן יְהוָה בְּיַד-פְּלִשְׁתִּים.
And Jehovah will give Israel also, with you, into the hand of the Philistines; and tomorrow you and your sons will be with me. The camp of Israel also Jehovah will give into the hand of the Philistines.’note
Y Jehová entregará también a Israel, contigo, en mano de los filisteos; y mañana tú y tus hijos estarán conmigo. También el campamento de Israel lo entregará Jehová en mano de los filisteos».
20
וַיְמַהֵר שָׁאוּל, וַיִּפֹּל מְלֹא-קוֹמָתוֹ אַרְצָה, וַיִּרָא מְאֹד, מִדִּבְרֵי שְׁמוּאֵל; גַּם-כֹּחַ, לֹא-הָיָה בוֹ--כִּי לֹא אָכַל לֶחֶם, כָּל-הַיּוֹם וְכָל-הַלָּיְלָה.

Y Saúl cayó en seguida a tierra cuan alto era, y tuvo gran temor por las palabras de Samuel; y tampoco había fuerza en él, porque no había comido pan en todo el día ni en toda la noche.
21
וַתָּבוֹא הָאִשָּׁה אֶל-שָׁאוּל, וַתֵּרֶא כִּי-נִבְהַל מְאֹד; וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, הִנֵּה שָׁמְעָה שִׁפְחָתְךָ בְּקוֹלֶךָ, וָאָשִׂים נַפְשִׁי בְּכַפִּי, וָאֶשְׁמַע אֶת-דְּבָרֶיךָ אֲשֶׁר דִּבַּרְתָּ אֵלָי.
And the woman came to Saul, and saw that he was greatly terrified; and she said to him: ‘Look — your maidservant has listened to your voice, and I put my life in my palm, and listened to your words that you spoke to me.note
Y la mujer vino a Saúl, y vio que estaba muy aterrado; y le dijo: «Mira, tu sierva ha escuchado tu voz, y puse mi vida en mi palma, y escuché tus palabras que me dijiste.
22
וְעַתָּה, שְׁמַע-נָא גַם-אַתָּה בְּקוֹל שִׁפְחָתֶךָ, וְאָשִׂמָה לְפָנֶיךָ פַּת-לֶחֶם, וֶאֱכוֹל; וִיהִי בְךָ כֹּחַ, כִּי תֵלֵךְ בַּדָּרֶךְ.
And now, please, listen also yourself to the voice of your maidservant, and let me set before you a piece of bread, and eat, so that there may be strength in you when you go on your way.’note
Y ahora, te ruego, escucha tú también la voz de tu sierva, y déjame poner delante de ti un pedazo de pan, y come, para que haya fuerza en ti cuando sigas tu camino».
23
וַיְמָאֵן, וַיֹּאמֶר לֹא אֹכַל, וַיִּפְרְצוּ-בוֹ עֲבָדָיו וְגַם-הָאִשָּׁה, וַיִּשְׁמַע לְקֹלָם; וַיָּקָם, מֵהָאָרֶץ, וַיֵּשֶׁב, אֶל-הַמִּטָּה.
But he refused, and said: ‘I will not eat.’ But his servants, and the woman also, pressed him, and he listened to their voice; and he rose from the ground and sat on the bed.note
Pero él rehusó, y dijo: «No comeré». Pero sus siervos, y también la mujer, le insistieron, y él escuchó su voz; y se levantó del suelo y se sentó en la cama.
24
וְלָאִשָּׁה עֵגֶל-מַרְבֵּק בַּבַּיִת, וַתְּמַהֵר וַתִּזְבָּחֵהוּ; וַתִּקַּח-קֶמַח וַתָּלָשׁ, וַתֹּפֵהוּ מַצּוֹת.
And the woman had a stall-fed calf in the house, and she hurried and slaughtered it; and she took flour and kneaded it, and baked it into unleavened bread.note
Y la mujer tenía en casa un ternero de engorde, y se apresuró y lo mató; y tomó harina y la amasó, y coció de ella panes sin levadura.
25
וַתַּגֵּשׁ לִפְנֵי-שָׁאוּל וְלִפְנֵי עֲבָדָיו, וַיֹּאכֵלוּ; וַיָּקֻמוּ וַיֵּלְכוּ, בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא. {פ}
And she brought it before Saul and before his servants, and they ate; and they rose and went away that night.note
Y lo trajo delante de Saúl y delante de sus siervos, y comieron; y se levantaron y se fueron aquella noche.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · “You will go out with me in the camp” — David’s double-edged answer, and “keeper of my head”

With me in the camp. “You must surely know that you will go out with me in the camp, you and your men”: yado’a teda, the verb doubled. The Philistines gather their camps “to fight against Israel”, and the king of Gath expects his servant to march with him against his own people. The chapter before ended with Achish’s confidence, “he will be my servant forever” (27:12, already on these pages); here he calls it in. The ASV has “Know thou assuredly, that thou shalt go out with me in the host”; the NIV “You must understand that you and your men will accompany me in the army.”

What your servant will do. “Therefore you will know what your servant will do”: lakhen attah teda et asher-ya’aseh avdekha. David answers Achish’s “you must surely know” with “you will know”, and says nothing of what it is he will do. The sentence can be heard as a promise or as a dodge, and the chapter does not say which; the answer comes when the Philistine commanders send David home (29:3–10, now on these pages). The KJV has “Surely thou shalt know what thy servant can do”; the NIV “Then you will see for yourself what your servant can do”; the NWT 2013 “You surely know what your servant will do.”

Keeper of my head. “Therefore I will make you keeper of my head all the days”: shomer le-roshi, the guard of the king’s person. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. “All the days” answers the “forever” of 27:12, and David, the guard the Philistines will not trust at the battle, is the man of whom Saul said “I know that you will surely be king” (24:20, already on these pages). The Greek has archisōmatophylax, “chief of the bodyguard”. The KJV has “Therefore will I make thee keeper of mine head for ever”; the NIV “I will make you my bodyguard for life”; the Douay “And I will appoint thee to guard my life for ever.”

Verses 3–6 · Samuel dead, the mediums removed — Shunem and Gilboa, and “Jehovah did not answer him”

Samuel had died. “Now Samuel had died, and all Israel had lamented him and buried him in Ramah, in his own city”: the death told at the start of chapter 25, “And Samuel died; and all Israel gathered together and lamented him, and they buried him in his house at Ramah” (25:1, already on these pages) (Samuel). The book repeats it here, three chapters later, because the scene that follows depends on it.

The mediums and the spiritists. “And Saul had removed the mediums and the spiritists from the land”: ha-ovot ve-ha-yidde’onim (ov; yidde’oni), the pair of the law, “Do not turn to mediums or seek out spiritists” (Leviticus 19:31; 20:6), and of the list of Deuteronomy, “one who asks of a ghost or a familiar spirit, or one who inquires of the dead” (Deuteronomy 18:11, all already on these pages). The verb is hesir, “removed”, from sur, to turn aside; in verses 15 and 16 the same root says that God “has turned away”, sar, from Saul. The ASV has “And Saul had put away those that had familiar spirits, and the wizards, out of the land”; the NWT 1984 “he had removed the spirit mediums and the professional foretellers of events from the land”; the NIV “Saul had expelled the mediums and spiritists from the land”; the Douay “all the magicians and soothsayers”.

Shunem and Gilboa. “And the Philistines gathered, and came and camped at Shunem; and Saul gathered all Israel, and they camped at Gilboa” (Shunem; Gilboa). The two camps face each other across the eastern arm of the valley of Jezreel, Shunem on the slope of the hill of Moreh and Gilboa on the ridge to the south. Shunem is a town of Issachar (Joshua 19:18), and the home of Abishag, “the Shunammite” (1 Kings 1:3, both already on these pages). On Gilboa Saul will die (31:1–6, now on these pages; 2 Samuel 1:6, already on these pages).

His heart trembled. “And Saul saw the camp of the Philistines, and he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly”: va-yecherad libbo, the heart of Eli on his seat by the road, “for his heart trembled for the ark of God” (4:13, already on these pages). The KJV has “and his heart greatly trembled”; the NIV “terror filled his heart”; the Geneva “and his heart was sore astonied”.

Jehovah did not answer him. “And Saul asked of Jehovah, and Jehovah did not answer him, either by dreams or by Urim or by prophets”: va-yish’al Sha’ul, the verb that is the king’s own name, Sha’ul, “asked” (sha’al). Before the battle at Michmash “Saul asked of God … But he did not answer him that day” (14:37, already on these pages); now there is no answer by any of the three means. The ephod, the priest’s means of putting a question to God (urim), went with Abiathar to David after the massacre at Nob (23:6, already on these pages). The Chronicler sums up Saul’s death another way: he died “also for asking of a ghost, to inquire, and did not inquire of Jehovah” (1 Chronicles 10:13–14). This verse uses sha’al and says Saul asked; Chronicles uses darash and says he did not. Readers have taken Chronicles to mean that asking with no answer and then turning to a ghost is not truly seeking Jehovah, or have let the two verdicts stand side by side. The Greek has “manifestations”, dēloi, for Urim. The ASV has “Jehovah answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah never answered him”; the Douay has “nor by priests” for Urim.

Verses 7–8 · “A woman who is mistress of a ghost” at En-dor — the disguised king, and “divine for me”

Mistress of a ghost. “Seek out for me a woman who is mistress of a ghost”: eshet ba’alat-ov, a woman who is ba’alah, owner or mistress, of an ov. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one, said twice. The law punished “a man or a woman who has a ghost or a familiar spirit” with death (Leviticus 20:27, already on these pages). “Inquire of her” is edreshah-bah, from darash (darash), the verb Chronicles uses for what Saul failed to do with Jehovah. The ASV has “Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit”; the NWT 1984 “a woman who is a mistress of spirit mediumship”; the NIV “Find me a woman who is a medium”; the Douay “Seek me a woman that hath a divining spirit”. The Greek calls her engastrimythos, “one who speaks from the belly”: a ventriloquist.

En-dor. Be-Ein Dor, “at the spring of Dor” (En-dor), a town of Manasseh’s within Issachar, “and the inhabitants of En-dor and its towns” (Joshua 17:11, already on these pages), north of the Philistine camp at Shunem.

Saul disguised himself. “And Saul disguised himself, and put on other garments, and went, he and two men with him, and they came to the woman by night”: va-yitchappes. The same form of the verb disguises two more kings on the eve of the battles in which they die: Ahab, who said to Jehoshaphat “I will disguise myself and go into the battle” (1 Kings 22:30), and Josiah at Megiddo (2 Chronicles 35:22, both yet to come in this translation). The king who was taller than all the people (9:2, already on these pages) goes in other clothes, by night. The Geneva has “Then Saul changed himself, and put on other raiment”; the KJV “And Saul disguised himself, and put on other raiment”; the TLB “by wearing ordinary clothing instead of his royal robes”.

Divine for me by the ghost. “Divine for me, please, by the ghost, and bring up for me the one I name to you”: qasomi-na li ba-ov, from qasam, the verb of qesem, divination (qesem). Samuel had told Saul at Gilgal, “rebellion is the sin of divination” (15:23, already on these pages). The written text spells the verb qesumi and the reading tradition qosomi (ketiv and qere), with the same sense. “Bring up”, ha’ali, is the direction of the dead: they are below, in the earth. The KJV has “I pray thee, divine unto me by the familiar spirit”; the Geneva “I pray thee, conjecture unto me by the familiar spirit”; the NWT 1984 “Employ divination, please, for me by spirit mediumship”; the NIV “Consult a spirit for me”.

Verses 9–11 · “Why are you laying a snare for my life?” — Saul swears by Jehovah, and “Bring up Samuel”

A snare for my life. “Look — you know what Saul has done, how he has cut off the mediums and the spiritists from the land. Why then are you laying a snare for my life, to have me put to death?”: hikhrit, “cut off”, a harder word than the narrator’s “removed” in verse 3, and mitnaqqesh be-nafshi. This form of the verb stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. She speaks of Saul to Saul. The KJV has “wherefore then layest thou a snare for my life, to cause me to die?”; the NWT 1984 “Why, then, are you acting like a trapper against my soul to have me put to death?”; the NIV “Why have you set a trap for my life to bring about my death?”

As Jehovah lives. “And Saul swore to her by Jehovah, saying: ‘As Jehovah lives, no guilt shall befall you in this matter’”: im-yiqqrekh avon, “if guilt shall befall you”, the oath’s form of a denial (avon). The king swears by Jehovah, who does not answer him, that the law of Jehovah will not touch a woman it condemns. Saul has sworn rash oaths before: “as Jehovah lives … he shall surely die” over Jonathan (14:39), and “As Jehovah lives, he shall not be put to death” over David (19:6, both already on these pages). The ASV has “As Jehovah liveth, there shall no punishment happen to thee for this thing”; the NWT 1984 “guilt for error will not befall you in this matter!”; the NIV “you will not be punished for this”.

Bring up Samuel. “Whom shall I bring up for you?” — “Bring up Samuel for me”. The man who, Samuel said, would not see him again “until the day of his death” (15:35, already on these pages) asks for him by name. The KJV has “Whom shall I bring up unto thee? And he said, Bring me up Samuel”; the TLB “Well, whom do you want me to bring up?”

Verse 12 · “Why have you deceived me? You are Saul!” — the woman sees Samuel, and who is it that came up?

The woman saw Samuel. “And the woman saw Samuel, and she cried out with a loud voice”. The narrator names him plainly, here and in every verse that follows. How she knows the stranger is Saul the text does not say; it moves straight from her sight of Samuel to her cry. “Why have you deceived me?”: lammah rimmitani. The words stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Jacob to Laban, the morning he found Leah, “Why then have you deceived me?” (Genesis 29:25), Saul to Michal after the window, “Why have you deceived me like this?” (19:17, both already on these pages), and this one. Saul once said it; now it is said to him. The ASV has “Why hast thou deceived me? for thou art Saul”; the NIV “Why have you deceived me? You are Saul!”; the NWT 1984 “she began crying out at the top of her voice”.

Who came up? Readers have answered three ways, each with a long pedigree. (1) Samuel himself. The narrator calls him Samuel, and what he says comes true. The Greek Wisdom of Sirach says of Samuel, “after his death he prophesied, and shewed the king his end, and lifted up his voice from the earth in prophecy” (Sirach 46:20). (2) A spirit in Samuel’s likeness. King James VI of Scotland, in his Daemonologie (1597), wrote that “it was not the spirit of Samuel, I grant”, but the devil permitted to take the likeness of a saint. The NWT 1984 and the NWT 2013 print the name in quotation marks from this verse to verse 20, “Samuel”, a doctrinal rendering that the Hebrew does not mark: the narrator writes the name with no sign of doubt. (3) The woman’s own craft. The Greek name for her, “one who speaks from the belly”, points that way, and the same Daemonologie puts in the mouth of its sceptic that Saul, “being himselfe in an other chalmer, and seeing nothing”, could not perceive “hir faining of hir voice”. This translation prints what the Hebrew prints, “Samuel”, and leaves the question where the chapter leaves it.

Not a witch. The woman of En-dor is famous as a witch, and her story was used as scriptural proof that witches exist. The Geneva Bible’s summary at the head of the chapter reads “Saul consulteth with a witch”. The Daemonologie begins its proof “that witchcraft, and Witches haue bene, and are” with “Saules Pythonisse”, a book written by the king whose name the King James Version carries. The chapter never calls her by the word of the law, “You shall not let a sorceress live”, mekhashefah (Exodus 22:18, already on these pages); she is ba’alat-ov, forbidden by the law for having a ghost, and the chapter records no harm done by her to anyone. In this chapter she is afraid for her life, keeps her word, and feeds a king who has fallen on her floor.

Verses 13–14 · “I see gods coming up out of the earth” — elohim with a plural verb, “an old man”, and the robe

Gods coming up. “I see gods coming up out of the earth”: elohim ra’iti olim min-ha-arets. Elohim is the ordinary word for God, plural in form and singular in sense (elohim); here its participle is plural, olim, “ones coming up”, as the people’s “gods who will go before us” have a plural verb at the golden calf (Exodus 32:1, already on these pages). Saul’s next question is singular, “What is his form?”, and her answer is one old man. Readers take elohim here as a god-like being, as a spirit of the dead, as Isaiah calls the dead a people’s elohim, “should not a people inquire of its gods, of the dead on behalf of the living?” (Isaiah 8:19), or as a company of figures of whom Samuel is one. This translation keeps the plural and puts the range here. The KJV has “I saw gods ascending out of the earth”; the ASV “I see a god coming up out of the earth”; the NWT 1984 “A god I saw coming up out of the earth”; the NIV “I see a ghostly figure coming up out of the earth”; the TLB “I see a specter coming up out of the earth”. The Greek has “gods”.

An old man, wrapped in a robe. “What is his form?” — “An old man is coming up, and he is wrapped in a robe”: to’oro, the word of David’s “a man of fine form” (16:18, already on these pages), and me’il (me’il). The robe follows Samuel through the book: the little robe his mother made him year by year (2:19), and the robe whose corner Saul seized and tore at Gilgal, when Samuel said “Jehovah has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today” (15:27–28, both already on these pages). The Greek has “an upright man”, orthios, where the Hebrew has zaqen, old. The ASV has “An old man cometh up; and he is covered with a robe”; the KJV “he is covered with a mantle”; the NWT 1984 “he has himself covered with a sleeveless coat”.

Saul knew. “And Saul knew that it was Samuel, and he bowed with his face to the ground and prostrated himself”. He knows from the robe. The KJV has “And Saul perceived that it was Samuel”; the Douay “he bowed himself with his face to the ground, and adored”.

Verses 15–19 · “Why have you disturbed me?” — “Jehovah has turned away from you”, the kingdom torn, and “tomorrow you and your sons will be with me”

Disturbed. “Why have you disturbed me, by bringing me up?”: hirgaztani, from ragaz, to shake, to stir (ragaz), the verb Isaiah uses of the world of the dead roused to meet the king of Babylon, “Sheol from beneath is stirred up for you” (Isaiah 14:9). The KJV has “Why hast thou disquieted me, to bring me up?”; the NIV “Why have you disturbed me by bringing me up?”; the Douay “Why hast thou disturbed my rest”.

To let me know what I shall do. “I am in great distress … God has turned away from me and does not answer me any more … So I have called you, to let me know what I shall do”: sar me’alai, as “the spirit of Jehovah had turned away from Saul” (16:14) and “Jehovah … had turned away from Saul” (18:12). At the beginning Samuel told the young king, “Seven days you shall wait, until I come to you and let you know what you shall do” (10:8, all already on these pages): the same verb, hoda’, and the same words, “what you shall do”. Saul asks the dead Samuel for what the living Samuel promised him at Gilgal. He names dreams and prophets, not Urim. The ASV has “I am sore distressed”; the NWT 1984 “I am in very sore straits”; the NIV “So I have called on you to tell me what to do.”

Your adversary. “Why then do you ask me, when Jehovah has turned away from you and has become your adversary?”: tish’aleni, the king’s name once more, and arekha, a rare word, taken as “your adversary” from its Aramaic cousin, Belteshazzar’s wish for the king, “and its interpretation for your enemies” (Daniel 4:16 in the Aramaic numbering, already on these pages). The Greek reads “and has gone over to your neighbour”, which looks forward to the next verse. The ASV has “and is become thine adversary”; the KJV “and is become thine enemy”; the Douay, following the Greek, “and is gone over to thy rival”.

Torn, and given to your neighbour. “And Jehovah has done for himself as he spoke by my hand: Jehovah has torn the kingdom out of your hand, and has given it to your neighbour, to David”: the words of Gilgal, “Jehovah has torn the kingdom of Israel from you today, and has given it to a neighbour of yours who is better than you” (15:28, already on these pages), and now the neighbour has a name. The Hebrew has lo, “for him” or “for himself”; the Greek has “to you”. The ASV has “And Jehovah hath done unto thee, as he spake by me”; the NWT 1984 “And Jehovah will do for himself just as he spoke by means of me”; the KJV “hath done to him, as he spake by me”.

Amalek. “Because you did not listen to the voice of Jehovah, and did not carry out his burning anger against Amalek”: the charge of chapter 15, “Why then did you not listen to the voice of Jehovah?” (15:19), and “to listen is better than sacrifice” (15:22, both already on these pages) (Amalek). Charon appo, “his burning anger”, is what Saul did not carry out when he spared Agag and the best of the flock. The ASV has “and didst not execute his fierce wrath upon Amalek”; the NWT 1984 “and you did not execute his burning anger against”.

With me. “And tomorrow you and your sons will be with me”: among the dead, where Samuel is. Saul and three of his sons die on Gilboa the next day (31:2–6, now on these pages). The Greek has “and tomorrow you and your sons with you shall fall”. The ASV has “and to-morrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me”; the NIV “and tomorrow you and your sons will be with me”; the TLB “and you and your sons will be here with me”.

Verses 20–23 · “His full height to the ground” — no strength, “I put my life in my palm”, and four times “listened to the voice”

His full height. “And Saul at once fell his full height to the ground”: melo-qomato. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. Qomah, height, is the word of Jehovah’s warning to Samuel at Bethlehem, “Do not look at his appearance, or at the height of his stature” (16:7, already on these pages); the tallest man in Israel lies the length of himself on the woman’s floor. “For he had not eaten bread all the day and all the night”: the king who once forbade his army to eat until evening (14:24, already on these pages) has now gone without himself. The ASV has “Then Saul fell straightway his full length upon the earth”; the KJV “Then Saul fell straightway all along on the earth”; the NIV “Immediately Saul fell full length on the ground”.

My life in my palm. “Your maidservant has listened to your voice, and I put my life in my palm”: va-asim nafshi ve-khappi, what Jonathan said David did when he went out to Goliath, “For he put his life in his palm and struck down the Philistine” (19:5, already on these pages). The first-person words “my life in my palm” stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Jephthah’s (Judges 12:3), this one, and the psalmist’s, “My life is in my palm continually” (Psalm 119:109, both already on these pages). The ASV has “and I have put my life in my hand”; the NWT 1984 “and I proceeded to put my soul in my palm”; the NIV “I took my life in my hands”.

Listen to the voice. Shama be-qol, “listen to the voice”, the charge Samuel brought against Saul in verse 18, runs through the end of the chapter: “your maidservant has listened to your voice” (verse 21); “listen also yourself to the voice of your maidservant” (verse 22); “and he listened to their voice” (verse 23). The king who would not listen to the voice of Jehovah listens to a woman of En-dor about a meal. “A piece of bread”, pat-lechem, is Abraham’s modest word for the feast he served his three guests, “and let me bring a piece of bread” (Genesis 18:5, already on these pages). And the same words began Saul’s oath over David: “And Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan; and Saul swore” (19:6, already on these pages). The ASV has “hearken thou also unto the voice of thy handmaid, and let me set a morsel of bread before thee”; the NIV “and let me give you some food”.

Pressed him. “But he refused … But his servants, and the woman also, pressed him”: va-yifretsu-vo, from parats, to break through. Many take it as a form of patsar, the verb of Lot at Sodom, who “pressed them hard” until his guests came in and ate his unleavened bread (Genesis 19:3, already on these pages). “And he rose from the ground and sat on the bed”, ha-mittah, the bed where Michal laid the teraphim (19:13, already on these pages). The KJV has “compelled him”; the ASV “constrained him”; the NWT 1984 “kept urging him”.

Verses 24–25 · A stall-fed calf and unleavened bread — the last supper of Saul, “and they went away that night”

A stall-fed calf. “And the woman had a stall-fed calf in the house”: egel-marbeq, a calf kept in the stall and fattened. The word marbeq stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one, Jeremiah’s hired soldiers “like stall-fed calves” (Jeremiah 46:21), Amos’s rich “eating … calves from the midst of the stall” (Amos 6:4), and Malachi’s “you will go out and leap like stall-fed calves” (Malachi 3:20 in the Hebrew numbering). The ASV has “a fatted calf in the house”; the KJV “a fat calf”; the NWT 1984 “a fattened calf”.

Slaughtered it, and unleavened bread. “And she hurried and slaughtered it; and she took flour and kneaded it, and baked it into unleavened bread”: va-tizbachehu, from zavach, the verb of sacrifice, here a meal (zevach). The hurrying, the calf and the kneaded flour are Abraham’s at Mamre, “Quick — three seahs of fine flour! Knead it, make cakes!” and “took a calf, tender and good” (Genesis 18:6–7), and the unleavened bread is Lot’s (Genesis 19:3, both already on these pages): the hospitality of the patriarchs, given by a medium to a doomed king. The NWT 1984 has “So she quickly sacrificed it”; the ASV “and she hasted, and killed it”; the NIV “which she butchered at once”.

That night. “And she brought it before Saul and before his servants, and they ate; and they rose and went away that night.” They came by night (verse 8), and they go out into the same night; it is the last meal of Saul’s that the book records. The ASV has “Then they rose up, and went away that night”; the TLB “Then they went out into the night.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact, distinct words: “camps” for machanot; “you must surely know” for yado’a teda; “keeper of my head” for shomer le-roshi; “all the days”; “lamented” for safad, as in 25:1; “removed” for hesir and “turned away” for sar, one root; “the mediums and the spiritists” for ovot and yidde’onim, as in Leviticus 20:6; “trembled” for charad, as in 4:13; “asked” for sha’al; “Urim”; “mistress of a ghost” for ba’alat-ov; “inquire” for darash; “disguised himself” for hitchappes; “divine” for qasam; “bring up” for he’elah; “cut off” for hikhrit; “laying a snare” for mitnaqqesh; “guilt” for avon; “deceived” for rimmah, as in 19:17 and Genesis 29:25; “gods” for elohim with a plural verb; “form” for to’ar, as in 16:18; “robe” for me’il, as in 2:19 and 15:27; “disturbed” for hirgiz; “let me know what I shall do”, as in 10:8; “adversary” for ar; “torn the kingdom … given it to your neighbour”, as in 15:28; “listen to the voice” for shama be-qol, as in 15:19 and 22; “burning anger” for charon af; “his full height” for melo-qomato, as in 16:7; “my life in my palm”, as in 19:5; “a piece of bread” for pat-lechem, as in Genesis 18:5; “pressed” for parats; “the bed” for mittah, as in 19:13; “stall-fed calf” for egel-marbeq; “slaughtered” for zavach; and “unleavened bread” for matsot, as in Genesis 19:3.

Echoes paid. Achish’s “servant forever” (27:12); Samuel’s death and burial at Ramah (25:1); the law of the medium and the spiritist (Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27; Deuteronomy 18:11); Eli’s trembling heart (4:13); Saul’s unanswered question (14:37); Abiathar and the ephod (23:6); En-dor in Manasseh’s list (Joshua 17:11); Shunem in Issachar’s (Joshua 19:18) and Abishag the Shunammite (1 Kings 1:3); Saul taller than all the people (9:2); “rebellion is the sin of divination”, the torn robe and the torn kingdom given to a neighbour, Samuel’s last sight of Saul, and “to listen is better than sacrifice” (15:19–35); Saul’s oaths by Jehovah (14:39; 19:6); “Why have you deceived me?” (Genesis 29:25; 1 Samuel 19:17); “gods who will go before us” (Exodus 32:1); “a man of fine form” (16:18); the little robe (2:19); the spirit of Jehovah turned away (16:14; 18:12); “let you know what you shall do” (10:8); the height of his stature (16:7); the fast of Saul’s army (14:24); David’s life in his palm (19:5); Jephthah’s (Judges 12:3); the psalmist’s (Psalm 119:109); Abraham’s piece of bread, calf and kneaded flour (Genesis 18:5–7); Lot’s pressing and unleavened bread (Genesis 19:3); Michal’s bed (19:13); the sorceress of the law (Exodus 22:18); the king’s enemies in Aramaic (Daniel 4:16); and Gilboa (2 Samuel 1:6).

Echoes planted. The Philistine commanders send David home (29:3–10, now on these pages); Saul and his sons on Gilboa (31:1–6, now on these pages); the Chronicler’s verdict on Saul (1 Chronicles 10:13–14); Ahab’s and Josiah’s disguises (1 Kings 22:30; 2 Chronicles 35:22); the dead as a people’s elohim (Isaiah 8:19); Sheol stirred up (Isaiah 14:9); and the stall-fed calves of Jeremiah, Amos and Malachi (Jeremiah 46:21; Amos 6:4; Malachi 3:20). None of the others is translated here yet.

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 29 — “Is not this David?”: the Philistine lords gather at Aphek and refuse to let David go down with them into the battle, for fear he will turn against them; Achish swears by Jehovah that David is “good in my eyes as an angel of God”, and sends him back to the land of the Philistines in the morning.
