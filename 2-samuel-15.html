Absalom makes himself a chariot and horses and fifty men to run before him, stands beside the way of the gate and tells each man with a suit that no one is appointed to hear him, wishing he were judge; he takes hold of those who bow to him and kisses them, and steals the heart of the men of Israel. At the end of ‘forty years’ he asks the king’s leave to pay a vow in Hebron, sends spies through the tribes to say ‘Absalom has become king in Hebron’ at the sound of the horn, and sends for Ahithophel. A messenger tells David that the heart of the man of Israel is after Absalom, and David says: Arise, and let us flee. He leaves ten concubines to keep the house, lets Ittai the Gittite choose to stay, sends the ark back with Zadok, climbs the ascent of the Olives weeping, barefoot and with his head covered, prays that Ahithophel’s counsel be made foolish, and sends Hushai the Archite back to the city to break it.
Some years after 2 Samuel 14 (the chapter’s own ‘forty years’ is the Hebrew’s number, not one that can be counted from what precedes it); it ends with Hushai and Absalom entering Jerusalem together.

1
וַיְהִי, מֵאַחֲרֵי כֵן, וַיַּעַשׂ לוֹ אַבְשָׁלוֹם, מֶרְכָּבָה וְסֻסִים; וַחֲמִשִּׁים אִישׁ, רָצִים לְפָנָיו.
And it was after this that Absalom made for himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men to run before him.note
Y fue después de esto que Absalón se hizo un carro y caballos, y cincuenta hombres que corrieran delante de él.
2
וְהִשְׁכִּים, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וְעָמַד, עַל-יַד דֶּרֶךְ הַשָּׁעַר; וַיְהִי כָּל-הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר-יִהְיֶה-לּוֹ-רִיב לָבוֹא אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ לַמִּשְׁפָּט, וַיִּקְרָא אַבְשָׁלוֹם אֵלָיו וַיֹּאמֶר אֵי-מִזֶּה עִיר אַתָּה, וַיֹּאמֶר, מֵאַחַד שִׁבְטֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל עַבְדֶּךָ.
And Absalom rose early and stood beside the way of the gate; and it was so that every man who had a suit to come to the king for judgment, Absalom called to him and said: ‘From what city are you?’ And he said: ‘Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel.’note
Y Absalón se levantó temprano y se puso al lado del camino de la puerta; y sucedía que a todo hombre que tenía un pleito para venir al rey a juicio, Absalón lo llamaba y le decía: «¿De qué ciudad eres?» Y él decía: «Tu siervo es de una de las tribus de Israel».
3
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו אַבְשָׁלוֹם, רְאֵה דְבָרֶיךָ טוֹבִים וּנְכֹחִים; וְשֹׁמֵעַ אֵין-לְךָ, מֵאֵת הַמֶּלֶךְ.
And Absalom said to him: ‘See, your words are good and straight; but a hearer you have not, from the king.’note
Y Absalón le decía: «Mira, tus palabras son buenas y rectas; pero quien te escuche de parte del rey, no lo tienes».
4
וַיֹּאמֶר, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, מִי-יְשִׂמֵנִי שֹׁפֵט, בָּאָרֶץ; וְעָלַי, יָבוֹא כָּל-אִישׁ אֲשֶׁר-יִהְיֶה-לּוֹ-רִיב וּמִשְׁפָּט--וְהִצְדַּקְתִּיו.
And Absalom said: ‘Who will set me judge in the land? Then to me would come every man who has a suit or a cause, and I would declare him in the right.’note
Y Absalón decía: «¿Quién me pondrá por juez en la tierra? Entonces vendría a mí todo hombre que tenga un pleito o una causa, y yo le daría la razón».
5
וְהָיָה, בִּקְרָב-אִישׁ, לְהִשְׁתַּחֲוֺת, לוֹ; וְשָׁלַח אֶת-יָדוֹ וְהֶחֱזִיק לוֹ, וְנָשַׁק לוֹ.
And it would be, when a man came near to bow down to him, that he would put out his hand and take hold of him, and kiss him.note
Y sucedía que cuando un hombre se acercaba para postrarse ante él, extendía su mano y lo sujetaba, y lo besaba.
6
וַיַּעַשׂ אַבְשָׁלוֹם כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה, לְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲשֶׁר-יָבֹאוּ לַמִּשְׁפָּט, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ; וַיְגַנֵּב, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אֶת-לֵב, אַנְשֵׁי יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {פ}
And Absalom did in this way to all Israel who came to the king for judgment; and Absalom stole the heart of the men of Israel.note
Y hacía Absalón de esta manera con todo Israel que venía al rey a juicio; y Absalón robó el corazón de los hombres de Israel.
7
וַיְהִי, מִקֵּץ אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה; וַיֹּאמֶר אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֵלְכָה נָּא וַאֲשַׁלֵּם אֶת-נִדְרִי אֲשֶׁר-נָדַרְתִּי לַיהוָה, בְּחֶבְרוֹן.
And it was at the end of forty years that Absalom said to the king: ‘Let me go, please, and pay my vow which I vowed to Jehovah, in Hebron.note
Y fue al cabo de cuarenta años que Absalón dijo al rey: «Déjame ir, por favor, y pagar mi voto que hice a Jehová, en Hebrón.
8
כִּי-נֵדֶר נָדַר עַבְדְּךָ, בְּשִׁבְתִּי בִגְשׁוּר בַּאֲרָם לֵאמֹר:  אִם-ישיב יָשׁוֹב יְשִׁיבֵנִי יְהוָה יְרוּשָׁלִַם, וְעָבַדְתִּי אֶת-יְהוָה.
For your servant vowed a vow while I lived in Geshur in Aram, saying: “If Jehovah will indeed bring me back to Jerusalem, then I will serve Jehovah.”’note
Porque tu siervo hizo un voto mientras yo habitaba en Guesur, en Aram, diciendo: “Si Jehová ciertamente me hace volver a Jerusalén, entonces serviré a Jehová”».
9
וַיֹּאמֶר-לוֹ הַמֶּלֶךְ, לֵךְ בְּשָׁלוֹם; וַיָּקָם, וַיֵּלֶךְ חֶבְרוֹנָה.  {פ}
And the king said to him: ‘Go in peace.’ And he arose and went to Hebron.note
Y el rey le dijo: «Ve en paz». Y se levantó y fue a Hebrón.
10
וַיִּשְׁלַח אַבְשָׁלוֹם מְרַגְּלִים, בְּכָל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל לֵאמֹר:  כְּשָׁמְעֲכֶם, אֶת-קוֹל הַשֹּׁפָר, וַאֲמַרְתֶּם, מָלַךְ אַבְשָׁלוֹם בְּחֶבְרוֹן.
And Absalom sent spies into all the tribes of Israel, saying: ‘When you hear the sound of the horn, then you shall say: “Absalom has become king in Hebron.”’note
Y Absalón envió espías a todas las tribus de Israel, diciendo: «Cuando oigan el sonido del cuerno, entonces dirán: “Absalón ha llegado a ser rey en Hebrón”».
11
וְאֶת-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, הָלְכוּ מָאתַיִם אִישׁ מִירוּשָׁלִַם, קְרֻאִים, וְהֹלְכִים לְתֻמָּם; וְלֹא יָדְעוּ, כָּל-דָּבָר.
And with Absalom there went two hundred men from Jerusalem, invited, and going in their integrity; and they knew nothing.note
Y con Absalón fueron doscientos hombres de Jerusalén, invitados, e iban en su integridad; y no sabían nada.
12
וַיִּשְׁלַח אַבְשָׁלוֹם אֶת-אֲחִיתֹפֶל הַגִּילֹנִי יוֹעֵץ דָּוִד, מֵעִירוֹ מִגִּלֹה, בְּזָבְחוֹ, אֶת-הַזְּבָחִים; וַיְהִי הַקֶּשֶׁר אַמִּץ, וְהָעָם הוֹלֵךְ וָרָב אֶת-אַבְשָׁלוֹם.
And Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s counselor, from his city, from Giloh, while he was offering the sacrifices; and the conspiracy was strong, and the people were going and growing in number with Absalom.note
Y Absalón mandó llamar a Ahitofel el gilonita, consejero de David, de su ciudad, de Gilo, mientras ofrecía los sacrificios; y la conspiración era fuerte, y el pueblo iba creciendo en número con Absalón.
13
וַיָּבֹא, הַמַּגִּיד, אֶל-דָּוִד, לֵאמֹר:  הָיָה לֶב-אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל, אַחֲרֵי אַבְשָׁלוֹם.
And a messenger came to David, saying: ‘The heart of the man of Israel has been after Absalom.’note
Y vino un mensajero a David, diciendo: «El corazón del hombre de Israel ha estado tras Absalón».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד לְכָל-עֲבָדָיו אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ בִירוּשָׁלִַם, קוּמוּ וְנִבְרָחָה--כִּי לֹא-תִהְיֶה-לָּנוּ פְלֵיטָה, מִפְּנֵי אַבְשָׁלֹם; מַהֲרוּ לָלֶכֶת, פֶּן-יְמַהֵר וְהִשִּׂגָנוּ וְהִדִּיחַ עָלֵינוּ אֶת-הָרָעָה, וְהִכָּה הָעִיר, לְפִי-חָרֶב.
And David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem: ‘Arise, and let us flee; for there will be no escape for us from Absalom. Hurry to go, lest he hurry and overtake us, and drive the evil down upon us, and strike the city with the mouth of the sword.’note
Y David dijo a todos sus siervos que estaban con él en Jerusalén: «Levántense, y huyamos; porque no habrá escape para nosotros de Absalón. Apresúrense a irse, no sea que se apresure y nos alcance, y haga caer el mal sobre nosotros, y hiera la ciudad a filo de espada».
15
וַיֹּאמְרוּ עַבְדֵי-הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ:  כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר-יִבְחַר אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ, הִנֵּה עֲבָדֶיךָ.
And the king’s servants said to the king: ‘According to all that my lord the king chooses — look, your servants.’note
Y los siervos del rey dijeron al rey: «Conforme a todo lo que mi señor el rey escoja — mira, tus siervos».
16
וַיֵּצֵא הַמֶּלֶךְ וְכָל-בֵּיתוֹ, בְּרַגְלָיו; וַיַּעֲזֹב הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֵת עֶשֶׂר נָשִׁים פִּלַגְשִׁים--לִשְׁמֹר הַבָּיִת.
And the king went out, and all his household at his feet; and the king left ten women, concubines, to keep the house.note
Y salió el rey, y toda su casa a sus pies; y el rey dejó diez mujeres, concubinas, para guardar la casa.
17
וַיֵּצֵא הַמֶּלֶךְ וְכָל-הָעָם, בְּרַגְלָיו; וַיַּעַמְדוּ, בֵּית הַמֶּרְחָק.
And the king went out, and all the people at his feet; and they stood at Beth-merhak.note
Y salió el rey, y todo el pueblo a sus pies; y se detuvieron en Bet-merhak.
18
וְכָל-עֲבָדָיו עֹבְרִים עַל-יָדוֹ, וְכָל-הַכְּרֵתִי וְכָל-הַפְּלֵתִי; וְכָל-הַגִּתִּים שֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ, אֲשֶׁר-בָּאוּ בְרַגְלוֹ מִגַּת, עֹבְרִים, עַל-פְּנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ.  {ס}
And all his servants were crossing at his side, and all the Cherethites and all the Pelethites; and all the Gittites, six hundred men who had come at his feet from Gath, were crossing before the king.note
Y todos sus siervos iban pasando a su lado, y todos los cereteos y todos los peleteos; y todos los geteos, seiscientos hombres que habían venido a sus pies desde Gat, iban pasando delante del rey.
19
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-אִתַּי הַגִּתִּי, לָמָּה תֵלֵךְ גַּם-אַתָּה אִתָּנוּ; שׁוּב וְשֵׁב עִם-הַמֶּלֶךְ כִּי-נָכְרִי אַתָּה, וְגַם-גֹּלֶה אַתָּה לִמְקוֹמֶךָ.
And the king said to Ittai the Gittite: ‘Why do you also go with us? Return and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner, and also an exile from your place.note
Y el rey dijo a Itai el geteo: «¿Por qué vas tú también con nosotros? Vuelve y quédate con el rey, porque eres extranjero, y además desterrado de tu lugar.
20
תְּמוֹל בּוֹאֶךָ, וְהַיּוֹם אנועך אֲנִיעֲךָ עִמָּנוּ לָלֶכֶת, וַאֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ, עַל אֲשֶׁר-אֲנִי הוֹלֵךְ; שׁוּב וְהָשֵׁב אֶת-אַחֶיךָ עִמָּךְ, חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת.
Yesterday you came, and today shall I make you wander with us, to go, when I am going where I am going? Return, and take your brothers back with you, in kindness and truth.’note
Ayer viniste, ¿y hoy te haré andar errante con nosotros, para ir yo adonde voy? Vuelve, y lleva de regreso a tus hermanos contigo, con bondad y verdad».
21
וַיַּעַן אִתַּי אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ, וַיֹּאמַר:  חַי-יְהוָה, וְחֵי אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ, כִּי אם    בִּמְקוֹם אֲשֶׁר יִהְיֶה-שָּׁם אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ אִם-לְמָוֶת אִם-לְחַיִּים, כִּי-שָׁם יִהְיֶה עַבְדֶּךָ.
And Ittai answered the king and said: ‘As Jehovah lives, and as my lord the king lives, surely in the place where my lord the king shall be, whether for death or for life, there your servant will be.’note
Y respondió Itai al rey y dijo: «Vive Jehová, y vive mi señor el rey, que en el lugar donde esté mi señor el rey, sea para muerte o para vida, allí estará tu siervo».
22
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-אִתַּי, לֵךְ וַעֲבֹר; וַיַּעֲבֹר אִתַּי הַגִּתִּי, וְכָל-אֲנָשָׁיו, וְכָל-הַטַּף, אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ.
And David said to Ittai: ‘Go and cross over.’ And Ittai the Gittite crossed over, and all his men, and all the little ones who were with him.note
Y David dijo a Itai: «Ve y pasa». Y pasó Itai el geteo, y todos sus hombres, y todos los pequeños que estaban con él.
23
וְכָל-הָאָרֶץ, בּוֹכִים קוֹל גָּדוֹל, וְכָל-הָעָם, עֹבְרִים; וְהַמֶּלֶךְ, עֹבֵר בְּנַחַל קִדְרוֹן, וְכָל-הָעָם עֹבְרִים, עַל-פְּנֵי-דֶרֶךְ אֶת-הַמִּדְבָּר.
And all the land was weeping with a loud voice, and all the people were crossing over; and the king was crossing the brook Kidron, and all the people were crossing over on the way to the wilderness.note
Y toda la tierra lloraba a gran voz, y todo el pueblo iba pasando; y el rey pasaba el torrente de Cedrón, y todo el pueblo iba pasando por el camino hacia el desierto.
24
וְהִנֵּה גַם-צָדוֹק וְכָל-הַלְוִיִּם אִתּוֹ, נֹשְׂאִים אֶת-אֲרוֹן בְּרִית הָאֱלֹהִים, וַיַּצִּקוּ אֶת-אֲרוֹן הָאֱלֹהִים, וַיַּעַל אֶבְיָתָר--עַד-תֹּם כָּל-הָעָם, לַעֲבוֹר מִן-הָעִיר.  {ס}
And look, Zadok also, and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of the covenant of God; and they set down the ark of God, and Abiathar went up, until all the people had finished crossing from the city.note
Y mira, también Sadoc, y todos los levitas con él, llevando el arca del pacto de Dios; y asentaron el arca de Dios, y Abiatar subió, hasta que todo el pueblo terminó de pasar de la ciudad.
25
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ לְצָדוֹק, הָשֵׁב אֶת-אֲרוֹן הָאֱלֹהִים הָעִיר:  אִם-אֶמְצָא חֵן, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה--וֶהֱשִׁבַנִי, וְהִרְאַנִי אֹתוֹ וְאֶת-נָוֵהוּ.
And the king said to Zadok: ‘Return the ark of God to the city. If I find favor in the eyes of Jehovah, he will bring me back, and let me see it and its dwelling place;note
Y el rey dijo a Sadoc: «Haz volver el arca de Dios a la ciudad. Si hallo gracia ante los ojos de Jehová, él me hará volver, y me dejará verla a ella y su morada;
26
וְאִם כֹּה יֹאמַר, לֹא חָפַצְתִּי בָּךְ:  הִנְנִי--יַעֲשֶׂה-לִּי, כַּאֲשֶׁר טוֹב בְּעֵינָיו.  {ס}
but if he says thus: “I have no delight in you” — here I am; let him do to me as is good in his eyes.’note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · 2 Samuel 15 Absalom steals the heart of Israel — a chariot and fifty runners, the way of the gate, a kiss for everyone, and a heart that is stolen

A chariot and fifty runners. “Absalom made for himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men to run before him”: “fifty men to run before him” stands in two verses, this one and 1 Kings 1:5 (already on these pages), where Adonijah son of Haggith does the same thing, having just said “I will be king”. It is what Samuel told Israel a king would do, take sons “to run before his chariot” (1 Samuel 8:11, already on these pages), and the word for the runners, ratsim, is the word for the king’s guard in 1 Samuel 22:17 (already on these pages). The Hebrew has the singular merkavah, “a chariot”. The KJV has “chariots and horses”. The Douay has “chariots, and horsemen”. The NIV has “provided himself with a chariot and horses”. The TLB has “bought a magnificent chariot”. The same version has “hired fifty footmen”, where the Hebrew says nothing of buying or hiring.

The way of the gate. “And Absalom rose early and stood beside the way of the gate”: the gate is where cases were heard, and the widow of Deuteronomy 25:7 (already on these pages) goes up to the gate to the elders. The ASV has “beside the way of the gate”. The NIV has “by the side of the road leading to the city gate”. The Geneva has “hard by the entering in of the gate”. The TLB has “went out to the gate of the city”. Absalom asks “From what city are you?” and the man answers with a tribe; the question is the same for all, and so is what Absalom says next.

A hearer you have not. “See, your words are good and straight; but a hearer you have not, from the king”: “a hearer you have not” stands in one verse, and the word for the missing man, shomea, is a hearer of cases. The NIV has “no representative of the king to hear you”. The KJV has “no man deputed of the king to hear thee”. The Douay has “no man appointed by the king to hear thee”. The NWT 1984 has “giving you a hearing”. Of David the book had said that he was “doing justice and righteousness for all his people” (2 Samuel 8:15, already on these pages), and Absalom’s offer in verse 4 uses the same root of righteousness.

Who will set me judge. “Who will set me judge in the land? Then to me would come every man who has a suit or a cause, and I would declare him in the right”: the Hebrew is a question, mi yesimeni shofet, and the versions turn it into a wish. The ASV has “Oh that I were made judge in the land”. The NWT 1984 has “O that I were appointed judge in the land”. The NIV has “If only I were appointed judge in the land”. The Douay has “O that they would make me judge over the land”. The last verb, vehitsdaqtiv, is “I would declare him in the right”, from the root of tsedeq. The ASV has “I would do him justice”. The NIV has “I would see that they receive justice”.

He kissed him. “He would put out his hand and take hold of him, and kiss him”: “took hold of him, and kissed him” stands in one verse. The kiss is the one Absalom himself received at the end of the last chapter, when “the king kissed Absalom” (2 Samuel 14:33, now on these pages). The TLB has “shook his hand instead”, where the Hebrew has the kiss. The NIV has “take hold of him and kiss him”.

Stole the heart. “And Absalom stole the heart of the men of Israel”: “stole”, said of a heart, stands in two verses, this one and Genesis 31:20 (already on these pages), where Jacob “stole the heart of Laban the Aramean” by leaving without telling him. The Hebrew has the singular “heart” here and in verse 13, the heart of a people taken as one. The NIV has “stole the hearts of the people of Israel”. The Douay has “enticed the hearts of the men of Israel”. The NWT 1984 has “kept stealing the hearts of the men of Israel”. The Greek has idiopoieito, “made his own”.

Verses 7–12 · A vow in Hebron — forty years, “indeed bring me back”, a horn in the tribes, two hundred who knew nothing, and Ahithophel from Giloh

Forty years. “And it was at the end of forty years that Absalom said to the king”: the Hebrew has arba‘im shanah, “forty years”, and Rahlfs’ Greek has the same, tesserakonta etōn. Nothing in the chapter can be counted forty years back: Absalom spent three years in Geshur (2 Samuel 13:38) and two full years in Jerusalem (14:28), both already on these pages, and David’s whole reign was forty years (2 Samuel 5:4, already on these pages). The NIV has “At the end of four years”. The TLB has “After four years”. The ASV has “at the end of forty years”. The KJV has “after forty years”. I translate the number the Hebrew and the Greek give and do not mend it.

Indeed bring me back. “Let me go, please, and pay my vow which I vowed to Jehovah, in Hebron. For your servant vowed a vow while I lived in Geshur in Aram”: the written text of verse 8 has yashiv and the reading tradition has yashov, both of the verb “to return”, so the sense is the same. The vow is “then I will serve Jehovah”, and the Hebrew of verse 8 does not say “in Hebron” or “sacrifice”. The NIV has “I will worship the Lord in Hebron”. The Douay has “I will offer sacrifice to the Lord”. The TLB has “I would sacrifice to him”. The ASV has “then I will serve Jehovah”. For Geshur the ASV has “Syria”, and the NIV has “Aram”. The Greek has Syria. Hebron is where the men of Judah anointed David (2 Samuel 2:4), where Absalom was born (3:3) and where all the tribes anointed David again (5:3), all already on these pages.

The sound of the horn. “When you hear the sound of the horn, then you shall say: ‘Absalom has become king in Hebron’”: the instrument is the shofar, a ram’s horn, and the cry is the one David himself orders for Solomon (1 Kings 1:34, already on these pages). The NIV has “the sound of the trumpets”. The ASV has “the sound of the trumpet”. The NWT 1984 has “the sound of the horn”. For the cry the KJV has “Absalom reigneth in Hebron”. The NWT 1984 has “has become king in Hebron”. The TLB has “has been crowned in Hebron”. The spies, meraggelim, are “footmen”, from regel, “foot”. The NIV has “secret messengers”.

In their integrity. “Two hundred men from Jerusalem, invited, and going in their integrity; and they knew nothing”: letummam is from tom, “wholeness, integrity”. The KJV has “in their simplicity”. The NIV has “quite innocently”. The NWT 1984 has “unsuspectingly”. The Douay has “simplicity of heart”. The text says what they knew and does not say what they thought when the horn sounded.

Ahithophel from Giloh. “Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David’s counselor, from his city, from Giloh”: Giloh is a town in the hill country of Judah (Joshua 15:51, already on these pages). Rahlfs’ Greek calls him ton Gelmōnaion and the town Gōla. The Bathsheba of 2 Samuel 11:3 (already on these pages) is the daughter of an Eliam, and an Eliam is named as son of Ahithophel the Gilonite in 2 Samuel 23:34 (now on these pages); the text does not say they are the same man. The Hebrew for the plot is qesher, “conspiracy”. The NIV has “the conspiracy gained strength”. The Douay has “a strong conspiracy”. The TLB has “the conspiracy became very strong”. The ASV has “the people increased continually with Absalom”.

Verses 13–18 · “Arise, and let us flee” — the heart of the man of Israel, a city spared, ten concubines, the Far House, and six hundred Gittites

The heart of the man of Israel. “The heart of the man of Israel has been after Absalom”: the Hebrew is singular, lev ish yisrael, as in verse 6, and here it is the messenger’s report to David. The KJV has “The hearts of the men of Israel are after Absalom”. The NWT 1984 has “The heart of the men of Israel has come to be behind Absalom”. The Douay has “All Israel with their whole heart followeth Absalom”. The TLB has “All Israel has joined Absalom in a conspiracy against you”.

Let us flee. “Arise, and let us flee”: nivrachah, the verb of David’s flight from Saul (1 Samuel 19:12 and 20:1, already on these pages), now said to his own servants about his own son. His reason is that “there will be no escape for us”, peleitah, and the evil he fears is that Absalom will “drive the evil down upon us”: vehidiach is from the root nadach, the verb of the banished son in 2 Samuel 14:13–14 (now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “no escaping for us”. The NIV has “bring ruin on us”. The KJV has “bring evil upon us”. He leaves so that the city will not be struck “with the mouth of the sword”. The NIV has “put the city to the sword”. The TLB has “both we and the city of Jerusalem will be saved”.

Ten concubines. “And the king left ten women, concubines, to keep the house”: “ten women, concubines” stands in two verses, here and 2 Samuel 20:3 (now on these pages). The Hebrew calls them pilagshim, which is not “wives”. The TLB has “ten of his young wives”. The NIV has “ten concubines”. The KJV has “ten women, which were concubines”. The household goes out beraglav, “at his feet”. The NWT 1984 has “at his feet”. The Douay has “on foot”. The ASV has “after him”.

The Far House. “And they stood at Beth-merhak”: “Beth-merhak” stands in one verse. The name has the article in the Hebrew and means “the far house”, and the verse does not say where it was. The ASV has “Beth-merhak”. The NWT 1984 has “Beth-merhak”. The KJV has “a place that was far off”. The NIV has “the edge of the city”. The Douay has “stood afar off from the house”.

Six hundred Gittites. “All the Cherethites and all the Pelethites; and all the Gittites, six hundred men who had come at his feet from Gath, were crossing before the king”: the Cherethites and Pelethites are David’s guard (2 Samuel 8:18, already on these pages). The number six hundred is David’s own band at Ziklag (1 Samuel 30:9, already on these pages), and six hundred men crossed with him to Achish king of Gath (1 Samuel 27:2, already on these pages); the verse does not say these are the same men. The ASV has “six hundred men that came after him from Gath”. The NIV has “who had accompanied him from Gath”. Rahlfs’ Greek adds that they “stood by the olive tree in the wilderness”, epi tēs elaias en tē erēmō.

Verses 19–23 · Ittai the Gittite — a foreigner and an exile, “in kindness and truth”, “for death or for life”, and a whole land weeping

A foreigner and an exile. “Return and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner, and also an exile from your place”: nokhri is “foreigner” and goleh “one in exile”. The Hebrew says only “the king”. The NIV has “stay with King Absalom”. The TLB has “to your king”. The NWT 1984 has “you are a foreigner and, besides, you are an exile from your place”. The KJV has “thou art a stranger, and also an exile”. The Geneva has “depart thou therefore to thy place”. “Ittai the Gittite” stands in three verses: verses 19 and 22, and 2 Samuel 18:2 (now on these pages), where David puts him over a third of the army. Gath is the town of Goliath (1 Samuel 17:4, already on these pages).

Make you wander. “Yesterday you came, and today shall I make you wander with us, to go, when I am going where I am going?”: the written text of verse 20 has anu‘akha and the reading tradition has ani‘akha, both of the verb for moving to and fro, so the sense is the same. The ASV has “go up and down with us”. The NIV has “wander about with us”. The NWT 1984 has “make you wander with us”. The Douay has “forced to go forth with us”. The Hebrew for where he is going is only “I am going on what I am going”. The NIV has “when I do not know where I am going”.

In kindness and truth. “Return, and take your brothers back with you, in kindness and truth”: chesed ve’emet has no verb in the Hebrew, and David used the same pair to the men of Jabesh-gilead (2 Samuel 2:6, already on these pages). The versions add one. The NIV has “May the Lord show you kindness and faithfulness”. The KJV has “mercy and truth be with thee”. The NWT 1984 has “[and may Jehovah exercise toward you]” in brackets. The Douay has “the Lord will shew thee mercy, and truth”. The Greek has a verb and a subject: kai kyrios poiēsei meta sou eleos kai alētheian, “and the Lord will do with you mercy and truth”.

For death or for life. “As Jehovah lives, and as my lord the king lives, surely in the place where my lord the king shall be, whether for death or for life, there your servant will be”: the order in the Hebrew is death first. The ASV has “whether for death or for life”. The NIV has “whether it means life or death”. The TLB has “whether it means life or death”. A foreigner who follows into the unknown is Ruth at Ruth 1:16–17 (already on these pages), and Ittai swears by Jehovah as she does.

The whole land weeping. “And all the land was weeping with a loud voice, and all the people were crossing over; and the king was crossing the brook Kidron”: the verb avar, “cross over”, runs through verses 18 to 24. The ASV has “all the country wept with a loud voice”. The NIV has “The whole countryside wept aloud”. The NWT 1984 has “the king was standing by the torrent valley of Kidron”, where the Hebrew has him crossing. Jesus goes out across the same valley on the night he is arrested (John 18:1, already on these pages).

Verses 24–29 · The ark sent back — “its dwelling place”, “Are you a seer?”, and the plains of the wilderness

The ark set down. “Zadok also, and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of the covenant of God; and they set down the ark of God, and Abiathar went up”: the Hebrew has Abiathar “went up”. The ASV has “Abiathar went up”. The NIV has “Abiathar offered sacrifices”. The NWT 1984 has “set the ark of the [true] God down by Abiathar”. Rahlfs’ Greek has the ark “of the covenant of the Lord”, diathēkēs kyriou, and adds apo Baithar, “from Bethar”, after it.

Its dwelling place. “Return the ark of God to the city. If I find favor in the eyes of Jehovah, he will bring me back, and let me see it and its dwelling place”: navehu is the word of Exodus 15:13 (already on these pages), “your holy dwelling”. David sends back the ark that Israel once fetched to be saved by (1 Samuel 4:3) and that he himself brought up to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6:17), both already on these pages. The ASV has “his habitation”. The NIV has “his dwelling place”. The NWT 1984 has “its abiding place”. The Douay has “his tabernacle”. The refusal he allows for, “I have no delight in you”, is followed by “let him do to me as is good in his eyes”, close to Eli’s “let him do what is good in his eyes” (1 Samuel 3:18, already on these pages) but not identical.

Are you a seer? “Are you a seer? Return to the city in peace”: the Hebrew is haro’eh attah, and ro’eh is the old word for a prophet. The ASV has “Art thou not a seer?”. The NWT 1984 has “You are a seer, are you?”. The Douay has “O seer”. The NIV has “Do you understand?”. The TLB has “Look, here is my plan”. The Greek has idete sy epistrepheis, “see, you are returning”, an imperative where the Hebrew has the question.

The plains of the wilderness. “See, I am lingering in the plains of the wilderness until word comes from you to tell me”: the written text has ba‘avrot, “at the fords”, and the reading tradition has ba‘arvot, “in the plains”, and I translate the pointed reading. Arvot is the word for the plains of Jericho (Joshua 4:13 and 5:10, already on these pages). The NIV has “at the fords in the wilderness”. The NWT 1984 has “by the fords of the wilderness”. The Douay has “in the plains of the wilderness”. The Geneva has “in the fields of the wilderness”. Rahlfs’ Greek has en Arabōth, “in Araboth”.

Verses 30–31 · The ascent of the Olives — a covered head, bare feet, and a prayer against Ahithophel

The ascent of the Olives. “And David was going up the ascent of the Olives, going up and weeping, with his head covered, and he was going barefoot”: “the ascent of the Olives” stands in one verse, and the Hebrew does not say “mount”. The NWT 1984 has “the ascent of the Olives”. The ASV has “the ascent of the mount of Olives”. The Douay has “the ascent of mount Olivet”. The TLB has “the road that led to the Mount of Olives”. The head covered and the feet bare are the signs of mourning, and the people with him do the first. The TLB has “his feet were bare as a sign of mourning”. The Mount of Olives is where Jehovah’s feet stand in Zechariah 14:4.

Make foolish. “Make foolish, please, the counsel of Ahithophel, O Jehovah”: sakkel is from the root of Saul’s “I have played the fool” (1 Samuel 26:21) and Samuel’s “You have acted foolishly” (13:13), both already on these pages. The ASV has “turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness”. The Douay has “Infatuate”. The TLB has “foolish advice”. The Greek has diaskedason, “scatter”, and kyrie ho theos mou, “O Lord my God”. The Hebrew as pointed has an active verb, higgid, “And David told, saying”, and the shelf versions read it as a passive. The Greek has anēngelē, “was told”, and the prayer is answered in the verses that follow, by the man who is waiting at the summit.

Verses 32–37 · Hushai the Archite — earth on his head, a burden, and the counsel to be broken

Where God was worshipped. “David came to the summit, where one bowed down to God”: ad-harosh, “to the head”. The ASV has “where God was worshipped”. The NIV has “where people used to worship God”. The Douay has “where he was about to adore the Lord”. Rahlfs’ Greek has Rhōs, a name.

Earth on his head. “Hushai the Archite, his tunic torn, and earth on his head”: “earth on his head” stands in three verses: the Benjaminite who brings news from the battle to Shiloh (1 Samuel 4:12), the man from Saul’s camp (2 Samuel 1:2), both already on these pages, and this one. “his tunic torn” stands in one verse. The NIV has “his robe torn and dust on his head”. The ASV has “his coat rent, and earth upon his head”. The NWT 1984 has “his robe ripped apart”. The Douay has “his garment rent”. The NIV has “Arkite”, and the ASV has “Archite”. The Archites are on the border of Joseph’s land (Joshua 16:2, already on these pages).

A burden, and a counsel to break. “If you cross over with me, then you will be a burden on me”: masa. The ASV has “a burden unto me”. The NWT 1984 has “a load upon me”. “Then you will break for me the counsel of Ahithophel”: the Hebrew verb is hefer, “annul, break”. The NIV has “frustrating Ahithophel’s advice”. The ASV has “defeat for me the counsel of Ahithophel”. The Geneva has “bring me the counsel of Ahithophel to nought”. The words David gives him, “Your servant I am, O king”, name no king; “a servant of your father” is what points them at Absalom.

Two sons, and a friend. “Look, there with them are their two sons, Ahimaaz for Zadok and Jonathan for Abiathar; and you shall send by their hand to me every word that you hear”: the two sons are the ones already in the king’s plan in verse 27. “Hushai, David’s friend, came to the city; and Absalom was coming into Jerusalem.” The NIV has “David’s confidant”. The NWT 1984 has “David’s companion”. The TLB has “getting there just as Absalom arrived”. The NWT 1984 has “he proceeded to come into Jerusalem”. The Greek has Hushai ho hetairos Dauid already in verse 32.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “stole the heart”, singular, as the Hebrew has it; “a hearer you have not”; “declare him in the right”; “the horn” for shofar; “in their integrity”; “the conspiracy”; “at his feet”; “ten women, concubines”; “Beth-merhak”; “a foreigner” and “an exile”; “in kindness and truth”; “Are you a seer?”; “the ascent of the Olives”; “make foolish”; “earth on his head”; and the pointed readings “make you wander” and “in the plains of the wilderness”, with the written forms in the notes.

Echoes paid. Adonijah’s fifty runners (1 Kings 1:5); the king who takes sons to run before his chariot (1 Samuel 8:11); the king’s runners (1 Samuel 22:17); the gate and the elders (Deuteronomy 25:7); David’s justice and righteousness (2 Samuel 8:15); the king’s kiss (2 Samuel 14:33); Jacob stealing Laban’s heart (Genesis 31:20); Hebron, where David was anointed twice and Absalom was born (2 Samuel 2:4; 3:3; 5:3); the horn and the cry for Solomon (1 Kings 1:34); Giloh (Joshua 15:51); Bathsheba, daughter of Eliam (2 Samuel 11:3); David’s flight from Saul (1 Samuel 19:12; 20:1); the banished son’s root (2 Samuel 14:13–14); the Cherethites and Pelethites (2 Samuel 8:18); six hundred men (1 Samuel 27:2; 30:9); Goliath’s Gath (1 Samuel 17:4); “kindness and truth” to Jabesh-gilead (2 Samuel 2:6); Ruth’s oath (Ruth 1:16–17); the Kidron on the night of the arrest (John 18:1); the holy dwelling (Exodus 15:13); the ark taken as a charm and brought up (1 Samuel 4:3; 2 Samuel 6:17); Eli’s “what is good in his eyes” (1 Samuel 3:18); the plains of Jericho (Joshua 4:13; 5:10); the fool in Saul and Samuel (1 Samuel 26:21; 13:13); earth on the head (1 Samuel 4:12; 2 Samuel 1:2); and the Archites (Joshua 16:2).

Echoes planted. Ziba’s donkeys and Shimei’s stones (2 Samuel 16:1, 5, now on these pages); Hushai’s “Long live the king” and the counsel to go in to the concubines (2 Samuel 16:16, 21, now on these pages); Jehovah ordaining that Ahithophel’s counsel be defeated, and the two sons at the well (2 Samuel 17:14, 17, now on these pages); the army put in three parts under Ittai (2 Samuel 18:2, now on these pages); the ten women shut up (2 Samuel 20:3, now on these pages); Eliam son of Ahithophel (2 Samuel 23:34); the heading of Psalm 3; Hushai the king’s friend (1 Chronicles 27:33); and the feet on the Mount of Olives (Zechariah 14:4). None of the others is translated here yet.
