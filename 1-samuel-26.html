“Is this your voice, my son David?” a second time: the Ziphites tell Saul again that David is hiding on the hill of Hachilah, and Saul goes down with three thousand chosen men. By night David and Abishai walk into the camp, where Saul sleeps within the ring with his spear stuck in the ground at his head and a deep sleep from Jehovah on them all. David will not let Abishai strike — “who has stretched out his hand against Jehovah’s anointed and been innocent?” — and takes the spear and the jug of water instead. From a mountain across the valley he shames Abner and pleads with Saul; Saul confesses “I have acted foolishly”, blesses him, and the two part for the last time.
Undated: in Saul’s reign, after Nabal’s death and before David goes over to Achish of Gath in chapter 27.

וַיָּבֹאוּ הַזִּפִים אֶל-שָׁאוּל, הַגִּבְעָתָה לֵאמֹר:  הֲלוֹא דָוִד מִסְתַּתֵּר בְּגִבְעַת הַחֲכִילָה, עַל פְּנֵי הַיְשִׁימֹן.
And the Ziphites came to Saul at Gibeah, saying: ‘Is not David hiding himself on the hill of Hachilah, facing the Jeshimon?’note
Y los zifeos vinieron a Saúl, a Guibeá, diciendo: «¿No está David escondiéndose en el collado de Haquilá, frente al Yesimón?».
2
וַיָּקָם שָׁאוּל, וַיֵּרֶד אֶל-מִדְבַּר-זִיף, וְאִתּוֹ שְׁלֹשֶׁת-אֲלָפִים אִישׁ, בְּחוּרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--לְבַקֵּשׁ אֶת-דָּוִד, בְּמִדְבַּר-זִיף.
And Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, and with him three thousand men, the chosen of Israel, to seek David in the wilderness of Ziph.note
Y Saúl se levantó y bajó al desierto de Zif, y con él tres mil hombres, los escogidos de Israel, para buscar a David en el desierto de Zif.
3
וַיִּחַן שָׁאוּל בְּגִבְעַת הַחֲכִילָה, אֲשֶׁר עַל-פְּנֵי הַיְשִׁימֹן--עַל-הַדָּרֶךְ; וְדָוִד, יֹשֵׁב בַּמִּדְבָּר, וַיַּרְא, כִּי בָא שָׁאוּל אַחֲרָיו הַמִּדְבָּרָה.
And Saul camped on the hill of Hachilah, which faces the Jeshimon, by the road. And David was staying in the wilderness, and he saw that Saul had come after him into the wilderness.note
Y Saúl acampó en el collado de Haquilá, que está frente al Yesimón, junto al camino. Y David se quedaba en el desierto, y vio que Saúl había venido tras él al desierto.
4
וַיִּשְׁלַח דָּוִד, מְרַגְּלִים; וַיֵּדַע, כִּי-בָא שָׁאוּל אֶל-נָכוֹן.
And David sent spies, and knew that Saul had come for certain.note
Y David envió espías, y supo que Saúl había venido con certeza.
5
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד, וַיָּבֹא אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר חָנָה-שָׁם שָׁאוּל, וַיַּרְא דָּוִד אֶת-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר שָׁכַב-שָׁם שָׁאוּל, וְאַבְנֵר בֶּן-נֵר שַׂר-צְבָאוֹ; וְשָׁאוּל שֹׁכֵב בַּמַּעְגָּל, וְהָעָם חֹנִים סְבִיבֹתָו.
And David arose and came to the place where Saul had camped; and David saw the place where Saul lay, and Abner son of Ner, the commander of his army. And Saul was lying within the ring, and the people were camped around him.note
Y David se levantó y vino al lugar donde Saúl había acampado; y David vio el lugar donde estaba acostado Saúl, y Abner hijo de Ner, el jefe de su ejército. Y Saúl estaba acostado dentro del cerco, y el pueblo acampaba alrededor de él.
6
וַיַּעַן דָּוִד וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל-אֲחִימֶלֶךְ הַחִתִּי, וְאֶל-אֲבִישַׁי בֶּן-צְרוּיָה אֲחִי יוֹאָב לֵאמֹר, מִי-יֵרֵד אִתִּי אֶל-שָׁאוּל, אֶל-הַמַּחֲנֶה; וַיֹּאמֶר אֲבִישַׁי, אֲנִי אֵרֵד עִמָּךְ.
And David answered and said to Ahimelech the Hittite, and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, saying: ‘Who will go down with me to Saul, to the camp?’ And Abishai said: ‘I will go down with you.’note
Y David respondió y dijo a Ahimelec el heteo, y a Abisai hijo de Sarvia, hermano de Joab, diciendo: «¿Quién bajará conmigo a Saúl, al campamento?». Y Abisai dijo: «Yo bajaré contigo».
7
וַיָּבֹא דָוִד וַאֲבִישַׁי אֶל-הָעָם, לַיְלָה, וְהִנֵּה שָׁאוּל שֹׁכֵב יָשֵׁן בַּמַּעְגָּל, וַחֲנִיתוֹ מְעוּכָה-בָאָרֶץ מְרַאֲשֹׁתָו; וְאַבְנֵר וְהָעָם, שֹׁכְבִים סְבִיבֹתָו.  {ס}
And David and Abishai came to the people by night; and look — Saul lying asleep within the ring, and his spear stuck in the ground at his head, and Abner and the people lying around him.note
Y David y Abisai llegaron al pueblo de noche; y mira, Saúl acostado, dormido, dentro del cerco, y su lanza clavada en tierra a su cabecera, y Abner y el pueblo acostados alrededor de él.
8
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲבִישַׁי אֶל-דָּוִד, סִגַּר אֱלֹהִים הַיּוֹם אֶת-אוֹיִבְךָ בְּיָדֶךָ; וְעַתָּה אַכֶּנּוּ נָא בַּחֲנִית וּבָאָרֶץ, פַּעַם אַחַת, וְלֹא אֶשְׁנֶה, לוֹ.
And Abishai said to David: ‘God has handed over your enemy into your hand today. And now let me strike him, please, with the spear to the ground, once; and I will not do it to him twice.’note
Y Abisai dijo a David: «Dios ha entregado hoy a tu enemigo en tu mano. Y ahora déjame herirlo, por favor, con la lanza contra la tierra, una vez; y no se lo haré dos veces».
9
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-אֲבִישַׁי, אַל-תַּשְׁחִיתֵהוּ:  כִּי מִי שָׁלַח יָדוֹ, בִּמְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה--וְנִקָּה.  {פ}
And David said to Abishai: ‘Do not destroy him; for who has stretched out his hand against Jehovah’s anointed and been innocent?’note
Y David dijo a Abisai: «No lo destruyas; porque ¿quién ha extendido su mano contra el ungido de Jehová y ha quedado inocente?».
10
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד חַי-יְהוָה, כִּי אִם-יְהוָה יִגֳּפֶנּוּ; אוֹ-יוֹמוֹ יָבוֹא וָמֵת, אוֹ בַמִּלְחָמָה יֵרֵד וְנִסְפָּה.
And David said: ‘As Jehovah lives, Jehovah himself will strike him; or his day will come, and he will die; or he will go down into battle and be swept away.note
Y David dijo: «Vive Jehová, que Jehová mismo lo herirá; o llegará su día, y morirá; o bajará a la batalla y será barrido.
11
חָלִילָה לִּי מֵיְהוָה, מִשְּׁלֹחַ יָדִי בִּמְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה; וְעַתָּה קַח-נָא אֶת-הַחֲנִית אֲשֶׁר מְרַאֲשֹׁתָו, וְאֶת-צַפַּחַת הַמַּיִם--וְנֵלְכָה-לָּנוּ.
Far be it from me, by Jehovah, to stretch out my hand against Jehovah’s anointed! And now take, please, the spear that is at his head, and the jug of water, and let us go.’note
¡Lejos esté de mí, por Jehová, extender mi mano contra el ungido de Jehová! Y ahora toma, por favor, la lanza que está a su cabecera, y la jarra de agua, y vámonos».
12
וַיִּקַּח דָּוִד אֶת-הַחֲנִית וְאֶת-צַפַּחַת הַמַּיִם, מֵרַאֲשֹׁתֵי שָׁאוּל, וַיֵּלְכוּ, לָהֶם; וְאֵין רֹאֶה וְאֵין יוֹדֵעַ וְאֵין מֵקִיץ, כִּי כֻלָּם יְשֵׁנִים--כִּי תַּרְדֵּמַת יְהוָה, נָפְלָה עֲלֵיהֶם.
And David took the spear and the jug of water from Saul’s head, and they went away; and there was no one who saw, and no one who knew, and no one who woke, for they were all asleep; for a deep sleep from Jehovah had fallen upon them.note
Y David tomó la lanza y la jarra de agua de la cabecera de Saúl, y se fueron; y no había quien viera, ni quien supiera, ni quien despertara, porque todos estaban dormidos; porque un sueño profundo de Jehová había caído sobre ellos.
13
וַיַּעֲבֹר דָּוִד הָעֵבֶר, וַיַּעֲמֹד עַל-רֹאשׁ-הָהָר מֵרָחֹק:  רַב הַמָּקוֹם, בֵּינֵיהֶם.
And David crossed over to the other side, and stood on the top of the mountain far off; great was the space between them.note
Y David pasó al otro lado, y se puso en la cumbre del monte, lejos; grande era el espacio entre ellos.
14
וַיִּקְרָא דָוִד אֶל-הָעָם, וְאֶל-אַבְנֵר בֶּן-נֵר לֵאמֹר, הֲלוֹא תַעֲנֶה, אַבְנֵר; וַיַּעַן אַבְנֵר וַיֹּאמֶר, מִי אַתָּה קָרָאתָ אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ.  {פ}
And David called out to the people and to Abner son of Ner, saying: ‘Will you not answer, Abner?’ And Abner answered and said: ‘Who are you, that you have called out to the king?’note
Y David llamó al pueblo y a Abner hijo de Ner, diciendo: «¿No vas a responder, Abner?». Y Abner respondió y dijo: «¿Quién eres tú, que has llamado al rey?».
15
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-אַבְנֵר הֲלוֹא-אִישׁ אַתָּה, וּמִי כָמוֹךָ בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, וְלָמָּה לֹא שָׁמַרְתָּ, אֶל-אֲדֹנֶיךָ הַמֶּלֶךְ:  כִּי-בָא אַחַד הָעָם, לְהַשְׁחִית אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ אֲדֹנֶיךָ.
And David said to Abner: ‘Are you not a man? And who is like you in Israel? Then why have you not kept watch over your lord the king? For one of the people came in to destroy the king your lord.note
Y David dijo a Abner: «¿No eres tú un hombre? ¿Y quién como tú en Israel? ¿Por qué, pues, no has vigilado a tu señor el rey? Porque uno del pueblo entró para destruir al rey tu señor.
16
לֹא-טוֹב הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, אֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ--חַי-יְהוָה כִּי בְנֵי-מָוֶת אַתֶּם, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-שְׁמַרְתֶּם עַל-אֲדֹנֵיכֶם עַל-מְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה; וְעַתָּה רְאֵה, אֵי-חֲנִית הַמֶּלֶךְ וְאֶת-צַפַּחַת הַמַּיִם--אֲשֶׁר מְרַאֲשֹׁתָו.
This thing that you have done is not good. As Jehovah lives, you are sons of death, because you have not kept watch over your lord, over Jehovah’s anointed. And now see where the king’s spear is, and the jug of water that was at his head.’note
No es bueno esto que has hecho. Vive Jehová, que ustedes son hijos de muerte, porque no han vigilado a su señor, al ungido de Jehová. Y ahora mira dónde está la lanza del rey, y la jarra de agua que estaba a su cabecera».
17
וַיַּכֵּר שָׁאוּל, אֶת-קוֹל דָּוִד, וַיֹּאמֶר, הֲקוֹלְךָ זֶה בְּנִי דָוִד; וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, קוֹלִי אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ.
And Saul recognized David’s voice, and said: ‘Is this your voice, my son David?’ And David said: ‘It is my voice, my lord the king.’note
Y Saúl reconoció la voz de David, y dijo: «¿Es esta tu voz, hijo mío David?». Y David dijo: «Es mi voz, mi señor el rey».
18
וַיֹּאמֶר, לָמָּה זֶּה אֲדֹנִי רֹדֵף אַחֲרֵי עַבְדּוֹ:  כִּי מֶה עָשִׂיתִי, וּמַה-בְּיָדִי רָעָה.
And he said: ‘Why is my lord pursuing after his servant? For what have I done, and what evil is in my hand?note
Y dijo: «¿Por qué persigue mi señor a su siervo? Porque ¿qué he hecho, y qué mal hay en mi mano?
19
וְעַתָּה, יִשְׁמַע-נָא אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֵת, דִּבְרֵי עַבְדּוֹ:  אִם-יְהוָה הֱסִיתְךָ בִי, יָרַח מִנְחָה, וְאִם בְּנֵי הָאָדָם אֲרוּרִים הֵם לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, כִּי-גֵרְשׁוּנִי הַיּוֹם מֵהִסְתַּפֵּחַ בְּנַחֲלַת יְהוָה לֵאמֹר לֵךְ עֲבֹד אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים.
And now let my lord the king hear, please, the words of his servant. If it is Jehovah who has incited you against me, let him smell an offering; but if it is the sons of men, cursed are they before Jehovah, for they have driven me out today from attaching myself to the inheritance of Jehovah, saying: “Go, serve other gods.”note
Y ahora oiga, por favor, mi señor el rey las palabras de su siervo. Si es Jehová quien te ha incitado contra mí, huela él una ofrenda; pero si son los hijos de los hombres, malditos son delante de Jehová, porque me han expulsado hoy para que no me una a la heredad de Jehová, diciendo: “Ve, sirve a otros dioses”.
20
וְעַתָּה, אַל-יִפֹּל דָּמִי אַרְצָה, מִנֶּגֶד, פְּנֵי יְהוָה:  כִּי-יָצָא מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, לְבַקֵּשׁ אֶת-פַּרְעֹשׁ אֶחָד, כַּאֲשֶׁר יִרְדֹּף הַקֹּרֵא, בֶּהָרִים.
And now let not my blood fall to the ground away from the presence of Jehovah; for the king of Israel has come out to seek a single flea, as one hunts the partridge in the mountains.’note
Y ahora no caiga mi sangre a tierra lejos de la presencia de Jehová; porque el rey de Israel ha salido a buscar una sola pulga, como quien persigue a la perdiz por los montes».
21
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל חָטָאתִי שׁוּב בְּנִי-דָוִד, כִּי לֹא-אָרַע לְךָ עוֹד, תַּחַת אֲשֶׁר יָקְרָה נַפְשִׁי בְּעֵינֶיךָ, הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה:  הִנֵּה הִסְכַּלְתִּי וָאֶשְׁגֶּה, הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד.
And Saul said: ‘I have sinned. Return, my son David, for I will not do you evil again, because my soul was precious in your eyes this day. Look — I have acted foolishly, and have erred very greatly.’note
Y Saúl dijo: «He pecado. Vuelve, hijo mío David, porque no te haré más mal, ya que mi alma ha sido preciosa a tus ojos este día. Mira, he obrado neciamente, y he errado muchísimo».
22
וַיַּעַן דָּוִד וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה החנית חֲנִית הַמֶּלֶךְ; וְיַעֲבֹר אֶחָד מֵהַנְּעָרִים, וְיִקָּחֶהָ.
And David answered and said: ‘Look — the king’s spear! Let one of the young men come over and take it.note
Y David respondió y dijo: «Mira, ¡la lanza del rey! Que pase uno de los jóvenes y la tome.
23
וַיהוָה יָשִׁיב לָאִישׁ, אֶת-צִדְקָתוֹ וְאֶת-אֱמֻנָתוֹ--אֲשֶׁר נְתָנְךָ יְהוָה הַיּוֹם, בְּיָד, וְלֹא אָבִיתִי, לִשְׁלֹחַ יָדִי בִּמְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה.
And Jehovah will return to each man his righteousness and his faithfulness; for Jehovah gave you today into my hand, and I was not willing to stretch out my hand against Jehovah’s anointed.note
Y Jehová hará volver a cada uno su justicia y su fidelidad; porque Jehová te dio hoy en mi mano, y yo no quise extender mi mano contra el ungido de Jehová.
24
וְהִנֵּה, כַּאֲשֶׁר גָּדְלָה נַפְשְׁךָ הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה--בְּעֵינָי:  כֵּן תִּגְדַּל נַפְשִׁי בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה, וְיַצִּלֵנִי מִכָּל-צָרָה.  {פ}
And look — as your soul was great this day in my eyes, so may my soul be great in the eyes of Jehovah, and may he deliver me out of all distress.’note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · The Ziphites a second time — the hill of Hachilah facing the Jeshimon, three thousand chosen men, and “come for certain”

The Ziphites again. “And the Ziphites came to Saul at Gibeah, saying: ‘Is not David hiding himself on the hill of Hachilah, facing the Jeshimon?’” They brought Saul nearly the same question three chapters ago: “Is not David hiding himself with us in the strongholds at Horesh, on the hill of Hachilah, which is south of the Jeshimon?” (23:19, already on these pages). Here it is shorter, with no “with us”, no strongholds and no Horesh, and the Greek puts “with us” back (meth’ hēmōn). Hachilah is named in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, 23:19 and verses 1 and 3 of this chapter (Hachilah; Ziph; the Jeshimon). “Facing” is al-penei, where 23:19 had mimin, “south of”. The KJV has “which is before Jeshimon”; the ASV reads the name as a noun, “which is before the desert”; the Douay “which is over against the wilderness”; the NIV “which faces Jeshimon”.

The same bones. The chapter tells, with differences, the story of the cave at En-gedi: the Ziphites, three thousand chosen men, a king asleep or unguarded in David’s power, “Jehovah’s anointed”, “Is this your voice, my son David?”, and a single flea (chapter 24, already on these pages, where the note on verses 20–22 sets out the question). This translation gives the two chapters the same English where their Hebrew is the same, so that a reader can lay them side by side.

Three thousand chosen men. “And Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, and with him three thousand men, the chosen of Israel”: the number of En-gedi, “three thousand chosen men out of all Israel” (24:2, already on these pages), and the wilderness of Ziph named twice in one verse. The heading of Psalm 54 remembers the Ziphites with the words of 23:19, “Is not David hiding himself with us?” (Psalm 54:2 in the Hebrew numbering). The NIV has “with his three thousand select Israelite troops”; the TLB “So Saul took his elite corps of three thousand troops and went to hunt him down.”

Come for certain. “And David sent spies, and knew that Saul had come for certain”: el-nakhon (nakhon). The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Saul’s order to the Ziphites, “come back to me with certainty” (23:23, already on these pages), and this one. Saul asked for certainty about David, and David’s spies now bring him certainty about Saul. The Greek reads the phrase another way: “that Saul had come, ready, out of Keilah”. The KJV has “and understood that Saul was come in very deed”; the ASV “and understood that Saul was come of a certainty”; the NIV “and learned that Saul had definitely arrived”; the Douay “and learned that he was most certainly come thither”.

Verses 5–7 · Within the ring — Abner son of Ner, Ahimelech the Hittite and Abishai son of Zeruiah, and the spear stuck in the ground

Within the ring. “And Saul was lying within the ring, and the people were camped around him”: ba-ma’gal (ma’gal), the circle a camp is drawn up in, the word of the morning David brought his brothers their food: “he came to the ring of the camp” (17:20, already on these pages). Saul sleeps at its centre with his army round him and Abner beside him, “Abner son of Ner, the commander of his army”, the title of 14:50 and 17:55 (14:50; 17:55, both already on these pages) (Abner). The KJV has “and Saul lay in the trench”; the ASV “and Saul lay within the place of the wagons”; the NWT 1984 “and Saul was lying in the camp enclosure”; the Geneva “for Saul lay in the fort”; the Douay “and Saul sleeping in a tent”. The Greek has lampēnē, a covered wagon, and the ASV’s wagons point the same way: ma’gal shares its root with agalah, a cart.

Who will go down with me? “And David answered and said to Ahimelech the Hittite, and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, saying: ‘Who will go down with me to Saul, to the camp?’” Ahimelech the Hittite is named in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one (Ahimelech the Hittite): not the priest of Nob (21:2, already on these pages), but a Hittite in David’s band, as Uriah the Hittite will be, one of his mighty men (2 Samuel 11:3, now on these pages; 23:39). He is asked, and does not answer. Abishai is named here for the first time, and so is Joab: this verse is the only one in 1 Samuel that names him (Abishai; Joab). Their mother Zeruiah is David’s sister, “And their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail. And the sons of Zeruiah: Abishai, and Joab, and Asahel, three” (1 Chronicles 2:16) (Zeruiah), so the man who volunteers is David’s nephew, and he is named after his mother. The KJV has “Who will go down with me to Saul to the camp?”; the TLB “Any volunteers to go down there with me?”; and for Abishai’s answer the NWT 1984 has “I myself shall go down with you.”

The spear at his head. “And look — Saul lying asleep within the ring, and his spear stuck in the ground at his head”: va-chanito me’ukhah va-arets meraashotav (chanit). The spear has been in Saul’s hand since the evil spirit came on him: he hurled it at David twice (18:10–11; 19:9–10) and at Jonathan (20:33), and he sat under the tamarisk at Gibeah “with his spear in his hand” (22:6, all already on these pages). Now it stands in the ground, out of his hand, while he sleeps. The last time the story shows it, on Gilboa, “Saul was leaning on his spear” (2 Samuel 1:6, already on these pages). “At his head”, meraashotav, is where Jacob put his stone at Bethel (Genesis 28:11) and where Michal put the net of goats’ hair on the image in David’s bed, “at its head” (19:13, both already on these pages). The KJV has “and his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster”; the ASV “with his spear stuck in the ground at his head”; the NWT 1984 “with his spear stuck into the earth at his head”; the Douay “and his spear fixed in the ground at his head”.

Verse 8 · “Let me strike him with the spear to the ground” — Abishai’s “once”, and the spear that struck the wall

Handed over. “God has handed over your enemy into your hand today”: sigger, the verb Saul used in the cave, “Jehovah handed me over into your hand” (24:18, already on these pages). In 23:20 the Ziphites promised to “hand him over into the king’s hand” (23:20, already on these pages), with the same root. The verb has gone round the circle: offered against David, said by Saul of himself, and now said of Saul by David’s man. David’s men in the cave said nearly the same thing, “Look — I am giving your enemy into your hand” (24:4, already on these pages). The Greek has “the Lord” for “God”. The Geneva has “God hath closed thine enemy into thine hand this day”; the Douay “God hath shut up thy enemy this day into thy hands”; the NWT 1984 “God has today surrendered your enemy into your hand”.

To the ground, once. “And now let me strike him, please, with the spear to the ground, once; and I will not do it to him twice”: akkennu na ba-chanit u-va-arets pa’am achat ve-lo eshneh lo. It is the shape of Saul’s own sentence when he hurled the spear at David, “I will strike David to the wall”, akkeh be-David u-va-qir (18:11, already on these pages): the same verb, and the man and what he would be pinned to joined by “and”. Saul missed, and “David turned aside from before him twice”, pa’amayim; Abishai promises one stroke, pa’am achat, with no need of a second. The ASV has “let me smite him, I pray thee, with the spear to the earth at one stroke, and I will not smite him the second time”; the NWT 1984 “pin him to the earth with the spear just once, and I shall not do it to him twice”; the NIV “Now let me pin him to the ground with one thrust of the spear; I won’t strike him twice.” The TLB has “I’ll pin him to the earth with it”.

Verses 9–11 · “Who has stretched out his hand against Jehovah’s anointed?” — strike, his day, or the battle, and the jug of water

Do not destroy him. “Do not destroy him; for who has stretched out his hand against Jehovah’s anointed and been innocent?”: al-tashchitehu, from shachat (shachat), and ve-niqqah, from naqah, to be clean of guilt, to go unpunished (naqah). The question expects the answer “no one”. In the cave David said “I will not stretch out my hand against my lord, for he is Jehovah’s anointed” (24:10, already on these pages) (mashiach); he will put the same two verbs to the Amalekite who says he killed Saul: “How were you not afraid to stretch out your hand to destroy Jehovah’s anointed?” (2 Samuel 1:14, already on these pages). The Greek has “do not humble him”. The ASV has “Destroy him not; for who can put forth his hand against Jehovah’s anointed, and be guiltless?”; the NWT 1984 “Do not bring him to ruin”; the NWT 2013 “Do not harm him”; the Douay “Kill him not”.

Three ways to die. “As Jehovah lives, Jehovah himself will strike him; or his day will come, and he will die; or he will go down into battle and be swept away”: three ends, and none of them by David’s hand. “Strike” is nagaf (nagaf), the verb of the chapter before: “Jehovah struck Nabal, and he died” (25:38, already on these pages). “Swept away” is nispah, from safah (safah), the word of Samuel’s warning at Gilgal, “you shall be swept away, both you and your king” (12:25), and of Abraham’s question, “Will you really sweep away the righteous with the wicked?” (Genesis 18:23, both already on these pages). The third way is the one that comes: Saul dies in battle on Gilboa, falling on his own sword (31:4–6, now on these pages). In the next verse of the book David turns the verb on himself: “Now I shall be swept away one day by the hand of Saul” (27:1, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah himself will deal him a blow” and “he will certainly be swept away”; the KJV “or he shall descend into battle, and perish”; the TLB “Surely God will strike him down some day, or he will die in battle or of old age.”

Far be it from me, by Jehovah. “Far be it from me, by Jehovah, to stretch out my hand against Jehovah’s anointed!”: chalilah li me-YHVH (chalilah), the oath of the cave (24:6, already on these pages). That form of it stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: 24:6, this verse, and Naboth’s refusal to sell his vineyard to Ahab (1 Kings 21:3). The NWT 1984 has “It is unthinkable, on my part, from Jehovah’s standpoint”; the NWT 2013 “It is unthinkable from Jehovah’s standpoint for me to lift my hand against the anointed of Jehovah!”; the TLB “But God forbid that I should kill the man he has chosen to be king!”

The jug of water. “And now take, please, the spear that is at his head, and the jug of water”: tsappachat ha-mayim (tsappachat). The word stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible: three in this chapter (verses 11, 12 and 16), and four in the story of Elijah: the widow’s jug of oil that did not fail (1 Kings 17:12, 14, 16), and the jug of water the angel left “at his head” when Elijah slept under the broom tree, “and look — at his head a cake baked on hot stones and a jug of water” (1 Kings 19:6), with the same meraashotav. Elijah wakes to find bread and water at his head; Saul will wake to find his water gone. The KJV has “and the cruse of water, and let us go”; the NWT 1984 “and the water jug”; the Geneva “and the pot of water, and let us go hence”; the Douay “and the cup of water”. The Greek has phakos, a flask.

Verse 12 · “A deep sleep from Jehovah” — no one saw, no one knew, no one woke

Three times “there is no”. “And David took the spear and the jug of water from Saul’s head, and they went away”; then ve-ein ro’eh ve-ein yode’a ve-ein meqits, three participles, seeing, knowing and waking, each with its own “there is not”. In the cave David cut off the corner of Saul’s robe, and afterward “David’s heart struck him” (24:5, already on these pages); here he takes what lies beside the king, not a piece of him, and the text says nothing of his heart. The NWT 1984 has “nor anyone taking note nor anyone waking up”; the Geneva “and no man saw it, nor marked it, neither did any awake”; the TLB “and they got away without anyone seeing them or even waking up”.

A deep sleep from Jehovah. “For they were all asleep; for a deep sleep from Jehovah had fallen upon them”: tardemat YHVH (tardemah). The noun stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible. Two are the sleeps in which God does his work while the man lies still: “So Jehovah God cast the human into a deep sleep” (Genesis 2:21) and “a deep sleep fell upon Abram” (Genesis 15:12, both already on these pages); the others are this verse, Isaiah 29:10, Job 4:13 and 33:15, and Proverbs 19:15. The narrator gives the reason two men could walk through a camp of three thousand and out again: the sleep was Jehovah’s, not David’s skill. The ASV has “because a deep sleep from Jehovah was fallen upon them”; the NWT 1984 “because it was a deep sleep from Jehovah that had fallen upon them”; the Geneva “a dead sleep”. The Greek has thambos, astonishment.

Verses 13–16 · “Are you not a man?” — across the valley, David calls to Abner, and “you are sons of death”

The other side. “And David crossed over to the other side, and stood on the top of the mountain far off; great was the space between them”: va-ya’avor … ha-ever, the verb and the noun from one root. In the wilderness of Maon the two sides of one mountain kept Saul and David apart while Saul closed in: “And Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side of the mountain” (23:26, already on these pages). Here David puts the valley between them before he speaks; at En-gedi he came straight out of the cave after Saul (24:8, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “the space between them being vast”; the NIV “there was a wide space between them”; the KJV “and stood on the top of an hill afar off”; the TLB “until they were at a safe distance”.

Will you not answer, Abner? “And David called out to the people and to Abner son of Ner, saying: ‘Will you not answer, Abner?’ And Abner answered and said: ‘Who are you, that you have called out to the king?’” David speaks to the commander, not the king, and Abner, hearing a voice from the mountain in the dark, does not know whose it is. “Called out”, qara, is said twice; it comes back in the name of a bird in verse 20. The Greek has Abner say “Who are you that calls me?” The Geneva has “Hearest thou not, Abner?”; the Douay “Who art thou, that criest, and disturbest the king?”; the TLB “Wake up, Abner!”

Are you not a man? “Are you not a man? And who is like you in Israel? Then why have you not kept watch over your lord the king?”: ha-lo-ish attah, and shamarta, from shamar (shamar). David is the guard who kept watch for nothing over Nabal’s flocks, “it was for a lie that I guarded all that this one has” (25:21, already on these pages), and now he charges the king’s guard with failing to keep his. “For one of the people came in to destroy the king your lord”: le-hashchit, the verb David forbade Abishai in verse 9. He accuses Abner of failing to stop what David himself would not do. The KJV adds a word, “Art not thou a valiant man?”; the NWT 1984 has “Are you not a man?”; the TLB makes it a taunt, “you’re a great fellow, aren’t you?”

Sons of death. “As Jehovah lives, you are sons of death, because you have not kept watch over your lord, over Jehovah’s anointed”: benei-mavet. The phrase, singular or plural, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Saul’s sentence on David at the new moon, “send and take him to me, for he is a son of death” (20:31, already on these pages); this verse; and David’s verdict on the rich man of Nathan’s parable, “As Jehovah lives, the man who did this is a son of death” (2 Samuel 12:5), passed before he knows the man is himself. Saul’s word against David comes back from David’s mouth against the men guarding Saul. The KJV has “ye are worthy to die”; the NWT 1984 “deserve to die”; the NIV “you and your men must die”; the Douay “you are the sons of death”.

See where the spear is. “And now see where the king’s spear is, and the jug of water that was at his head”: the evidence, as the corner of the robe was in the cave, “see — yes, see the corner of your robe in my hand” (24:11, already on these pages). There David showed what he had cut off; here he shows what he has carried away. The NIV has “Look around you. Where are the king’s spear and water jug that were near his head?”

Verses 17–18 · “Is this your voice, my son David?” — the question asked a second time, and answered

Saul recognized David’s voice. “And Saul recognized David’s voice”: va-yakker, the verb of recognition in Genesis, where Isaac “did not recognize him” (Genesis 27:23) and Joseph “saw his brothers and recognized them” (Genesis 42:7, both already on these pages). It is night, and the king knows by the voice alone the man his commander did not know. His question is the question of the cave word for word, ha-qolkha zeh beni David, “Is this your voice, my son David?” (24:16, already on these pages). In the cave Saul asked it and wept, and no answer is recorded; here David answers: “It is my voice, my lord the king”, qoli adoni ha-melekh. The Greek has him answer “Your servant, lord king”. The NWT 1984 has “It is my voice, my lord the king.”; the NIV “Yes it is, my lord the king.”; the TLB “Is that you, my son David?”

What evil is in my hand? “Why is my lord pursuing after his servant? For what have I done, and what evil is in my hand?”: lammah zeh, “why is this that”, and mah asiti, David’s first words to Jonathan after Naioth, “What have I done? What is my iniquity, and what is my sin before your father?” (20:1, already on these pages). In the cave he told Saul “there is no evil or rebellion in my hand” (24:11); here he asks Saul to name it. The KJV’s “thus” renders zeh: “Wherefore doth my lord thus pursue after his servant?”; the Douay has “Wherefore doth my lord persecute his servant?”; the NWT 2013 “and what am I guilty of?”

Verses 19–20 · “If Jehovah has incited you” — let him smell an offering, “Go, serve other gods”, and the partridge in the mountains

If Jehovah has incited you. “If it is Jehovah who has incited you against me, let him smell an offering”: hesitkha vi, from sut, to incite, to stir up against (sut). David allows that the pursuit may come from Jehovah, and the book has said as much: “an evil spirit of Jehovah came upon Saul, as he sat in his house with his spear in his hand” (19:9, already on these pages). The same verb names the census, “and he incited David against them, saying, ‘Go, number Israel and Judah’” (2 Samuel 24:1), where Chronicles gives the inciting to Satan (1 Chronicles 21:1), and Jehovah says it to the Accuser of Job, “you incited me against him” (Job 2:3). “Let him smell an offering”: yarach minchah (minchah), as after the flood “Jehovah smelled the soothing aroma” and spoke of never again striking every living thing (Genesis 8:21, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “let him smell a grain offering”; the Geneva “let him smell the savor of a sacrifice”; the KJV “let him accept an offering”; the TLB “then let him accept my peace offering”. The Greek has “may your sacrifice be smelled”.

Driven out from the inheritance. “But if it is the sons of men, cursed are they before Jehovah, for they have driven me out today from attaching myself to the inheritance of Jehovah”: gereshuni ha-yom, Cain’s words to Jehovah, “See, you have driven me today from the face of the ground” (Genesis 4:14, already on these pages), the verb and the “today” together. “From attaching myself”, me-histappeach, is from safach (safach); this reflexive form stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one, and its simple form is the plea of Eli’s descendants, “Please attach me to one of the priest’s offices” (2:36, already on these pages). The inheritance of Jehovah (nachalah) is his land and his people, and to be driven from it is to be sent among foreign gods. The NWT 1984 has “because they have driven me out today from feeling myself attached to the inheritance of Jehovah”; the KJV “from abiding in the inheritance”; the NIV “They have driven me today from my share in the”.

“Go, serve other gods.” The men who drive him out are made to say it: “Go, serve other gods”. Deuteronomy sets exile and foreign gods side by side, “and there you will serve other gods, wood and stone” (Deuteronomy 28:36; 28:64; compare 4:28, all already on these pages). In the next chapter David goes over to Achish of Gath (27:1–2, now on these pages). The Douay has “Go, serve strange gods”; the TLB “and you have sent me away to worship heathen gods”.

Away from the presence of Jehovah. “And now let not my blood fall to the ground away from the presence of Jehovah”: mi-neged penei YHVH, to die in a foreign land, outside the inheritance. The ASV has “let not my blood fall to the earth away from the presence of Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “before the face of Jehovah”; the TLB “Must I die on foreign soil, far from the presence of Jehovah?”

A single flea, and the partridge. “For the king of Israel has come out to seek a single flea”: par’osh echad (par’osh), the flea of the cave, “After a dead dog — after a single flea” (24:14, already on these pages). The word stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible, and these are the only two where it means the insect. “As one hunts the partridge in the mountains”: ha-qore (qore), “the caller”, the bird named for its cry. David has just “called out” from the top of the mountain (verse 14), and Abner asked who it was that “called out to the king”; the bird hunted in the mountains is the man on the mountain. Jeremiah has the bird once more, “As the partridge that broods but does not hatch” (Jeremiah 17:11). The Greek reads “to seek my soul, as the night-raven hunts in the mountains”, with no flea. The KJV has “as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains”; the NIV “The king of Israel has come out to look for a flea”; the TLB, like the Greek, has David’s life instead of the flea: “Why should the king of Israel come out to hunt my life like a partridge on the mountains?”

Verses 21–22 · “I have sinned … I have acted foolishly” — Saul’s confession, and “Look — the king’s spear!”

I have sinned. Return. “And Saul said: ‘I have sinned. Return, my son David’”: chatati, the word Saul said twice to Samuel at Gilgal (15:24; 15:30, both already on these pages), where he went on, “and return with me”. There he asked the prophet to come back; here he asks David, and David does not. “For I will not do you evil again, because my soul was precious in your eyes this day”: yaqrah nafshi, the verb the book used of David’s name, “and his name became very precious” (18:30, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “in view of the fact that my soul has been precious in your eyes this day”; the NIV “Because you considered my life precious today”; the TLB “for you saved my life today”.

Acted foolishly, and erred. “Look — I have acted foolishly, and have erred very greatly”: hiskalti, from sakhal (sakhal), Samuel’s word to Saul after the sacrifice at Gilgal, “You have acted foolishly” (13:13, already on these pages). Then it was the prophet’s verdict; now Saul says it of himself. Va-eshgeh is from shagah (shagah), the verb of the sin done without meaning it: “if the whole congregation of Israel errs” (Leviticus 4:13); “when you err” (Numbers 15:22, both already on these pages). The KJV has “behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly”; the NWT 1984 “I have acted foolishly and am very much mistaken”; the NIV “Surely I have acted like a fool and have been terribly wrong”; the Douay “and have been ignorant in very many things”.

The spear, O king. “And David answered and said: ‘Look — the king’s spear!’”: the written text has ha-chanit ha-melekh, “the spear, O king!”, and the reading tradition chanit ha-melekh, “the king’s spear” (ketiv and qere). The ASV follows the written text: “Behold the spear, O king!”; the KJV the reading: “Behold the king’s spear!”; the NWT 1984 has “Here is the spear of the king”; the TLB “Here is your spear, sir”. “Let one of the young men come over and take it”: David does not cross back, and the valley stays between them. He gives back the spear and says nothing more of the jug of water. The Douay has “let one of the king's servants come over and fetch it”.

Verses 23–25 · “Jehovah will return to each man his righteousness” — “out of all distress”, and Saul’s last words to David

Each man his righteousness. “And Jehovah will return to each man his righteousness and his faithfulness”: yashiv, the verb of the chapter before, where “the evil of Nabal Jehovah has returned on his own head” (25:39, already on these pages), and emunato, his faithfulness (emunah). “For Jehovah gave you today into my hand, and I was not willing to stretch out my hand against Jehovah’s anointed”: ve-lo aviti, “I was not willing”. The NWT 1984 has “And Jehovah it is who will repay to each one his own righteousness and his own faithfulness”; the KJV “but I would not stretch forth mine hand”; the NWT 2013 “but I was unwilling to lift my hand”.

Great in my eyes. “And look — as your soul was great this day in my eyes, so may my soul be great in the eyes of Jehovah”: gadlah … tigdal. Saul said his soul was “precious” in David’s eyes (verse 21); David answers with “great”, and moves the scale from his own eyes to Jehovah’s. The KJV has “as thy life was much set by this day in mine eyes”; the NWT 1984 “just as your soul was great this day in my eyes”; the NWT 2013 “Just as your life was precious to me this day”; the NIV “As surely as I valued your life today”.

Out of all distress. “And may he deliver me out of all distress”: ve-yatsileni mi-kol-tsarah (natsal; tsarah). The words “out of all distress” stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: this prayer; twice in the oath David will swear as king, “As Jehovah lives, who has redeemed my soul out of all distress” (2 Samuel 4:9, now on these pages), the second time in his old age, “out of every distress” (1 Kings 1:29, already on these pages); and in Psalm 54, whose heading names the Ziphites: “For out of all distress he has delivered me” (Psalm 54:9 in the Hebrew numbering). The prayer at Hachilah becomes the oath of his old age. The KJV has “and let him deliver me out of all tribulation”; the NWT 1984 “that he may deliver me out of all distress”.

You will surely act, and surely prevail. “Blessed are you, my son David; you will both surely act and surely prevail”: gam asoh ta’aseh ve-gam yakhol tukhal, two verbs, each doubled. In the cave Saul said “I know that you will surely be king”, with the same doubling (24:20, already on these pages). The second verb is Caleb’s, “for we are surely able against it”, yakhol nukhal (Numbers 13:30), and Jacob’s at the Jabbok, “you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed”, va-tukhal (Genesis 32:29, both already on these pages). The Greek has “Blessed are you, child”, without the name. The ASV has “thou shalt both do mightily, and shalt surely prevail”; the KJV “thou shalt both do great things, and also shalt still prevail”; the NWT 1984 “Not only will you without fail work but you will also without fail come off the winner”; the Douay “and truly doing thou shalt do, and prevailing thou shalt prevail”; the TLB “You shall do heroic deeds and be a great conqueror”.

David went on his way. “And David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place.” After the cave “Saul went to his house; and David and his men went up to the stronghold” (24:22, already on these pages). This time there is no oath asked, and the two do not meet again: Saul’s blessing is the last thing he says to David. David next hears of him from the Amalekite who brings him “the crown that was on his head” (2 Samuel 1:10, already on these pages). The NIV has “and Saul returned home”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact, distinct words: “hiding himself” for mistatter, as in 23:19; “facing” for al-penei, where 23:19 has “south of”; “three thousand men, the chosen of Israel”, as in 24:2; “for certain” for el-nakhon, as in 23:23; “the commander of his army”, as in 14:50; “within the ring” for ba-ma’gal, as in 17:20; “at his head” for meraashotav, as in Genesis 28:11 and 19:13; “handed over” for sigger, as in 24:18; “strike him … to the ground”, the shape of 18:11’s “strike David to the wall”; “destroy” for hishchit, in verses 9 and 15; “stretched out his hand against Jehovah’s anointed”, as in 24:6 and 10; “innocent” for niqqah; “strike” for nagaf, as in 25:38; “swept away” for nispah, as in 12:25 and Genesis 18:23; “Far be it from me, by Jehovah”, as in 24:6; “the jug of water” for tsappachat ha-mayim; “a deep sleep” for tardemah, as in Genesis 2:21 and 15:12; “the other side” for ha-ever; “called out” for qara; “Are you not a man?” for ha-lo-ish attah, without the KJV’s “valiant”; “kept watch” for shamar; “sons of death”, as in 20:31; “recognized” for hikkir; “Is this your voice, my son David?”, as in 24:16; “what evil is in my hand”, as in 24:11; “incited” for hesit; “smell an offering” for yarach minchah; “driven me out today”, as in Genesis 4:14; “attaching myself” for histappeach, as in 2:36; “the inheritance of Jehovah”; “away from the presence of Jehovah”; “a single flea”, as in 24:14; “the partridge” for ha-qore; “I have sinned”, as in 15:24 and 30; “precious” for yaqar, as in 18:30; “acted foolishly” for hiskil, as in 13:13; “erred” for shagah; “the king’s spear”, the reading of ketiv “the spear, O king”; “return” for heshiv, as in 25:39; “great in my eyes” for gadal; “out of all distress” for mi-kol-tsarah; and “surely act and surely prevail” for the doubled verbs, as in Numbers 13:30.

Echoes paid. The Ziphites, the hill of Hachilah and “come back to me with certainty” (23:19–23); the two sides of the mountain (23:26); the three thousand chosen men, the corner of the robe, David’s heart striking him, “Far be it from me, by Jehovah”, “my lord the king”, “I will not stretch out my hand against my lord”, “no evil or rebellion in my hand”, the single flea, “Is this your voice, my son David?”, “Jehovah handed me over into your hand”, “you will surely be king”, and Saul going home (24:2–22); the ring of the camp (17:20); Abner, commander of the army (14:50; 17:55); Ahimelech the priest (21:2); Saul’s spear hurled at David and at Jonathan and held at Gibeah (18:10–11; 19:9–10; 20:33; 22:6); Jacob’s stone and Michal’s net “at his head” (Genesis 28:11; 19:13); the Ziphites’ “hand him over” (23:20); Nabal struck (25:38); Samuel’s “you shall be swept away” (12:25); Abraham’s “sweep away the righteous” (Genesis 18:23); the deep sleeps of Adam and Abram (Genesis 2:21; 15:12); David guarding Nabal’s flocks “for a lie” (25:21); “he is a son of death” (20:31); Isaac and Joseph recognizing (Genesis 27:23; 42:7); “What have I done?” (20:1); the evil spirit of Jehovah (19:9); the soothing aroma (Genesis 8:21); Cain driven out “today” (Genesis 4:14); “attach me to one of the priest’s offices” (2:36); serving other gods in exile (Deuteronomy 4:28; 28:36, 64); “I have sinned” at Gilgal (15:24, 30); David’s name precious (18:30); “You have acted foolishly” (13:13); erring (Leviticus 4:13; Numbers 15:22); Nabal’s evil returned on his head (25:39); and Caleb’s “surely able” and Jacob’s “prevailed” (Numbers 13:30; Genesis 32:29).

Echoes planted. David turning “swept away” on himself, and going over to Achish (27:1–2, now on these pages); Saul’s death on Gilboa (31:4–6, now on these pages); Uriah the Hittite (2 Samuel 11:3, now on these pages; 23:39); the sons of Zeruiah (1 Chronicles 2:16); “the man who did this is a son of death” (2 Samuel 12:5); the census incited (2 Samuel 24:1; 1 Chronicles 21:1); “you incited me against him” (Job 2:3); David’s oath “out of all distress” (2 Samuel 4:9, now on these pages); Psalm 54 (54:2, 9); Naboth’s “Far be it from me, by Jehovah” (1 Kings 21:3); Elijah’s jugs of oil and water (1 Kings 17:12–16; 19:6); the deep sleeps of Isaiah, Job and Proverbs (Isaiah 29:10; Job 4:13; 33:15; Proverbs 19:15); and Jeremiah’s partridge (Jeremiah 17:11). None of the others is translated here yet. Already here from later in the story: Saul leaning on his spear, the crown, and “How were you not afraid?” (2 Samuel 1:6, 10, 14), and the oath “out of every distress” in David’s old age (1 Kings 1:29).
