🕰
Moses turns from historical narrative to direct exhortation: do not add to or subtract from the law, remember Horeb's voice with no form, make no carved image of anything in creation, and remember what happened at Baal-peor. A prophecy of future exile and return follows, then the chapter's central argument that Jehovah alone is God. Moses closes by designating three cities of refuge east of the Jordan, and a colophon marks the end of the historical review.
Same setting as chapters 1-3 — the plains of Moab, in the fortieth year (1:3) — the hinge between the historical review (chapters 1-3) and the law that follows; no new narrated event, only the turn in Moses' own address.

Deuteronomy 4 — The Mister Translation

1
וְעַתָּה יִשְׂרָאֵל, שְׁמַע אֶל-הַחֻקִּים וְאֶל-הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְלַמֵּד אֶתְכֶם, לַעֲשׂוֹת--לְמַעַן תִּחְיוּ, וּבָאתֶם וִירִשְׁתֶּם אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי אֲבֹתֵיכֶם, נֹתֵן לָכֶם.
And now, Israel, listen to the statutes and the ordinances that I am teaching you to do, so that you may live, and go in and take possession of the land that Jehovah, the God of your fathers, is giving you.note
Y ahora, Israel, escucha los estatutos y las ordenanzas que te enseño a poner por obra, para que vivas, y entres y tomes posesión de la tierra que Jehová, el Dios de tus padres, te da.
2
לֹא תֹסִפוּ, עַל-הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם, וְלֹא תִגְרְעוּ, מִמֶּנּוּ--לִשְׁמֹר, אֶת-מִצְוֺת יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי, מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם.
You shall not add to the word that I am commanding you, and you shall not take away from it, so that you may keep the commandments of Jehovah your God that I am commanding you.note
No añadirás a la palabra que yo te mando, ni quitarás de ella, para que guardes los mandamientos de Jehová tu Dios que yo te mando.
3
עֵינֵיכֶם, הָרֹאוֹת, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה יְהוָה, בְּבַעַל פְּעוֹר:  כִּי כָל-הָאִישׁ, אֲשֶׁר הָלַךְ אַחֲרֵי בַעַל-פְּעוֹר--הִשְׁמִידוֹ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, מִקִּרְבֶּךָ.
Your own eyes have seen what Jehovah did at Baal-peor — for every man who went after the Baal of Peor, Jehovah your God destroyed him from your midst.note
Tus propios ojos han visto lo que Jehová hizo en Baal-peor —pues a todo hombre que fue tras el Baal de Peor, Jehová tu Dios lo destruyó de en medio de ti.
4
וְאַתֶּם, הַדְּבֵקִים, בַּיהוָה, אֱלֹהֵיכֶם--חַיִּים כֻּלְּכֶם, הַיּוֹם.
But you, who held fast to Jehovah your God, are all alive today.note
Pero ustedes, los que se aferraron a Jehová su Dios, están vivos hoy, todos ustedes.
5
רְאֵה לִמַּדְתִּי אֶתְכֶם, חֻקִּים וּמִשְׁפָּטִים, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוַּנִי, יְהוָה אֱלֹהָי:  לַעֲשׂוֹת כֵּן--בְּקֶרֶב הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם בָּאִים שָׁמָּה לְרִשְׁתָּהּ.
See, I have taught you statutes and ordinances, just as Jehovah my God commanded me, for you to do them in the midst of the land that you are entering to take possession of it.note
Miren, les he enseñado estatutos y ordenanzas, tal como Jehová mi Dios me mandó, para que ustedes obren así en medio de la tierra a la cual van a entrar para tomar posesión de ella.
6
וּשְׁמַרְתֶּם, וַעֲשִׂיתֶם--כִּי הִוא חָכְמַתְכֶם וּבִינַתְכֶם, לְעֵינֵי הָעַמִּים:  אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁמְעוּן, אֵת כָּל-הַחֻקִּים הָאֵלֶּה, וְאָמְרוּ רַק עַם-חָכָם וְנָבוֹן, הַגּוֹי הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה.
So keep them and do them, for that is your wisdom and your understanding in the sight of the peoples, who will hear all these statutes and say, ‘Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people.’note
Guárdenlos, pues, y pónganlos por obra, porque esa es su sabiduría y su entendimiento ante los ojos de los pueblos, que oirán todos estos estatutos y dirán: «Sin duda, esta gran nación es un pueblo sabio y entendido».
7
כִּי מִי-גוֹי גָּדוֹל, אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ אֱלֹהִים קְרֹבִים אֵלָיו, כַּיהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ, בְּכָל-קָרְאֵנוּ אֵלָיו.
For what great nation is there that has a god so near to it as Jehovah our God is, whenever we call upon him?note
Porque ¿qué gran nación hay que tenga un dios tan cercano a ella como lo está Jehová nuestro Dios, cada vez que lo invocamos?
8
וּמִי גּוֹי גָּדוֹל, אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ חֻקִּים וּמִשְׁפָּטִים צַדִּיקִם, כְּכֹל הַתּוֹרָה הַזֹּאת, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי נֹתֵן לִפְנֵיכֶם הַיּוֹם.
And what great nation is there that has statutes and ordinances as righteous as this whole instruction that I am setting before you today?note
¿Y qué gran nación hay que tenga estatutos y ordenanzas tan justos como toda esta instrucción que hoy pongo delante de ustedes?
9
רַק הִשָּׁמֶר לְךָ וּשְׁמֹר נַפְשְׁךָ מְאֹד, פֶּן-תִּשְׁכַּח אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים אֲשֶׁר-רָאוּ עֵינֶיךָ וּפֶן-יָסוּרוּ מִלְּבָבְךָ, כֹּל, יְמֵי חַיֶּיךָ; וְהוֹדַעְתָּם לְבָנֶיךָ, וְלִבְנֵי בָנֶיךָ.
Only take heed to yourself, and keep your soul diligently, lest you forget the things your eyes have seen, and lest they depart from your heart all the days of your life; and you shall make them known to your children and your children’s children —note
Solamente cuídate a ti mismo, y guarda tu alma con diligencia, no sea que olvides las cosas que tus ojos han visto, y no sea que se aparten de tu corazón todos los días de tu vida; y las darás a conocer a tus hijos y a los hijos de tus hijos —
10
יוֹם, אֲשֶׁר עָמַדְתָּ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ בְּחֹרֵב, בֶּאֱמֹר יְהוָה אֵלַי הַקְהֶל-לִי אֶת-הָעָם, וְאַשְׁמִעֵם אֶת-דְּבָרָי:  אֲשֶׁר יִלְמְדוּן לְיִרְאָה אֹתִי, כָּל-הַיָּמִים אֲשֶׁר הֵם חַיִּים עַל-הָאֲדָמָה, וְאֶת-בְּנֵיהֶם, יְלַמֵּדוּן.
the day that you stood before Jehovah your God at Horeb, when Jehovah said to me, ‘Assemble the people to me, and I will let them hear my words, so that they may learn to fear me all the days that they live on the earth, and may teach their children.’note
el día que estuviste de pie delante de Jehová tu Dios en Horeb, cuando Jehová me dijo: «Reúneme al pueblo, y les haré oír mis palabras, para que aprendan a temerme todos los días que vivan sobre la tierra, y para que enseñen a sus hijos».
11
וַתִּקְרְבוּן וַתַּעַמְדוּן, תַּחַת הָהָר; וְהָהָר בֹּעֵר בָּאֵשׁ, עַד-לֵב הַשָּׁמַיִם--חֹשֶׁךְ, עָנָן וַעֲרָפֶל.
And you came near and stood at the foot of the mountain, and the mountain was burning with fire up to the heart of the heavens — darkness, cloud, and thick darkness.note
Y ustedes se acercaron y se pararon al pie del monte; y el monte ardía en fuego hasta el corazón de los cielos, con oscuridad, nube y densa tiniebla.
12
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֲלֵיכֶם, מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ:  קוֹל דְּבָרִים אַתֶּם שֹׁמְעִים, וּתְמוּנָה אֵינְכֶם רֹאִים זוּלָתִי קוֹל.
And Jehovah spoke to you out of the midst of the fire; you heard the sound of words, but you saw no form — only a voice.note
Y Jehová les habló de en medio del fuego; ustedes oían el sonido de las palabras, pero no veían ninguna forma —solo una voz.
13
וַיַּגֵּד לָכֶם אֶת-בְּרִיתוֹ, אֲשֶׁר צִוָּה אֶתְכֶם לַעֲשׂוֹת--עֲשֶׂרֶת, הַדְּבָרִים; וַיִּכְתְּבֵם, עַל-שְׁנֵי לֻחוֹת אֲבָנִים.
And he declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to do — the ten words — and he wrote them on two tablets of stone.note
Y él les declaró su pacto, que les mandó poner por obra —las diez palabras— y las escribió sobre dos tablas de piedra.
14
וְאֹתִי צִוָּה יְהוָה, בָּעֵת הַהִוא, לְלַמֵּד אֶתְכֶם, חֻקִּים וּמִשְׁפָּטִים:  לַעֲשֹׂתְכֶם אֹתָם--בָּאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם עֹבְרִים שָׁמָּה לְרִשְׁתָּהּ.
And Jehovah commanded me at that time to teach you statutes and ordinances, for you to do them in the land that you are crossing over to take possession of it.note
Y Jehová me mandó en aquel tiempo enseñarles estatutos y ordenanzas, para que ustedes los pusieran por obra en la tierra a la cual van a pasar para tomar posesión de ella.
15
וְנִשְׁמַרְתֶּם מְאֹד, לְנַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם:  כִּי לֹא רְאִיתֶם, כָּל-תְּמוּנָה, בְּיוֹם דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֲלֵיכֶם בְּחֹרֵב, מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ.
So watch yourselves very carefully — for you saw no form on the day that Jehovah spoke to you at Horeb out of the midst of the fire —note
Así que cuídense mucho —pues no vieron ninguna forma el día que Jehová les habló en Horeb de en medio del fuego—
16
פֶּן-תַּשְׁחִתוּן--וַעֲשִׂיתֶם לָכֶם פֶּסֶל, תְּמוּנַת כָּל-סָמֶל:  תַּבְנִית זָכָר, אוֹ נְקֵבָה.
lest you act corruptly and make for yourselves a carved image, the form of any figure, the likeness of male or female,note
no sea que se corrompan y se hagan una imagen tallada, la forma de alguna figura, la semejanza de varón o de hembra,
17
תַּבְנִית, כָּל-בְּהֵמָה אֲשֶׁר בָּאָרֶץ; תַּבְנִית כָּל-צִפּוֹר כָּנָף, אֲשֶׁר תָּעוּף בַּשָּׁמָיִם.
the likeness of any animal that is on the earth, the likeness of any winged bird that flies in the heavens,note
la semejanza de cualquier animal que hay en la tierra, la semejanza de cualquier ave alada que vuela en los cielos,
18
תַּבְנִית, כָּל-רֹמֵשׂ בָּאֲדָמָה; תַּבְנִית כָּל-דָּגָה אֲשֶׁר-בַּמַּיִם, מִתַּחַת לָאָרֶץ.
the likeness of anything that crawls on the ground, the likeness of any fish that is in the water under the earth;note
la semejanza de cualquier cosa que se arrastra sobre la tierra, la semejanza de cualquier pez que hay en las aguas debajo de la tierra;
19
וּפֶן-תִּשָּׂא עֵינֶיךָ הַשָּׁמַיְמָה, וְרָאִיתָ אֶת-הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ וְאֶת-הַיָּרֵחַ וְאֶת-הַכּוֹכָבִים כֹּל צְבָא הַשָּׁמַיִם, וְנִדַּחְתָּ וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוִיתָ לָהֶם, וַעֲבַדְתָּם--אֲשֶׁר חָלַק יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, אֹתָם, לְכֹל הָעַמִּים, תַּחַת כָּל-הַשָּׁמָיִם.
and lest you lift up your eyes to the heavens, and see the sun and the moon and the stars, all the host of heaven, and be drawn away and bow down to them and serve them — things that Jehovah your God has allotted to all the peoples under the whole heavens.note
y no sea que alces tus ojos a los cielos, y veas el sol, la luna y las estrellas, todo el ejército de los cielos, y te dejes arrastrar y te inclines ante ellos y les sirvas —cosas que Jehová tu Dios ha repartido a todos los pueblos debajo de todos los cielos.
20
וְאֶתְכֶם לָקַח יְהוָה, וַיּוֹצִא אֶתְכֶם מִכּוּר הַבַּרְזֶל מִמִּצְרָיִם, לִהְיוֹת לוֹ לְעַם נַחֲלָה, כַּיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
But you, Jehovah has taken, and brought out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be to him a people of inheritance, as you are this day.note
Pero a ustedes, Jehová los tomó, y los sacó del horno de hierro, de Egipto, para ser para él un pueblo de herencia, como lo son hoy.
21
וַיהוָה הִתְאַנַּף-בִּי, עַל-דִּבְרֵיכֶם; וַיִּשָּׁבַע, לְבִלְתִּי עָבְרִי אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן, וּלְבִלְתִּי-בֹא אֶל-הָאָרֶץ הַטּוֹבָה, אֲשֶׁר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ נֹתֵן לְךָ נַחֲלָה.
And Jehovah was angry with me because of your words, and he swore that I should not cross the Jordan, and that I should not go into the good land that Jehovah your God is giving you as an inheritance.note
Y Jehová se enojó conmigo a causa de las palabras de ustedes, y juró que yo no cruzaría el Jordán, ni entraría en la buena tierra que Jehová tu Dios te da como herencia.
22
כִּי אָנֹכִי מֵת בָּאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת, אֵינֶנִּי עֹבֵר אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן; וְאַתֶּם, עֹבְרִים, וִירִשְׁתֶּם, אֶת-הָאָרֶץ הַטּוֹבָה הַזֹּאת.
For I must die in this land; I am not crossing the Jordan. But you are crossing, and you will take possession of that good land.note
Porque yo he de morir en esta tierra; no cruzo el Jordán. Pero ustedes van a cruzar, y tomarán posesión de esa buena tierra.
23
הִשָּׁמְרוּ לָכֶם, פֶּן-תִּשְׁכְּחוּ אֶת-בְּרִית יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר כָּרַת, עִמָּכֶם; וַעֲשִׂיתֶם לָכֶם פֶּסֶל תְּמוּנַת כֹּל, אֲשֶׁר צִוְּךָ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ.
Take heed to yourselves, lest you forget the covenant of Jehovah your God, which he made with you, and make for yourselves a carved image, the form of anything that Jehovah your God has forbidden you.note
Cuídense, no sea que olviden el pacto de Jehová su Dios, que él hizo con ustedes, y se hagan una imagen tallada, la forma de cualquier cosa que Jehová tu Dios te ha prohibido.
24
כִּי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, אֵשׁ אֹכְלָה הוּא:  אֵל, קַנָּא.  {פ}
For Jehovah your God is a devouring fire, a jealous God.note
Porque Jehová tu Dios es fuego devorador, un Dios celoso.
25
כִּי-תוֹלִיד בָּנִים וּבְנֵי בָנִים, וְנוֹשַׁנְתֶּם בָּאָרֶץ; וְהִשְׁחַתֶּם, וַעֲשִׂיתֶם פֶּסֶל תְּמוּנַת כֹּל, וַעֲשִׂיתֶם הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה-אֱלֹהֶיךָ, לְהַכְעִיסוֹ.
When you father children and children’s children, and have grown old in the land, and act corruptly, and make a carved image, the form of anything, and do what is evil in the eyes of Jehovah your God, to provoke him —note
Cuando engendres hijos e hijos de hijos, y hayan envejecido en la tierra, y se corrompan, y hagan una imagen tallada, la forma de cualquier cosa, y hagan lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová tu Dios, para provocarlo —
26

Translator’s Notes

Verse 1 · “that you may live” — the sermon’s legal core opens with a verb of hearing

“Statutes and ordinances” (chuqqim u-mishpatim) is the pairing that will now run through this whole chapter — vv1, 5, 6, 8, 14, 40 and 45 all repeat it, seven times in forty-nine verses. This translation keeps the two nouns distinct throughout, matching the ASV; several shelf versions fold them into one English word (“regulations,” “law,” “decisiones judiciales”), which loses the fact that Hebrew names two different kinds of legal material side by side.

★ The verb is “listen” (shema) — the same root that becomes the name of the central Jewish declaration three chapters later, “Hear, O Israel” (Deuteronomy 6:4, already on these pages). Before Moses gives a single statute, he tells Israel to LISTEN, and ties that listening directly to survival: “that you may live, and go in and possess the land.” Obedience is framed here not as duty for its own sake but as the precondition for staying alive in the land at all — a formula that returns almost unchanged at the chapter’s own close (v40).

Verse 2 · “you shall not add to the word” — the Bible’s oldest closed-canon formula

★ This is the earliest appearance, in the order this project is translating, of a sentence that becomes one of the most consequential in the whole Bible: a text sealing itself against its own alteration. The same two verbs, in the same order — lo tosifu (‘you shall not add’) and lo tigre’u (‘you shall not take away,’ from the root gara, already tracking a different sense of the same verb at Numbers 27, already on these pages) — recur almost verbatim three chapters later, at Deuteronomy 13:1 in the Hebrew numbering (not yet on these pages; confirmed directly against the archived Hebrew text: lo tosef alav ve-lo tigra mimenu, ‘you shall not add to it, and you shall not take away from it’). The identical anxiety — a book worried about its own boundary — closes the entire Christian canon at Revelation 22:18-19 (already on these pages, though not yet in Spanish): anyone who ADDS to the words of this prophecy has the plagues added to him, and anyone who TAKES AWAY from its words has his share in the tree of life taken away. Sixty-six books apart, and the same two verbs stand guard at both ends.

⚠ Of the seven English witnesses checked, five different verb-pairs are in play. NIV and the NWT keep ‘add’/‘take away’ or ‘subtract’; KJV and ASV use ‘add’/‘diminish’; Douay-Rheims reads ‘add…take away’, matching NIV/NWT; TLB paraphrases to ‘add other laws or subtract from these’; and the Geneva Bible paraphrases furthest from the verbs themselves, ‘ye shall put nothing unto the word… neither shall ye take ought there from.’ On the Spanish side there is no real split: TNM, RV60, NVI and the RV 1909 all agree closely on ‘añadir…quitar.’

Verses 3–4 · Baal-peor, revisited — the sermon’s first illustration, and Deuteronomy 3’s own promised payoff

★ This is the payoff Deuteronomy 3’s own closing note predicted while checking its own forward boundary: “the same Baal-peor this chapter’s closing verse camps beside becomes, one chapter later, the example Moses reaches for first.” It is exactly that — the FIRST illustration once the sermon’s legal core opens, and it is not hypothetical. Israel is still camped in the valley opposite Beth-peor (3:29, already on these pages) when Moses says “your own eyes have seen.” Every man who joined himself to the Baal of Peor (Numbers 25:3, already on these pages) is dead; everyone else is alive, hearing this sermon, in the same valley. The chapter’s whole argument for obedience opens with a body count its own audience personally supplied.

“Held fast” (v4, davaq) is the same cleaving-verb already tracked at davaq — everywhere else this project has met it, the word describes a marriage bond (Genesis 2:24; Numbers 36:6, both already on these pages). Here it describes loyalty to a god instead of loyalty to a spouse — the identical verb applied to the one bond Deuteronomy will spend the rest of the book insisting must not have a rival.

Verses 5–8 · “what great nation is there” — Israel’s law argued as evidence to outsiders

This is a genuinely unusual argument for obeying a law: not that God commanded it, but that OTHER PEOPLES will be impressed by it. “This great nation is a wise and understanding people” (v6) is put in the mouth of outside observers, not Israel itself — the law’s value is measured, in part, by how it reads to people who never received it.

⚠ The double question in vv7–8 makes two separate claims, and this translation keeps them separate rather than collapsing them into one rhetorical flourish. V7 is about GOD’S NEARNESS (“what great nation has a god so near”); v8 is about the LAW’S OWN RIGHTEOUSNESS (“statutes and ordinances so righteous”). Access to God and the moral quality of the legal code are offered as two independent proofs of the nation’s greatness, not one restated twice.

Verses 9–14 · Horeb retold — a voice with no form, and the Ten Words

★ V12 states the single fact everything from v15 onward is built on: “you heard the sound of words, but you saw no form — only a voice.” Sound without sight. The noun temunah, ‘form,’ recurs five times across this one chapter (vv12, 15, 16, 23, 25) — Israel did not see one at Horeb, and that single reported absence becomes the legal basis for what Israel must never manufacture afterward. What they didn’t see decides what they may not make.

“The ten words” (aseret ha-devarim, v13) renders the Hebrew literally rather than the traditional ‘ten commandments.’ Devarim, ‘words,’ is the very noun that gives this book its own Hebrew name — Deuteronomy opens, “these are the WORDS that Moses spoke” (1:1, already on these pages) — so the covenant document at Horeb and the sermon reporting it share, in Hebrew, the identical noun.

Verses 15–20 · no form seen, therefore no image made — Deuteronomy’s case against every carved thing

This is the chapter’s central argument, and one of the most famous passages in the whole book. Four categories of forbidden image span the whole created order — land animals, birds, crawling things, and fish (vv17–18) — the same fourfold sweep Genesis 1 uses to describe creation itself, now turned into an exhaustive list of what must NOT be carved: nothing in the world Jehovah made may stand in for him.

★ “Carved image” (pesel) and “form” (temunah) — see n9 — appear together here for the first time on these pages. NIV translates pesel outright as ‘an idol,’ collapsing the object into its abuse; KJV, ASV and Geneva keep ‘graven image’, and only the NWT keeps ‘carved image’, matching this translation — all four a manufacturing description that leaves the theological verdict to the sentence around the noun rather than to the noun itself.

⚠ V19’s sun, moon and stars are described as things Jehovah “allotted to all the peoples under the whole heavens” — a striking claim the chapter does not pause to explain. Astral worship among the OTHER nations is framed less as simple error and more as a permitted-but-not-for-Israel arrangement. Every witness checked keeps the giving/allotting sense rather than softening it toward mere neglect, though the verb itself varies — NIV ‘apportioned,’ NWT ‘allotted,’ TNM 1987 ‘ha repartido’ — the RV60 reaches for a different verb again, ‘concedido’ (granted), and the TNM’s own 2019 revision moves to ‘se los ha dado’ (has given), the identical 1984/1987-vs-2013/2019 drift already tracked elsewhere on this shelf.

The “iron furnace” (v20) is a physical, not merely emotional, image for Egypt — the literal apparatus for smelting ore, applied to what Israel itself endured there. Nachalah, ‘inheritance,’ names what Israel becomes to God as a result — the same word that ran through Deuteronomy 2’s whole argument about what four nations already held (already on these pages), now pointed in the opposite direction: instead of God granting LAND to a nation, God takes a NATION as his own holding.

Verses 21–22 · “because of your words” — the third and last time Moses names his own exclusion

★ This is the third and final occurrence of a citation Deuteronomy 1:38’s own note already counted in advance: “three times in Deuteronomy (1:37, 3:26, 4:21), Moses says he was excluded because of them.” Checking the Hebrew of all three directly shows something that note’s own English phrasing flattens: the wording is not repeated at all. 1:37 uses biglalchem, ‘on account of you,’ from the root galal, ‘to roll’; 3:26 uses lema’ankhem, ‘for your sake,’ from ma’an, ‘purpose’; this verse uses al-divreikhem — literally ‘concerning your WORDS,’ from davar, the very root that names the whole book. Three appearances, three different Hebrew idioms, one unresolved tension with Numbers 20’s own account of Moses’ actual fault at the waters of Meribah, already flagged and left open at 1:38.

⚠ The English shelf mostly erases the difference the Hebrew keeps. ASV and KJV render THIS verse ‘for your sakes’ — the identical English phrase they also used at 3:26, even though the underlying Hebrew word has changed from ma’an to davar. Only the Geneva Bible and the Douay-Rheims keep the literal sense here: Geneva reads ‘the Lord was angry with me for your WORDS,’ and Douay, independently, ‘the Lord was angry with me for your WORDS’ — the one place on the whole English shelf where the noun survives translation. On the Spanish side, the RV 1909’s ‘sobre vuestros NEGOCIOS’ (‘concerning your affairs’) comes closest to a literal ‘matters’ sense; NVI’s ‘por culpa de ustedes’ (‘through your fault’) and both TNM editions’ own ‘por causa de’/‘por culpa de’ all smooth toward the same idea as the English majority’s ‘because of you,’ without keeping the literal noun.

Verses 23–24 · “a devouring fire, a jealous God” — Deuteronomy’s severest name for its own God

The warning against the carved image is restated, echoing v16 almost word for word, and then given its reason in the starkest terms available: eish okhlah, ‘a devouring fire,’ and el qanna, ‘a jealous God.’

★ Qanna is an adjective the Hebrew Bible uses only of God — this is its second appearance on these pages, after Exodus 34:14 (already on these pages), where the same word closes the covenant remade after the golden calf: “Jehovah, whose NAME is Jealous, is a jealous God.” Whatever else changes between Sinai and the plains of Moab, forty years later, this one description of God does not.

Of the seven English witnesses checked, NIV, KJV and Geneva read ‘consuming fire…jealous God’; the ASV alone matches this translation’s own ‘devouring fire,’ a real if minor point of agreement; and the NWT prefers ‘exacting exclusive devotion’ for qanna, a periphrasis rather than a translation, matched in Spanish by the TNM’s own ‘que exige devoción exclusiva.’ RV60, the RV 1909 and NVI all keep ‘Dios celoso’ plainly.

Verses 25–31 · the exile Moses predicts before it happens — and the mercy buried inside the threat

★ This is the earliest sustained prophecy of exile in the whole Torah, delivered before Israel has even crossed the Jordan. Idolatry — repeating v16’s own language of the carved image almost verbatim — leads to destruction and scattering “among the peoples,” left “few in number,” the very fate history will later record for both the northern and southern kingdoms.

What keeps this from being a bare threat is vv29–31: even IN exile, seeking God “with all your heart and all your soul” finds him, because — the reason given plainly — “Jehovah your God is a merciful God” (el rachum). That is the identical phrase that opens the great self-description Jehovah gives of his own name at Exodus 34:6 (already on these pages): “Jehovah, Jehovah, a God merciful and gracious.” The threat and the mercy are spoken by the same voice, in the same breath, some four decades before either one comes true.

Verses 32–40 · “has anything so great as this ever happened” — Deuteronomy’s argument for one God

★ This is one of the most emphatic monotheistic statements anywhere in the Hebrew Bible, made twice in nine verses with almost identical wording: “Jehovah, he is God… there is no other” (v35), and again, expanded, “Jehovah, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth below; there is no other” (v39). Of eleven witnesses checked — NIV, KJV, ASV, NWT (both editions), TNM (both editions), RV60, RV 1909 and NVI — ten keep the plain force of it; only the Geneva Bible strengthens v35 further, reading ‘that there is none but HE ALONE’ rather than the more common ‘beside him.’

The argument that gets Israel there is entirely historical, not philosophical. Has any nation EVER heard a god speak and lived (v33)? Has any god EVER attempted to take a nation for himself out of the middle of another nation (v34)? Both questions expect the answer no, and both are answered from Israel’s own recent experience rather than from reasoning about the nature of divinity in the abstract.

V37’s “he loved your fathers” is the first time in Deuteronomy that Jehovah’s choice of Israel is explained by a single, unqualified motive — not size, not merit, not military use, simply love (ahavah) for the patriarchs, extended forward onto their own descendants who never met them.

Verses 41–43 · Bezer, Ramoth and Golan — Moses acts before any law says he should

★ Notice the timing: this is a bare narrative aside, placed before Deuteronomy has given a single word of actual refuge-law (that waits for Deuteronomy 19, not yet on these pages) and years before Joshua formally activates the whole six-city system (Joshua 20–21, not yet on these pages). Moses simply acts — he designates three cities on the Transjordan side, for the very tribes whose Transjordan allotment this book has just finished narrating (3:12–17, already on these pages).

⚠ The technical legal term for these towns — arei ha-miqlat, ‘cities of refuge,’ discussed at miqlat (twenty occurrences elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible, eleven of them in Numbers 35, already on these pages) — is conspicuously NOT used here. Moses calls them simply “three cities.” The fixed legal name comes later; the thing itself comes first.

Bezer, Ramoth in Gilead, and Golan in Bashan are all named here for the first time on these pages, assigned respectively to Reuben, Gad and the half tribe of Manasseh — the exact three groups Deuteronomy 3 just settled east of the Jordan.

Verses 44–49 · a colophon, and a mountain that picks up a fourth name

These six verses function as a colophon — a closing label, of the kind ancient documents append to identify what they are and where they were given — restating in summary what the book has already narrated: the defeat of Sihon and Og, the Transjordan conquest, the valley opposite Beth-peor where Israel is camped. Nothing here is new content; the book is formally marking a structural seam before the sermon’s language shifts.

★ V48 gives Hermon a FOURTH name, and it is worth being precise about the letters. Deuteronomy 3’s own note already tracked three: Hermon itself, Sirion (the Sidonians’ name, 3:9, already on these pages), and Senir (the Amorites’ name). Here the Hebrew reads har Sion — spelled with a sin and an aleph — “Mount Sion — that is, Hermon.” Searched consonantally, this exact spelling occurs in exactly TWO verses in the whole Hebrew Bible: this one, and an unrelated town of the same consonants in Issachar’s territory (Joshua 19:19, not yet on these pages, a different place entirely). It is emphatically NOT the famous Zion of Jerusalem, spelled with a tsade and occurring 156 times — a wholly different Hebrew word that only looks and sounds alike once it is carried into English or Spanish letters.

⚠ Most likely “Sion” here is simply a scribal variant of “Sirion” itself (the very word from three chapters earlier, with one letter swapped) — which is exactly how two witnesses on the shelf treat it: the NIV and TLB both translate it outright as ‘Mount SIRION,’ silently correcting the spelling rather than transliterating it. Every other version checked — KJV, ASV, Geneva, Douay-Rheims, both NWT editions, and the whole Spanish shelf (RV60, RV 1909, NVI, both TNM editions) — keeps the literal ‘Sion’/‘Siyón’/‘Sión,’ reproducing a name that on the page sits one letter from the most famous hill in the Bible. This translation keeps ‘Sion’ too, precisely because the ambiguity is real and worth a reader noticing, rather than silently resolved the way NIV and TLB resolve it.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English agree on all 49 verses, with no versification shift anywhere in this chapter (checked directly against every shelf version fetched, all of which map the same content to the same verse numbers straight through v48’s Sion/Hermon line). The Masoretic text marks three petuchot — after v24, after v40, and closing the chapter after v49 — dividing the chapter roughly into the case against images (vv1–24), the prophecy of exile and the argument for one God (vv25–40), and the narrative close (vv41–49). The whole chapter still falls inside Parashat Va’etchanan, which Deuteronomy 3’s own closing note located as beginning at 3:23. The rendering worth flagging is v21’s “because of your words” — see n21 for why the three occurrences of this exclusion formula (1:37, 3:26, 4:21) use three genuinely different Hebrew idioms rather than one repeated phrase. Elsewhere the established renderings hold: Jehovah for the divine name, instruction for torah, and “statutes and ordinances” kept as two distinct nouns for chuqqim u-mishpatim throughout.

Library. One new dictionary entry in both languages: yasaph, ‘to add’ — the verb of v2’s closed-canon formula, discussed alongside its paired opposite gara. Six existing dictionary entries extended, each in both languages (all six already carried a Spanish edition; correcting an earlier mischeck of this project’s own registry): pesel and temunah (the chapter’s central image-and-form pairing), qanna (its second occurrence on these pages, after Exodus 34:14), miqlat (extended to note that this chapter conspicuously does NOT use the term it will later name), nachalah (vv20, 38, the word pointed in the opposite direction), and gara (its textual, rather than petitionary, use). Three new encyclopedia entries in both languages, each with atlas coordinates: Bezer, Ramoth in Gilead, and Golan — the first three cities of refuge, named here before the legal term for them exists. One existing encyclopedia entry extended in both languages: Hermon, gaining its fourth recorded name and the Sion/Sirion/Zion distinction (n44).

Echoes paid. Deuteronomy 3:29’s own forward-plant, the Baal-peor illustration, arriving as this chapter’s first example (n3). 1:38’s three-part citation of Moses’ own exclusion (1:37, 3:26, 4:21), completed at v21 — and shown, on inspection, to use three different Hebrew idioms rather than one repeated phrase (n21). Exodus 34:14’s qanna, recurring at v24 (n23). Exodus 34:6’s “a God merciful and gracious,” echoed at v31 (n25). Deuteronomy 3’s Transjordan tribal allotment, put to use at vv41–43 in the naming of the first three refuge cities (n41). And Deuteronomy 3’s own three-names-for-Hermon note, extended to a fourth at v48 (n44).

Patterns worth carrying forward. First: Deuteronomy 3’s own closing note flagged the three-names-one-thing device (Hermon/Sirion/Senir) as “worth watching for a third instance” on some OTHER subject; instead the very same mountain has now supplied a fourth name to itself, which suggests the device is less a habit of this book’s narration and more a simple fact about how many neighboring languages this one summit touched. Second: this chapter is unusually thick with a device not seen this concentrated before — several separate pairs of near-synonyms or near-identical spellings that the shelf regularly flattens (galal/ma’an/davar at n21; Sion/Sirion/Zion at n44; pesel/temunah at n15) — worth watching whether Deuteronomy leans on this kind of near-miss vocabulary more than Numbers did.
