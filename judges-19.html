A Levite of the far side of the hill country of Ephraim takes a concubine from Bethlehem in Judah; she leaves him for her father's house, and after four months he goes to bring her back. Her father keeps him feasting day after day, and on the fifth day he leaves late, passes Jebus (Jerusalem) rather than lodge among foreigners, and reaches Gibeah of Benjamin at sunset. Only an old man from Ephraim takes them in. That night the men of the city surround the house, as at Sodom; the Levite pushes his concubine out to them, and in the morning she lies at the doorway with her hands on the threshold. At home he cuts her body into twelve pieces and sends them through all Israel: 'Nothing like this has happened since the day the sons of Israel came up from Egypt.'
Undated within the period of the judges; Judges 20:28 puts Phinehas son of Eleazar, Aaron's grandson, in office at the time of the war that follows, which would set these closing chapters early in the period rather than after Samson.

1
וַיְהִי בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם, וּמֶלֶךְ אֵין בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיְהִי אִישׁ לֵוִי, גָּר בְּיַרְכְּתֵי הַר-אֶפְרַיִם, וַיִּקַּח-לוֹ אִשָּׁה פִילֶגֶשׁ, מִבֵּית לֶחֶם יְהוּדָה.
In those days, when there was no king in Israel, there was a Levite sojourning on the far side of the hill country of Ephraim, who took for himself a woman, a concubine, from Bethlehem in Judah.note
En aquellos días, cuando no había rey en Israel, había un levita que residía como forastero en el extremo de la serranía de Efraín, el cual tomó para sí una mujer, una concubina, de Belén de Judá.
2
וַתִּזְנֶה עָלָיו, פִּילַגְשׁוֹ, וַתֵּלֶךְ מֵאִתּוֹ אֶל-בֵּית אָבִיהָ, אֶל-בֵּית לֶחֶם יְהוּדָה; וַתְּהִי-שָׁם, יָמִים אַרְבָּעָה חֳדָשִׁים.
His concubine played the harlot against him, and she went away from him to her father’s house, to Bethlehem in Judah, and was there some days — four months.note
Su concubina se prostituyó contra él, y se fue de su lado a la casa de su padre, a Belén de Judá, y estuvo allí unos días —cuatro meses.
3
וַיָּקָם אִישָׁהּ וַיֵּלֶךְ אַחֲרֶיהָ, לְדַבֵּר עַל-לִבָּהּ להשיבו לַהֲשִׁיבָהּ, וְנַעֲרוֹ עִמּוֹ, וְצֶמֶד חֲמֹרִים; וַתְּבִיאֵהוּ, בֵּית אָבִיהָ, וַיִּרְאֵהוּ אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה, וַיִּשְׂמַח לִקְרָאתוֹ.
Her husband got up and went after her, to speak to her heart and bring her back; his young man was with him, and a pair of donkeys. She brought him into her father’s house, and when the girl’s father saw him, he rejoiced to meet him.note
Su marido se levantó y fue tras ella, para hablarle al corazón y hacerla volver; su criado iba con él, y un par de asnos. Ella lo hizo entrar en la casa de su padre, y cuando el padre de la joven lo vio, se alegró de recibirlo.
4
וַיַּחֲזֶק-בּוֹ חֹתְנוֹ אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה, וַיֵּשֶׁב אִתּוֹ שְׁלֹשֶׁת יָמִים; וַיֹּאכְלוּ, וַיִּשְׁתּוּ, וַיָּלִינוּ, שָׁם.
His father-in-law, the girl’s father, held him fast, and he stayed with him three days; they ate and drank, and lodged there.note
Su suegro, el padre de la joven, lo retuvo, y se quedó con él tres días; comieron y bebieron, y pasaron allí la noche.
5
וַיְהִי בַּיּוֹם הָרְבִיעִי, וַיַּשְׁכִּימוּ בַבֹּקֶר וַיָּקָם לָלֶכֶת; וַיֹּאמֶר אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה אֶל-חֲתָנוֹ, סְעָד לִבְּךָ פַּת-לֶחֶם--וְאַחַר תֵּלֵכוּ.
On the fourth day they rose early in the morning, and he got up to go; but the girl’s father said to his son-in-law: ‘Refresh your heart with a piece of bread, and after that you may go.’note
Al cuarto día madrugaron por la mañana, y él se levantó para irse; pero el padre de la joven dijo a su yerno: «Conforta tu corazón con un bocado de pan, y después se podrán ir».
6
וַיֵּשְׁבוּ, וַיֹּאכְלוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם יַחְדָּו--וַיִּשְׁתּוּ; וַיֹּאמֶר אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה, אֶל-הָאִישׁ, הוֹאֶל-נָא וְלִין, וְיִיטַב לִבֶּךָ.
So they sat down, and the two of them ate together and drank; and the girl’s father said to the man: ‘Be willing, please, to spend the night, and let your heart be glad.’note
Se sentaron, pues, y los dos comieron juntos y bebieron; y el padre de la joven dijo al hombre: «Ten a bien, por favor, pasar la noche, y que se alegre tu corazón».
7
וַיָּקָם הָאִישׁ, לָלֶכֶת; וַיִּפְצַר-בּוֹ, חֹתְנוֹ, וַיָּשָׁב, וַיָּלֶן שָׁם.
The man got up to go, but his father-in-law pressed him, and he stayed again and spent the night there.note
El hombre se levantó para irse, pero su suegro insistió, y se quedó otra vez y pasó allí la noche.
8
וַיַּשְׁכֵּם בַּבֹּקֶר בַּיּוֹם הַחֲמִישִׁי, לָלֶכֶת, וַיֹּאמֶר אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה סְעָד-נָא לְבָבְךָ, וְהִתְמַהְמְהוּ עַד-נְטוֹת הַיּוֹם; וַיֹּאכְלוּ, שְׁנֵיהֶם.
He rose early in the morning on the fifth day to go, and the girl’s father said: ‘Refresh your heart, please, and linger until the day declines.’ And the two of them ate.note
Al quinto día madrugó por la mañana para irse, y el padre de la joven dijo: «Conforta tu corazón, por favor, y quédense hasta que decline el día». Y los dos comieron.
9
וַיָּקָם הָאִישׁ לָלֶכֶת, הוּא וּפִילַגְשׁוֹ וְנַעֲרוֹ; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ חֹתְנוֹ אֲבִי הַנַּעֲרָה הִנֵּה נָא רָפָה הַיּוֹם לַעֲרוֹב, לִינוּ-נָא הִנֵּה חֲנוֹת הַיּוֹם לִין פֹּה וְיִיטַב לְבָבֶךָ, וְהִשְׁכַּמְתֶּם מָחָר לְדַרְכְּכֶם, וְהָלַכְתָּ לְאֹהָלֶךָ.
The man got up to go, he and his concubine and his young man; and his father-in-law, the girl’s father, said to him: ‘Look — the day has sunk toward evening; spend the night, please. Look — the day is coming to its end; spend the night here, and let your heart be glad, and tomorrow you can rise early for your journey and go to your tent.’note
El hombre se levantó para irse, él y su concubina y su criado; y su suegro, el padre de la joven, le dijo: «Mira —el día ya declina hacia la tarde; pasen la noche, por favor. Mira —el día se acaba; pasa aquí la noche, y que se alegre tu corazón, y mañana madrugarán para su camino y te irás a tu tienda».
10
וְלֹא-אָבָה הָאִישׁ, לָלוּן, וַיָּקָם וַיֵּלֶךְ וַיָּבֹא עַד-נֹכַח יְבוּס, הִיא יְרוּשָׁלִָם; וְעִמּוֹ, צֶמֶד חֲמוֹרִים חֲבוּשִׁים, וּפִילַגְשׁוֹ, עִמּוֹ.
But the man would not spend the night; he got up and went, and came as far as opposite Jebus — that is, Jerusalem; and with him were a pair of saddled donkeys, and his concubine was with him.note
Pero el hombre no quiso pasar la noche; se levantó y se fue, y llegó hasta enfrente de Jebús —que es Jerusalén—; y con él iban un par de asnos ensillados, y su concubina iba con él.
11
הֵם עִם-יְבוּס, וְהַיּוֹם רַד מְאֹד; וַיֹּאמֶר הַנַּעַר אֶל-אֲדֹנָיו, לְכָה-נָּא וְנָסוּרָה אֶל-עִיר-הַיְבוּסִי הַזֹּאת--וְנָלִין בָּהּ.
When they were near Jebus the day was far gone, and the young man said to his master: ‘Come, please, let us turn aside to this city of the Jebusites and spend the night in it.’note
Cuando estaban cerca de Jebús el día había declinado mucho, y el criado dijo a su señor: «Ven, por favor, desviémonos a esta ciudad de los jebuseos y pasemos en ella la noche».
12
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, אֲדֹנָיו, לֹא נָסוּר אֶל-עִיר נָכְרִי, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-מִבְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל הֵנָּה; וְעָבַרְנוּ, עַד-גִּבְעָה.
His master said to him: ‘We will not turn aside to a city of foreigners, who are not of the sons of Israel; we will pass on as far as Gibeah.’note
Su señor le dijo: «No nos desviaremos a una ciudad de extranjeros, que no son de los hijos de Israel; pasaremos hasta Guibeá».
13
וַיֹּאמֶר לְנַעֲרוֹ, לְךָ וְנִקְרְבָה בְּאַחַד הַמְּקֹמוֹת; וְלַנּוּ בַגִּבְעָה, אוֹ בָרָמָה.
He said to his young man: ‘Come, let us draw near to one of the places, and spend the night in Gibeah or in Ramah.’note
Dijo a su criado: «Ven, acerquémonos a uno de esos lugares, y pasemos la noche en Guibeá o en Ramá».
14
וַיַּעַבְרוּ, וַיֵּלֵכוּ; וַתָּבֹא לָהֶם הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ, אֵצֶל הַגִּבְעָה אֲשֶׁר לְבִנְיָמִן.
So they passed on and went, and the sun went down on them near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.note
Pasaron, pues, y siguieron, y se les puso el sol junto a Guibeá, que pertenece a Benjamín.
15
וַיָּסֻרוּ שָׁם, לָבוֹא לָלוּן בַּגִּבְעָה; וַיָּבֹא, וַיֵּשֶׁב בִּרְחוֹב הָעִיר, וְאֵין אִישׁ מְאַסֵּף-אוֹתָם הַבַּיְתָה, לָלוּן.
They turned aside there to go in and spend the night in Gibeah; he went in and sat down in the city square, and no one took them into the house to spend the night.note
Se desviaron allá para entrar a pasar la noche en Guibeá; él entró y se sentó en la plaza de la ciudad, y nadie los recogió en casa para pasar la noche.
16
וְהִנֵּה אִישׁ זָקֵן, בָּא מִן-מַעֲשֵׂהוּ מִן-הַשָּׂדֶה בָּעֶרֶב, וְהָאִישׁ מֵהַר אֶפְרַיִם, וְהוּא-גָר בַּגִּבְעָה; וְאַנְשֵׁי הַמָּקוֹם, בְּנֵי יְמִינִי.
And look — an old man was coming in from his work in the field in the evening. The man was from the hill country of Ephraim, and he was sojourning in Gibeah; and the men of the place were Benjaminites.note
Y mira —un anciano venía de su trabajo en el campo al atardecer. El hombre era de la serranía de Efraín, y residía como forastero en Guibeá; y los hombres del lugar eran benjaminitas.
17
וַיִּשָּׂא עֵינָיו, וַיַּרְא אֶת-הָאִישׁ הָאֹרֵחַ--בִּרְחֹב הָעִיר; וַיֹּאמֶר הָאִישׁ הַזָּקֵן אָנָה תֵלֵךְ, וּמֵאַיִן תָּבוֹא.
He lifted up his eyes and saw the traveller in the city square; and the old man said: ‘Where are you going, and where do you come from?’note
Alzó los ojos y vio al caminante en la plaza de la ciudad; y el anciano dijo: «¿Adónde vas, y de dónde vienes?».
18
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, עֹבְרִים אֲנַחְנוּ מִבֵּית-לֶחֶם יְהוּדָה עַד-יַרְכְּתֵי הַר-אֶפְרַיִם--מִשָּׁם אָנֹכִי, וָאֵלֵךְ עַד-בֵּית לֶחֶם יְהוּדָה; וְאֶת-בֵּית יְהוָה, אֲנִי הֹלֵךְ, וְאֵין אִישׁ, מְאַסֵּף אוֹתִי הַבָּיְתָה.
He said to him: ‘We are passing from Bethlehem in Judah to the far side of the hill country of Ephraim; I am from there. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to the house of Jehovah; and no one takes me into his house.note
Él le dijo: «Vamos de paso desde Belén de Judá hasta el extremo de la serranía de Efraín; yo soy de allí. Fui a Belén de Judá, y voy a la casa de Jehová; y nadie me recoge en su casa.
19
וְגַם-תֶּבֶן גַּם-מִסְפּוֹא, יֵשׁ לַחֲמוֹרֵינוּ, וְגַם לֶחֶם וָיַיִן יֶשׁ-לִי וְלַאֲמָתֶךָ, וְלַנַּעַר עִם-עֲבָדֶיךָ:  אֵין מַחְסוֹר, כָּל-דָּבָר.
There is both straw and fodder for our donkeys, and bread and wine too for me and for your maidservant, and for the young man with your servants; there is no lack of anything.’note
Tenemos paja y forraje para nuestros asnos, y también pan y vino para mí y para tu sierva, y para el joven que va con tus siervos; no nos falta nada».
20
וַיֹּאמֶר הָאִישׁ הַזָּקֵן שָׁלוֹם לָךְ, רַק כָּל-מַחְסוֹרְךָ עָלָי; רַק בָּרְחוֹב, אַל-תָּלַן.
The old man said: ‘Peace to you! Only let all your lack be on me; only do not spend the night in the square.’note
El anciano dijo: «¡Paz a ti! Solo que todo lo que te falte quede a mi cargo; solo no pases la noche en la plaza».
21
וַיְבִיאֵהוּ לְבֵיתוֹ, ויבול וַיָּבָל לַחֲמוֹרִים; וַיִּרְחֲצוּ, רַגְלֵיהֶם, וַיֹּאכְלוּ, וַיִּשְׁתּוּ.
So he brought him into his house and gave fodder to the donkeys; they washed their feet, and ate and drank.note
Lo llevó, pues, a su casa y dio forraje a los asnos; se lavaron los pies, y comieron y bebieron.
22
הֵמָּה, מֵיטִיבִים אֶת-לִבָּם, וְהִנֵּה אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר אַנְשֵׁי בְנֵי-בְלִיַּעַל נָסַבּוּ אֶת-הַבַּיִת, מִתְדַּפְּקִים עַל-הַדָּלֶת; וַיֹּאמְרוּ, אֶל-הָאִישׁ בַּעַל הַבַּיִת הַזָּקֵן לֵאמֹר, הוֹצֵא אֶת-הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר-בָּא אֶל-בֵּיתְךָ, וְנֵדָעֶנּוּ.
While they were making their hearts glad, look — the men of the city, sons of worthlessness, surrounded the house, beating on the door; and they said to the old man, the master of the house: ‘Bring out the man who came into your house, that we may know him.’note
Mientras alegraban su corazón, mira —los hombres de la ciudad, hijos de la nada, rodearon la casa, golpeando la puerta; y dijeron al anciano, el dueño de la casa: «Saca al hombre que entró en tu casa, para que lo conozcamos».
23
וַיֵּצֵא אֲלֵיהֶם, הָאִישׁ בַּעַל הַבַּיִת, וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם, אַל-אַחַי אַל-תָּרֵעוּ נָא; אַחֲרֵי אֲשֶׁר-בָּא הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה, אֶל-בֵּיתִי--אַל-תַּעֲשׂוּ, אֶת-הַנְּבָלָה הַזֹּאת.
The man, the master of the house, went out to them and said to them: ‘No, my brothers, please do not do evil; since this man has come into my house, do not do this outrage.note
El hombre, el dueño de la casa, salió a ellos y les dijo: «No, hermanos míos, por favor no hagan el mal; ya que este hombre ha entrado en mi casa, no hagan esta infamia.
24
הִנֵּה בִתִּי הַבְּתוּלָה וּפִילַגְשֵׁהוּ, אוֹצִיאָה-נָּא אוֹתָם וְעַנּוּ אוֹתָם, וַעֲשׂוּ לָהֶם, הַטּוֹב בְּעֵינֵיכֶם; וְלָאִישׁ הַזֶּה לֹא תַעֲשׂוּ, דְּבַר הַנְּבָלָה הַזֹּאת.
Look — here are my virgin daughter and his concubine. Let me bring them out; violate them, and do to them what is good in your eyes; but to this man do not do this outrageous thing.’note
Miren —aquí están mi hija virgen y la concubina de él. Déjenme sacarlas; viólenlas, y hagan con ellas lo que bien les parezca a sus ojos; pero a este hombre no le hagan esta cosa infame».
25
וְלֹא-אָבוּ הָאֲנָשִׁים, לִשְׁמֹעַ לוֹ, וַיַּחֲזֵק הָאִישׁ בְּפִילַגְשׁוֹ, וַיֹּצֵא אֲלֵיהֶם הַחוּץ; וַיֵּדְעוּ אוֹתָהּ וַיִּתְעַלְּלוּ-בָהּ כָּל-הַלַּיְלָה, עַד-הַבֹּקֶר, וַיְשַׁלְּחוּהָ, בעלות כַּעֲלוֹת הַשָּׁחַר.
But the men would not listen to him; so the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them, outside; and they knew her and abused her all night until the morning, and let her go as the dawn came up.note
Pero los hombres no quisieron escucharlo; entonces el hombre tomó a su concubina y la sacó afuera, a ellos; y la conocieron y abusaron de ella toda la noche hasta la mañana, y la dejaron ir al subir el alba.
26
וַתָּבֹא הָאִשָּׁה, לִפְנוֹת הַבֹּקֶר; וַתִּפֹּל פֶּתַח בֵּית-הָאִישׁ, אֲשֶׁר-אֲדוֹנֶיהָ שָּׁם--עַד-הָאוֹר.
The woman came at the turning of the morning and fell down at the doorway of the man’s house where her master was, until it was light.note
La mujer vino al despuntar la mañana y cayó a la entrada de la casa del hombre donde estaba su señor, hasta que fue de día.
27
וַיָּקָם אֲדֹנֶיהָ בַּבֹּקֶר, וַיִּפְתַּח דַּלְתוֹת הַבַּיִת, וַיֵּצֵא, לָלֶכֶת לְדַרְכּוֹ; וְהִנֵּה הָאִשָּׁה פִילַגְשׁוֹ, נֹפֶלֶת פֶּתַח הַבַּיִת, וְיָדֶיהָ, עַל-הַסַּף.
Her master rose in the morning, opened the doors of the house, and went out to go on his way; and look — the woman, his concubine, had fallen at the doorway of the house, with her hands on the threshold.note
Su señor se levantó por la mañana, abrió las puertas de la casa y salió para seguir su camino; y mira —la mujer, su concubina, estaba caída a la entrada de la casa, con las manos sobre el umbral.
28
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלֶיהָ קוּמִי וְנֵלֵכָה, וְאֵין עֹנֶה; וַיִּקָּחֶהָ, עַל-הַחֲמוֹר, וַיָּקָם הָאִישׁ, וַיֵּלֶךְ לִמְקֹמוֹ.
He said to her: ‘Get up, let us go.’ But there was no answer. Then he put her on the donkey, and the man got up and went to his place.note
Él le dijo: «Levántate, vámonos». Pero no hubo respuesta. Entonces la puso sobre el asno, y el hombre se levantó y se fue a su lugar.
29
וַיָּבֹא אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ, וַיִּקַּח אֶת-הַמַּאֲכֶלֶת וַיַּחֲזֵק בְּפִילַגְשׁוֹ, וַיְנַתְּחֶהָ לַעֲצָמֶיהָ, לִשְׁנֵים עָשָׂר נְתָחִים; וַיְשַׁלְּחֶהָ, בְּכֹל גְּבוּל יִשְׂרָאֵל.
When he came to his house, he took the knife, and seized his concubine, and cut her up, limb by limb, into twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the territory of Israel.note
Cuando llegó a su casa, tomó el cuchillo, y tomó a su concubina, y la despedazó, miembro por miembro, en doce pedazos, y la envió por todo el territorio de Israel.
30
וְהָיָה כָל-הָרֹאֶה, וְאָמַר לֹא-נִהְיְתָה וְלֹא-נִרְאֲתָה כָּזֹאת, לְמִיּוֹם עֲלוֹת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה; שִׂימוּ-לָכֶם עָלֶיהָ, עֻצוּ וְדַבֵּרוּ.  {פ}
And everyone who saw it said: ‘Nothing like this has happened or been seen from the day the sons of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day. Consider it, take counsel, and speak.’note
Y todo el que lo veía decía: «Nunca ha sucedido ni se ha visto cosa semejante desde el día en que los hijos de Israel subieron de la tierra de Egipto hasta este día. Considérenlo, deliberen, y hablen».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · The Levite’s concubine — “played the harlot against him”, four months in Bethlehem, and “to speak to her heart”

Another Levite. The refrain returns with its words turned round, “when there was no king in Israel” (compare Judges 18:1, now on these pages), and with it another Levite: this one lives in Ephraim and takes a wife from Bethlehem, where the Levite of Judges 17:7 (now on these pages) came from. She is ishah pilegesh, “a woman, a concubine”, a pilegesh, a wife of lower standing; the two words stand together in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and at verse 27. She is never named.

“Played the harlot against him.” The Hebrew vattizneh alav uses the verb of prostitution, with a preposition that makes it an offence against him. The witnesses do not agree on what it means. The A Greek text has “was angry with him”, as though reading another verb, and the B text only “went away from him”. The KJV has “played the whore against him”, the ASV “played the harlot against him”, the NWT 1984 “began to commit fornication against him”, and the NIV “she was unfaithful to him”; the TLB follows the Greek, “she became angry with him”, and the Douay has only “she left him”. Josephus says they quarrelled because she did not return his love, and that she left him “in the fourth month” (Antiquities 5.136–137, numbered 5.2.8 in Whiston).

“To speak to her heart.” After four months he goes after her “to speak to her heart and bring her back”, the words of Shechem to Dinah (Genesis 34:3) and of Joseph to his brothers (Genesis 50:21), both now on these pages. The written Hebrew has “to bring him back”, the margin “to bring her back”. The ASV has “to speak kindly unto her”, the NWT 1984 “to speak consolingly to her”. Her father “rejoiced to meet him” and “held him fast”: vayyachazeq, the verb that returns when the Levite “seized” her at verses 25 and 29.

Verses 5–9 · A father-in-law’s hospitality — “Refresh your heart with a piece of bread”, and “let your heart be glad”

Abraham’s bread. On the fourth day the father says, “Refresh your heart with a piece of bread”: pat lechem and the verb sa’ad, Abraham’s words to his three guests at Mamre, “let me bring a piece of bread, so you can refresh yourselves” (Genesis 18:5, now on these pages). The story of hospitality at Mamre was followed by the night at Sodom; this one will be too. The ASV has “Strengthen thy heart with a morsel of bread”, the NWT 1984 “Sustain your heart with a bit of bread”.

“Let your heart be glad.” Twice he urges, “let your heart be glad” (vv. 6 and 9): yitav libbekha, the glad heart of the Danites’ priest (Judges 18:20, now on these pages). “Be willing, please”, ho’el na, is the verb of the Levite who “was willing to dwell with the man” (Judges 17:11, now on these pages). The ASV has “Be pleased, I pray thee, to tarry all night, and let thy heart be merry”.

Day after day. The days are counted: three, then the fourth, then the fifth, each with “he rose early … to go” and a reason to stay. On the fifth day the father’s last plea runs to two “Look”s: the day “has sunk toward evening”, the day “is coming to its end”. The Levite leaves late, and the lateness is what puts the travellers on the road at sunset.

Verses 10–15 · Jebus, which is Jerusalem — “a city of foreigners”, and the square at Gibeah

Jebus. The road passes “opposite Jebus — that is, Jerusalem”, a city the sons of Benjamin had not taken: “the Jebusite has dwelt with the sons of Benjamin in Jerusalem to this day” (Judges 1:21, now on these pages). The young man wants to stop there; his master will not turn aside “to a city of foreigners, who are not of the sons of Israel”. The words ir nokhri, “a city of foreigners”, stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “the city of a foreigner, that is not of the children of Israel”; the NWT 1984 “a city of foreigners who are no part of the sons of Israel”. Josephus says the city was “of the Canaanites” (Antiquities 5.140).

Gibeah. They go on to Gibeah, “which belongs to Benjamin”, and the sun goes down on them there. The Levite sits “in the city square”, and “no one took them into the house to spend the night”. The phrase comes twice (vv. 15 and 18), and the city of Israelites does what the Levite feared from the city of foreigners.

Verses 16–21 · An old man from Ephraim — “the house of Jehovah”, “no lack of anything”, and the night in the square

A sojourner. The one man who takes them in is not a Benjaminite: he is “from the hill country of Ephraim”, and “he was sojourning in Gibeah”, gar, the resident stranger’s verb (gur). “The men of the place were Benjaminites.” His questions, “Where are you going, and where do you come from?”, are a host’s questions.

“The house of Jehovah.” The Levite says he is “going to the house of Jehovah”. Both Greek texts have “to my house”, as though the Hebrew beti, “my house”, had been read as an abbreviation of beit YHWH, or the other way round. The ASV keeps the Hebrew, “I am now going to the house of Jehovah”, and the Douay has “the house of God”; the NWT 1984 follows the Greek, “it is to my own house that I am going”.

Lot’s square. The Levite has straw and fodder, bread and wine: “there is no lack of anything”, words that stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in the spies’ report on Laish (Judges 18:10, now on these pages). The old man answers, “only do not spend the night in the square”, as Lot pressed his two guests, who had said “we will spend the night in the square” (Genesis 19:2, now on these pages). Then “they washed their feet”, words that stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Joseph’s house (Genesis 43:24, now on these pages).

Verses 22–24 · Gibeah and Sodom — “that we may know him”, “do not do evil”, and “this outrage”

The men of the city. While the travellers are “making their hearts glad”, “the men of the city, sons of worthlessness”, surround the house: benei beliyya’al (beliyaal), the words of Deuteronomy 13:14 (now on these pages). The phrase stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible, among them the sons of Eli at 1 Samuel 2:12. The KJV keeps “sons of Belial”; the ASV has “certain base fellows”, the NWT 1984 “mere good-for-nothing men”.

Sodom again. What they say and what the old man answers are the words of Sodom. “Bring out the man who came into your house, that we may know him” is “Bring them out to us, that we may know them” (Genesis 19:5); “No, my brothers, please do not do evil” is Lot’s “Please, my brothers — do not do this evil” (19:7); and the offer of a daughter, “do to them what is good in your eyes”, is Lot’s offer of his two (19:8), all now on these pages. To “know” is the Hebrew yada, as in “the man knew his wife”. The NIV says it outright, “so we can have sex with him”. At Sodom the guests struck the men at the door with blindness (Genesis 19:11, now on these pages); at Gibeah the guest saves himself with his concubine.

“This outrage.” The old man calls it nevalah (nevalah), the word for the outrage done to Dinah, “an outrage in Israel” (Genesis 34:7, now on these pages). The words “this outrage” stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: twice here (vv. 23 and 24), and in Tamar’s “do not do this outrage” to her brother (2 Samuel 13:12). He offers the two women to be “violated”, innah, Shechem’s verb, who “violated” Dinah (Genesis 34:2). The KJV and the ASV have “humble ye them”; the NWT 1984 “YOU rape them”; the NIV “you can use them”.

Verses 25–28 · The night at Gibeah — “he seized his concubine”, her hands on the threshold, and “there was no answer”

The night. The men will not listen, and “the man seized his concubine and brought her out to them”: “his concubine”, so the man is the Levite himself. “They knew her and abused her all night until the morning”: vayit’allelu, the verb of Jehovah’s “how I dealt harshly with Egypt” (Exodus 10:2, now on these pages). The ASV has “they knew her, and abused her all the night until the morning”; the NIV “they raped her and abused her throughout the night”. Josephus has the young men of Gibeah seize her themselves and drag her off (Antiquities 5.146).

Her master. She comes back “at the turning of the morning” and falls “at the doorway of the man’s house where her master was”, and lies there “until it was light”. The chapter calls him “her master” here and in the next verse. He opens the doors “to go on his way”, and there she is: “the woman, his concubine, had fallen at the doorway of the house, with her hands on the threshold”.

“There was no answer.” “Get up, let us go,” he says. “But there was no answer.” The Hebrew does not say when she died. Both Greek texts say it: the A text “but she had died”, the B text “for she was dead”, and so do the TLB, “for she was dead”, and the Douay, “perceiving she was dead”. The ASV keeps the silence, “but none answered”. Josephus says she died at the door of grief and shame, not daring to face her husband (Antiquities 5.147).

Verses 29–30 · Twelve pieces through Israel — “the knife”, “nothing like this”, and “consider it, take counsel, and speak”

The knife. At home “he took the knife”, ha-ma’akhelet, with the article. The word with the article stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, and twice of Abraham on Mount Moriah, who “took in his hand the fire and the knife” and “took the knife to slaughter his son” (Genesis 22:6 and 22:10, now on these pages). He “cut her up, limb by limb, into twelve pieces”: the verb nittach is the cutting of a sacrifice, “you shall cut the ram into its pieces” (Exodus 29:17, now on these pages), and Saul will use it of a yoke of oxen sent through Israel to summon the tribes (1 Samuel 11:7). The NWT 1984 has “the slaughtering knife” and “according to her bones”; the ASV “limb by limb”; the Douay “a sword”.

“Nothing like this.” “Nothing like this has happened or been seen from the day the sons of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day.” The words lo nihyetah, “has not happened”, stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible; one is the cry over Egypt’s firstborn, “such as there has never been” (Exodus 11:6, now on these pages). Hosea will look back to “the days of Gibeah” (Hosea 9:9 and 10:9).

“Consider it, take counsel, and speak.” The chapter ends on three commands, and the Hebrew does not say whose they are: the onlookers’ to one another, or the Levite’s to his messengers. The A Greek text makes them the Levite’s, adding the words he gave the men he sent, “Thus you shall say to every man of Israel: Has anything like this happened…?”. The ASV has “consider it, take counsel, and speak”; the NWT 1984 “Set YOUR hearts upon it, take counsel and speak”. Josephus says he sent the pieces to each tribe with orders to tell who had caused her death (Antiquities 5.149, numbered 5.2.8 in Whiston).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a woman, a concubine” for ishah pilegesh; “played the harlot against him” for vattizneh alav; “to speak to her heart”, as with Shechem and Joseph; “Refresh your heart with a piece of bread”, as with Abraham; “let your heart be glad” for yitav libbekha; “the city square” for rechov, as with Lot; “sons of worthlessness” for benei beliyya’al; “that we may know him” and “do not do evil”, as at Sodom; “outrage” for nevalah; “violate” for innah; “abused” for hit’allel; “seized” for hechezik; and “the knife” for ha-ma’akhelet, as with Abraham.

Echoes paid. The refrain of Judges 18:1 returns in other words, and the glad heart of 18:20 is pressed on another Levite. Abraham’s bread at Genesis 18:5, Lot’s square and his doorway at Genesis 19:2–8, Dinah’s “outrage” at Genesis 34:7, and Abraham’s knife at Genesis 22:10 all come back at Gibeah. The Jebusites left in Jerusalem at Judges 1:21 are the foreigners the Levite will not trust.

Echoes planted. “Consider it, take counsel” is answered at Judges 20:7, and “sons of worthlessness” and “an outrage in Israel” at 20:6 and 20:13. Saul cuts up his oxen at 1 Samuel 11:7, Tamar says “do not do this outrage” at 2 Samuel 13:12, and Hosea remembers “the days of Gibeah” at 9:9 and 10:9. None of these is translated here yet.

Next installment in this book: Judges 20 — all Israel gathers “as one man” at Mizpah, from Dan to Beersheba; the Levite tells his story, Benjamin refuses to give up the men of Gibeah, and the tribes go to war against their own brother, losing twice before the ambush that burns the city.
