🏺 Encyclopedia

The Disciple Whom Jesus Loved

An unnamed disciple who appears only in the Fourth Gospel: reclining ‘close beside Jesus’ at the last supper (John 13:23), standing near the cross when Jesus entrusts his mother to him (19:26–27), and, in John 20, running ahead of Peter to the empty tomb, where he ‘saw and believed’ (20:2–10). He appears twice more in the last chapter (21:7, 20, already on these pages), where the Gospel itself corrects a rumor that he would not die (21:23), and its closing verses speak of him as the one who ‘testifies about these things’ and ‘wrote these things’ (21:24). The phrase is the Gospel's only label for him: no verse gives his name. ⚠ Who he was is genuinely open, and this entry does not settle it. The oldest and most widespread tradition, from Irenaeus onward, identifies him with John son of Zebedee and so with the Gospel's author. Many modern scholars doubt that and have proposed others — Lazarus, whom his sisters call ‘the one you love’ (11:3); an otherwise unknown Jerusalem disciple like the ‘other disciple’ known to the high priest at 18:15 (whom that verse does not equate with him); or a figure meant as the ideal disciple rather than one particular man. ⚠ The Greek describes the love with two different verbs — agapaō at 13:23, 19:26, 21:7 and 21:20, phileō at 20:2 — a swap this Gospel makes without comment elsewhere, and one that should not be made to carry an argument about who he was. The Living Bible prints the identification as though it stood in the Greek: ‘Simon Peter and me’ (20:2).
