🏺 Encyclopedia

Joshua

Moses' military commander and eventual successor, introduced here with no genealogy and no backstory — already trusted to choose men and lead them against Amalek while Moses climbs a hill (Exodus 17:9-13). ⚠ His name at this point is likely still HOSHEA, 'he saves'; Moses renames him YEHOSHUA, folding in the divine name, only later (Numbers 13:16) — the same root that gives, through Greek, the name JESUS. He becomes Moses' personal attendant (Exodus 24:13; 33:11), one of only two of the twelve spies to bring back a good report of the land (Numbers 13-14), and is commissioned as Moses' successor before Moses dies (Deuteronomy 31; Joshua 1) — leading the conquest that gives his own book its name. ⚠ Numbers 34:17 gives him a second commission alongside the first: with Eleazar the priest he is to take possession of the land FOR Israel — an administrative job, not a military one, and he is named second to the priest who casts the lots. ⭐ His FIRST charge in Deuteronomy comes earlier than either of those, and in two stages: Deuteronomy 3:21-22 only tells him, secondhand through Moses, that his own eyes have seen what was done to Sihon and Og; the actual strengthening waits until 3:28, where God has Moses 'encourage him, and strengthen him' — the first time the two roots later fixed into Joshua 1's own 'be strong and resolute' appear in one sentence. ⭐ The commissioning itself happens in Deuteronomy 31 (now on these pages), and it happens twice: publicly, from Moses, in the sight of all Israel (31:7), and then privately, inside the tent of meeting, from God (31:23) — and the one verse of Deuteronomy that names the tent of meeting at all is the verse that summons the two men into it. ⚠ The Hebrew of that second charge never says who gives it: the verb carries no subject, and it is the speaker's own words, I myself will be with you, that settle the question. ⭐ Deuteronomy 34:9 (now on these pages) reports the hand-off complete: 'Joshua son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his hands on him' — still HANDS, plural, the same unauthorized addition Numbers 27:23 already made to Moses' own single-hand instructions, three chapters and one book apart. ⭐ His first move against the land itself is Joshua 2:1 (now on these pages): he sends two spies ‘secretly’ from Shittim — two, not twelve, and with the verb ragal rather than Numbers 13's tur — and the report he receives back is a Canaanite woman's sentence, Jehovah has given all the land into our hand. ⭐ Joshua 3 (now on these pages) is where he is made great: this day I will begin to make you great in the eyes of all Israel (v7), Deuteronomy 2:25’s ‘this day I will begin’ with him as its object, and 1:5’s ‘as I was with Moses, I will be with you’ said a second time. He rises early (v1, a habit the book will repeat at 6:12, 7:16 and 8:10), commands the priests and the people, and does not himself lead the procession — the ark does. ⭐ Joshua 4:14 (now on these pages) is where 3:7’s promise is paid: on that day Jehovah made Joshua great in the eyes of all Israel, and they feared him as they had feared Moses — the Torah’s last words, ‘in the sight of all Israel’ (Deuteronomy 34:12), now said of him. He sets up twelve stones twice, in the riverbed and at Gilgal, and gives the fathers the words their sons are to hear (4:21–24). ⭐ Joshua 5 (now on these pages): at Jehovah’s command he makes knives of flint and circumcises the whole wilderness-born generation (5:2–3), keeps the first Passover in the land (5:10), and at Jericho meets the commander of the army of Jehovah, asks him whose side he is on, falls on his face at the answer no, and takes off his sandal as Moses did at the bush (5:13–15) — the successor given his predecessor’s first sentence from God, with one sandal where Moses had two. ★ Joshua 7 (now on these pages) is his worst day: three thousand men routed at Ai, and he tears his clothes and lies on his face before the ark until evening, praying the wilderness generation’s own complaint — why did you ever bring this people across the Jordan, to give us into the hand of the Amorites? (7:7), the sentence of Deuteronomy 1:27 that he and Caleb once tore their clothes against — and Moses’ own argument, what will you do for your great name? God’s answer is Get up! (7:10), and Rashi hears in it that he stayed in the camp instead of going out before the people. He rises early a third time in the book, sifts the nation tribe by tribe, calls the thief my son (7:19) and asks him for a confession, and stones him in a valley that takes its name from his own warning of 6:18.
