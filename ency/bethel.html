🏺 Encyclopedia

Bethel

A hill-country town on Ephraim's own southern border, renamed by Jacob after his dream of a stairway between earth and heaven: 'Surely Jehovah is in this place' (Genesis 28:19, already on these pages) — 'but Luz was the name of the city before.' Jacob returns and renames it a second time, building an altar and calling it El-Bethel (Genesis 35:7, already on these pages; Genesis 35 is yet to come in this translation in Spanish, where Genesis 28 has also not shipped). Joshua 16:2 (now on these pages) runs this chapter's own border 'from Bethel to Luz' without repeating either of Genesis's own glosses that the two names are one place — the one of the three Hebrew occurrences of this directional form (Genesis 35:6, Joshua 16:2, 18:13) that does not. Joshua 18:13 (now on these pages) is the second to add the gloss, ‘that is, Bethel’, runs Benjamin’s northern border along the shoulder of Luz, and counts Bethel among Benjamin’s own towns (18:22). Centuries later Jeroboam sets up one of his two golden calves here (1 Kings 12:29), and Amos is told outright to stop prophesying at it (Amos 7:13) — the dreamed-of house of God becomes the kingdom's own idolatrous shrine. ⭐ It is the first town named in Samuel’s yearly circuit as judge, “Bethel and Gilgal and Mizpah” (1 Samuel 7:16, now on these pages).
