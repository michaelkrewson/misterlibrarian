🏺 Encyclopedia

Jericho

A city in the Jordan valley just north of the Dead Sea, and one of the oldest continuously occupied settlements known anywhere — excavation has found a stone tower and wall at Tell es-Sultan dating to roughly 8000 BC, several thousand years before writing. It sits about 250 metres BELOW sea level, watered by a spring, which is why a town is there at all in that heat. ⚠ Hebrews 11:30 credits the fall of its walls to faith; the archaeology of the Late Bronze Age destruction and its date has been argued over for a century and is not settled here. Jericho returns in the Gospels as the city Jesus passes through for Zacchaeus and for blind Bartimaeus, and as the destination of the road in the parable of the good Samaritan. ⚠ Joshua 2:15 (now on these pages) has Rahab's house in the side of the city wall, two Hebrew words for one wall (qir, a house-wall; chomah, a rampart) — the shape of a casemate fortification, rooms built between two parallel walls, known from several Iron Age sites; whether such a wall stood at Jericho at the time of the story is the same unsettled question as the one above. The Septuagint's verse omits the clause altogether. ⭐ Joshua 3:16 (now on these pages) has Israel cross the Jordan ‘opposite Jericho’ — the city is the fixed point the crossing is measured from, as Adam is the point the standing water is measured from, some thirty kilometres upstream. ⭐ Joshua 5:10 (now on these pages) keeps the first Passover in the land on the plains of Jericho, in sight of the shut city; at 5:13 Joshua is at Jericho — the Hebrew preposition is in, which Rashi reads as the city’s outskirts — when a man with a drawn sword stands opposite him and the message about the city’s fall (6:2) begins. ★ Joshua 7 (now on these pages) is what its spoil cost: Achan took one good mantle of Shinar, two hundred shekels of silver, and one tongue of gold from the devoted things (7:21), and the next city, Ai, beat Israel on the descent. The rabbis, Rashi reports, thought a Babylonian king kept a palace here and wore the mantle on visits. At 8:2 the spoil of Ai is given to Israel to keep — Jericho was the one city whose plunder was withheld.
