🏺 Encyclopedia

Nahash the Ammonite

King of the Ammonites. He besieges Jabesh-gilead and, when the town asks for a covenant, offers one on condition of gouging out every man’s right eye, “as a reproach on all Israel”; he grants seven days, and Saul, newly chosen king, musters Israel at Bezek and scatters the Ammonite camp (1 Samuel 11, now on these pages). His name is spelled like nachash, “serpent”. Samuel later names his attack as the moment the people demanded a king (1 Samuel 12:12, now on these pages), though in 1 Samuel 8 the demand comes before Nahash appears; David speaks of the kindness Nahash showed him and sends condolences to his son Hanun (2 Samuel 10:2, now on these pages). ⚠ Josephus says Nahash had blinded the right eyes of the peoples beyond the Jordan he conquered, so that with the left eye behind the shield they were useless in war, and that he was killed in the battle at Jabesh (Antiquities 6.69–70, 79); the second claim does not sit with 2 Samuel 10:2, where he is still alive in David’s day. A Hebrew manuscript from Qumran, 4QSama, is reported to carry a further paragraph before the siege, about his putting out the right eyes of the Israelites beyond the Jordan (see 1 Samuel 10:27).
