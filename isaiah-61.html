A speaker anointed by the Spirit to bring good news to the lowly, bind up the broken in heart and proclaim liberty, a year of favor and a day of vengeance, and to comfort the mourners of Zion: a headdress for ashes, oaks of righteousness, the old ruins rebuilt, strangers as shepherds, a people called priests, a double portion, an everlasting covenant, and a garment of salvation like a bridegroom’s, with righteousness sprouting before all the nations.
Undated in the chapter itself. It is filed with the exile and return because the ruined cities and the mourners of Zion are the picture it works with; the chapter does not name a king, a year or an event. ⚠ Who the speaker is, the prophet or the servant, is left open by the Hebrew, and the reading at Nazareth (Luke 4:18–21) is one answer to it.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verse 1 · Isaiah 61:1 “The Spirit of the Lord Jehovah is upon me” — anointed to bring good news, to bind up, to proclaim liberty

⭐ Who is “me”. The speaker is not named: “the Spirit of the Lord Jehovah is upon me” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one, and the reader is left to decide whether it is the prophet, the servant of the earlier chapters, or a figure the chapter does not name. The tradition that reads it aloud in Nazareth and says “today this scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing” gave it one answer (Luke 4:18–21, now on these pages), and that chapter’s notes lay out what the Greek reading changed. The KJV has “The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me”, the ASV “The Spirit of the Lord Jehovah is upon me”, the NIV “The Spirit of the Sovereign Lord is on me”, the NWT 1984 “The spirit of the Sovereign Lord Jehovah is upon me” and the Douay “The spirit of the Lord is upon me”.

⭐ Anointed, and good news. “Jehovah has anointed me” stands in this one verse, the verb mashach that is behind mashiach; here the anointing is for speaking, with the Spirit, not with oil. What the anointed one is sent for is levaser, to bring good news, and “to bring good news to the lowly” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “to preach good tidings unto the meek”, the NIV “to proclaim good news to the poor”, the NWT 1984 “to tell good news to the meek ones”, the TLB “to bring good news to the suffering and afflicted” and the Douay “to preach to the meek”. The Greek of Luke has “the poor”, and the saying that Jesus uses to answer John, “the poor have good news brought to them” (Matthew 11:5, now on these pages), is this verb.

⭐ Bind up, and liberty. “to bind up the broken in heart” stands in this one verse, with chavash, to bind a wound. The KJV has “to bind up the brokenhearted”, the NWT 1984 “to bind up the brokenhearted”, the TLB “to comfort the brokenhearted” and the Douay “to heal the contrite of heart”. Then “to proclaim to captives liberty” stands in this one verse, with deror (dictionary), the word of the jubilee (Leviticus 25:10, now on these pages). The KJV has “to proclaim liberty to the captives”, the NIV “to proclaim freedom for the captives” and the Douay “to preach a release to the captives”.

⚠ “An opening”. The last line is two words, peqach-qoach, an opening, the second a doubled form of the first (stands in this one verse), and the Hebrew does not say what is opened. The KJV has “the opening of the prison to them that are bound”, the ASV “the opening of the prison”, the NWT 1984 “the wide opening [of the eyes] even to the prisoners”, the TLB “to open the eyes of the blind” and the Douay “deliverance to them that are shut up”. Luke has “recovery of sight to the blind”, the Greek Old Testament’s line, and the same note explains why the Hebrew cannot be turned into it.

Verse 2 · Isaiah 61:2 “a year of favor for Jehovah, and a day of vengeance for our God” — where the reading stops

⭐ A year, and a day. “A year of favor” stands in this one verse, shenat ratson, and it is set against “a day of vengeance”, which stands in four verses: this one, Isaiah 34:8, Isaiah 63:4 and Proverbs 6:34, none of the others translated here yet. In Isaiah 34:8 and 63:4 the day of vengeance and a year of reckoning stand side by side, as here, and it is the pair that this verse inherits. The KJV has “the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of vengeance of our God”, the ASV “the year of Jehovah’s favor”, the NIV “to proclaim the year of the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “the year of goodwill on the part of Jehovah” and the TLB “the time of God’s favor to them has come, and the day of his wrath to their enemies”.

⭐ Where Luke stopped. In Nazareth the reading ends at “the year of Jehovah’s favor” and leaves the rest of the verse unread, “and a day of vengeance for our God” (Luke 4:19, now on these pages; the notes there carry the discussion). The Hebrew does not break there: “to proclaim a year of favor for Jehovah, and a day of vengeance for our God” is one infinitive, and “to comfort all who mourn” is the next in the same run. “To comfort all who mourn” stands in this one verse, with nacham, to comfort (dictionary), the verb that opens Isaiah 40:1 (now on these pages), and it is the promise the Beatitude takes up, “blessed are those who mourn” (Matthew 5:4, now on these pages). The KJV has “to comfort all that mourn”, the NWT 1984 “to comfort all the mourning ones” and the NIV “to comfort all who mourn”.

Verse 3 · Isaiah 61:3 “a headdress in place of ashes” — oil of gladness, a mantle of praise, and oaks of righteousness

⭐ Headdress for ashes. The Hebrew plays with the two words: pe’er, a headdress, a beautiful covering, and efer, ashes, are the same three consonants in a different order, and “a headdress in place of ashes” stands in this one verse. The ashes are what the mourners of Zion have put on their heads, and what replaces them goes on the same place. The KJV has “beauty for ashes”, the ASV “a garland for ashes”, the NIV “a crown of beauty instead of ashes”, the NWT 1984 “a headdress instead of ashes”, the Douay “a crown for ashes” and the TLB “beauty for ashes”.

⭐ Oil, and a mantle. “The oil of gladness” stands in two verses, this one and Psalm 45:8, where it is the oil on a king at his wedding. “A mantle of praise” stands in this one verse, set against ruach kehah, a faint spirit. The KJV has “the oil of joy for mourning, the garment of praise for the spirit of heaviness”, the NIV “a garment of praise instead of a spirit of despair”, the NWT 1984 “the mantle of praise instead of the downhearted spirit” and the Douay “a garment of praise for the spirit of grief”.

⭐ Oaks of righteousness. “Oaks of righteousness” stands in this one verse, with eilei, a word for a strong tree, an oak or terebinth, and the same consonants can also be read as rams or mighty ones. “The planting of Jehovah” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “trees of righteousness”, the NIV “oaks of righteousness”, the NWT 1984 “big trees of righteousness”, the TLB “strong and graceful oaks” and the Douay “the mighty ones of justice”.

Verses 4–5 · Isaiah 61:4–5 “they will build the ruins of long ago” — and strangers will pasture your flocks

⭐ The old ruins. The people who were mourners are builders: “the ruins of long ago” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one, Isaiah 58:12, Jeremiah 25:9 and Jeremiah 49:13 (Jeremiah 25:9 now on these pages), and in Jeremiah 25:9 they are the ruins Jehovah says he will make. So the line reverses a sentence: the same ruins, now raised. “The cities of waste” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “they shall build the old wastes”, the NIV “They will rebuild the ancient ruins”, the NWT 1984 “they must rebuild the long-standing devastated places”, the TLB “they shall rebuild the ancient ruins” and the Douay “they shall build the places that have been waste from of old”. The cities are “desolations of generation and generation”, the same doubling as at Psalm 90:1 (now on these pages).

⭐ Strangers as shepherds. Verse 5 gives the labor to others: zarim and benei nekhar, strangers and the sons of the foreigner, will stand and pasture your flocks and be your plowmen and vinedressers. The KJV has “strangers shall stand and feed your flocks”, the NIV “Strangers will shepherd your flocks”, the NWT 1984 “strangers will actually stand and shepherd the flocks”, the TLB “Foreigners shall be your servants” and the Douay “the dressers of your vines”. The Hebrew states it without comment.

Verses 6–7 · Isaiah 61:6–7 “you, the priests of Jehovah” — ministers of our God, and a double portion

⭐ A nation of priests. “And you”, the pronoun is emphatic, set against the strangers: “priests of Jehovah” stands in four verses, two of them about the priests of Nob (1 Samuel 22:17 and 22:21), then 2 Chronicles 13:9 and this one. “Ministers of our God” stands in this one verse. The idea that a whole people is priestly is the one at Sinai, “a kingdom of priests” (Exodus 19:6, now on these pages), and the phrase stands in that one verse; the New Testament takes it up for the church (1 Peter 2:9, now on these pages). The KJV has “ye shall be named the Priests of the Lord”, the NIV “you will be called priests of the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “YOU will be called” (it prints the plural in capitals) and the TLB “You shall be called priests of the Lord”.

⭐ “You will boast”. “You will eat the wealth of the nations” stands in this one verse, and the last verb, titamaru, is a word that stands in this one verse: the versions differ on it. The KJV has “in their glory shall ye boast yourselves”, the NIV “in their riches you will boast”, the NWT 1984 “in their glory YOU will speak elatedly about yourselves”, the Geneva “shall be exalted with their glory”, the Douay “you shall pride yourselves in their glory” and the TLB “shall glory in their riches”.

⭐ A double portion. Mishneh is a second, a double; the law gave the firstborn “two parts” (Deuteronomy 21:17, now on these pages), in a different expression, and the image is the same: in place of shame, a firstborn’s share. “Everlasting joy” stands in three verses: Isaiah 35:10, Isaiah 51:11 and this one, neither of the others translated here yet. The KJV has “for your shame ye shall have double”, the ASV “Instead of your shame ye shall have double”, the NIV “Instead of your shame you will receive a double portion”, the NWT 1984 “Instead of YOUR shame there will be a double portion” and the TLB “Instead of shame and dishonor, you shall have a double portion of prosperity and everlasting joy”.

Verses 8–9 · Isaiah 61:8–9 “I, Jehovah, love justice” — robbery in a burnt offering, and an everlasting covenant

⚠ “Robbery in a burnt offering”. The line is sone gazel be’olah, “hating robbery in a burnt offering”, and “hating robbery” stands in this one verse. The Hebrew text has olah, the burnt offering, which is what the old English versions print, and the sentence then says that Jehovah hates an offering made from what was robbed; many readers take the consonants as avlah, wrongdoing, which is what the modern versions print. The KJV has “I hate robbery for burnt offering”, the Geneva “hate robbery for burnt offering”, the Douay “hate robbery in a holocaust”, the ASV “I hate robbery with iniquity”, the NIV “I hate robbery and wrongdoing”, the NWT 1984 “hating robbery along with unrighteousness” and the TLB “I hate robbery and wrong”. “Loves justice” stands in two verses, this one and Psalm 37:28 (now on these pages).

⭐ “An everlasting covenant I will cut”. “An everlasting covenant I will cut” in this form stands in this one verse, and its sister is the promise of the earlier chapter, “I will cut with you an everlasting covenant” (Isaiah 55:3, now on these pages). The KJV has “I will make an everlasting covenant with them”, the NWT 1984 “an indefinitely lasting covenant I shall conclude toward them” and the Douay “I will make a perpetual covenant with them”. Verse 9 gives the sign of it: “the seed Jehovah has blessed” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “the seed which the Lord hath blessed”, the NIV “a people the Lord has blessed” and the NWT 1984 “the offspring whom Jehovah has blessed”.

Verses 10–11 · Isaiah 61:10–11 “I will greatly rejoice in Jehovah” — a bridegroom like a priest, and righteousness that sprouts

⭐ The speaker answers. The voice changes to a first person: “I will greatly rejoice” stands in this one verse, the verb said twice (sos asis), with “a robe of righteousness” in this one verse. The clothing is salvation and righteousness, set beside the headdress of verse 3. The KJV has “he hath covered me with the robe of righteousness”, the NIV “arrayed me in a robe of his righteousness”, the NWT 1984 “with the sleeveless coat of righteousness he has enwrapped me” and the TLB “draped about me the robe of righteousness”.

⭐ A bridegroom, as a priest. “Like a bridegroom who acts as a priest” stands in this one verse, yekhahen, a verb made from the noun kohen, priest: the bridegroom puts on his headdress as a priest does. The KJV has “as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments”, the NIV “as a bridegroom adorns his head like a priest”, the NWT 1984 “like the bridegroom who, in a priestly way, puts on a headdress” and the Douay “as a bridegroom decked with a crown”. The bride in the last image has an echo in the new Jerusalem “prepared as a bride adorned for her husband” (Revelation 21:2, now on these pages).

⭐ Sprout. The last verse uses a farmer’s image, with the verb tsamach: the form tatsmiach stands in three verses, Genesis 3:18, Isaiah 45:8 and this one; in Genesis the ground, under the curse, sprouts thorns (Genesis 3:18, now on these pages), and here the earth and the garden sprout righteousness and praise before all the nations. The KJV has “the Lord God will cause righteousness and praise to spring forth”, the NIV “the Sovereign Lord will make righteousness and praise spring up”, the NWT 1984 “will cause the sprouting of righteousness and of praise”, the Douay “so shall the Lord God make justice to spring forth” and the TLB “His righteousness shall be like a budding tree”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the lowly” for anavim; “anointed” for mashach; “bind up” for chavash; “liberty” for deror; “a year of favor” for shenat ratson; “comfort” for nacham; “a headdress” for pe’er and “ashes” for efer; “oaks of righteousness” for eilei hatsedek; “priests” for kohanim; “an everlasting covenant”; “the Lord Jehovah” for Adonai Yehovih and “Jehovah” for the name. The Hebrew has no written and read pairs in this chapter.

Echoes paid. The reading at Nazareth (Luke 4:18–21); the good news to the poor in Matthew 11:5; the jubilee liberty of Leviticus 25:10; the comfort of Isaiah 40:1; the mourners of Matthew 5:4; the old ruins of Jeremiah 25:9; the kingdom of priests of Exodus 19:6 and 1 Peter 2:9; the double portion of Deuteronomy 21:17; the justice-lover of Psalm 37:28; the everlasting covenant of Isaiah 55:3; the bride of Revelation 21:2; and the thorns of Genesis 3:18.

Echoes planted. The other days of vengeance (Isaiah 34:8, 63:4, Proverbs 6:34); the oil of gladness (Psalm 45:8); the other “ruins of long ago” (Isaiah 58:12, Jeremiah 49:13); the priests of Nob (1 Samuel 22:17, 22:21) and 2 Chronicles 13:9; the other everlasting joys (Isaiah 35:10, 51:11); and the other sprouting (Isaiah 45:8). None of these is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Isaiah stands on these pages at chapters 1, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14, 26, 40, 41, 43, 53, 54, 55, 58 and 61, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: Isaiah 2–5, 8, 10–25, 27–39, 42, 44–52, 54, 56–60 and 62–66 are still to come.
