Solomon is king over all Israel. His officials are listed: priests, scribes, a recorder, the army, the household, the forced labor; and twelve officers over all Israel, each sustaining the king and his household for a month in the year, from the hill country of Ephraim to Gilead and Bashan. Two of them have married Solomon’s daughters. Judah and Israel are many, like the sand by the sea, eating and drinking and rejoicing.
The settled years of Solomon’s reign, with the house of Jehovah not yet built: the kingdom organized by months, and at ease.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · 1 Kings 4 Solomon’s officials — a priest, scribes and a recorder, “the king’s friend”, and “forced labor”

The officials he had. “And King Solomon was king over all Israel. And these were the officials he had: Azariah son of Zadok the priest”: the ASV has “And king Solomon was king over all Israel” and “princes”, the NIV “ruled over all Israel” and “chief officials”, the NWT 1984 “continued king over all Israel” and “princes”, and the Douay “reigned over all Israel”. The word “priest” follows “Zadok” and may belong to either name: the ASV has “Azariah the son of Zadok, the priest”, the NIV “Azariah son of Zadok—the priest”. The TLB puts the list in one paragraph, “Here is a list of King Solomon’s cabinet members”.

Scribes and a recorder. “Elihoreph and Ahijah, sons of Shisha, scribes; Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud, the recorder”: the ASV has “scribes” and “the recorder”, the NIV “secretaries” and “recorder”, the NWT 1984 “secretaries” and “the recorder”. A Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was David’s recorder (2 Samuel 8:16; 20:24), and David’s scribe is Seraiah and then Sheva (8:17; 20:25, all now on these pages); the text does not say that the recorder is the same man.

Zadok and Abiathar. “And Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the army; and Zadok and Abiathar were priests”: Benaiah was put over the army in Joab’s place (1 Kings 2:35), and Abiathar was driven out of the priesthood, “to fulfill the word of Jehovah” (2:26–27, both now on these pages); this verse names him a priest, and the page does not say how the two fit. The ASV has “Zadok and Abiathar were priests”, the KJV “the priests”, the NIV “Zadok and Abiathar—priests”, and the NWT 1984 “were priests”.

“The king’s friend.” “And Azariah son of Nathan was over the officers; and Zabud son of Nathan, a priest, was the king’s friend”: “the king’s friend”, re’eh hammelekh, stands only here; Hushai is “David’s friend” (2 Samuel 15:37; 16:16, now on these pages). The ASV has “chief minister, and the king’s friend”, the KJV “principal officer”, the NIV “a priest and adviser to the king”, and the NWT 1984 “a priest, the friend of the king”. “The officers” are the nitsavim of verse 7, and Ahishar “was over the household”; Adoniram “was over the forced labor”: the NWT 1984 has “over those conscripted for forced labor”, the NIV “in charge of forced labor”, the ASV “over the men subject to taskwork”, and the KJV “over the tribute”. David’s Adoram was “over the forced labour” (2 Samuel 20:24).

Verses 7–9 · 1 Kings 4 The twelve officers — a month each, “the son of Hur”, and four towns

A month each. “And Solomon had twelve officers over all Israel, and they sustained the king and his household: each one had a month in the year to sustain him”: the verb is kilkel, “sustain”, the one of Barzillai who “had sustained the king” at Mahanaim (2 Samuel 19:33, now on these pages), and “each one” is written echad and read ha’echad; the page prints the reading. The ASV has “who provided victuals for the king and his household: each man had to make provision for a month in the year”. The NIV has “supplied provisions for the king and the royal household”. The NWT 1984 has “they provided the king and his household with food”. The TLB has “responsible for requisitioning food from the people for the king’s household”.

The twelve. The names are twelve: the son of Hur, the son of Deker, the son of Hesed, the son of Abinadab, Baana son of Ahilud, the son of Geber, Ahinadab son of Iddo, Ahimaaz, Baana son of Hushai, Jehoshaphat son of Paruah, Shimei son of Ela, and Geber son of Uri (verses 8–19), and the twelve months are the twelve officers’ turns. The NWT 1984 has “twelve deputies”, the NIV “twelve district governors”, the Geneva “twelve officers”, and the Douay “twelve governors”.

“The son of Hur.” “And these are their names: the son of Hur, in the hill country of Ephraim”: the first five names are written ben with a hyphen, “son-of-Hur”, and the versions split on whether each is a name or a father. The ASV has “Ben-hur”, “Ben-deker” and “Ben-hesed”. The NIV has “Ben-Hur”. The Douay has “Benhur”. The KJV has “The son of Hur”, and so has the NWT 1984. This page keeps “the son of Hur”, since the Hebrew gives no other name.

Four towns. “The son of Deker, in Makaz and in Shaalbim and Beth-shemesh and Elon-beth-hanan”: “Elon-beth-hanan” stands only here, and Shaalbim is where “the Amorite was determined to dwell”, until they “became forced labour” (Judges 1:35, now on these pages). The ASV has “Ben-deker, in Makaz, and in Shaalbim, and Beth-shemesh, and Elon-beth-hanan”. The NIV has “Makaz, Shaalbim, Beth Shemesh and Elon Bethhanan”. The NWT 1984 has “Makaz and in Shaalbim and Beth-shemesh and Elon-beth-hanan”. The Douay has “Macces, and in Salebim, and in Bethsames”.

Verses 10–14 · 1 Kings 4 Hesed, Abinadab, Baana, Geber and Ahinadab — Hepher, Dor, Taanach, Argob’s “wall and bronze bar”, and Mahanaim

Hesed, and Abinadab’s Taphath. “The son of Hesed, in Arubboth; to him belonged Socoh and all the land of Hepher; the son of Abinadab, in all the heights of Dor; Taphath, the daughter of Solomon, was his wife”: Hepher is the town of “the king of Hepher” (Joshua 12:17, now on these pages). The ASV has “to him pertained Socoh, and all the land of Hepher” and “all the height of Dor (he had Taphath the daughter of Solomon to wife)”. The NIV has “Naphoth Dor”. The NWT 1984 has “all the mountain ridge of Dor”. The KJV has “all the region of Dor”. The word is nafat, and “the nafat of Dor”, written with an aleph, stands only here; Joshua 12:23 has it written with a vav, and Joshua 11:2 has nafot (Joshua 11:2, now on these pages).

Baana son of Ahilud. “In Taanach and Megiddo, and all Beth-shean, which is beside Zarethan below Jezreel, from Beth-shean to Abel-meholah, as far as beyond Jokmeam”: Taanach “by the waters of Megiddo” (Judges 5:19), Beth-shan, where Saul’s body was fastened to the wall (1 Samuel 31:10), and Zarethan, beside which the Jordan stood in a heap (Joshua 3:16, all now on these pages). The ASV has “as far as beyond Jokmeam”. The KJV has “Zartanah” and “Jokneam”. The NIV has “Beth Shan next to Zarethan below Jezreel”. The NWT 1984 has “to the region of Jokmeam”. Jehoshaphat the recorder is also “son of Ahilud” (verse 3), and the text does not say they are brothers.

Geber, and Argob. “The son of Geber, in Ramoth-gilead; to him belonged the villages of Jair son of Manasseh, which are in Gilead; to him belonged the region of Argob, which is in Bashan — sixty great cities, with wall and bronze bar”: Jair’s villages are Havvoth-jair (Numbers 32:41), and Argob’s sixty cities are the “sixty cities, all the region of Argob” with “high walls, gates, and bars” (Deuteronomy 3:4–5, both now on these pages); “bronze bar” stands only here. The ASV has “threescore great cities with walls and brazen bars”. The NIV has “bronze gate bars”. The NWT 1984 has “the tent villages of Jair” and “wall and copper bar”. The Geneva has “bars of brass”.

Mahanaim. “Ahinadab son of Iddo, in Mahanaim”: the town where Abner brought Ish-bosheth (2 Samuel 2:8) and where David stayed when he fled (17:24, both now on these pages). The ASV has “Ahinadab the son of Iddo, in Mahanaim”, the KJV “had Mahanaim”, the NWT 1984 “in Mahanaim”, and the Douay “was chief in Manaim”.

Verses 15–18 · 1 Kings 4 Ahimaaz and Basemath, Baana son of Hushai, and the tribes the list names

Ahimaaz and Basemath. “Ahimaaz, in Naphtali; he also took Basemath the daughter of Solomon as a wife”: the “also” follows Taphath (verse 11). A son of Zadok named Ahimaaz carried David’s messages (2 Samuel 15:27; 17:17, now on these pages), and the text does not say he is the same man; Basemath is also the name of Esau’s wife (Genesis 26:34) and of Ishmael’s daughter (36:3, both now on these pages). The ASV has “he also took Basemath the daughter of Solomon to wife”. The NWT 1984 has “he, too, took Basemath, Solomon’s daughter, as a wife”. The NIV has “he had married Basemath daughter of Solomon”.

Baana son of Hushai. “Baana son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth”: David’s Hushai is “the Archite” and “David’s friend” (2 Samuel 16:16, now on these pages), and the text does not say that this is his son. The ASV has “Baana the son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth”. The NWT 1984 has “Baana the son of Hushai, in Asher and Bealoth”. The NIV has “in Asher and in Aloth”. The KJV has “in Aloth”. The Douay has “in Aser and in Baloth”.

Issachar and Benjamin. “Jehoshaphat son of Paruah, in Issachar; Shimei son of Ela, in Benjamin”: the ASV has “Jehoshaphat the son of Paruah, in Issachar” and “Shimei the son of Ela, in Benjamin”. The NWT 1984 has “Shimei the son of Ela, in Benjamin”. The KJV has “Elah”. The Douay has “Semei the son of Ela”. This Shimei is not Shimei son of Gera (1 Kings 2:8, now on these pages).

The tribes named. Five tribes are named in the list of regions: Ephraim (verse 8), Naphtali, Asher, Issachar and Benjamin (verses 15–18); the rest are towns and lands, Dor, Gilead and Bashan among them. Judah first appears in verse 20, where “Judah and Israel” are many; no officer is placed in Judah by name. The pair is “Judah and Israel”, in that order.

Verses 19–20 · 1 Kings 4 “One officer in the land”, and Judah and Israel “many, like the sand” — eating, drinking and rejoicing

“And one officer.” “Geber son of Uri, in the land of Gilead, the land of Sihon king of the Amorites and of Og king of Bashan; and one officer who was in the land”: “and one officer” stands only here, and the versions split on what it means. The ASV has “and he was the only officer that was in the land”. The NIV has “He was the only governor over the district”. The NWT 1984 has “and there was one deputy [over all the other deputies] that were in the land”. The Geneva has “and was officer alone in the land”. The Douay has “over all that were in that land”. Sihon and Og are the two kings beyond the Jordan (Numbers 21:24, now on these pages).

Many, like the sand. “Judah and Israel were many, like the sand that is by the sea for multitude”: the sand is the promise to Abraham, “like the sand that is on the shore of the sea” (Genesis 22:17), and the sand of the Philistine host (1 Samuel 13:5, both now on these pages); “for multitude” echoes Solomon’s “cannot be numbered or counted for multitude” (1 Kings 3:8), and David’s count of the people was 800,000 and 500,000 (2 Samuel 24:9, now on these pages). The ASV has “Judah and Israel were many as the sand which is by the sea in multitude”. The NWT 1984 has “like the grains of sand that are by the sea for multitude”. The NIV has “as numerous as the sand on the seashore”. The Douay has “innumerable”.

Eating and drinking and rejoicing. “Eating and drinking and rejoicing” stands only here; the Amalekites are “eating and drinking and celebrating” (1 Samuel 30:16, now on these pages), with a different third word. The NWT 1984 has “eating and drinking and rejoicing”. The ASV has “eating and drinking and making merry”. The KJV has “eating and drinking, and making merry”. The NIV has “they ate, they drank and they were happy”. The TLB has “a wealthy, populous, contented nation”. The chapter ends here in the Hebrew; English chapter 4 goes on for fourteen more verses, which the Hebrew numbers as the first fourteen of chapter 5.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “officials”; “the recorder”, as in 2 Samuel 8:16; “over the army”; “the king’s friend”; “forced labor”, as in 2 Samuel 20:24; “officers” for nitsavim; “sustained” for kilkel, as in 2 Samuel 19:33; “the son of Hur”, kept as a father and not a name; “to him belonged”; “the heights of Dor”; “wall and bronze bar”; “one officer”; and “eating and drinking and rejoicing”; with the formulas that differ in the notes.

Echoes paid. David’s recorder, scribes and forced labor (2 Samuel 8:16–17; 20:24–25); Benaiah in Joab’s place and Abiathar driven out (1 Kings 2:26–27, 35); Hushai, David’s friend (2 Samuel 15:37; 16:16); Barzillai who sustained the king (2 Samuel 19:33); the Amorites of Shaalbim made forced labor (Judges 1:35); Hepher’s king (Joshua 12:17); Taanach, Beth-shan and Zarethan (Judges 5:19; 1 Samuel 31:10; Joshua 3:16); Jair’s villages and Argob’s sixty cities (Numbers 32:41; Deuteronomy 3:4–5); Mahanaim, Ish-bosheth’s and David’s (2 Samuel 2:8; 17:24); Ahimaaz son of Zadok (2 Samuel 15:27; 17:17); Basemath of Esau and Ishmael (Genesis 26:34; 36:3); Sihon and Og (Numbers 21:24); the sand of Abraham’s promise (Genesis 22:17); the count that “cannot be numbered” (1 Kings 3:8; 2 Samuel 24:9); and the eating and drinking of the Amalekites (1 Samuel 30:16).

Echoes planted. Solomon’s rule “from the River unto the land of the Philistines”, his daily provision, his forty thousand stalls of horses, and his wisdom “even as the sand that is on the sea-shore” (1 Kings 5:1–9); his three thousand proverbs (5:12); and the levy of thirty thousand with Adoniram over it (5:27–28). All of these stand, in the Hebrew numbering, in the next chapter, which is yet to come in this translation.
