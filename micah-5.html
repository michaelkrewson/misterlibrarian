Bethlehem Ephrathah, little among the thousands of Judah: from you one will come out to Jehovah to be ruler in Israel, whose goings forth are from of old; he gives them up until she who bears has borne; the one who stands and shepherds in the strength of Jehovah, and this one will be peace when Assyria comes, with seven shepherds and eight princes; the remnant of Jacob like dew from Jehovah and like a lion among the nations; and Jehovah cuts off horses, chariots, cities, sorceries, carved images, pillars and Asherim, and takes vengeance on the nations that have not listened.
Eighth century BC, with the Assyrian threat of verses 4–5 as the horizon; the book dates Micah to the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah. ⚠ The chapter is numbered five in the Hebrew and in this page from Bethlehem on; the English Bibles number the same verse 5:2.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Micah 5:1–2 “And you, Bethlehem Ephrathah” — one comes out to me, whose goings forth are from of old

⚠ Numbering. This page follows the Hebrew numbering, in which this chapter begins at what the English Bibles on the shelf call 5:2, Bethlehem. Their 5:1, the siege and the rod on the cheek of the judge of Israel, is 4:14 in the Hebrew, and every verse here is one lower than in the English: the Bethlehem verse is 5:1 here and 5:2 in the English, and the last verse is 14 here and 15 there. Verse numbers on this page are the Hebrew ones.

⭐ Bethlehem Ephrathah. “Bethlehem Ephrathah” stands in this one verse, with the old name beside the new (efrat): Rachel is buried “on the way to Ephrath — that is, Bethlehem” (Genesis 35:19, now on these pages, and 48:7); the blessing of Ruth is “do valiantly in Ephrathah and make a name in Bethlehem” (Ruth 4:11, now on these pages); and David is “the son of this Ephrathite of Bethlehem in Judah” (1 Samuel 17:12, now on these pages). “Little to be” stands in this one verse, and “the thousands of Judah” stands in four verses: 1 Samuel 23:23 (now on these pages), here, and Zechariah 12:5 and 12:6; elef is a thousand, and a clan that was counted by the thousand. The KJV has “though thou be little among the thousands of Judah”, the ASV “which art little to be among the thousands of Judah”, the NIV “though you are small among the clans of Judah”, the NWT 1984 “the one too little to get to be among the thousands of Judah”, the Douay “art a little one among the thousands of Juda” and the TLB “you are but a small Judean village”. Matthew 2:6 prints the same line as “by no means least among the rulers of Judah” (now on these pages), with “least” for the Hebrew “little”.

⭐ From you, to me. “From you to me one will come out” stands in this one verse: the one who comes out is for Jehovah, li, “to me”. And “ruler in Israel” stands in two verses, this one and 2 Chronicles 7:18, the promise to Solomon, “There shall not be cut off for you a man ruling in Israel” (now on these pages). The word is moshel, a ruler, and the Hebrew has no word for king. The KJV has “out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel”, the NIV “out of you will come for me one who will be ruler over Israel”, the NWT 1984 “from you there will come out to me the one who is to become ruler in Israel”, the Douay “out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be the ruler in Israel” and the TLB “you will be the birthplace of my King who is alive from everlasting ages past”, with a king the Hebrew does not have. Matthew’s next clause, “a ruler who will shepherd my people Israel”, is not Micah’s: “you will shepherd my people Israel” stands in two verses, 2 Samuel 5:2 (now on these pages) and 1 Chronicles 11:2. John 7:42 asks about Bethlehem, “the village where David was” (now on these pages).

⭐ His goings forth. “And his goings forth” stands in this one verse (motsaah), the noun of the verb that opens the line: yetse, “will come out”, and motsa’otav, “his goings out”, are one root. “From of old, from the days of olam” stands in this one verse (olam), the word that this translation also leaves as it is at Ecclesiastes 3:11. The KJV has “whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting”, the ASV “from of old, from everlasting”, the NIV “whose origins are from of old, from ancient times”, the NWT 1984 “whose origin is from early times, from the days of time indefinite”, the Douay “his going forth is from the beginning, from the days of eternity”, the Geneva “from the beginning and from everlasting” and the TLB “alive from everlasting ages past”. Whether it is an origin, an ancestry going back to the days of David, or a person who has been going out since the days of olam, the Hebrew does not say.

⭐ Until she who bears has borne. “The time that she who bears has borne” stands in this one verse; the Hebrew has no son in it, and the NIV has “until the time when she who is in labor bears a son” and the TLB “until she who is to give birth has her son”, where the KJV has “until the time that she which travaileth hath brought forth” and the NWT 1984 “until the time that she who is giving birth actually gives birth”. Isaiah 7:14 has the same kind of figure with the participle, a young woman bearing (now on these pages). Who “them” is, whom he gives up, the verse does not say; the NIV has “Israel will be abandoned” and the TLB “God will abandon his people to their enemies”. And “the rest of his brothers” stands in this one verse, who return; the KJV has “the remnant of his brethren shall return unto the children of Israel” and the Douay “the remnant of his brethren shall be converted to the children of Israel”, with shuv read as converting.

Verses 3–5 · Micah 5:3–5 He will stand and shepherd — this one will be peace — seven shepherds and eight princes

⭐ He will stand, and shepherd. “and shepherd in the strength of Jehovah” stands in this one verse (ra’ah), and “in the majesty of the name of Jehovah” stands in this one verse; the ruler has Jehovah for his God. “To the ends of the earth” stands in three verses: here, Zechariah 9:10 and Psalm 72:8. The KJV has “he shall stand and feed in the strength of the Lord, in the majesty of the name of the Lord his God”, the NIV “He will stand and shepherd his flock in the strength of the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “in the superiority of the name of Jehovah his God” and the Douay “in the height of the name of the Lord his God”. The next word is veyashavu, and its consonants can be read “they will dwell” or “they will return”: the KJV has “they shall abide”, the NIV “they will live securely”, the NWT 1984 “they will certainly keep dwelling” and the Douay “they shall be converted”.

⚠ “This one will be peace.” “And this one will be peace” stands in this one verse (shalom). The Hebrew has zeh, “this”, and shalom; it does not have “man” and it does not have “our”. It can be “this one” or “this”, the state of things described next. The KJV has “And this man shall be the peace”, the ASV “And this man shall be our peace”, the NIV “And he will be our peace”, the NWT 1984 “And this one must become peace”, the Geneva “And he shall be our peace” and the TLB “He will be our Peace”. Ephesians 2:14 says it of Jesus, “he himself is our peace” (now on these pages). The time is “when he comes into our land”, which stands in two verses, this one and the next, and the Assyrian treads on our palaces here and on our border there.

⭐ Seven shepherds, and eight. “Seven shepherds” stands in this one verse, and “princes of men” stands in this one verse (nasikh). The form is a number and the next one, as in “for three transgressions and for four” (Amos 1:3, now on these pages). The KJV has “seven shepherds, and eight principal men”, the NIV “seven shepherds, even eight commanders”, the NWT 1984 “seven shepherds, yes, eight dukes of mankind”, the Douay “seven shepherds, and eight principal men” and the TLB “he will appoint seven shepherds to watch over us, eight princes to lead us”. The palaces are armenot: the KJV has “palaces”, the NIV “fortresses”, the NWT 1984 “dwelling towers” and the Douay “houses”.

⚠ They will shepherd the land of Assyria. “And they will shepherd the land of Assyria” stands in this one verse, with the verb of verse 3, ra’ah, shepherd; and “the land of Nimrod” stands in this one verse, the Nimrod of Genesis 10:8 from whose land “he went out to Assyria” (Genesis 10:11, now on these pages). The shelf is divided on the verb: the KJV has “waste the land of Assyria with the sword”, the NIV “who will rule the land of Assyria with the sword”, the NWT 1984 “they will actually shepherd the land of Assyria with the sword”, the Douay “they shall feed the land of Assyria with the sword”, the Geneva “destroy Assyria with the sword” and the TLB “They will rule Assyria with drawn swords”. The second noun, bifticheha, is “at its entrances” in the KJV (“in the entrances thereof”) and the NWT 1984 (“in its entrances”), while the NIV has “drawn sword” and the Douay “the spears thereof”. The line “and he will deliver from Assyria” stands in this one verse.

Verses 6–8 · Micah 5:6–8 The remnant of Jacob — like dew, and like a lion

⭐ Like dew. “The remnant of Jacob” stands in two verses, this one and the next (she’erit), and “in the midst of many peoples” stands in the same two; both verses begin vehayah she’erit Ya’aqov. “Like dew from Jehovah” stands in this one verse, and “like showers on the grass” stands in two verses, here and Deuteronomy 32:2, where the teaching falls like it (now on these pages; revivim). The dew is that which “does not wait for man”, which stands in this one verse: it comes from Jehovah and not from anyone’s effort. The KJV has “as a dew from the Lord, as the showers upon the grass, that tarrieth not for man, nor waiteth for the sons of men”, the NIV “like dew from the Lord, like showers on the grass, which do not wait for anyone or depend on man”, the NWT 1984 “that does not hope for man or wait for the sons of earthling man”, the Douay “as drops upon the grass, which waiteth not for man, nor tarrieth for the children of men” and the TLB “refresh the world like a gentle dew or the welcome showers of rain”, where the dew refreshes the world and the remnant is the one who does it.

⭐ Like a lion. “like a lion among the beasts of the forest” stands in this one verse, “like a young lion among the flocks of sheep” stands in this one verse, and “tramples and tears, and there is none to deliver” stands in this one verse. The same verb is in verse 5: there vehitsil, “he will deliver”, from Assyria, and here ein matsil, “there is none to deliver”, from the lion. The same remnant is dew and lion, in the same words, in the midst of the same peoples. The KJV has “among the Gentiles” and “both treadeth down, and teareth in pieces, and none can deliver”, the ASV “among the nations”, the NIV “which mauls and mangles as it goes”, the NWT 1984 “and there is no deliverer”, the Douay “there is none to deliver” and the TLB “The nations will be like helpless sheep before her!”

⭐ Your hand will be lifted up. “Your hand will be lifted up over your adversaries” stands in this one verse; the address turns to “you”, singular, and the verb can be a statement or a wish. The KJV has “Thine hand shall be lifted up upon thine adversaries”, the ASV “Let thy hand be lifted up above thine adversaries”, the NIV “Your hand will be lifted up in triumph over your enemies”, the NWT 1984 “Your hand will be high above your adversaries”, the Douay “Thy hand shall be lifted up over thy enemies” and the TLB “She will stand up to her foes”. “Will be cut off” is the first of five forms of karat in verses 8–12.

Verses 9–14 · Micah 5:9–14 I will cut off your horses, your sorceries, your pillars — and vengeance on the nations that have not listened

⭐ I will cut off. The verb is hikhrati, “I will cut off”, four times, at verses 9, 10, 11 and 12, after yikkaretu, “will be cut off”, at verse 8. “And I will cut off your horses” stands in this one verse, and “and I will destroy your chariots” stands in this one verse. The king in Deuteronomy was not to multiply horses (Deuteronomy 17:16, now on these pages). Jehovah speaks here in the first person, after “says Jehovah” at verse 9; the Name is written four times in the chapter, at verse 3 (twice), verse 6 and verse 9. The KJV has “I will cut off thy horses out of the midst of thee, and I will destroy thy chariots”, the NIV “I will destroy your horses from among you and demolish your chariots”, the NWT 1984 “I will cut off your horses from the midst of you and destroy your chariots”, the Douay “I will take away thy horses out of the midst of thee” and the TLB “I will destroy all the weapons you depend on”.

⭐ Cities, strongholds, sorceries. The cities are cut off and the strongholds torn down, and then the things of the hand: “sorceries”, the consonants of which stand in five verses, among them Exodus 7:11, of Pharaoh’s sorcerers (now on these pages); “and soothsayers” stands in this one verse (onen, kashaph). The KJV has “witchcrafts out of thine hand; and thou shalt have no more soothsayers”, the NIV “I will destroy your witchcraft and you will no longer cast spells”, the NWT 1984 “no practicers of magic will you continue to have”, the Geneva “I will cut off thine enchanters out of thine hand” and the TLB “there will be no more fortune-tellers to consult”.

⭐ Images, pillars, Asherim. “Your carved images and your pillars” stands in this one verse (pesel), a pair that Leviticus 26:1 forbids in its own spelling (now on these pages), and “and you will no more bow down to the work of your hands” stands in this one verse. “Your Asherim” stands in this one verse (asherah; Deuteronomy 16:21, now on these pages). The KJV has “graven images” and “standing images” and, at verse 13, “groves”; the Geneva has “groves” and the Douay “groves” too, where the ASV has “Asherim”, the NIV “Asherah poles”, the NWT 1984 “sacred poles” and the TLB “heathen shrines”. For the images, the NIV has “idols and your sacred stones”, the NWT 1984 “graven images and your pillars” and the Douay “graven things, and thy statues”.

⭐ Vengeance on the nations that have not listened. “And I will execute in anger and in wrath” stands in this one verse, and the last clause is asher lo shame’u, “that have not listened”. The KJV has “upon the heathen, such as they have not heard”, the ASV “upon the nations which hearkened not”, the NIV “on the nations that have not obeyed me”, the NWT 1984 “that have not obeyed”, the Douay “that have not given ear” and the TLB “the nations who refuse to obey me”. The root is the one of the next chapter’s first word, shim’u, “Hear, please” (Micah 6:1, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “Bethlehem Ephrathah” with both names; “ruler” for moshel, with no king; “goings forth” for motsa’ot; “olam” left as it is; “shepherd” for ra’ah at verses 3 and 5 alike; “this one will be peace” with no “man” and no “our”; “the remnant of Jacob” for the same two words at verses 6 and 7; “deliver” for the one root at verses 5 and 7; “cut off” for karat; “Asherim” for asherim; “Jehovah” for the Name, written four times: verse 3 (twice), verse 6 and verse 9.

Echoes paid. Matthew 2:6; John 7:42; Genesis 35:19, 48:7, 10:8 and 10:11; Ruth 4:11; 1 Samuel 17:12 and 23:23; 2 Samuel 5:2; 2 Chronicles 7:18; Isaiah 7:14; Ephesians 2:14; Amos 1:3; Deuteronomy 32:2, 17:16 and 16:21; Exodus 7:11; Leviticus 26:1; Micah 6:1.

Echoes planted. Zechariah 9:10, 12:5 and 12:6; Psalm 72:8; 1 Chronicles 11:2. None of these is translated here yet.
