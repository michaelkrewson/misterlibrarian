Kish of Benjamin loses his she-donkeys and sends his son Saul, a young man taller than all the people, to look for them with a servant. After three days in the land of Zuph the servant proposes asking the man of God in the town, the seer. Jehovah has told Samuel the day before that he will send a man from Benjamin to be anointed leader over Israel. Samuel meets Saul at the gate, tells him the donkeys are found, seats him at the head of thirty guests at the high place, sets the kept portion before him, and at dawn takes him to the edge of the town to give him the word of God.
The day before and the day of Saul's arrival, at the end of Samuel's judgeship (1 Samuel 9:15, 26).

1
וַיְהִי-אִישׁ מבן ימין מִבִּנְיָמִין, וּשְׁמוֹ קִישׁ בֶּן-אֲבִיאֵל בֶּן-צְרוֹר בֶּן-בְּכוֹרַת בֶּן-אֲפִיחַ--בֶּן-אִישׁ יְמִינִי:  גִּבּוֹר, חָיִל.
Now there was a man of Benjamin, whose name was Kish son of Abiel, son of Zeror, son of Becorath, son of Aphiah, son of a Benjamite — a mighty man of valour.note
Y había un hombre de Benjamín, cuyo nombre era Cis hijo de Abiel, hijo de Zeror, hijo de Becorat, hijo de Afía, hijo de un benjamita: un hombre valiente y poderoso.
2
וְלוֹ-הָיָה בֵן וּשְׁמוֹ שָׁאוּל, בָּחוּר וָטוֹב, וְאֵין אִישׁ מִבְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, טוֹב מִמֶּנּוּ; מִשִּׁכְמוֹ וָמַעְלָה, גָּבֹהַּ מִכָּל-הָעָם.
And he had a son whose name was Saul, a young man, and good; there was no man among the sons of Israel better than he: from his shoulders upward he was taller than all the people.note
Y tenía un hijo cuyo nombre era Saúl, joven y bueno; no había entre los hijos de Israel hombre mejor que él: de los hombros arriba era más alto que todo el pueblo.
3
וַתֹּאבַדְנָה, הָאֲתֹנוֹת, לְקִישׁ, אֲבִי שָׁאוּל; וַיֹּאמֶר קִישׁ אֶל-שָׁאוּל בְּנוֹ, קַח-נָא אִתְּךָ אֶת-אַחַד מֵהַנְּעָרִים, וְקוּם לֵךְ, בַּקֵּשׁ אֶת-הָאֲתֹנֹת.
And the she-donkeys of Kish, Saul’s father, were lost. And Kish said to Saul his son: ‘Take now with you one of the servants, and arise, go, look for the she-donkeys.’note
Y se perdieron las asnas de Cis, padre de Saúl. Y Cis dijo a Saúl su hijo: «Toma ahora contigo a uno de los criados, y levántate, ve, busca las asnas».
4
וַיַּעֲבֹר בְּהַר-אֶפְרַיִם וַיַּעֲבֹר בְּאֶרֶץ-שָׁלִשָׁה, וְלֹא מָצָאוּ; וַיַּעַבְרוּ בְאֶרֶץ-שַׁעֲלִים וָאַיִן, וַיַּעֲבֹר בְּאֶרֶץ-יְמִינִי וְלֹא מָצָאוּ.
And he passed through the hill country of Ephraim, and passed through the land of Shalishah, but they did not find them; and they passed through the land of Shaalim, and they were not there; and he passed through the land of the Benjamites, but they did not find them.note
Y pasó por la región montañosa de Efraín, y pasó por la tierra de Salisá, y no las hallaron; y pasaron por la tierra de Saalim, y no estaban; y pasó por la tierra de los benjamitas, y no las hallaron.
5
הֵמָּה, בָּאוּ בְּאֶרֶץ צוּף, וְשָׁאוּל אָמַר לְנַעֲרוֹ אֲשֶׁר-עִמּוֹ, לְכָה וְנָשׁוּבָה--פֶּן-יֶחְדַּל אָבִי מִן-הָאֲתֹנוֹת, וְדָאַג לָנוּ.
When they came to the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant who was with him: ‘Come, let us go back, or my father will leave off the she-donkeys and be anxious about us.’note
Cuando llegaron a la tierra de Zuf, Saúl dijo a su criado que estaba con él: «Ven, volvámonos, no sea que mi padre deje las asnas y se inquiete por nosotros».
6
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, הִנֵּה-נָא אִישׁ-אֱלֹהִים בָּעִיר הַזֹּאת, וְהָאִישׁ נִכְבָּד, כֹּל אֲשֶׁר-יְדַבֵּר בּוֹא יָבוֹא; עַתָּה, נֵלְכָה שָּׁם--אוּלַי יַגִּיד לָנוּ, אֶת-דַּרְכֵּנוּ אֲשֶׁר-הָלַכְנוּ עָלֶיהָ.
But he said to him: ‘Look now — there is a man of God in this city, and the man is held in honour; all that he says surely comes about. Now let us go there; perhaps he will tell us about the way on which we have set out.’note
Pero él le dijo: «Mira, hay en esta ciudad un hombre de Dios, y el hombre es honrado; todo lo que dice ciertamente sucede. Vamos allá ahora; quizá nos diga algo del camino por el que hemos salido».
7
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל לְנַעֲרוֹ, וְהִנֵּה נֵלֵךְ וּמַה-נָּבִיא לָאִישׁ--כִּי הַלֶּחֶם אָזַל מִכֵּלֵינוּ, וּתְשׁוּרָה אֵין-לְהָבִיא לְאִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים:  מָה, אִתָּנוּ.
And Saul said to his servant: ‘But look — if we go, what shall we bring the man? For the bread is gone from our bags, and there is no present to bring to the man of God. What do we have?’note
Y Saúl dijo a su criado: «Pero mira, si vamos, ¿qué le llevaremos al hombre? Porque el pan se ha acabado en nuestras alforjas, y no hay presente que llevar al hombre de Dios. ¿Qué tenemos?».
8
וַיֹּסֶף הַנַּעַר, לַעֲנוֹת אֶת-שָׁאוּל, וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה נִמְצָא בְיָדִי רֶבַע שֶׁקֶל כָּסֶף; וְנָתַתִּי לְאִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים, וְהִגִּיד לָנוּ אֶת-דַּרְכֵּנוּ.
And the servant answered Saul again and said: ‘Look, there is found in my hand a quarter of a shekel of silver; I will give it to the man of God, and he will tell us our way.’note
Y el criado volvió a responder a Saúl y dijo: «Mira, se halla en mi mano un cuarto de siclo de plata; se lo daré al hombre de Dios, y él nos indicará nuestro camino».
9
לְפָנִים בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, כֹּה-אָמַר הָאִישׁ בְּלֶכְתּוֹ לִדְרוֹשׁ אֱלֹהִים, לְכוּ וְנֵלְכָה, עַד-הָרֹאֶה:  כִּי לַנָּבִיא הַיּוֹם, יִקָּרֵא לְפָנִים הָרֹאֶה.
(Formerly in Israel, when a man went to inquire of God, this is what he said: ‘Come, let us go to the seer’; for the one called a prophet today was formerly called a seer.)note
(Antes en Israel, cuando un hombre iba a consultar a Dios, decía así: «Vengan y vamos al vidente»; porque al que hoy se llama profeta antes se le llamaba vidente.)
10
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל לְנַעֲרוֹ טוֹב דְּבָרְךָ, לְכָה נֵלֵכָה; וַיֵּלְכוּ, אֶל-הָעִיר, אֲשֶׁר-שָׁם, אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים.
And Saul said to his servant: ‘Your word is good; come, let us go.’ And they went to the city where the man of God was.note
Y Saúl dijo a su criado: «Bien dices; ven, vamos». Y fueron a la ciudad donde estaba el hombre de Dios.
11
הֵמָּה, עֹלִים בְּמַעֲלֵה הָעִיר, וְהֵמָּה מָצְאוּ נְעָרוֹת, יֹצְאוֹת לִשְׁאֹב מָיִם; וַיֹּאמְרוּ לָהֶן, הֲיֵשׁ בָּזֶה הָרֹאֶה.
As they were going up the ascent to the city, they found girls going out to draw water, and they said to them: ‘Is the seer here?’note
Mientras subían por la cuesta de la ciudad, hallaron unas muchachas que salían a sacar agua, y les dijeron: «¿Está aquí el vidente?».
12
וַתַּעֲנֶינָה אוֹתָם וַתֹּאמַרְנָה יֵּשׁ, הִנֵּה לְפָנֶיךָ; מַהֵר עַתָּה, כִּי הַיּוֹם בָּא לָעִיר--כִּי זֶבַח הַיּוֹם לָעָם, בַּבָּמָה.
And they answered them and said: ‘He is; look — he is ahead of you. Hurry now, for today he has come to the city, because the people have a sacrifice today on the high place.note
Y ellas les respondieron y dijeron: «Sí está; mira, está delante de ti. Date prisa ahora, porque hoy ha venido a la ciudad, porque el pueblo tiene hoy un sacrificio en el lugar alto.
13
כְּבֹאֲכֶם הָעִיר כֵּן תִּמְצְאוּן אֹתוֹ בְּטֶרֶם יַעֲלֶה הַבָּמָתָה לֶאֱכֹל, כִּי לֹא-יֹאכַל הָעָם עַד-בֹּאוֹ--כִּי-הוּא יְבָרֵךְ הַזֶּבַח, אַחֲרֵי-כֵן יֹאכְלוּ הַקְּרֻאִים; וְעַתָּה עֲלוּ, כִּי-אֹתוֹ כְהַיּוֹם תִּמְצְאוּן אֹתוֹ.
As soon as you come into the city you will find him, before he goes up to the high place to eat; for the people will not eat until he comes, because he blesses the sacrifice; after that the invited eat. Now go up, for about this time you will find him.’note
En cuanto entren en la ciudad lo hallarán, antes de que suba al lugar alto a comer; porque el pueblo no comerá hasta que él llegue, pues él bendice el sacrificio; después comen los convidados. Suban, pues, ahora, porque a esta hora lo hallarán».
14
וַיַּעֲלוּ, הָעִיר; הֵמָּה, בָּאִים בְּתוֹךְ הָעִיר, וְהִנֵּה שְׁמוּאֵל יֹצֵא לִקְרָאתָם, לַעֲלוֹת הַבָּמָה.  {ס}
So they went up to the city. As they were coming into the middle of the city, look — Samuel was coming out toward them, to go up to the high place.note
Y subieron a la ciudad. Cuando entraban en medio de la ciudad, mira, Samuel salía hacia ellos para subir al lugar alto.
15
וַיהוָה, גָּלָה אֶת-אֹזֶן שְׁמוּאֵל, יוֹם אֶחָד, לִפְנֵי בוֹא-שָׁאוּל לֵאמֹר.
Now Jehovah had uncovered Samuel’s ear one day before Saul came, saying:note
Y Jehová había descubierto el oído de Samuel un día antes de que llegara Saúl, diciendo:
16
כָּעֵת מָחָר אֶשְׁלַח אֵלֶיךָ אִישׁ מֵאֶרֶץ בִּנְיָמִן, וּמְשַׁחְתּוֹ לְנָגִיד עַל-עַמִּי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְהוֹשִׁיעַ אֶת-עַמִּי, מִיַּד פְּלִשְׁתִּים:  כִּי רָאִיתִי אֶת-עַמִּי, כִּי בָּאָה צַעֲקָתוֹ אֵלָי.
‘About this time tomorrow I will send you a man from the land of Benjamin, and you shall anoint him as leader over my people Israel, and he will save my people from the hand of the Philistines; for I have seen my people, for their outcry has come to me.’note
«Mañana a esta hora te enviaré un hombre de la tierra de Benjamín, y lo ungirás como caudillo sobre mi pueblo Israel, y él salvará a mi pueblo de la mano de los filisteos; porque he visto a mi pueblo, porque su clamor ha llegado hasta mí».
17
וּשְׁמוּאֵל, רָאָה אֶת-שָׁאוּל; וַיהוָה עָנָהוּ--הִנֵּה הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר אָמַרְתִּי אֵלֶיךָ, זֶה יַעְצֹר בְּעַמִּי.
And Samuel saw Saul, and Jehovah answered him: ‘Here is the man of whom I said to you, This one will restrain my people.’note
Y Samuel vio a Saúl, y Jehová le respondió: «Aquí está el hombre de quien te dije: Este refrenará a mi pueblo».
18
וַיִּגַּשׁ שָׁאוּל אֶת-שְׁמוּאֵל, בְּתוֹךְ הַשָּׁעַר; וַיֹּאמֶר הַגִּידָה-נָּא לִי, אֵי-זֶה בֵּית הָרֹאֶה.
And Saul approached Samuel in the middle of the gate and said: ‘Tell me, please, where is the house of the seer?’note
Y Saúl se acercó a Samuel en medio de la puerta, y dijo: «Dime, por favor, ¿dónde está la casa del vidente?».
19
וַיַּעַן שְׁמוּאֵל אֶת-שָׁאוּל, וַיֹּאמֶר אָנֹכִי הָרֹאֶה--עֲלֵה לְפָנַי הַבָּמָה, וַאֲכַלְתֶּם עִמִּי הַיּוֹם; וְשִׁלַּחְתִּיךָ בַבֹּקֶר, וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר בִּלְבָבְךָ אַגִּיד לָךְ.
And Samuel answered Saul and said: ‘I am the seer. Go up before me to the high place, and you shall eat with me today; and in the morning I will send you away, and all that is in your heart I will tell you.note
Y Samuel respondió a Saúl y dijo: «Yo soy el vidente. Sube delante de mí al lugar alto, y comerán hoy conmigo; y por la mañana te despediré, y todo lo que hay en tu corazón te lo diré.
20
וְלָאֲתֹנוֹת הָאֹבְדוֹת לְךָ, הַיּוֹם שְׁלֹשֶׁת הַיָּמִים--אַל-תָּשֶׂם אֶת-לִבְּךָ לָהֶם, כִּי נִמְצָאוּ; וּלְמִי, כָּל-חֶמְדַּת יִשְׂרָאֵל--הֲלוֹא לְךָ, וּלְכֹל בֵּית אָבִיךָ.  {ס}
And as for the she-donkeys that were lost to you three days ago, do not set your heart on them, for they have been found. And for whom is all the desire of Israel? Is it not for you, and for all your father’s house?’note
Y en cuanto a las asnas que se te perdieron hace hoy tres días, no pongas tu corazón en ellas, porque se han hallado. ¿Y para quién es todo el deseo de Israel? ¿No es para ti y para toda la casa de tu padre?».
21
וַיַּעַן שָׁאוּל וַיֹּאמֶר, הֲלוֹא בֶן-יְמִינִי אָנֹכִי מִקְּטַנֵּי שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וּמִשְׁפַּחְתִּי הַצְּעִרָה, מִכָּל-מִשְׁפְּחוֹת שִׁבְטֵי בִנְיָמִן; וְלָמָּה דִּבַּרְתָּ אֵלַי, כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה.  {ס}
And Saul answered and said: ‘Am I not a Benjamite, from the smallest of the tribes of Israel, and my clan the least of all the clans of the tribe of Benjamin? Why then have you spoken to me in this way?’note
Y Saúl respondió y dijo: «¿No soy yo benjamita, de la más pequeña de las tribus de Israel, y mi clan el menor de todos los clanes de la tribu de Benjamín? ¿Por qué, pues, me has hablado de esta manera?».
22
וַיִּקַּח שְׁמוּאֵל אֶת-שָׁאוּל וְאֶת-נַעֲרוֹ, וַיְבִיאֵם לִשְׁכָּתָה; וַיִּתֵּן לָהֶם מָקוֹם בְּרֹאשׁ הַקְּרוּאִים, וְהֵמָּה כִּשְׁלֹשִׁם אִישׁ.
And Samuel took Saul and his servant and brought them into the hall, and gave them a place at the head of the invited; and they were about thirty men.note
Y Samuel tomó a Saúl y a su criado, y los llevó a la sala, y les dio lugar a la cabecera de los convidados, que eran unos treinta hombres.
23
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל, לַטַּבָּח, תְּנָה אֶת-הַמָּנָה, אֲשֶׁר נָתַתִּי לָךְ--אֲשֶׁר אָמַרְתִּי אֵלֶיךָ, שִׂים אֹתָהּ עִמָּךְ.
And Samuel said to the cook: ‘Bring the portion that I gave you, of which I said to you, Set it aside by you.’note
Y Samuel dijo al cocinero: «Trae la porción que te di, de la cual te dije: Guárdala contigo».
24
וַיָּרֶם הַטַּבָּח אֶת-הַשּׁוֹק וְהֶעָלֶיהָ וַיָּשֶׂם לִפְנֵי שָׁאוּל, וַיֹּאמֶר הִנֵּה הַנִּשְׁאָר שִׂים-לְפָנֶיךָ אֱכֹל--כִּי לַמּוֹעֵד שָׁמוּר-לְךָ לֵאמֹר, הָעָם קָרָאתִי; וַיֹּאכַל שָׁאוּל עִם-שְׁמוּאֵל, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא.
And the cook lifted up the thigh and what was on it, and set it before Saul. And he said: ‘Look — what was kept back! Set it before you and eat; for it has been kept for you for the appointed time, since I said, I have invited the people.’ And Saul ate with Samuel that day.note
Y el cocinero alzó el muslo y lo que había sobre él, y lo puso delante de Saúl. Y dijo: «¡Mira lo que se reservó! Ponlo delante de ti y come, porque para el tiempo señalado se te guardó, desde que dije: He convidado al pueblo». Y Saúl comió con Samuel aquel día.
25
וַיֵּרְדוּ מֵהַבָּמָה, הָעִיר; וַיְדַבֵּר עִם-שָׁאוּל, עַל-הַגָּג.
And they came down from the high place to the city, and he spoke with Saul on the roof.note
Y bajaron del lugar alto a la ciudad, y él habló con Saúl en la azotea.
26

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · Kish and his son Saul — “a mighty man of valour”, “a young man, and good”, taller from the shoulders up, and the lost she-donkeys

A man of Benjamin. “Now there was a man of Benjamin”: the book opened Elkanah’s story the same way, “Now there was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim” (1 Samuel 1:1, now on these pages), and a second story begins. The written text has “from Ben-yamin” as two words, the margin “from Benjamin” as one. Kish gets five generations and the title “a mighty man of valour”, gibbor chayil, the words used of Boaz (Ruth 2:1, now on these pages). The ASV has “a mighty man of valor”; the NWT 1984 “a man mighty in wealth”; the NIV “a man of standing”. Josephus calls Kish “a man of good family and good character” (Antiquities 6.45, numbered 6.4.1 in Whiston).

“A young man, and good.” Saul is bachur va-tov, the two words of the last chapter’s warning that the king would take “your best young men”, bachureikhem ha-tovim (1 Samuel 8:16, now on these pages): the king himself is one of them. Tov covers good and good-looking, and “there was no man among the sons of Israel better than he” keeps both. His name, Sha’ul, is the verb of the people “asking” for a king (8:10; sha’al). “From his shoulders upward he was taller than all the people.” The KJV has “a choice young man, and a goodly”; the NWT 1984 “young and handsome”; the ASV “from his shoulders and upward he was higher than any of the people”.

The she-donkeys. Kish’s atonot are lost, and Saul and a servant go looking. Josephus says Kish delighted in them more than in any of his other possessions (Antiquities 6.46). The search runs through the hill country of Ephraim, Shalishah and Shaalim, then “the land of the Benjamites”, written erets yemini, the same short form as the “Benjamite” of verse 1; the Greek gives other names for the stages. It ends in “the land of Zuph”, the name of Samuel’s ancestor in 1 Samuel 1:1, so the unnamed town ahead lies in the prophet’s family country. “My father will leave off the she-donkeys and be anxious about us.” The ASV has “lest my father leave off caring for the asses, and be anxious for us”; the NIV “stop thinking about the donkeys and start worrying about us”.

Verses 6–14 · The man of God and “the seer” — a quarter shekel of silver, the narrator’s aside on prophet and seer, girls at the water, and the high place

“All that he says surely comes about.” The servant knows “a man of God in this city”, the title of the nameless prophet who came to Eli (1 Samuel 2:27) and of Moses (Deuteronomy 33:1, both now on these pages; ish ha-Elohim). “The man is held in honour”, nikhbad, from kaved, “heavy”, and “all that he says surely comes about”: the opposite of the false prophet whose word “does not happen and does not come about” (Deuteronomy 18:22), and the man of whom it was said that Jehovah “let none of his words fall to the ground” (1 Samuel 3:19, both now on these pages). The ASV has “all that he saith cometh surely to pass”; the NWT 1984 “All that he says comes true without fail”.

A present, a quarter shekel. “There is no present to bring to the man of God”: the word teshurah stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and its sense comes from this verse alone. The servant has “a quarter of a shekel of silver”. Josephus says the two did not know that the prophet took no fee (Antiquities 6.48). The Geneva has “the fourth part of a shekel of silver”; the TLB “I have a dollar!”

Prophet and seer. Verse 9 is an aside to a later reader: “for the one called a prophet today was formerly called a seer.” It stands two verses before the girls are asked “Is the seer here?”, so that the old word will not puzzle: the aside is written for readers who no longer used it. Seer is ro’eh, “one who sees”, from ra’ah; it is spelled with a different first letter from the ro‘eh who is a shepherd. Prophet is navi, and to “inquire of God” is darash. The ASV has “for he that is now called a Prophet was beforetime called a Seer”; the NWT 1984 “For the prophet of today used to be called a seer in former times”.

Girls at the water, the high place. On the ascent the two meet “girls going out to draw water”, as Abraham’s servant waited where “the daughters of the men of the city are coming out to draw water” (Genesis 24:13) and as Moses met the daughters of the priest of Midian (Exodus 2:16, both now on these pages); in those stories the meeting at the water leads to a marriage, and here it leads to Samuel. “The people have a sacrifice today on the high place”, the bamah, and “he blesses the sacrifice; after that the invited eat”. The ASV has “for the people have a sacrifice to-day in the high place”; the KJV “because he doth bless the sacrifice; and afterwards they eat that be bidden”.

Verses 15–21 · “I have seen my people, for their outcry has come to me” — Samuel’s ear uncovered, a leader (nagid) to be anointed, “this one will restrain my people”, and “Am I not a Benjamite?”

An uncovered ear. “Jehovah had uncovered Samuel’s ear”: the idiom of telling someone what he does not yet know, as Boaz said to his kinsman, “I thought I would uncover your ear” (Ruth 4:4, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “he had uncovered the ear of Samuel”; the ASV “Now Jehovah had revealed unto Samuel”.

A leader, not yet a king. “You shall anoint him as leader over my people Israel”: the title is nagid, “leader”, not the melekh, “king”, the elders asked for, and the act is anointing (mashiach). “He will save my people from the hand of the Philistines”, though the chapter before said the Philistines “did not come again into the territory of Israel” in Samuel’s days (1 Samuel 7:13, now on these pages). “For I have seen my people, for their outcry has come to me” are the words of the burning bush, “I have surely seen the affliction of my people … the outcry of the sons of Israel has come to me” (Exodus 3:7, 9, now on these pages). The Greek has “the affliction of my people” here too. The NWT 1984 supplies it in brackets, “I have seen [the affliction of] my people”; the ASV has “thou shalt anoint him to be prince over my people Israel”.

“This one will restrain my people.” The verb is atsar, “restrain, hold back”, the verb of Sarai’s “Jehovah has kept me from bearing” (Genesis 16:2, now on these pages), here of holding a people in check, governing. The NWT 1984 has “This is the one that will keep my people within bounds”; the ASV “this same shall have authority over my people”; the KJV “this same shall reign over my people”.

“Am I not a Benjamite?” “Do not set your heart on them … And for whom is all the desire of Israel?”: chemdah, desire, or the things desired. The KJV has “And on whom is all the desire of Israel?”; the ASV “And for whom is all that is desirable in Israel?”. Saul answers as Gideon did, “my clan is the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father’s house” (Judges 6:15): “Am I not a Benjamite, from the smallest of the tribes of Israel, and my clan the least?” Benjamin was the tribe Israel had wept over, “that today one tribe should be missing from Israel” (Judges 21:3, both now on these pages). In Josephus Saul tells Samuel that he is mocking him (Antiquities 6.51).

Verses 22–27 · Saul at the head of the table — about thirty guests, the kept portion and the thigh, the roof, and “stand still … that I may let you hear the word of God”

The head of the table. Samuel brings Saul and his servant “into the hall” and gives them “a place at the head of the invited; and they were about thirty men”. The Greek has about seventy, and so does Josephus (Antiquities 6.52, numbered 6.4.1 in Whiston). The NIV has “about thirty in number”; the ASV “who were about thirty persons”.

The portion and the thigh. “Bring the portion that I gave you”: manah, the word of Elkanah’s “portions” and of Hannah’s “double portion” (1 Samuel 1:4–5, now on these pages). The cook “lifted up the thigh and what was on it”: the thigh, shoq, is the priest’s share of a peace offering, “the right thigh you shall give to the priest” (Leviticus 7:32, now on these pages), and here it is set before Saul. Josephus calls it a royal portion (Antiquities 6.52). The ASV has “the cook took up the thigh, and that which was upon it”; the KJV “the cook took up the shoulder”. The words that follow are hard Hebrew, “for it has been kept for you for the appointed time, since I said, I have invited the people”. The ASV has “unto the appointed time hath it been kept for thee”; the NWT 1984 “to the appointed time they have reserved it for you that you may eat with those invited”.

On the roof. “He spoke with Saul on the roof.” The Greek reads instead that they spread a bed for Saul on the roof, and begins verse 26 “and he lay down”. The Douay has both, “he spoke with Saul upon the top of the house: and he prepared a bed for Saul on the top of the house”. In verse 26 the written text has “the roof” and the margin “to the roof”. The NWT 1984 has “he continued speaking with Saul on the housetop”.

“The word of God.” At the edge of the town the servant is sent ahead, and Samuel says, “but you, stand still now, that I may let you hear the word of God.” The chapter stops there; the anointing comes in the next verse (1 Samuel 10:1, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “stand still now that I may let you hear the word of God”; the KJV “stand thou still a while, that I may shew thee the word of God”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a mighty man of valour” for gibbor chayil, as in Ruth 2:1; “a young man, and good” for bachur va-tov, as in 8:16; “she-donkeys”; “a man of God”; “held in honour” for nikhbad; “a present” for teshurah; “seer” for ro’eh and “prophet” for navi; “uncovered … ear”, as in Ruth 4:4; “leader” for nagid; “outcry”, as in Exodus 3:9; “restrain” for atsar; “clan”, as in Judges 6:15; and “portion” for manah, as in 1:4–5.

Echoes paid. The people who were “asking” (8:10) get Saul, “asked”; the king who will take “your best young men” (8:16) is himself “a young man, and good”; the opening “Now there was a man” of 1:1 begins a second story; and the “portion” of Elkanah’s table (1:4–5) is set before Saul.

Echoes planted. The anointing (1 Samuel 10:1), and Saul “taller than all the people from his shoulders upward” when he is chosen (10:23), both now on these pages.
