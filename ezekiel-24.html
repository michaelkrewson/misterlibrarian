Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · Ezekiel 24:1–2 write down this very day

⭐ The date. The ninth year, tenth month, tenth day, counted as the book counts from King Jehoiachin’s exile (1:2), and often reckoned as early 588 BCE. 2 Kings 25:1 gives the same month and day, in the ninth year of a reign, for the arrival of the king of Babylon before Jerusalem; Jeremiah 39:1 names the reign as King Zedekiah’s.

⭐ This very day. Etsem, ‘bone’, used for ‘the very’ day, as at Genesis 7:13 and Deuteronomy 32:48. It is said twice.

⚠ Pressed against. The verb is samakh, to lean on or press, not a technical word for siege, and ‘the king pressed’ in these words stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The NIV has “has laid siege to Jerusalem”, the ASV “drew close unto Jerusalem”. The first word ‘write’ has one form written and another read (ketiv/qere).

Verses 3–5 · Ezekiel 24:3–5 the parable of the pot

⭐ A parable. As at 17:2, to the rebellious house. It reads like a cooking song.

⭐ Set on the pot. The words stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: 2 Kings 4:38, where Elisha orders a stew, and here. Chapter 11 quoted men who called the city a pot and themselves the flesh.

⭐ Pieces. Natach is the cutting of an offering into its pieces (Leviticus 1:6).

⚠ Bones under it. Verse 5 says to pile ‘the bones’ under the pot. Some read a word for wood or logs: the NIV has “Pile wood beneath it for the bones”, the ASV “a pile of wood for the bones”; the KJV keeps “burn also the bones under it”.

Verses 6–8 · Ezekiel 24:6–8 the rust and the blood on the bare rock

⭐ City of blood. The name of 22:2. ‘Woe, city’ stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, verses 6 and 9.

⚠ Rust. The word chel’atah, ‘its rust’, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in this chapter (verses 6, 11, 12). The KJV has “scum”.

⭐ Not covered. Blood poured out was to be covered with dust (Leviticus 17:13). Here it is left on bare rock, where it can be seen, as Abel’s blood cried from the ground (Genesis 4:10). ‘She set it’ (verse 7) and ‘I have put’ (verse 8) are two verbs: what she did openly he has now fixed there.

⭐ No lot. Nothing is chosen out; piece after piece comes out.

Verses 9–12 · Ezekiel 24:9–12 the fire under the empty pot

⭐ Finish. ‘Finish off the flesh’ and ‘its rust may be finished’ have the root printed ‘bring to an end’ at 22:15. ‘Uncleanness’ and ‘melted’ are chapter 22’s too.

⚠ Spices or broth. The words of verse 10 are literally ‘spice the spicing’. The KJV has “spice it well”, the ASV “make thick the broth”, the NIV “mixing in the spices”.

⚠ Verse 12. The first two words are very hard. The ASV has “She hath wearied herself with toil”, the KJV “She hath wearied herself with lies”.

Verses 13–14 · Ezekiel 24:13–14 ‘I have spoken; it is coming’

⭐ You. The city is addressed as a feminine ‘you’. ‘Depravity’ is the word of chapters 16, 22 and 23.

⭐ Rest my fury. As at 5:13 and 16:42.

⭐ Not relent. The root of ‘relent’ is the one printed ‘appeased’ at 5:13. ‘I will do it’ is the form printed ‘done it’ at 22:14.

⚠ They will judge you. The verb is a past form, said of what is to come. The Douay has “I will judge thee”.

Verses 15–18 · Ezekiel 24:15–18 the death of Ezekiel’s wife

⭐ The desire of your eyes. The words stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: 1 Kings 20:6 and here. The chapter says who is meant only in verse 18, and does not give her name. The same two words are printed ‘pleasant to look at’ at Lamentations 2:4.

⭐ A stroke. Maggefah, printed ‘plague’ at Numbers 14:37: a sudden blow.

⭐ No mourning. He may groan, silently, but not lament or weep. The headdress stays on (compare Leviticus 10:6, where Aaron is told not to let his hair hang loose), and so do the sandals, where David mourning went barefoot (2 Samuel 15:30). The upper lip is covered by one declared unclean (Leviticus 13:45); compare Micah 3:7, of seers put to shame.

⚠ The bread of men. Usually taken as food brought to mourners. The KJV keeps “the bread of men”; the NIV has “the customary food of mourners”.

Verses 19–24 · Ezekiel 24:19–24 ‘you will do as I have done’

⭐ They ask. The people see what he is doing and ask what it means for them.

⭐ My sanctuary. What his wife was to him, the sanctuary is to them. ‘The pride of your power’ is the phrase of Leviticus 26:19, and ‘the desire of your eyes’ repeats verse 16. ‘Desire’ and ‘pity’ are machmad and machmal, one letter apart.

⭐ Waste away. As at 4:17; the verb is printed ‘rot away’ at Leviticus 26:39. ‘Moan’ is not the ‘groan’ of verse 17.

⭐ Ezekiel. The prophet is named in this book only at 1:3 and here. He is a portent, as at 12:11: what he does, they will do.

Verses 25–27 · Ezekiel 24:25–27 the fugitive, and the mouth opened

⭐ The longing of their soul. Literally what they lift their soul to.

⭐ The fugitive. One who has escaped, as the man who brought news to Abram (Genesis 14:13); the same noun is printed of those who escape at 6:8–9 and 7:16. 33:21–22 tells of one arriving.

⭐ Mute no longer. 3:26–27 made him mute except when Jehovah opened his mouth.

⚠ With the fugitive. The Hebrew says the mouth will be opened ‘with’ him. The KJV has “to him which is escaped”, the NIV “you will speak with him”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “rust” at 6, 11 and 12; “bare rock” at 7 and 8; “desire of your eyes” at 16, 21 and 25; “lament” and “weep” at 16 and 23; and “portent” at 24 and 27.

Echoes paid. The pot of chapter 11; the city of blood of 22:2; the portent of 12:11; and the silence of 3:26–27.

Echoes planted. The fugitive arrives at Ezekiel 33:21; the bare rock again at Ezekiel 26:4.

Ezekiel 25, now on these pages: words against Ammon, Moab, Edom and the Philistines.
