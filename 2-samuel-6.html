Jerusalem: David gathers thirty thousand chosen men and sets the ark on a new cart at Baale-judah (Kiriath-jearim); Uzzah puts out his hand at the threshing floor of Nacon and dies beside the ark, and the place is named Perez-uzzah; the ark stays three months with Obed-edom the Gittite, whose house is blessed, then David brings it up with a sacrifice after six steps, whirling in a linen ephod, to the tent he has pitched; Michal despises him in her heart and meets him with mockery, and has no child to the day of her death.
After the capture of Zion and the Philistine battles (2 Samuel 5); the ark had stood at Kiriath-jearim since the Philistines returned it (1 Samuel 7:1–2). The chapter does not date the move; the ark stays three months with Obed-edom (6:11).

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · The ark goes up from Baale-judah — thirty thousand chosen men, the Name over the cherubim, a new cart, and a procession making merry

The chosen men. “And David again gathered all the chosen men in Israel, thirty thousand”: the Hebrew does not say why an army goes to fetch an ark. The Greek has “seventy thousand”. The ASV has “all the chosen men of Israel”; the NIV “all the able young men of Israel”; the NWT 1984 “all the choice men in Israel”; the TLB “thirty thousand special troops”. Saul had taken “three thousand chosen men” against David (1 Samuel 24:2, already on these pages).

Baale-judah. “From Baale-judah, to bring up from there the ark of God”: “Baale-judah” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. It is Kiriath-jearim, which Joshua’s lists call “Baalah — that is Kiriath-jearim” and “Kiriath-baal — that is, Kiriath-jearim” (Joshua 15:9, 60), where the ark had stood in “the house of Abinadab on the hill” since the Philistines sent it back (1 Samuel 7:1–2, all already on these pages); the Chronicles say “to Baalah, that is, to Kiriath-jearim” (1 Chronicles 13:6). The ASV has “Baale-judah”; the KJV “Baale of Judah”; the NIV “Baalah in Judah”; the NWT 1984 “Baʹal·e-juʹdah”; the TLB “Baal-judah”; the Douay “of the men of Juda”. The Greek reads the name as a title, “from the rulers of Judah”.

The Name over the cherubim. “The ark of God, over which the Name is called, the name of Jehovah of armies, who sits upon the cherubim above it”: “is called the Name, the name” (niqra shem shem, the word doubled) stands in one verse. A name called over a thing is a claim of ownership (see the note at Deuteronomy 28:10, already on these pages). “Who sits upon the cherubim” stands in four verses. They are Shiloh’s ark carried out against the Philistines (1 Samuel 4:4, already on these pages), this one, and two more (2 Kings 19:15; Isaiah 37:16). The ASV has “which is called by the Name, even the name of Jehovah of hosts that sitteth above the cherubim”; the NIV “which is called by the Name, the name of the Lord Almighty, who is enthroned between the cherubim on the ark”; the KJV “that dwelleth between the cherubims”; the NWT 1984 “where a name is called on, the name of Jehovah of armies, sitting on the cherubs”; the Douay “upon which the name of the Lord of hosts is invoked, who sitteth over it upon the cherubims”; the TLB “enthroned above the Guardian Angels”.

A new cart. “And they set the ark of God on a new cart, and carried it from the house of Abinadab on the hill; and Uzzah and Ahio, the sons of Abinadab, were driving the new cart”: “a new cart” stands in three verses. They are the Philistine priests’ advice for sending the ark back (1 Samuel 6:7, already on these pages), this verse and 1 Chronicles 13:7. The Philistines had sent it home on such a cart with two cows. For Israel the ark was made with poles in rings “to carry the ark by them” (Exodus 25:14), the sons of Kohath carried the holy things “on the shoulder” (Numbers 7:9), and “they shall not touch the holy thing, lest they die” (Numbers 4:15, all already on these pages); the narrator makes no comparison. “Ahio” can also be read “his brother”, and the Greek has “his brothers”. The ASV has “in the hill”; the KJV and the Geneva “in Gibeah”; the Douay “in Gabaa”; the NIV “on the hill”; the NWT 1984 “which was on the hill”; the TLB “hillside home”.

Making merry. “And David and all the house of Israel were making merry before Jehovah with every kind of cypress wood, and with lyres and with harps and with timbrels, and with rattles and with cymbals”: “making merry before Jehovah” stands in one verse; the root is sachaq, the “make sport” of the young men at Gibeon (2 Samuel 2:14) and the “made merry” of the women who met David (1 Samuel 18:7, both already on these pages). The Hebrew has no word “instruments”; it names the wood, berosh, cypress or juniper or fir, and the lyres (kinnorot) and harps (nevalim) are those of 1 Samuel 10:5 (already on these pages). The word for “rattles” (mena’ane’im) stands in one verse, and “cymbals” are tseltselim, of the same root as the whirring insect of Deuteronomy 28:42 (see tzelatzal). The ASV has “instruments made of fir-wood, and with harps, and with psalteries, and with timbrels, and with castanets, and with cymbals”; the KJV “instruments made of fir wood”; the NIV “castanets, harps, lyres, timbrels, sistrums and cymbals”; the NWT 1984 “instruments of juniper wood”; the TLB “waving branches of juniper trees”; the Douay “instruments made of wood”.

Verses 6–8 · Uzzah and the ark — Nacon’s threshing floor, the oxen that let it slip, “for the error”, and Perez-uzzah

Nacon’s threshing floor. “And they came as far as the threshing floor of Nacon”: “the threshing floor of Nacon” stands in one verse. The Chronicles name it Chidon (1 Chronicles 13:9) and the Greek has “Nodab”. The ASV has “Nacon”; the KJV “Nachon’s threshingfloor”; the Geneva “Nachon’s threshing floor”; the NIV “Nakon”; the NWT 1984 “Naʹcon”.

The oxen let it slip. “And Uzzah put out his hand to the ark of God and took hold of it, for the oxen let it slip”: the Hebrew gives no reason for the hand but the slip. The root shamat stands in eleven verses of the Hebrew Bible. It is the word of the seventh-year release; see the note at Deuteronomy 15:1–3 and shemittah (already on these pages). The ASV has “for the oxen stumbled”; the NIV “because the oxen stumbled”; the KJV “for the oxen shook it”; the Geneva “for the oxen did shake it”; the Douay “because the oxen kicked and made it lean aside”; the NWT 1984 “for the cattle nearly caused an upset”; the TLB “the oxen stumbled and Uzzah put out his hand to steady the Ark”, a motive the Hebrew does not give. The Greek has “the calf pulled it away”.

For the error. “And the anger of Jehovah burned against Uzzah, and God struck him down there for the error; and he died there beside the ark of God”: the word for “the error”, shal, is obscure and the versions guess. The ASV has “for his error”; the KJV “for his error”; the NIV has “because of his irreverent act”; the NWT 1984 “for the irreverent act”; the Douay “for his rashness”; the Geneva “for his fault”; the TLB “for doing this”. The Chronicles give the cause as “because he put forth his hand to the ark” (1 Chronicles 13:10). The earlier sudden deaths at the ark’s approach are those of the men of Beth-shemesh “because they had gazed at the ark of Jehovah” (1 Samuel 6:19) and of Aaron’s sons, “and they died before Jehovah” (Leviticus 10:2, both already on these pages).

Perez-uzzah. “And David was angry, because Jehovah had broken through in a breach against Uzzah; and he called that place Perez-uzzah, to this day”: “Perez-uzzah” stands in one verse. “Jehovah has broken through” stands in five verses, and the first of the five is David’s own “Jehovah has broken through my enemies before me, like a breach of waters” at Baal-perazim (2 Samuel 5:20, already on these pages); see perets. The breach that was a victory on the Philistines is now against a man of Israel at the ark. David’s anger burns as Jehovah’s did in verse 7. The ASV has “Jehovah had broken forth upon Uzzah”; the KJV “had made a breach upon Uzzah”; the NIV “wrath had broken out against Uzzah”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah had broken through in a rupture against Uzʹzah”; the Douay “the Lord had struck Oza”; the Geneva “the Lord had smitten Uzzah”; the TLB “The Place of Wrath upon Uzzah”. The Greek has “the Cutting of Oza”.

Verses 9–12 · “How shall the ark of Jehovah come to me?” — David’s fear, Obed-edom the Gittite, and a house blessed for three months

“How shall the ark come to me?” “And David feared Jehovah that day, and said: ‘How shall the ark of Jehovah come to me?’”: the verb is yare, “feared”, and the question and the decision of verse 10 follow from it. The NIV has “How can the ark of the Lord ever come to me?”; the NWT 1984 “How will the ark of Jehovah come to me?”; the TLB “How can I ever bring the Ark home?”; the ASV “How shall the ark of Jehovah come unto me?”; the Geneva “Therefore David that day feared the Lord”.

Obed-edom the Gittite. “And David turned it aside to the house of Obed-edom the Gittite”: “Obed-edom the Gittite” stands in three verses. They are verses 10 and 11 here and 1 Chronicles 13:13. A Gittite is a man of Gath, the city of Goliath (1 Samuel 17:4, already on these pages); the text gives no reason for the choice. The ASV has “carried it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite”; the NIV “he took it to the house of Obed-Edom the Gittite”; the NWT 1984 “had it carried aside”; the Douay “caused it to be carried into the house of Obededom the Gethite”.

A house blessed. “And the ark of Jehovah stayed in the house of Obed-edom the Gittite three months; and Jehovah blessed Obed-edom and all his house”: the same blessing of a house as Jehovah’s of the Egyptian steward’s “for Joseph’s sake” (Genesis 39:5, already on these pages), here “because of the ark of God”. In verse 12 the report is brought to David, and he goes. The ASV has “Jehovah hath blessed the house of Obed-edom, and all that pertaineth unto him”; the NIV “has blessed the household of Obed-Edom and everything he has”; the NWT 1984 “on account of the ark of the [true] God”; the TLB “he brought the Ark to the City of David with a great celebration”; the Douay “And there were with David seven choirs, and calves for victims”, a clause that the Greek has at verse 13 and the Hebrew not at all. The Chronicles give a reason for the breach: “No one may carry the ark of God except the Levites”, and “Jehovah our God made a breach upon us, for we sought him not according to the ordinance” (1 Chronicles 15:2, 13).

Verses 13–15 · Six steps, a sacrifice, David whirling in a linen ephod, and the shout and the ram’s horn

Six steps. “And it happened, when the bearers of the ark of Jehovah had marched six steps, that he sacrificed an ox and a fatling”: “six steps” stands in one verse. “Marched” and “steps” come from the root of tse’adah, the “sound of marching” in the treetops that told David Jehovah had gone out before him (2 Samuel 5:24, already on these pages); see tsa’ad. The ASV has “had gone six paces, he sacrificed an ox and a fatling”; the KJV “oxen and fatlings”; the NIV “had taken six steps, he sacrificed a bull and a fattened calf”; the NWT 1984 “had marched six steps, he immediately sacrificed a bull and a fatling”; the Douay “an ox and a ram”; the Geneva “an ox, and a fat beast”; the TLB “they stopped and waited so that he could sacrifice an ox and a fat lamb”. The Greek has “seven choirs and a sacrifice, a calf and a lamb”.

Whirling. “And David was whirling with all his might before Jehovah”: mekharker stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and verse 16. The Chronicles’ account of Michal’s looking has other words, “dancing and making merry” (1 Chronicles 15:29). The ASV has “David danced before Jehovah with all his might”; the NIV “David was dancing before the Lord with all his might”; the NWT 1984 “David was dancing around before Jehovah with all his power”; the Douay “David danced with all his might before the Lord”. The Greek has David “striking up on tuned instruments”.

A linen ephod. “And David was girded with a linen ephod”: “a linen ephod” stands in four verses. They are the boy Samuel (1 Samuel 2:18), the eighty-five priests of Nob (1 Samuel 22:18, both already on these pages), this verse, and 1 Chronicles 15:27, where David also wears “a robe of fine linen”; see ephod. The NIV has “Wearing a linen ephod, David was dancing”; the TLB “was wearing priests’ clothing”; the NWT 1984 “girded with an ephʹod of linen”; the ASV “girded with a linen ephod”. The Greek has “a special robe”.

The shout and the ram’s horn. “With a shout and with the sound of the ram’s horn”: teruah, the shout, and shofar, the ram’s horn Saul blew from Geba (1 Samuel 13:3, already on these pages). The ASV has “with shouting, and with the sound of the trumpet”; the NIV “with shouts and the sound of trumpets”; the NWT 1984 “joyful shouting and sound of horn”; the TLB “much shouting and blowing of trumpets”; the Douay “brought the ark of the covenant of the Lord with joyful shouting”, with “of the covenant” added.

Verses 16–19 · Michal at the window — the tent for the ark, burnt offerings and peace offerings, and a loaf, an eshpar and a raisin cake for everyone

Michal at the window. “Michal daughter of Saul looked out through the window and saw King David leaping and whirling before Jehovah; and she despised him in her heart”: “looked out through the window” (nishqefah be’ad hachalon) stands in two verses. They are this one and 1 Chronicles 15:29. The window is hers a second time: she had let David down through it (1 Samuel 19:12), and Sisera’s mother looks out of hers (Judges 5:28, both already on these pages). “Leaping” (mefazzez) stands in one verse. “And she despised him” stands in two verses, the same two. She is “Michal daughter of Saul” (and again in verses 20 and 23), not “Michal, David’s wife” as at 1 Samuel 25:44 (already on these pages). The ASV has “looked out at the window”; the KJV “looked through a window”; the NIV “watched from a window”; the NWT 1984 “looked down through the window”; the Douay “looking out through a window”; the TLB “watched from a window”. The Greek has “peered through the lattice” and calls her Melchol.

The tent. “They brought in the ark of Jehovah and set it in its place, in the midst of the tent that David had pitched for it; and David offered up burnt offerings before Jehovah, and peace offerings”: a tent, not the house at Shiloh where Samuel had lain down “in the temple of Jehovah, where the ark of God was” (1 Samuel 3:3, already on these pages); it is “the tent” from which Zadok will take the horn of oil (1 Kings 1:39, already on these pages). After the offerings David “blessed the people in the name of Jehovah of armies”, the Name of verse 2, as Aaron blessed the people when he came down from his offerings (Leviticus 9:22, already on these pages). The ASV has “in the midst of the tent”; the KJV “in the midst of the tabernacle”; the Geneva “in the midst of the Tabernacle”; the Douay “in the midst of the tabernacle”; the NIV “inside the tent that David had pitched for it”; the NWT 1984 “inside the tent that David had pitched for it”; the TLB “inside the tent that David had prepared for it”.

A loaf, an eshpar and a raisin cake. “To each one a loaf of bread, and one eshpar, and one raisin cake”: “Eshpar” stands in two verses, this one and its parallel in the Chronicles. “Raisin cake” in the singular (ashishah) stands in the same two. The plural is the “raisin cakes” of Song of Solomon 2:5 and Hosea 3:1, and the Chronicles’ verse is 1 Chronicles 16:3. “Loaf” is challat lechem, see challah. The meaning of eshpar is not known; see eshpar. The NIV has “a loaf of bread, a cake of dates and a cake of raisins”; the NWT 1984 “a ring-shaped cake of bread and a date cake and a raisin cake”; the ASV “a cake of bread, and a portion of flesh, and a cake of raisins”; the KJV “a cake of bread, and a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine”; the Geneva “a piece of flesh, and a bottle of wine”; the Douay “a piece of roasted beef, and fine flour fried with oil”; the TLB “a loaf of bread, some wine, and a cake of raisins”. The Greek has “a loaf, a griddle-cake, and a cake fried in a pan”, and adds “from Dan to Beersheba”.

Verses 20–23 · “How honored the king of Israel has been today” — Michal’s mockery, David’s answer before Jehovah, and Michal childless to the day of her death

How honored. “And David returned to bless his house. And Michal daughter of Saul came out to meet David, and said: ‘How honored the king of Israel has been today, who uncovered himself today in the eyes of the maidservants of his servants, as one of the empty men uncovers himself outright!’”: Obed-edom’s house is blessed (verses 11–12), David blesses the people (verse 18) and comes home to bless his own. Nikhbad, “honored”, is the root of kavod, “glory” and “weight”. “Uncovered himself today” stands in one verse. “The empty men” (hareqim) stands in one verse; they are the men Abimelech hired, “empty and reckless men” (Judges 9:4), and those who “gathered to Jephthah” (Judges 11:3, both already on these pages). The Hebrew does not say how much David wore under the linen ephod. The ASV has “How glorious was the king of Israel to-day, who uncovered himself”; the NIV “going around half-naked in full view of the slave girls”; the NWT 1984 “just as one of the empty-headed men uncovers himself outright”; the TLB “He exposed himself to the girls along the street like a common pervert!”; the Douay “uncovering himself before the handmaids of his servants”; the Geneva “which was uncovered today in the eyes of the maidens”. The Greek adds that she “blessed him” before she spoke.

Before Jehovah. “Before Jehovah, who chose me above your father and above all his house, to appoint me leader over the people of Jehovah, over Israel — and I will make merry before Jehovah”: the sentence has no verb until its last clause, and “leader” is nagid, the word of the tribes at Hebron (2 Samuel 5:2), of Abigail (1 Samuel 25:30) and of Samuel to Saul (13:14), which David now says himself; “above your father” is the end of Saul’s kingdom (1 Samuel 15:28, all already on these pages). “And I will make merry before Jehovah” stands in one verse, the root of verse 5. The ASV has “to appoint me prince over the people of Jehovah”; the KJV “to appoint me ruler”; the NIV “when he appointed me ruler”; the NWT 1984 “to put me in command as leader over Jehovah’s people Israel, and I will celebrate before Jehovah”; the TLB “appointed me as leader of Israel”.

Honored again. “And I will be held more lightly than this yet, and be low in my own eyes; but with the maidservants of whom you spoke, with them I shall be honored”: unqallothi is from qalal, “to be light”; “I shall be honored” is the same root as “How honored” in verse 20, and David hands Michal’s word back to her. The ASV has “I will be yet more vile than this, and will be base in mine own sight: but of the handmaids of whom thou hast spoken, of them shall I be had in honor”; the NIV “I will become even more undignified than this”; the NWT 1984 “I will make myself even more lightly esteemed than this”; the TLB “I am willing to look even more foolish than this”; the Douay “I will both play and make myself meaner than I have done”.

No child. “And Michal daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death”: “had no child” stands in one verse. The Hebrew begins “and Michal”, with no cause given; the KJV and the Geneva supply “Therefore Michal the daughter of Saul had no child”, the Douay “Therefore Michol the daughter of Saul had no child”, the ASV has “And Michal the daughter of Saul had no child unto the day of her death”, the NIV “And Michal daughter of Saul had no children to the day of her death”, and the NWT 1984 “So, as regards Miʹchal, Saul’s daughter, she came to have no child”. Michal had been given to Palti son of Laish and taken back from him (1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:15, already on these pages), and 2 Samuel 21:8 speaks of “the five sons of Michal the daughter of Saul, whom she bore to Adriel”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the chosen men”; “a new cart”, as in 1 Samuel 6:7; “making merry” for sachaq, as in 1 Samuel 18:7; “let it slip” for shamat; “for the error”; “broken through in a breach” for parats, as in 2 Samuel 5:20; “marched” and “steps” from the root of 5:24; “whirling” for mekharker; “a linen ephod”, as in 1 Samuel 2:18; “the ram’s horn”, as in 1 Samuel 13:3; “the tent”; “eshpar” left as the Hebrew word; “honored” for nikhbad and ikkaved; “the empty men”, as in Judges 9:4; “leader” for nagid; and “Michal daughter of Saul”.

Echoes paid. “Jehovah has broken through” at Baal-perazim and at Uzzah (2 Samuel 5:20); the sound of marching and the steps of the bearers (2 Samuel 5:24); “the house of Abinadab on the hill” (1 Samuel 7:1–2); the new cart (1 Samuel 6:7); the Name over the cherubim (1 Samuel 4:4; Deuteronomy 28:10); the law’s poles, shoulders and “lest they die” (Exodus 25:14; Numbers 7:9; 4:15); the men of Beth-shemesh and Aaron’s sons (1 Samuel 6:19; Leviticus 10:2); the release verb (Deuteronomy 15:1–3); the house blessed for the ark as the Egyptian’s for Joseph (Genesis 39:5); Gath (1 Samuel 17:4); the boy Samuel’s and the priests’ linen ephod (1 Samuel 2:18; 22:18); the ram’s horn (1 Samuel 13:3); Michal at the window, let down David and seen with Sisera’s mother (1 Samuel 19:12; Judges 5:28; 1 Samuel 25:44); the tent, and Zadok’s horn of oil from it (1 Kings 1:39); Aaron’s blessing after the offerings (Leviticus 9:22); the empty men of Abimelech and Jephthah (Judges 9:4; 11:3); “leader” (2 Samuel 5:2; 1 Samuel 25:30; 13:14); the end of Saul’s kingdom (1 Samuel 15:28); and Palti (1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:15).

Echoes planted. David’s wish for a house for the ark, and Jehovah’s “house” for him (2 Samuel 7:2, now on these pages); Michal’s five sons in the Hebrew of 2 Samuel 21:8; the Chronicles’ ordered procession, with Chidon, the Levites, Beeliada and the robe of fine linen (1 Chronicles 13:6–14; 15:1–16:3); the cherubim of Hezekiah’s prayer (2 Kings 19:15; Isaiah 37:16); and the raisin cakes of Song of Solomon 2:5 and Hosea 3:1. None of these is translated here yet.
