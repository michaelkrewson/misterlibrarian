Y la recubrirás de oro puro —por dentro y por fuera la recubrirás—, y le harás una moldura de oro alrededor.
12
וְיָצַקְתָּ לּוֹ, אַרְבַּע טַבְּעֹת זָהָב, וְנָתַתָּה, עַל אַרְבַּע פַּעֲמֹתָיו; וּשְׁתֵּי טַבָּעֹת, עַל-צַלְעוֹ הָאֶחָת, וּשְׁתֵּי טַבָּעֹת, עַל-צַלְעוֹ הַשֵּׁנִית.
And you shall cast four rings of gold for it, and put them on its four feet — two rings on the one side of it, and two rings on the other side of it.note
Y le fundirás cuatro anillos de oro, y los pondrás en sus cuatro patas —dos anillos en un lado, y dos anillos en el otro lado.
13
וְעָשִׂיתָ בַדֵּי, עֲצֵי שִׁטִּים; וְצִפִּיתָ אֹתָם, זָהָב.
And you shall make poles of acacia wood, and overlay them with gold.note
Y harás varas de madera de acacia, y las recubrirás de oro.
14
וְהֵבֵאתָ אֶת-הַבַּדִּים בַּטַּבָּעֹת, עַל צַלְעֹת הָאָרֹן, לָשֵׂאת אֶת-הָאָרֹן, בָּהֶם.
And you shall put the poles into the rings on the sides of the ark, to carry the ark by them.note
Y meterás las varas en los anillos, a los lados del arca, para llevar el arca con ellas.
15
בְּטַבְּעֹת, הָאָרֹן, יִהְיוּ, הַבַּדִּים: לֹא יָסֻרוּ, מִמֶּנּוּ.
The poles shall remain in the rings of the ark; they shall not be removed from it.note
Las varas permanecerán en los anillos del arca; no se quitarán de ella.
16
וְנָתַתָּ, אֶל-הָאָרֹן--אֵת, הָעֵדֻת, אֲשֶׁר אֶתֵּן, אֵלֶיךָ.
And you shall put into the ark the testimony that I will give you.note
Y pondrás en el arca el testimonio que yo te daré.
17
וְעָשִׂיתָ כַפֹּרֶת, זָהָב טָהוֹר: אַמָּתַיִם וָחֵצִי אָרְכָּהּ, וְאַמָּה וָחֵצִי רָחְבָּהּ.
And you shall make a cover of pure gold: two and a half cubits its length, and a cubit and a half its width.note
Y harás una cubierta de oro puro: dos codos y medio de largo, y un codo y medio de ancho.
18
וְעָשִׂיתָ שְׁנַיִם כְּרֻבִים, זָהָב; מִקְשָׁה תַּעֲשֶׂה אֹתָם, מִשְּׁנֵי קְצוֹת הַכַּפֹּרֶת.
And you shall make two cherubim of gold; of hammered work you shall make them, from the two ends of the cover.note
Y harás dos querubines de oro; los harás de obra labrada a martillo, en los dos extremos de la cubierta.
19
וַעֲשֵׂה כְּרוּב אֶחָד מִקָּצָה, מִזֶּה, וּכְרוּב-אֶחָד מִקָּצָה, מִזֶּה; מִן-הַכַּפֹּרֶת תַּעֲשׂוּ אֶת-הַכְּרֻבִים, עַל-שְׁנֵי קְצוֹתָיו.
And make one cherub from the one end, and one cherub from the other end; of one piece with the cover you shall make the cherubim, on its two ends.note
Y haz un querubín en un extremo, y otro querubín en el otro extremo; de una sola pieza con la cubierta haréis los querubines, en sus dos extremos.
20
וְהָיוּ הַכְּרֻבִים פֹּרְשֵׂי כְנָפַיִם לְמַעְלָה, סֹכְכִים בְּכַנְפֵיהֶם עַל-הַכַּפֹּרֶת, וּפְנֵיהֶם, אִישׁ אֶל-אָחִיו; אֶל-הַכַּפֹּרֶת--יִהְיוּ, פְּנֵי הַכְּרֻבִים.

Y los querubines extenderán sus alas hacia arriba, cubriendo la cubierta con sus alas, con sus rostros el uno hacia el otro —hacia la cubierta estarán vueltos los rostros de los querubines.
21
וְנָתַתָּ אֶת-הַכַּפֹּרֶת עַל-הָאָרֹן, מִלְמָעְלָה; וְאֶל-הָאָרֹן--תִּתֵּן אֶת-הָעֵדֻת, אֲשֶׁר אֶתֵּן אֵלֶיךָ.

Y pondrás la cubierta encima del arca, y en el arca pondrás el testimonio que yo te daré.

33

34
וּבַמְּנֹרָה, אַרְבָּעָה גְבִעִים: מְשֻׁקָּדִים--כַּפְתֹּרֶיהָ, וּפְרָחֶיהָ.

Y en el candelabro mismo, cuatro copas en forma de flor de almendro, con sus botones y sus flores.
35
וְכַפְתֹּר תַּחַת שְׁנֵי הַקָּנִים מִמֶּנָּה, וְכַפְתֹּר תַּחַת שְׁנֵי הַקָּנִים מִמֶּנָּה, וְכַפְתֹּר, תַּחַת-שְׁנֵי הַקָּנִים מִמֶּנָּה--לְשֵׁשֶׁת, הַקָּנִים, הַיֹּצְאִים, מִן-הַמְּנֹרָה.

36
כַּפְתֹּרֵיהֶם וּקְנֹתָם, מִמֶּנָּה יִהְיוּ; כֻּלָּהּ מִקְשָׁה אַחַת, זָהָב טָהוֹר.

Sus botones y sus brazos serán de una sola pieza con él; todo él una sola pieza de obra labrada a martillo, de oro puro.
37

Notes on this translation

A contribution, not a tax — and a wardrobe of materials before a single wall is built

‘That they take for me a contribution’ (v2) is deliberately not a command to hand something over. The offering is qualified twice in one clause — ‘from every man WHOSE HEART MOVES HIM’ — a voluntary gift, not the fixed half-shekel census levy this book requires later (Exodus 30:13, already on these pages). The whole tabernacle, on its own terms, is funded by willingness before it is funded by silver.

⚠ The materials list (vv3–7) reads like an inventory before it reads like theology, and one item on it has defeated translators for centuries: the tachash skins (v5). KJV reads ‘badgers’ skins’ — following Martin Luther’s guess that the Hebrew sounded like the German Dachs, badger, though badgers neither live in Sinai nor yield a hide large enough to cover a tent. NIV reads ‘the other durable leather’ in the main text with ‘sea cow’ as a footnoted alternative, following the leading modern guess — an Arabic cognate applied to dolphins, seals, and dugongs in the Red Sea. This translation keeps the Hebrew, on the same policy used elsewhere for words this genuinely uncertain.

‘Let them make me a sanctuary, THAT I MAY DWELL AMONG THEM’ (v8) states the whole purpose of the next fifteen chapters in one line, and the preposition matters. Not ‘that I may dwell IN IT’ — the text does not say the building houses God the way a house houses a family. It says the building lets God dwell among the people; the structure is the occasion, the people are the address. ⚠ V9’s pattern (tavnit), shown to Moses and never described to the reader, governs everything that follows for the rest of this section — Moses is not designing a building, he is copying one he has been shown.

⚠ A box no one may ever set down, and a name Tyndale invented

The poles that carry the ark ‘shall not be removed from it’ (v15) — a single, easy-to-miss clause with a real practical consequence. Every other piece of tabernacle furniture in this chapter has poles fitted for carrying; only the ark is told, explicitly, that its poles stay in the rings permanently. The most sacred object in the whole structure is built, by law, to never be set down and never be touched directly — it travels perpetually ready to move, which is exactly the condition a people crossing a wilderness needs.

⚠ ‘Cover’ (kapporet, v17) is the literal word, and ‘mercy seat’ is not a translation of it — it is a 16th-century theological gloss. The Hebrew root, kaphar, means to cover or wipe away, with a secondary sense of atone; William Tyndale coined ‘mercy seat’ under the influence of Luther’s German Gnadenstuhl, ‘seat of grace’ — a beautiful phrase, and not what the Hebrew word itself says. KJV and ASV both keep Tyndale’s ‘mercy seat’; NIV renders the more literal ‘atonement cover’, the reading closer to what this translation follows. The Septuagint’s Greek, hilastērion, ‘the thing that atones’, and the Vulgate’s Latin propitiatorium both preserve the same secondary sense — a covering that also atones — without inventing a piece of furniture the Hebrew word never names.

The cherubim here are not the first cherubim on these pages, and the contrast is the whole point. The only other cherubim so far stood at Eden’s gate, ‘to guard the way to the tree of life’ (Genesis 3:24, already on these pages) — barring humanity FROM God’s presence. These two, wings spread and faces bowed toward each other over the cover, do not guard an exit; they frame an entrance. ‘There I will MEET with you’ (v22) is the promise the Eden cherubim made structurally impossible, now made from between an identical pair of figures.

Bread that is never eaten by the one it is set before

The table’s whole purpose is compressed into its last clause: ‘bread of the presence’, before me always (v30). Twelve loaves — not named as twelve here, but fixed at that number once the practice is spelled out in full later — kept perpetually in Jehovah’s own sight on a table built specifically to hold them, replaced on a schedule the chapter never states. This is the earliest institution of the table itself; the fuller weekly-replacement ritual is developed only much later, at Leviticus 24:5–9, not yet on these pages.

⚠ This table’s bread gets one of the more surprising afterlives of any object in this chapter. When David, fleeing Saul, needs food for himself and his men, the priest at Nob gives him exactly this bread — and centuries later Jesus cites the episode by name to defend his own disciples for plucking grain on the sabbath: ‘have you not read what David did’ (Matthew 12:3–4, already on these pages, whose own note traces the practice to Leviticus rather than to the table’s actual origin here). A piece of furniture specified in a materials list becomes, a millennium and a half later, the precedent for a rabbi’s argument about the sabbath.

A lamp with no human hand feeding its oil, six centuries before it needs one

The lampstand is not assembled from parts — it is ‘hammered work’, one solid piece of gold beaten into shape, base and shaft and six branches and their almond-blossom cups all ‘of one piece with it’ (vv31, 36). Nothing here is soldered, screwed, or joined; the whole object is a single sculpture struck out of a single mass of metal, a manufacturing detail the text repeats three times as if daring the reader to imagine how.

⚠ This is not the last lampstand on these pages — it recurs almost exactly, and also not quite, in a vision seen roughly nine hundred years later. Zechariah’s fourth night-vision opens on ‘a lampstand, all of it gold… and seven lamps on it’ (Zechariah 4:2, already on these pages) — the same seven-lamp count, the same solid-gold construction. But Zechariah’s version has grown a feature this one does not: a bowl and pipes feeding it oil automatically from two olive trees standing beside it, so that no priest ever has to trim or refill it by hand. This chapter’s lampstand needs v37’s human ‘they shall light its lamps’; Zechariah’s does not — the difference between a lamp that is tended and one that tends itself is exactly the difference the later vision is built to make.

The chapter closes where it opened: ‘according to their pattern, which is being shown to you ON THE MOUNTAIN’ (v40) restates v9’s tavnit almost word for word. Everything between those two lines — the ark, the cover, the cherubim, the table, the lampstand — is presented as a copy of something Moses is shown, never as his own design.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. ‘Contribution’ kept for terumah (v2) rather than ‘offering’, to keep it distinct from the sacrificial olah/zevach vocabulary of the preceding chapters — this is a construction fund, not an altar rite. ‘Cover’ kept for kapporet (v17) rather than the traditional ‘mercy seat’, per the note above. ‘Bread of the presence’ kept literal for lechem panim (v30) rather than the archaic ‘shewbread’. Verse numbering follows the Hebrew exactly; Hebrew and English Exodus 25 both run 1–40.

A note on order: Exodus stands on these pages at chapters 1–37. Exodus 26, the tabernacle’s own curtains, frames, and veils — the structure this chapter’s furniture stands inside — Exodus 27, its altar and enclosing courtyard, and Exodus 28, the priesthood’s own garments, are all now on these pages too. Exodus 29, the ordination ceremony, Exodus 30, the incense altar and the half-shekel census, and Exodus 31, the naming of Bezalel and Oholiab and the sabbath command that closes the whole instruction, and Exodus 32, the golden calf and Moses’ intercession, and Exodus 33, Moses’ plea to see Jehovah’s glory and his glimpse of God’s back from the cleft of the rock, and Exodus 34, the covenant remade and Moses’ own face left shining, and Exodus 35, the people’s own gifts for the tabernacle at last brought forward, and Exodus 36, the craftsmen at work and the people’s giving finally restrained, and Exodus 37, the ark and the table and the lampstand actually built, are now on these pages too. Exodus 38, the bronze altar and the courtyard built at last, remains open, along with the rest of the book.
