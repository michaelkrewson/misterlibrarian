“Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands”: Jonathan’s soul is bound up with David’s and he gives him his robe and bow; the women’s song turns Saul against him, the evil spirit rushes on Saul and he hurls the spear; Merab is promised and given to another, and Michal, who loves David, is given to him for a hundred Philistine foreskins, of which David brings two hundred.
Undated: in Saul’s reign, after the fight with Goliath of chapter 17, while David serves at Saul’s court and leads his men in the Philistine wars.

1 Samuel 18 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
וַיְהִי, כְּכַלֹּתוֹ לְדַבֵּר אֶל-שָׁאוּל, וְנֶפֶשׁ יְהוֹנָתָן, נִקְשְׁרָה בְּנֶפֶשׁ דָּוִד; ויאהבו וַיֶּאֱהָבֵהוּ יְהוֹנָתָן, כְּנַפְשׁוֹ.
And it came about, when he had finished speaking to Saul, that the soul of Jonathan was bound up with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul.note
Y aconteció que, cuando él acabó de hablar con Saúl, el alma de Jonatán quedó atada al alma de David, y Jonatán lo amó como a su propia alma.
2
וַיִּקָּחֵהוּ שָׁאוּל, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא; וְלֹא נְתָנוֹ, לָשׁוּב בֵּית אָבִיו.
And Saul took him that day, and did not let him return to his father’s house.note
Y Saúl lo tomó aquel día, y no lo dejó volver a la casa de su padre.
3
וַיִּכְרֹת יְהוֹנָתָן וְדָוִד, בְּרִית, בְּאַהֲבָתוֹ אֹתוֹ, כְּנַפְשׁוֹ.
And Jonathan and David cut a covenant, because he loved him as his own soul.note
Y Jonatán y David cortaron un pacto, porque él lo amaba como a su propia alma.
4
וַיִּתְפַּשֵּׁט יְהוֹנָתָן, אֶת-הַמְּעִיל אֲשֶׁר עָלָיו, וַיִּתְּנֵהוּ, לְדָוִד; וּמַדָּיו, וְעַד-חַרְבּוֹ וְעַד-קַשְׁתּוֹ וְעַד-חֲגֹרוֹ.
And Jonathan stripped off the robe that was on him and gave it to David, and his garments, even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his belt.note
Y Jonatán se quitó el manto que llevaba puesto y se lo dio a David, y sus ropas, hasta su espada, y su arco, y su cinturón.
5
וַיֵּצֵא דָוִד בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁלָחֶנּוּ שָׁאוּל, יַשְׂכִּיל, וַיְשִׂמֵהוּ שָׁאוּל, עַל אַנְשֵׁי הַמִּלְחָמָה; וַיִּיטַב, בְּעֵינֵי כָל-הָעָם, וְגַם, בְּעֵינֵי עַבְדֵי שָׁאוּל.  {פ}
And David went out; wherever Saul sent him, he acted wisely, and Saul set him over the men of war; and it was good in the eyes of all the people, and also in the eyes of Saul’s servants.note
Y David salía; adondequiera que Saúl lo enviaba, actuaba con acierto, y Saúl lo puso sobre los hombres de guerra; y fue bueno a los ojos de todo el pueblo, y también a los ojos de los siervos de Saúl.
6
וַיְהִי בְּבוֹאָם, בְּשׁוּב דָּוִד מֵהַכּוֹת אֶת-הַפְּלִשְׁתִּי, וַתֵּצֶאנָה הַנָּשִׁים מִכָּל-עָרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל לשור לָשִׁיר וְהַמְּחֹלוֹת, לִקְרַאת שָׁאוּל הַמֶּלֶךְ--בְּתֻפִּים בְּשִׂמְחָה, וּבְשָׁלִשִׁים.
And it came about as they came, when David returned from striking down the Philistine, that the women came out from all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet King Saul, with timbrels, with joy, and with lutes.note
Y aconteció que, cuando venían, al volver David de herir al filisteo, salieron las mujeres de todas las ciudades de Israel cantando y danzando al encuentro del rey Saúl, con panderos, con alegría y con laúdes.
7
וַתַּעֲנֶינָה הַנָּשִׁים הַמְשַׂחֲקוֹת, וַתֹּאמַרְןָ:  הִכָּה שָׁאוּל בַּאֲלָפָו, וְדָוִד בְּרִבְבֹתָיו.
And the women answered one another as they made merry, and said: ‘Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands.’note
Y las mujeres se respondían unas a otras mientras festejaban, y decían: «Saúl hirió a sus miles, y David a sus diez miles».
8
וַיִּחַר לְשָׁאוּל מְאֹד, וַיֵּרַע בְּעֵינָיו הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, וַיֹּאמֶר נָתְנוּ לְדָוִד רְבָבוֹת, וְלִי נָתְנוּ הָאֲלָפִים; וְעוֹד לוֹ, אַךְ הַמְּלוּכָה.
And Saul was very angry, and this word was evil in his eyes; and he said: ‘They have given David ten thousands, and to me they have given the thousands; and what is left for him but the kingship?’note
Y Saúl se enojó mucho, y esta palabra fue mala a sus ojos; y dijo: «A David le han dado diez miles, y a mí me han dado los miles; ¿qué le queda sino el reino?».
9
וַיְהִי שָׁאוּל, עון עוֹיֵן אֶת-דָּוִד, מֵהַיּוֹם הַהוּא, וָהָלְאָה.  {ס}
And Saul was eyeing David from that day on.note
Y Saúl miraba con recelo a David desde aquel día en adelante.
10
וַיְהִי מִמָּחֳרָת, וַתִּצְלַח רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים רָעָה אֶל-שָׁאוּל וַיִּתְנַבֵּא בְתוֹךְ-הַבַּיִת, וְדָוִד מְנַגֵּן בְּיָדוֹ, כְּיוֹם בְּיוֹם; וְהַחֲנִית, בְּיַד-שָׁאוּל.
And it came about the next day that an evil spirit of God rushed on Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house; and David was playing with his hand, as day by day; and the spear was in Saul’s hand.note
Y aconteció al día siguiente que un espíritu malo de Dios irrumpió sobre Saúl, y él profetizaba en medio de la casa; y David tocaba con su mano, como cada día; y la lanza estaba en la mano de Saúl.
11
וַיָּטֶל שָׁאוּל, אֶת-הַחֲנִית, וַיֹּאמֶר, אַכֶּה בְדָוִד וּבַקִּיר; וַיִּסֹּב דָּוִד מִפָּנָיו, פַּעֲמָיִם.
And Saul hurled the spear, and said: ‘I will strike David to the wall.’ And David turned aside from before him twice.note
Y Saúl arrojó la lanza, y dijo: «Heriré a David contra la pared». Y David se apartó de delante de él dos veces.
12
וַיִּרָא שָׁאוּל, מִלִּפְנֵי דָוִד:  כִּי-הָיָה יְהוָה עִמּוֹ, וּמֵעִם שָׁאוּל סָר.
And Saul was afraid of David, because Jehovah was with him and had turned away from Saul.note
Y Saúl tuvo miedo de David, porque Jehová estaba con él y se había apartado de Saúl.
13
וַיְסִרֵהוּ שָׁאוּל מֵעִמּוֹ, וַיְשִׂמֵהוּ לוֹ שַׂר-אָלֶף; וַיֵּצֵא וַיָּבֹא, לִפְנֵי הָעָם.  {ס}
And Saul turned him away from his side, and made him his commander of a thousand; and he went out and came in before the people.note
Y Saúl lo apartó de su lado, y lo puso por su jefe de un millar; y él salía y entraba delante del pueblo.
14
וַיְהִי דָוִד לְכָל-דְּרָכָו, מַשְׂכִּיל; וַיהוָה, עִמּוֹ.
And David was acting wisely in all his ways, and Jehovah was with him.note
Y David actuaba con acierto en todos sus caminos, y Jehová estaba con él.
15
וַיַּרְא שָׁאוּל, אֲשֶׁר-הוּא מַשְׂכִּיל מְאֹד; וַיָּגָר, מִפָּנָיו.
And Saul saw that he was acting very wisely, and he was in dread of him.note
Y Saúl vio que actuaba con mucho acierto, y le tenía pavor.
16
וְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל וִיהוּדָה, אֹהֵב אֶת-דָּוִד:  כִּי-הוּא יוֹצֵא וָבָא, לִפְנֵיהֶם.  {פ}
But all Israel and Judah loved David, for he went out and came in before them.note
Pero todo Israel y Judá amaban a David, porque él salía y entraba delante de ellos.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-דָּוִד, הִנֵּה בִתִּי הַגְּדוֹלָה מֵרַב אֹתָהּ אֶתֶּן-לְךָ לְאִשָּׁה--אַךְ הֱיֵה-לִי לְבֶן-חַיִל, וְהִלָּחֵם מִלְחֲמוֹת יְהוָה; וְשָׁאוּל אָמַר, אַל-תְּהִי יָדִי בּוֹ, וּתְהִי-בוֹ, יַד-פְּלִשְׁתִּים.  {ס}
And Saul said to David: ‘Look — my elder daughter Merab, her I will give you as a wife; only be a valiant man for me, and fight the battles of Jehovah.’ For Saul said: ‘Let not my hand be on him, but let the hand of the Philistines be on him.’note
Y Saúl dijo a David: «Mira, a mi hija mayor Merab te la daré por mujer; solamente sé para mí un hombre esforzado, y pelea las batallas de Jehová». Porque Saúl decía: «Que no sea mi mano contra él, sino que sea contra él la mano de los filisteos».
18
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-שָׁאוּל, מִי אָנֹכִי וּמִי חַיַּי, מִשְׁפַּחַת אָבִי, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל--כִּי-אֶהְיֶה חָתָן, לַמֶּלֶךְ.
And David said to Saul: ‘Who am I, and what is my life, my father’s clan in Israel, that I should be son-in-law to the king?’note
Y David dijo a Saúl: «¿Quién soy yo, y qué es mi vida, la familia de mi padre en Israel, para que yo sea yerno del rey?».
19
וַיְהִי, בְּעֵת תֵּת אֶת-מֵרַב בַּת-שָׁאוּל--לְדָוִד; וְהִיא נִתְּנָה לְעַדְרִיאֵל הַמְּחֹלָתִי, לְאִשָּׁה.
And it came about, at the time when Merab, Saul’s daughter, should have been given to David, that she was given to Adriel the Meholathite as a wife.note
Y aconteció que, al tiempo en que Merab, hija de Saúl, había de ser dada a David, fue dada por mujer a Adriel el meholatita.
20
וַתֶּאֱהַב מִיכַל בַּת-שָׁאוּל, אֶת-דָּוִד; וַיַּגִּדוּ לְשָׁאוּל, וַיִּשַׁר הַדָּבָר בְּעֵינָיו.
And Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David; and they told Saul, and the thing was right in his eyes.note
Y Mical, hija de Saúl, amaba a David; y se lo dijeron a Saúl, y la cosa fue recta a sus ojos.
21
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶתְּנֶנָּה לּוֹ, וּתְהִי-לוֹ לְמוֹקֵשׁ, וּתְהִי-בוֹ, יַד-פְּלִשְׁתִּים; וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל אֶל-דָּוִד, בִּשְׁתַּיִם תִּתְחַתֵּן בִּי הַיּוֹם.
And Saul said: ‘I will give her to him, that she may be a snare to him, and that the hand of the Philistines may be on him.’ And Saul said to David: ‘By two you will become my son-in-law today.’note
Y Saúl dijo: «Se la daré, para que le sea un lazo, y para que la mano de los filisteos sea contra él». Y Saúl dijo a David: «Por dos serás hoy mi yerno».
22
וַיְצַו שָׁאוּל אֶת-עֲבָדָו, דַּבְּרוּ אֶל-דָּוִד בַּלָּט לֵאמֹר, הִנֵּה חָפֵץ בְּךָ הַמֶּלֶךְ, וְכָל-עֲבָדָיו אֲהֵבוּךָ; וְעַתָּה, הִתְחַתֵּן בַּמֶּלֶךְ.
And Saul commanded his servants: ‘Speak to David in secret, saying, “Look — the king delights in you, and all his servants love you; and now, become the king’s son-in-law.”’note
Y Saúl mandó a sus siervos: «Hablen a David en secreto, diciendo: “Mira, el rey se complace en ti, y todos sus siervos te aman; ahora, pues, hazte yerno del rey”».
23
וַיְדַבְּרוּ עַבְדֵי שָׁאוּל, בְּאָזְנֵי דָוִד, אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים, הָאֵלֶּה; וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, הַנְקַלָּה בְעֵינֵיכֶם הִתְחַתֵּן בַּמֶּלֶךְ, וְאָנֹכִי, אִישׁ-רָשׁ וְנִקְלֶה.
And Saul’s servants spoke these words in David’s ears. And David said: ‘Is it a light thing in your eyes to become the king’s son-in-law, when I am a poor man and lightly esteemed?’note
Y los siervos de Saúl dijeron estas palabras a oídos de David. Y David dijo: «¿Les parece cosa liviana hacerse yerno del rey, siendo yo un hombre pobre y tenido en poco?».
24
וַיַּגִּדוּ עַבְדֵי שָׁאוּל, לוֹ--לֵאמֹר:  כַּדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, דִּבֶּר דָּוִד.
And Saul’s servants told him, saying: ‘In words like these David spoke.’note
Y los siervos de Saúl le informaron, diciendo: «Con palabras como estas habló David».
25
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל כֹּה-תֹאמְרוּ לְדָוִד, אֵין-חֵפֶץ לַמֶּלֶךְ בְּמֹהַר, כִּי בְּמֵאָה עָרְלוֹת פְּלִשְׁתִּים, לְהִנָּקֵם בְּאֹיְבֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ; וְשָׁאוּל חָשַׁב, לְהַפִּיל אֶת-דָּוִד בְּיַד-פְּלִשְׁתִּים.
And Saul said: ‘Thus you shall say to David: “The king has no delight in a bride-price, but in a hundred foreskins of the Philistines, to take vengeance on the king’s enemies.”’ For Saul planned to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines.note
Y Saúl dijo: «Así dirán a David: “El rey no se complace en una dote, sino en cien prepucios de filisteos, para vengarse de los enemigos del rey”». Porque Saúl pensaba hacer caer a David en mano de los filisteos.
26
וַיַּגִּדוּ עֲבָדָיו לְדָוִד, אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, וַיִּשַׁר הַדָּבָר בְּעֵינֵי דָוִד, לְהִתְחַתֵּן בַּמֶּלֶךְ; וְלֹא מָלְאוּ, הַיָּמִים.
And his servants told David these words, and the thing was right in David’s eyes, to become the king’s son-in-law. And the days were not yet full;note
Y sus siervos dijeron estas palabras a David, y la cosa fue recta a los ojos de David, hacerse yerno del rey. Y los días no se habían cumplido;
27
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד וַיֵּלֶךְ הוּא וַאֲנָשָׁיו, וַיַּךְ בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים מָאתַיִם אִישׁ, וַיָּבֵא דָוִד אֶת-עָרְלֹתֵיהֶם, וַיְמַלְאוּם לַמֶּלֶךְ לְהִתְחַתֵּן בַּמֶּלֶךְ; וַיִּתֶּן-לוֹ שָׁאוּל אֶת-מִיכַל בִּתּוֹ, לְאִשָּׁה.  {ס}
and David rose and went, he and his men, and struck down among the Philistines two hundred men; and David brought their foreskins, and they counted them out in full to the king, that he might become the king’s son-in-law. And Saul gave him Michal his daughter as a wife.note
y David se levantó y fue, él y sus hombres, e hirió entre los filisteos a doscientos hombres; y David trajo sus prepucios, y se los contaron completos al rey, para hacerse yerno del rey. Y Saúl le dio por mujer a su hija Mical.
28
וַיַּרְא שָׁאוּל וַיֵּדַע, כִּי יְהוָה עִם-דָּוִד; וּמִיכַל בַּת-שָׁאוּל, אֲהֵבַתְהוּ.
And Saul saw and knew that Jehovah was with David; and Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved him.note
Y Saúl vio y supo que Jehová estaba con David; y Mical, hija de Saúl, lo amaba.
29
וַיֹּאסֶף שָׁאוּל, לֵרֹא מִפְּנֵי דָוִד--עוֹד; וַיְהִי שָׁאוּל אֹיֵב אֶת-דָּוִד, כָּל-הַיָּמִים.  {פ}
And Saul was yet more afraid of David; and Saul was David’s enemy all the days.note
Y Saúl tuvo todavía más miedo de David; y Saúl fue enemigo de David todos los días.
30
וַיֵּצְאוּ, שָׂרֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; וַיְהִי מִדֵּי צֵאתָם, שָׂכַל דָּוִד מִכֹּל עַבְדֵי שָׁאוּל, וַיִּיקַר שְׁמוֹ, מְאֹד.  {ס}
And the commanders of the Philistines went out; and it came about, as often as they went out, that David acted more wisely than all Saul’s servants; and his name became very precious.note
Y salieron los jefes de los filisteos; y aconteció que, cada vez que salían, David actuaba con más acierto que todos los siervos de Saúl; y su nombre llegó a ser muy precioso.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · “The soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David” — Jonathan’s love, a covenant, the prince’s robe and bow, and the verses the Greek does not have

Bound up. “The soul of Jonathan was bound up with the soul of David”: niqsherah, from qashar, to tie or bind. The one other place where a soul is bound up with a soul is Judah’s plea for Benjamin, “his own soul is bound up with the boy’s soul” (Genesis 44:30, already on these pages). The same verb later means to conspire: Saul will say to his own men, “all of you have conspired against me” (22:8). “And Jonathan loved him as his own soul”: the scribes kept two spellings here, written ויאהבו and read ויאהבהו, and both mean “and he loved him”. “As his own soul”, ke-nafsho, is the law’s phrase for the closest of friends, “your friend who is as your own soul” (Deuteronomy 13:7, already on these pages). It is the second love in David’s story: Saul “loved him greatly” first (16:21, already on these pages). The ASV and KJV have “the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David”; the NWT 1984 “very soul became bound up with the soul of David”; the NIV “Jonathan became one in spirit with David”.

A covenant, and a love read three ways. “And Jonathan and David cut a covenant”: karat berit, the verb of cutting that is the usual Hebrew way of making a covenant, as when Abraham and Abimelech “cut a covenant” (Genesis 21:27, already on these pages). The verb is singular, with Jonathan first, and the NIV follows that, “Jonathan made a covenant with David”; the ASV has “Then Jonathan and David made a covenant”, and the Douay “And David and Jonathan made a covenant”. Readers take this love in more than one way: as the language of treaty loyalty, which the note on David’s lament sets out at 2 Samuel 1:26 (already on these pages), “Your love to me was more wonderful than the love of women”; as the deep friendship most traditions have seen in it; and, in some modern readings, as erotic. The Hebrew verb ahav covers all of these and decides none of them.

The robe and the bow. “And Jonathan stripped off the robe that was on him and gave it to David”: the me’il, the robe of Samuel’s childhood (2:19) and the robe that tore at Gilgal (15:27, both already on these pages). The king’s son gives his robe to the man who will have the kingdom. “And his garments”, madav, the word of the garments Saul put on David and David took off again (17:38, already on these pages). “Even to his sword, and to his bow, and to his belt”: on the day of Michmash only Saul and Jonathan had a sword (13:22, already on these pages), and Jonathan gives his away; his bow is the one David will praise over his body, “the bow of Jonathan did not turn back” (2 Samuel 1:22, already on these pages). The ASV has “And Jonathan stripped himself of the robe that was upon him”; the NWT 1984 “stripped himself of the sleeveless coat that was on him”; the Douay “the coat with which he was clothed”; the TLB “and sealed the pact by giving him his robe, sword, bow, and belt”.

The verses the Greek does not have. The Greek as Rahlfs prints it, from the Vatican manuscript, has nothing of verses 1–5, nor verses 10–11, 17–19 or 30, and it is shorter in verses 6, 8, 12, 21, 26 and 29; the notes say where. In that Greek the chapter runs from the battle straight to the women’s song, as in chapter 17 (already on these pages) it ran from Saul’s court straight to the fight. Readers differ, as they do there: the shorter Greek may keep an older form of the story, or a translator may have shortened a story that tells some things twice.

Verse 5 · “David behaved himself wisely” — acting wisely, the verb of Joshua 1, and good in the eyes of all

He acted wisely. “Wherever Saul sent him, he acted wisely”: yaskil, from sakal, to have insight and so to succeed, the verb of Jehovah’s charge to Joshua, “so that you may act wisely wherever you go” (Joshua 1:7, already on these pages). It comes back three more times in this chapter, in verses 14, 15 and 30, and in verse 15 it is what Saul sees and dreads. The ASV has “and behaved himself wisely: and Saul set him over the men of war”; the NWT 1984 “Wherever Saul would send him he would act prudently”; the Douay “he behaved himself prudently”; the NIV “David was so successful”.

In the eyes of all. “And it was good in the eyes of all the people, and also in the eyes of Saul’s servants”: the eyes come back in verse 8, where the women’s song is “evil in his eyes”, and the servants come back in verse 22 as Saul’s go-betweens. The ASV has “and it was good in the sight of all the people, and also in the sight of Saul’s servants”; the KJV “and he was accepted in the sight of all the people”.

Verses 6–7 · “Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands” — timbrels and dances, an instrument of three, and a song that ranks the king second

Timbrels and dances. “The women came out from all the cities of Israel, singing and dancing, to meet King Saul, with timbrels”: women came out so to meet a victory before, Miriam “with timbrels and dances” at the sea (Exodus 15:20) and Jephthah’s daughter “with timbrels and with dances” at Mizpah (Judges 11:34, both already on these pages). “Singing” is a place where the scribes kept two forms: the letters written are לשור, the letters read are לשיר, “to sing”, and the two differ by one letter. “And with lutes”: shalishim, from the number three. As an instrument the word stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one, and its shape is not known: a three-stringed lute, a triangle and a rattle have all been proposed. The same letters elsewhere name a chariot officer, usually explained as the third man in the chariot, “with officers over all of them” (Exodus 14:7, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “with tambourines, with rejoicing and with lutes”; the ASV “with timbrels, with joy, and with instruments of music”; the Geneva “and with rebecks”; the Douay “with timbrels of joy, and cornets”; the NIV “with timbrels and lyres”. The Greek has no first half of the verse, and its women come out “to meet David”, not the king, with cymbals.

The song. “And the women answered one another as they made merry”: va-ta’anenah, they sang in answer, one group to another, and ha-mesachaqot, from sachaq, to laugh or play, the verb of the people who “rose up to make merry” at the calf (Exodus 32:6) and of Samson made to “make sport” in Gaza (Judges 16:25, both already on these pages). “Saul has struck down his thousands, and David his ten thousands”: thousand and ten thousand are a pair Hebrew verse uses often, with the bigger number in the second line, “How could one chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight” (Deuteronomy 32:30) and “A thousand will fall at your side, and ten thousand at your right hand” (Psalm 91:7, both already on these pages). The singers may mean only to praise both men; Saul hears a ranking. The song stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and twice more in the mouths of Philistines (21:12 and 29:5, both now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “Saul has struck down his thousands, And David his tens of thousands”; the ASV “Saul hath slain his thousands, And David his ten thousands”; the KJV “And the women answered one another as they played”; the Geneva “And the women sang by course in their play”.

Verses 8–9 · “What can he have more but the kingdom?” — Saul names the neighbour of 15:28, and an eye that watches

Only the kingship left. “And Saul was very angry, and this word was evil in his eyes”: the people’s eyes found David good in verse 5; the king’s find the song evil. “And what is left for him but the kingship?”: ha-melukhah, the word of “Saul took the kingship over Israel” (14:47, already on these pages). Samuel had told him that Jehovah had torn the kingdom from him “and has given it to a neighbour of yours who is better than you” (15:28, already on these pages); Saul now puts a name to the neighbour. The Greek has no last clause. The ASV has “and what can he have more but the kingdom?”; the NWT 1984 “and there is yet only the kingship to give him!”; the TLB “Next they’ll be making him their king!”; the Douay “They have given David ten thousands, and to me they have given but a thousand”.

Eyeing. “And Saul was eyeing David from that day on”: oyen, a verb made from ayin, eye. As read it stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. Here again the scribes kept two forms: the letters written are עון, which could be read avon, guilt, and the letters read are עוין, “eyeing”; this translation prints the read form. The Greek has hypoblepomenos, looking at him askance. The ASV and KJV have “And Saul eyed David from that day and forward”; the NWT 1984 “was continually looking suspiciously at David”; the Douay “did not look on David with a good eye”.

Verses 10–11 · “An evil spirit from God came mightily upon Saul” — the spirit rushes, Saul prophesies, and the spear is hurled

The spirit rushes. “An evil spirit of God rushed on Saul”: tatslach, from tsalach, the verb with which the spirit of God rushed on Saul among the prophets and at Jabesh (10:10, 11:6) and on David at his anointing (16:13, all already on these pages). Now it is the evil spirit that came on Saul after the spirit of Jehovah left him (16:14, already on these pages). “And he prophesied in the midst of the house”: va-yitnabbe, the same form as “and he prophesied among them” at 10:10, the frenzy of the prophets’ band, here a raving inside the palace. The ASV has “an evil spirit from God came mightily upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house”; the NWT 1984 “so that he behaved like a prophet within the house”; the TLB “he began to rave like a madman”. “And David was playing with his hand, as day by day”: the lyre-player of 16:23 (already on these pages), whose playing used to send the evil spirit away. The NWT 1984 has “while David was playing music with his hand, as in former days”.

The spear. “And the spear was in Saul’s hand”: the chanit, the king’s own weapon, never far from his hand from here on. “And Saul hurled the spear”: va-yatel, from the verb of hetil, to hurl, as “Jehovah hurled a great wind onto the sea” (Jonah 1:4, already on these pages). “I will strike David to the wall”: akkeh ve-David u-va-qir, “I will strike into David and into the wall”, to pin him there. “And David turned aside from before him twice”. The Greek has neither verse; it keeps only the later spear, at 19:9–10 (now on these pages). The ASV has “I will smite David even to the wall. And David avoided out of his presence twice”; the NWT 1984 “I will pin David even to the wall!”; the KJV “And Saul cast the javelin”.

Verses 12–16 · “He went out and came in before the people” — afraid, turned away, a commander of a thousand, dread, and the love of all Israel and Judah

Turned away, and turned away. “And Saul was afraid of David, because Jehovah was with him and had turned away from Saul”: sar, the verb of “the spirit of Jehovah had turned away from Saul” (16:14, already on these pages). “And Saul turned him away from his side”: va-yesirehu, the same verb in its causative form. Jehovah turned away from Saul; Saul turns David away from himself. The Greek has only “And Saul was afraid of David” in verse 12. “And made him his commander of a thousand”: sar elef, the rank to whom Jesse sent his cheeses, “the commander of the thousand” (17:18, already on these pages). The ASV has “Therefore Saul removed him from him, and made him his captain over a thousand”; the NWT 1984 “appointed him as chief of a thousand for him”.

Going out and coming in. “And he went out and came in before the people”: the idiom of leading a people in war and in peace. Moses asked for a man “who will go out before them and who will come in before them” (Numbers 27:17), said at a hundred and twenty “I can no longer go out and come in” (Deuteronomy 31:2), and Caleb at eighty-five was still strong “to go out and to come in” (Joshua 14:11, all already on these pages). It comes again in verse 16, and it is why all Israel and Judah love him. The ASV has “and he went out and came in before the people”; the NIV “and David led the troops in their campaigns”.

Dread, and love. “And Saul saw that he was acting very wisely, and he was in dread of him”: va-yagor, from gur in its sense of dread. The chapter climbs: Saul is afraid (verse 12), in dread (verse 15), yet more afraid (verse 29). “Jehovah was with him”: the servant’s word at 16:18 (already on these pages), said by the narrator now in verses 12, 14 and 28. “But all Israel and Judah loved David”: Israel and Judah are named apart, as they were counted apart at Bezek and Telaim (11:8, 15:4, both already on these pages). The ASV has “he stood in awe of him”; the NWT 1984 “so that he was scared of him”, and of verse 16 “were lovers of David”; the Douay “began to beware of him”.

Verses 17–19 · “Fight Jehovah’s battles” — Merab promised and given away, a valiant man, and “Who am I?”

The promised daughter. “Look — my elder daughter Merab, her I will give you as a wife”: the reward the men of Israel spoke of before the fight, “and will give him his daughter” (17:25, already on these pages), now given with a condition. “Only be a valiant man for me”: ben-chayil, as in “any mighty man or any valiant man” (14:52, already on these pages). “And fight the battles of Jehovah”: milchamot Yehovah stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Abigail’s blessing of David (25:28, now on these pages); spelled shorter, the same two words name the lost Book of the Wars of Jehovah (Numbers 21:14, already on these pages). “For Saul said: Let not my hand be on him, but let the hand of the Philistines be on him”: the plan, said to himself, that he will say again in verses 21 and 25. The ASV has “only be thou valiant for me, and fight Jehovah’s battles”; the NWT 1984 “and fight the wars of Jehovah”; the NIV “only serve me bravely”.

Who am I? “Who am I, and what is my life, my father’s clan in Israel, that I should be son-in-law to the king?”: mi chayyay, “who is my life”. As pointed the word is “my life”; many take it as an old word for one’s kinsfolk, which fits the clan that follows. The question is Saul’s own to Samuel, “and my clan the least of all the clans” (9:21), and Gideon’s, “my clan is the poorest in Manasseh” (Judges 6:15, both already on these pages). The ASV has “Who am I, and what is my life, or my father’s family in Israel”; the NWT 1984 “Who am I and who are my kinsfolk”; the NIV “what is my family or my clan in Israel”.

Given to another. “She was given to Adriel the Meholathite as a wife”: a man of Abel-meholah, the town by which Gideon’s pursuit ran (Judges 7:22, already on these pages). The Hebrew and the Greek of 2 Samuel 21:8 both name “the five sons of Michal … whom she bore to Adriel”; many readers take it as Merab, the daughter this verse gives him, and some manuscripts are reported to read so. The Greek has none of verses 17–19. The ASV has “that she was given unto Adriel the Meholathite to wife”; the NWT 1984 “she herself had already been given”; the Douay “that she was given to Hadriel the Molathite to wife”.

Verses 20–21 · “Michal Saul’s daughter loved David” — a love Saul is glad of, a snare, and “a second time” or “by two”?

Michal loved David. “And Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David”: the last of the four loves counted in the note at 16:21 (already on these pages) promised, after Saul, Jonathan, and all Israel and Judah. In this chapter Jonathan, all Israel and Judah, Michal and Saul’s servants love David; the text never says that David loves anyone. “And the thing was right in his eyes”: va-yishar, the word of Samson’s “she is right in my eyes” (Judges 14:3, already on these pages). The ASV has “And Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved David: and they told Saul, and the thing pleased him”; the NWT 1984 “the matter was to his liking”; the NIV “Now Saul’s daughter Michal was in love with David”.

A snare. “That she may be a snare to him”: moqesh, the word for what Gideon’s ephod became, “a snare to Gideon and to his house” (Judges 8:27, already on these pages). “By two you will become my son-in-law today”: bi-shtayim, “by two”. Readings differ: “a second time”, after Merab; or “by one of the two” daughters; the Greek has no second half of the verse at all. The verb is hitchatten, to make oneself a chatan, a son-in-law by marriage, the alliance the law forbade with the peoples of the land, “you shall not make marriages with them” (Deuteronomy 7:3, already on these pages); it stands in verses 21, 22, 23, 26 and 27. The ASV has “Thou shalt this day be my son-in-law a second time”; the KJV “in the one of the twain”; the NWT 1984 “By [one of] the two women you will form a marriage alliance with me today”; the Douay “In two things thou shalt be my son in law this day”.

Verses 22–24 · “Seemeth it to you a light thing to be a king’s son in law?” — the king’s delight, and a poor man lightly esteemed

The king delights in you. “Speak to David in secret, saying, Look — the king delights in you, and all his servants love you”: chafets, to take pleasure in, the word of Shechem who “delighted in Jacob’s daughter” (Genesis 34:19, already on these pages; see chafets). Verse 25 turns it round: the king has “no delight in a bride-price”. The ASV has “Commune with David secretly, and say, Behold, the king hath delight in thee”; the NWT 1984 “Speak to David secretly”; the NIV “the king likes you”.

Light, and lightly esteemed. “Is it a light thing in your eyes to become the king’s son-in-law, when I am a poor man and lightly esteemed?”: two words that sound alike and come from two roots. Ha-nekallah is from qalal, to be light; niqleh is from qalah, to be held cheap, the verb of the law that limits a flogging “lest … your brother be made light in your eyes” (Deuteronomy 25:3, already on these pages). David means that he has nothing to pay for a king’s daughter, and verse 25 answers him with a price. “In words like these David spoke”: the servants carry the words both ways, from the king to David and back. The ASV and KJV have “seeing that I am a poor man, and lightly esteemed?”; the NWT 1984 “when I am a man of little means and lightly esteemed?”; the Geneva “a poor man and of small reputation”; the NIV “I’m only a poor man and little known”.

Verses 25–27 · “An hundred foreskins of the Philistines” — a bride-price, vengeance, two hundred men, and a count made full before the days were

The bride-price. “The king has no delight in a bride-price”: mohar, the price a bridegroom paid the bride’s father. The noun stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Shechem’s “Pile on me a very great bride-price and gift” (Genesis 34:12), the law of the seduced girl, “he must pay the bride-price for her to become his wife” (Exodus 22:16, both already on these pages), and here. “But in a hundred foreskins of the Philistines”: orlot, the mark of the “uncircumcised Philistine” of David’s own boast (17:36, already on these pages). “To take vengeance on the king’s enemies”: Saul’s oath at Michmash, “until I have taken vengeance on my enemies” (14:24, already on these pages). “For Saul planned to make David fall by the hand of the Philistines”: the third time the plan is told. The ASV has “The king desireth not any dowry, but a hundred foreskins of the Philistines”; the NWT 1984 “The king has delight, not in marriage money, but in a hundred foreskins”; the NIV “The king wants no other price for the bride than a hundred Philistine foreskins”.

Full days, and a full count. “And the days were not yet full”: lo male’u ha-yamim, the time Saul set had not run out. “And they counted them out in full to the king”: va-yemalle’um, the same verb, “they made them full”. Before the days were full, the count was. “Two hundred men”: twice the price. The Greek has “a hundred men”, and it has no “the days were not yet full”; and when David later sends for Michal he says “my wife Michal, whom I betrothed to me for a hundred foreskins of the Philistines” (2 Samuel 3:14). The ASV has “and they gave them in full number to the king”; the KJV “and they gave them in full tale to the king”; the NIV “They counted out the full number to the king”; the Douay “And after a few days David rose up”.

Verses 28–30 · “His name was much set by” — Jehovah with David, Saul his enemy all the days, and a name made precious

Saul saw and knew. “And Saul saw and knew that Jehovah was with David; and Michal, Saul’s daughter, loved him”: the Greek has “and all Israel loved him” in place of Michal. “And Saul was yet more afraid of David; and Saul was David’s enemy all the days”: oyev, enemy, the word Saul used of the Philistines in verse 25, “the king’s enemies”. The Greek has no second half of verse 29. The ASV has “And Saul saw and knew that Jehovah was with David”, and “and Saul was David’s enemy continually”; the NIV “and he remained his enemy the rest of his days”.

A precious name. “And the commanders of the Philistines went out”: sarei Felishtim, a different title from the “lords of the Philistines”, the seranim (5:8, already on these pages). “David acted more wisely than all Saul’s servants”: the fourth time of sakal in the chapter. “And his name became very precious”: va-yiqar shemo, from yaqar, costly or precious, as wisdom is “more precious than corals” (Proverbs 3:15, already on these pages). The Greek has no verse 30. The NWT 1984 has “and his name came to be very precious”; the ASV and KJV “so that his name was much set by”; the Douay “and his name became very famous”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “bound up with” for niqsherah, as in Genesis 44:30; “as his own soul”, as in Deuteronomy 13:7; “cut a covenant” for karat berit, as in Genesis 21:27; “robe” for me’il and “garments” for madav, as in 2:19, 15:27 and 17:38; “acted wisely” for sakal in all four places, as in Joshua 1:7; “timbrels” with “dances”, as in Exodus 15:20 and Judges 11:34; the read forms “singing” and “eyeing”, with the written forms in the notes; “the kingship” for melukhah, as in 14:47; “rushed on” for tsalach, as in 10:10 and 16:13; “hurled”, as in Jonah 1:4; “turned away” for sur in verses 12 and 13 alike; “commander of a thousand”, as in 17:18; “went out and came in”, as in Numbers 27:17; “afraid”, “in dread” and “more afraid” for the three steps of Saul’s fear; “a valiant man” for ben-chayil, as in 14:52; “right in his eyes”, as in Judges 14:3; “snare” for moqesh, as in Judges 8:27; “become son-in-law” for hitchatten throughout; “delight” for chafets in verses 22 and 25 alike; “lightly esteemed”; “bride-price” for mohar, as in Genesis 34:12; “take vengeance”, as in 14:24; and “full” for male in verses 26 and 27 alike.

Echoes paid. The love of 16:21 runs on through Jonathan, all Israel and Judah, and Michal, as 16:21’s note said it would; Judah’s soul bound up with Benjamin’s (Genesis 44:30); the friend as your own soul (Deuteronomy 13:7); the robe of 2:19 and 15:27, Saul’s garments of 17:38, and the only two swords of 13:22; acting wisely as Joshua was told to (Joshua 1:7); Miriam’s timbrels and dances and Jephthah’s daughter (Exodus 15:20; Judges 11:34); thousand and ten thousand from Deuteronomy 32:30 and Psalm 91:7; the neighbour better than Saul (15:28); the spirit rushing (10:10; 11:6; 16:13), the evil spirit and the lyre (16:14–23); Jehovah with David, as the servant said at 16:18; going out and coming in (Numbers 27:17; Deuteronomy 31:2; Joshua 14:11); Israel and Judah counted apart (11:8; 15:4); the reward of 17:25; the least of the clans (9:21; Judges 6:15); Abel-meholah (Judges 7:22); Gideon’s snare (Judges 8:27); delight from Genesis 34; “made light” from Deuteronomy 25:3; the bride-price of Genesis 34 and Exodus 22; the uncircumcised of 17:36; Saul’s vengeance of 14:24; the lords of the Philistines (5:8); and wisdom more precious than corals (Proverbs 3:15).

Echoes planted. The song sung again by Philistines at 21:12 and 29:5 (both now on these pages); the spear thrown again at 19:9–10 and Michal letting David down from a window (19:11–17), both now on these pages; the covenant renewed (20:16–17 and 23:18, both now on these pages); “all of you have conspired against me” (22:8, now on these pages); Abigail’s “the battles of Jehovah” (25:28, now on these pages); the hundred foreskins David names when he takes Michal back (2 Samuel 3:14); and the five sons born to Adriel (2 Samuel 21:8). None of the others is translated here yet.
