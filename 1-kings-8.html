In the seventh month, Ethanim, the elders and all Israel gather and the priests bring the ark of the covenant from the city of David into the inner sanctuary under the cherubim; it holds only the two tablets. The cloud fills the house and the priests cannot stand because of the glory. Solomon blesses the assembly, tells of David’s heart and the house, and prays at the altar with his hands spread: no God like you, the heaven of heavens cannot contain you, and then hear in heaven for the oath, defeat, drought, plague, the foreigner, war and captivity. He blesses the assembly again, offers twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep, hallows the middle of the court, and keeps the feast fourteen days.
The seventh month of a year the text does not give; the house was finished in the eighth month of the eleventh year (1 Kings 6:38).

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–9 · 1 Kings 8 The ark brought up — the feast in Ethanim, the priests and the Levites, a congregation that cannot be counted, the inner sanctuary, the poles, and the two tablets

The month Ethanim. “And all the men of Israel were assembled to King Solomon at the feast, in the month of Ethanim — it is the seventh month”: the Hebrew ha-Ethanim stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, and only here is it a month; in Micah 6:2 the same letters are the “enduring ones, foundations of the earth” (now on these pages). The house was finished “in the eleventh year, in the month of Bul — it is the eighth month” (1 Kings 6:38, now on these pages), so the seventh month of that year had already gone by, and this verse gives no year. The ASV has “at the feast, in the month Ethanim”, the NWT 1984 “in the lunar month of Ethanim in the festival”, the NIV “at the time of the festival in the month of Ethanim”, and the TLB “at the time of the Tabernacle Festival in the month of October”, a month name and a feast name the Hebrew does not give.

Priests, Levites, and the tent. “And all the elders of Israel came, and the priests took up the ark”: the parallel in Chronicles has “and the Levites took up the ark” (2 Chronicles 5:4), and in verse 4 both the priests and the Levites bring things up. “And the tent of meeting, and all the holy vessels that were in the tent”: “tent of meeting” stands in one verse of 1 Kings, this one. The verse does not say which tent it is; David had pitched one for the ark in the city of David (2 Samuel 6:17, now on these pages), and the high place at Gibeon is said to have had Moses’ tent (see high place). The KJV has “the tabernacle of the congregation”, the NIV “the tent of meeting”, and the Douay “the tabernacle of the covenant”.

A congregation, and an assembly. “And King Solomon and all the congregation of Israel who were gathered to him”: the Hebrew word here is edah, “congregation”, and in verses 14, 22, 55 and 65 it is qahal, “assembly” (and the verb “assemble” stands in verses 1 and 2). This translation keeps the two apart, and the versions do not. The ASV keeps them apart as this page does (“congregation” in verse 5, “assembly” in verse 14); the KJV has “congregation” in both; the NWT 1984 has “assembly” in verse 5 and “congregation” in verse 14, the reverse. The sheep and cattle “could not be counted or numbered for multitude”: the NWT 1984 has “could not be counted or numbered”, the NIV “could not be recorded or counted”.

The inner sanctuary, and the wings. “Into the inner sanctuary of the house, the most holy place, under the wings of the cherubim”: the place was prepared “to put there the ark of the covenant of Jehovah” (1 Kings 6:19), and the cherubim are the two of olive wood in the inner sanctuary (1 Kings 6:23, now on these pages). These are not the cherubim of the ark’s own cover (Exodus 25:22, now on these pages): the cover, kapporet, stands in no verse of 1 Kings, and this chapter does not mention it. The ASV has “into the oracle of the house”, the KJV “into the oracle of the house”, the NWT 1984 “to the innermost room of the house”, and the NIV “in the inner sanctuary of the temple”.

The poles. “And they made the poles long, and the ends of the poles were seen from the holy place before the inner sanctuary, but they were not seen outside; and they were there to this day”: the poles were to stay in the rings, “they shall not be removed from it” (Exodus 25:15, now on these pages), so a carrying ark has become a placed one. The verb is a causative, “they made long”, with no subject named. The ASV has “the staves were so long”, the KJV “they drew out the staves”, the NWT 1984 “the poles proved to be long”, and the NIV “these poles were so long”. “To this day” is the narrator’s clause and the verse does not date it; the TLB has “they remain there to this day”.

Only the two tablets. “There was nothing in the ark but the two tablets of stone that Moses had set down there at Horeb”: Moses put the tablets in the ark he had made (Deuteronomy 10:5, now on these pages). The letter to the Hebrews counts more inside it, “a golden jar holding the manna, and Aaron’s rod that budded, and the tablets of the covenant” (Hebrews 9:4, now on these pages); the manna was set “before the Testimony” (Exodus 16:34) and the rod “before the testimony” (Numbers 17:25, 17:10 in English Bibles; both now on these pages). This verse agrees with the older stories and not with the list in Hebrews. The ASV has “nothing in the ark save the two tables of stone”, the NWT 1984 “nothing in the Ark but the two stone tablets”, and the TLB “nothing in the Ark at that time except the two stone tablets”, a time the Hebrew does not give.

Verses 10–13 · 1 Kings 8 The cloud and the glory — the priests who cannot stand, the thick darkness, and a house of habitation

The cloud fills the house. “And the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud; for the glory of Jehovah filled the house of Jehovah”: it is the sentence of the tent, where the cloud covered the tent of meeting and “Moses was not able to enter the tent of meeting, because the cloud settled on it, and the glory of Jehovah filled the tabernacle” (Exodus 40:35, now on these pages). “The glory of Jehovah filled” with a house as its object stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one, 2 Chronicles 5:14 and 7:2, and Ezekiel 44:4. The ASV has “the glory of Jehovah filled the house of Jehovah”, the NWT 1984 “unable to stand to do their ministering”, the NIV “the priests could not perform their service”, and the TLB “The priests have to go outside because the glory of the Lord is filling the entire building”, a verse in the present tense that has the priests leave, where the Hebrew has them unable to stand.

The thick darkness. “Jehovah has said that he would dwell in the thick darkness”: the word, araphel, stands in fifteen verses of the Hebrew Bible. The first is Sinai, where “Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was” (Exodus 20:21, 20:17 in the Hebrew numbering), and it is “darkness, cloud, and thick darkness” at the foot of the mountain (Deuteronomy 4:11, both now on these pages). “To dwell in the thick darkness” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and 2 Chronicles 6:1. Solomon does not say where Jehovah said it. The ASV has “the thick darkness”, the KJV “the thick darkness”, the NIV “a dark cloud”, the Douay “a cloud”, and the NWT 1984 “the thick gloom”.

A house of habitation. “I have surely built you a house of habitation, a fixed place for your dwelling forever”: “a house of habitation” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and 2 Chronicles 6:2. “A fixed place for your dwelling” is the phrase of the song at the sea, “the place, Jehovah, that you made for your dwelling” (Exodus 15:17, now on these pages). The ASV has “a house of habitation, a place for thee to dwell in for ever”, the NWT 1984 “a house of lofty abode”, the NIV “a magnificent temple”, the Douay “to be thy most firm throne for ever”, and the TLB “a lovely home on earth”. The thick darkness and the house are set side by side and the verses do not reconcile them; the prayer takes up the dwelling again at verse 27.

Verses 14–21 · 1 Kings 8 The king blesses the assembly — a promise by mouth and by hand, a city not chosen, a heart that did well, and a son who builds

He turned his face. “And the king turned his face about and blessed all the assembly of Israel; and all the assembly of Israel was standing”: the king has been facing the house, and now faces the people; the prayer proper is later, at the altar (verse 22). The ASV has “blessed all the assembly of Israel”, the KJV “blessed all the congregation of Israel”, and the NIV “the king turned around and blessed them”.

By mouth and by hand. “Who spoke with his mouth to David my father, and with his hand has fulfilled it”: the pair is repeated in the prayer, “you spoke with your mouth, and with your hand you have fulfilled it, as it is this day” (verse 24). What was spoken is in 2 Samuel 7, where Nathan brings the word to David (2 Samuel 7:12–13, now on these pages). The ASV has “who spake with his mouth unto David thy father, and hath with his hand fulfilled it”, the NWT 1984 “who spoke by his own mouth with David my father, and by his own hand has given fulfillment”, and the TLB “who has done today what he promised”.

No city; David. “From the day that I brought my people Israel out of Egypt, I did not choose a city out of all the tribes of Israel to build a house for my name to be there; but I chose David to be over my people Israel”: the choosing of a place for the name is the language of Deuteronomy, “the place that Jehovah your God chooses out of all your tribes, to put his name there” (Deuteronomy 12:5, 12:11, now on these pages), and here the choosing falls first on a man. “And I chose David” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. The ASV has “I chose no city… but I chose David to be over my people Israel”, the NWT 1984 “but I shall choose David”, and the TLB “I didn’t appoint a place for my Temple, but I appointed a man to be my people’s leader”.

“You did well.” “Because it was in your heart to build a house for my name, you did well that it was in your heart”: this is not a sentence of 2 Samuel 7. There David’s wish is a house of cedar for the ark (7:2) and the word that comes is a question, “Will you build me a house for my dwelling?” (7:5, now on these pages); and the verb heitiv, in the form that stands here, stands in no verse of 2 Samuel. Solomon reports the refusal as an approval, and the page does not say where the sentence comes from. “Your son, who comes out of your loins”: the word is chalatsayim, “out of your loins” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one, 2 Chronicles 6:9, and the promise that “kings shall come out of your loins” to Jacob (Genesis 35:11, now on these pages). In 2 Samuel the seed “shall come out of your own body” (7:12), a different word. The ASV has “thou didst well that it was in thy heart”, the NWT 1984 “you did well, because it proved to be close to your heart”, the NIV “You did well to have it in your heart”, and the TLB “I am glad you want to do it”.

A place for the ark. “And I have set there a place for the ark, in which is the covenant of Jehovah”: the ark is the place, and the covenant is “in” it, as the two tablets are (verse 9). The ASV has “wherein is the covenant of Jehovah”, and the NWT 1984 “where the covenant of Jehovah is”.

Verses 22–30 · 1 Kings 8 Solomon at the altar — hands spread, no God like you, the heaven of heavens, eyes open night and day, and a name in the house

Hands toward heaven. “And Solomon stood before the altar of Jehovah in front of all the assembly of Israel, and spread out his hands toward heaven”: “he spread out his hands” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one, its two parallels in 2 Chronicles 6:12–13, and Moses, who “spread out his palms to Jehovah; and the thunders and the hail ceased” (Exodus 9:33, now on these pages). Chronicles adds that Solomon had made a platform in the middle of the court, five cubits by five and three high, and “kneeled down upon his knees” on it (2 Chronicles 6:13), and the Hebrew word for the platform is the word for the laver, kiyyor. Kings has him standing here and kneeling at verse 54. The ASV has “spread forth his hands toward heaven”, the NWT 1984 “spread his palms out to the heavens”, and the NIV “spread out his hands toward heaven”.

No God like you. “Jehovah, God of Israel, there is no God like you”: David had said “there is none like you” in his own prayer (2 Samuel 7:22, now on these pages), and “there is none like you,” said to Jehovah, stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, read out of the hits: 2 Samuel 7:22, here, Jeremiah 10:6–7, Psalm 86:8, and the two Chronicles parallels (none of the others translated here yet). “Keeping the covenant and the kindness”: the phrase stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, Deuteronomy 7:9 (“keeping the covenant and the steadfast love”, now on these pages), this one, 2 Chronicles 6:14 and Daniel 9:4. The ASV has “who keepest covenant and lovingkindness”, the KJV “who keepest covenant and mercy”, the NWT 1984 “keeping the covenant and the loving-kindness”, and the NIV “you who keep your covenant of love”.

“If only your sons.” “There shall not be cut off for you a man from before me, sitting on the throne of Israel, only if your sons keep their way”: the condition is David’s own deathbed charge, “If your sons keep their way, to walk before me in truth with all their heart and with all their soul” (1 Kings 2:4, now on these pages), and it is the “if” of the word that came in the middle of the building (1 Kings 6:12). The ASV has “if only thy children take heed to their way”, the KJV “so that thy children take heed to their way”, the NWT 1984 “if only your sons will take care of their way”, and the NIV “if only your descendants are careful in all they do”.

The heaven of heavens. “But will God indeed dwell on the earth? Look, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain you — how much less this house that I have built!”: “the heaven of heavens” stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 10:14 (“to Jehovah your God belong the heavens and the heavens of the heavens”, now on these pages), this one, 2 Chronicles 2:5 (2:6 in English Bibles) and 6:18, Psalm 148:4 and Nehemiah 9:6; the first four have “and” (“heaven and the heaven of heavens”) and the last two do not. Stephen sets the house and the Most High side by side: “But Solomon built him a house. But the Most High does not dwell in things made with hands” (Acts 7:47–48, now on these pages). The ASV has “behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee”, the NWT 1984 “the heaven of the heavens, themselves cannot contain you”, the NIV “The heavens, even the highest heaven, cannot contain you”, and the TLB “even the skies and the highest heavens cannot contain you”.

Eyes open, and a name. “That your eyes may be open toward this house night and day, toward the place of which you said, ‘My name shall be there’”: the name is Deuteronomy’s, “to make his name dwell there” (Deuteronomy 12:11), and in answer Jehovah says “I have hallowed this house… to put my name there forever; and my eyes and my heart shall be there perpetually” (1 Kings 9:3, now on these pages). The prayer then sends the hearing to heaven: “hear in the place of your dwelling, in heaven” (verse 30). The words for prayer pile up in verse 28, tefillah “prayer”, techinnah “supplication” and rinnah “cry”; the noun techinnah comes back in verses 30, 38, 45, 49, 52 and 54, and the verb from the same root in verses 33, 47 and 59. The ASV has “the cry and… the prayer”, the NWT 1984 “the entreating cry and… the prayer”, and the NIV “his plea for mercy”. “Hear in heaven, the place of your dwelling”: the NIV has “Hear from heaven, your dwelling place”, and the Douay “hear them in the place of thy dwelling in heaven; and when thou hearest, shew them mercy”.

Verses 31–40 · 1 Kings 8 The first cases — an oath at the altar, defeat, no rain, and every plague and every heart

Seven cases. The prayer works through its cases one by one, each opening with “if” or “when” and closing with “then hear in heaven”: the oath (31–32), defeat (33–34), no rain (35–36), famine and plague (37–40), the foreigner (41–43), war (44–45) and captivity (46–53). The count of seven is this page’s reading of the paragraphs, not the text’s own.

An oath at the altar. “If a man sins against his neighbor, and an oath is laid on him to make him swear, and the oath comes before your altar in this house”: the Hebrew is alah, and it stands three times in the verse. The ASV has “an oath be laid upon him to cause him to swear, and he come and swear before thine altar”, the NWT 1984 “actually lays a cursing upon him to bring him under liability to the curse”, the NIV “is required to take an oath”, and the TLB “swears that he didn’t do it”. “Declaring the wicked guilty… and declaring the righteous right” is the language of the court, “the righteous one is declared righteous and the wicked one declared wicked” (Deuteronomy 25:1, now on these pages). The ASV has “condemning the wicked… justifying the righteous” and the NWT 1984 “pronouncing the wicked one wicked… pronouncing the righteous one righteous”.

Defeat, and no rain. “When your people Israel are struck down before an enemy because they sin against you, and they turn back to you and confess your name”: the sequence of sin, defeat, confession and return is the sequence of Leviticus, “if they confess their iniquity… if then their uncircumcised heart is humbled” (Leviticus 26:40–41, now on these pages). “When the heavens are shut up and there is no rain”: it is the sky “like iron” of Leviticus 26:19 and “your skies that are over your head will be bronze” of Deuteronomy 28:23, now on these pages. “When you afflict them” is the shelf’s reading throughout: the ASV has “when thou dost afflict them”, the KJV “when thou afflictest them”, the NWT 1984 “because you kept afflicting them”, and the NIV “because you have afflicted them”. “Bring them back to the ground that you gave to their fathers”: the Hebrew has adamah, “ground”, here and in verse 40, and erets, “land”, in verse 36, “give rain on your land”; the ASV has “the land” in all three, and the NWT 1984 has “the ground” in verses 34 and 40 and “your land” in verse 36.

The list of ills. “If there is famine in the land, if there is pestilence, if there is blight, mildew, locust, caterpillar”: blight, shiddafon, stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, and mildew, yerakon, stands beside it in each of them: Deuteronomy 28:22 (now on these pages), this one, Amos 4:9, Haggai 2:17 and 2 Chronicles 6:28. The list is the curse of Deuteronomy turned into an occasion for prayer. “Locust, caterpillar” with no “and” between stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible; Chronicles has “locust and caterpillar”. “In the land of their gates” is the idiom for the land of their cities: the ASV has “in the land of their cities”, the NWT 1984 “in the land of their gates”, and the KJV “in the land of their cities”. The KJV has “blasting, mildew, locust, or… caterpiller” and the NIV “blight or mildew, locusts or grasshoppers”.

The plague of his heart. “Who each know the plague of his own heart, and spread out their hands toward this house”: the parallel has “each man his plague and his pain” (2 Chronicles 6:29). “Whose heart you know — for you, you alone, know the heart of all the sons of man”: it is the claim that Jehovah “sees into the heart” (1 Samuel 16:7, now on these pages), here made the ground for giving “to each man according to all his ways”. The ASV has “thou only knowest the hearts of all the children of men”, the NWT 1984 “you yourself alone well know the heart of all the sons of mankind”, and the NIV “you alone know every human heart”. The fear of Jehovah is the purpose: “so that they may fear you all the days that they live on the face of the ground”.

Verses 41–45 · 1 Kings 8 The foreigner who comes from a far land, and the people who go out to war

The foreigner. “And also to the foreigner, who is not of your people Israel, and comes from a far land for your name’s sake”: the word is nokhri, the one who belongs elsewhere, and not the ger who lives among Israel. “The foreigner,” singular, with the article, stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible when the feminine plurals of 1 Kings 11:8, Ezra 10:11 and Nehemiah 13:26 are left out: Deuteronomy 15:3 and 29:21, these two verses, and 2 Chronicles 6:32–33. In Deuteronomy the foreigner “who comes from a distant land” is one of those who will see the plagues of the land and ask, “Why has Jehovah done this to this land?” (Deuteronomy 29:21–23, 29:22–24 in English Bibles, now on these pages), and Jehovah’s answer to this prayer comes back to the question (1 Kings 9:8–9, now on these pages). Here the same foreigner has come to pray. Isaiah has the foreigner joined to Jehovah and the house “a house of prayer for all peoples” (Isaiah 56:3, 6–7). The ASV has “the foreigner”, the KJV “a stranger”, the NWT 1984 “the foreigner”, and the NIV “the foreigner who does not belong to your people Israel”.

The name called on the house. “For they will hear of your great name and your strong hand and your outstretched arm”: the “strong hand” and “outstretched arm” are the Exodus formula (Deuteronomy 4:34), and the purpose is the knowing: “so that all the peoples of the earth may know your name, to fear you as your people Israel do, and to know that your name is called on this house that I have built”. The ASV has “thine outstretched arm”, the NWT 1984 “your stretched-out arm”, the NIV “your outstretched arm”, and the Douay “thy stretched out arm”. “Is called on this house” is the ASV’s “this house which I have built is called by my name”, the NWT 1984’s “your name itself has been called upon this house”, and the NIV’s “this house I have built bears your Name”.

Toward the city. “If your people go out to war against their enemy, by the way you send them, and they pray to Jehovah in the direction of the city that you have chosen and the house that I have built for your name”: the prayer is turned toward the city; the same direction comes back in verse 48. The ASV has “toward the city which thou hast chosen”, the KJV “toward the city which thou hast chosen”, the NWT 1984 “in the direction of the city that you have chosen”, and the Douay “towards the way of the city, which thou hast chosen”. “And do their justice” is the ASV’s “maintain their cause”, the NWT 1984’s “execute judgment for them”, and the TLB’s “hear their prayer and help them”.

Verses 46–53 · 1 Kings 8 The captivity — no man who does not sin, a heart brought back, the iron furnace, and a people set apart

No man who does not sin. “If they sin against you — for there is no man who does not sin — and you are angry with them and give them over before an enemy”: “a man who does not sin” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and 2 Chronicles 6:36. Ecclesiastes says it with a righteous man who does good (Ecclesiastes 7:20). The ASV has “there is no man that sinneth not”, the KJV “there is no man that sinneth not”, the NWT 1984 “there is no man that does not sin”, and the TLB “and who doesn’t?”.

Brought back to the heart. “And they bring it back to their heart in the land where they were taken captive”: the Hebrew, heshivu el libbam, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible in this form, with 2 Chronicles 6:37 reading levavam, and the verb with “heart” is Deuteronomy’s, “take it to your heart” (Deuteronomy 4:39) and “you take them to heart among all the nations where Jehovah your God has driven you, and return to Jehovah your God… with all your heart and all your soul, then Jehovah your God will turn your captivity and have compassion on you” (Deuteronomy 30:1–3, both now on these pages). Verses 48 and 50 follow it point by point: the return with “all their heart and with all their soul”, and “give them to compassion before their captors”. The ASV has “yet if they shall bethink themselves”, the KJV “yet if they shall bethink themselves”, the NWT 1984 “and they indeed come to their senses”, and the NIV “and if they have a change of heart”.

Three verbs. “We have sinned and done wrong, we have acted wickedly”: the three verbs are chatanu, he’evinu and rasha’nu, and the middle one, he’evinu, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one, 2 Chronicles 6:37 and Psalm 106:6, “We have sinned with our fathers; we have done wrong, we have acted wickedly” (neither of the others translated here yet), and the three verbs come together in each. The ASV has “We have sinned, and have done perversely, we have dealt wickedly”, the KJV “We have sinned, and have done perversely, we have committed wickedness”, the NWT 1984 “We have sinned and erred, we have acted wickedly”, and the NIV “We have sinned, we have done wrong, we have acted wickedly”.

The iron furnace; set apart. “For they are your people and your inheritance, whom you brought out of Egypt, from the midst of the iron furnace”: “the iron furnace” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, Deuteronomy 4:20 (“Jehovah has taken, and brought out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be to him a people of inheritance”, now on these pages), this one, and Jeremiah 11:4. “For you set them apart for yourself as an inheritance from all the peoples of the earth”: Jehovah says it of himself, “who has set you apart from the peoples” (Leviticus 20:24, now on these pages), and here Solomon says it back. The ASV has “thou didst separate them from among all the peoples of the earth”, the KJV “thou didst separate them from among all the people of the earth”, the NWT 1984 “you yourself separated them as your inheritance”, and the NIV “you singled them out”. The prayer closes by naming him, Adonai Jehovah: the ASV has “O Lord Jehovah” and the NWT 1984 “O Sovereign Lord Jehovah”.

Verses 54–61 · 1 Kings 8 He rises from his knees — rest given, not one word fallen, a heart whole, and Jehovah, he is God

From his knees. “He rose from before the altar of Jehovah, from kneeling on his knees, with his hands spread out toward heaven”: at verse 22 he stood with his hands spread; the kneeling is told only at the end. The ASV has “from kneeling on his knees with his hands spread forth toward heaven”, the NWT 1984 “from bending down upon his knees”, and the Douay “for he had fixed both knees on the ground”. This blessing is the second, after the one at verse 14.

Rest, and not one word. “Blessed be Jehovah, who has given rest to his people Israel according to all that he spoke; not one word has fallen of all his good word”: menuchah, “rest”, stands in one verse of 1 Kings, this one. “Not one word has fallen” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: the closing formula of the conquest, “Not one word failed of all the good thing that Jehovah had spoken to the house of Israel” (Joshua 21:45) and Joshua’s deathbed “not one word has failed of all the good things” (Joshua 23:14, both now on these pages), and this one. Solomon’s “all his good word” is singular, as in Joshua 21:45, and Joshua 23:14 has the plural. The ASV has “there hath not failed one word of all his good promise”, the NWT 1984 “There has not failed one word of all his good promise”, the NIV “Not one word has failed of all the good promises”, and the TLB “not one word has failed of all the wonderful promises”.

Be with us. “May Jehovah our God be with us as he was with our fathers; let him not leave us or forsake us”: the promise of Deuteronomy, “He will not let go of you and he will not forsake you” (Deuteronomy 31:6, 31:8), is turned here into a wish. “To turn our hearts to him”: the ASV has “that he may incline our hearts unto him”, the NWT 1984 “so as to incline our heart to himself”, and the NIV “May he turn our hearts to him”. “As each day requires” is the ASV’s “as every day shall require” and the NIV’s “according to each day’s need”.

Jehovah, he is God. “So that all the peoples of the earth may know that Jehovah, he is God; there is no other”: “Jehovah, he is God” stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 4:35 and 4:39 (“there is no other besides him”, now on these pages), this one, the people at Carmel (1 Kings 18:39) and Manasseh, who “prayed to him, and he was moved by him, and heard his supplication, and brought him back to Jerusalem, to his kingdom” and knew it (2 Chronicles 33:13; neither of the last two translated here yet). The ASV has “that Jehovah, he is God; there is none else”, the NWT 1984 “that Jehovah is the [true] God. There is no other”, and the NIV “that the Lord is God and that there is no other”.

A heart whole. “And let your heart be whole with Jehovah our God, to walk in his statutes and to keep his commandments, as at this day”: the speech turns from “us” and “our” to “you”, and the one heart is plural in the Hebrew suffix. “As at this day” answers the first one, “as it is this day” (verse 24), where the promise was kept; here it is a duty. Of this heart Solomon himself will be said, “his heart was not whole with Jehovah his God, as was the heart of David his father” (1 Kings 11:4). The ASV has “Let your heart therefore be perfect with Jehovah our God”, the KJV “Let your heart therefore be perfect”, the NWT 1984 “YOUR heart must prove to be complete”, the NIV “may your hearts be fully committed”, and the Douay “Let our hearts also be perfect”.

Verses 62–66 · 1 Kings 8 The sacrifices and the feast — a hundred and forty-two thousand animals, the middle of the court, fourteen days, and the people sent home

The numbers. “Twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep”: together a hundred and forty-two thousand animals, and over fourteen days (verse 65) about ten thousand a day, if they were spread evenly, which the verses do not say. The numbers are the same in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 7:5). They are peace offerings, shelamim, of which only the fat goes to the altar and the rest is a shared meal. The ASV has “two and twenty thousand oxen, and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep”, the NWT 1984 “twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep”, the NIV “twenty-two thousand cattle and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep and goats”, and the TLB “a total of 22,000 oxen and 120,000 sheep and goats”.

Dedicated. “And the king and all the children of Israel dedicated the house of Jehovah”: the verb, chanak, “dedicate”, stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: the man who has built a house and not yet dedicated it (Deuteronomy 20:5), the child to be trained (Proverbs 22:6), both now on these pages, this one, and 2 Chronicles 7:5. The ASV has “dedicated the house of Jehovah”, the KJV “dedicated the house of the LORD”, the NWT 1984 “inaugurate the house of Jehovah”, and the TLB “dedicated the Temple”.

The middle of the court. “On that day the king hallowed the middle of the court that was before the house of Jehovah — for there he made the burnt offering and the grain offering and the fat of the peace offerings; for the bronze altar that was before Jehovah was too small to hold” them: “the bronze altar” stands in one verse of 1 Kings, this one. The chapters of the building do not list it among the work of Hiram (1 Kings 7:40, now on these pages); the altar of 7:48 is the golden one. It appears here already standing, and too small. It is the altar that Ahaz moves from before the house (2 Kings 16:14). The ASV has “the brazen altar that was before Jehovah was too little”, the NWT 1984 “the copper altar that is before Jehovah was too small”, the NIV “the bronze altar that stood before the Lord was too small”, and the Geneva “the brazen altar… was too little to receive”.

Lebo-hamath to the Brook of Egypt. “A great assembly, from Lebo-hamath to the Brook of Egypt”: the north and the south limits of the land (see Hamath and the Brook of Egypt). The ASV has “from the entrance of Hamath unto the brook of Egypt”, the KJV “from the entering in of Hamath unto the river of Egypt”, the NWT 1984 “from the entering in of Hamath down to the torrent valley of Egypt”, the NIV “from Lebo Hamath to the Wadi of Egypt”, and the Douay “from the entrance of Emath to the river of Egypt”.

Seven and seven; the eighth day. “Seven days and seven days — fourteen days. On the eighth day he sent the people away”: Chronicles separates the two sevens, “they kept the dedication of the altar seven days, and the feast seven days”, and “on the eighth day they held a solemn assembly”, and says the people were sent away “on the three and twentieth day of the seventh month” (2 Chronicles 7:8–10). If the dedication came first, as there, the days run 8th to 14th, then 15th to 21st (the Feast of Booths begins on the fifteenth, Leviticus 23:34), the eighth day is the 22nd, and the people leave on the 23rd; Kings gives only the count. Booths ends on its eighth day with a solemn assembly (Leviticus 23:36, now on these pages). The ASV has “On the eighth day he sent the people away”, the NIV “On the following day he sent the people away”, and the TLB “Afterwards Solomon sent the people home”, a sentence with no day in it.

To their tents. “And they went to their tents, rejoicing and glad of heart for all the goodness that Jehovah had done for David his servant and for Israel his people”: “to their tents” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and 2 Chronicles 7:10. The ASV has “went unto their tents joyful and glad of heart”, the NIV “went home, joyful and glad in heart”, and the Douay “went to their dwellings rejoicing”. It is the idiom of a people dismissed, and in the same book the same people go the same way at the division: “to your tents, O Israel… So Israel departed unto their tents” (1 Kings 12:16).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “congregation” for edah (verse 5) and “assembly” for qahal (verses 14, 22, 55, 65); “the inner sanctuary” and “the most holy place”, as in 1 Kings 6:19; “the holy place”; “the thick darkness” for araphel; “a fixed place for your dwelling” for makhon leshivtekha, as in Exodus 15:17; “the heaven of heavens”, as in Deuteronomy 10:14; “kindness” for chesed, as in 1 Kings 3:6; “the foreigner” for nokhri; “the ground” for adamah and “the land” for erets; “burnt offering”, “grain offering” and “peace offerings”; “dedicated” for chanak; “Lebo-hamath”, as at Numbers 13; and “Jehovah, he is God”, as in Deuteronomy 4:35; with the formulas that differ in the notes.

Echoes paid. The finished house and the seventh month after the eighth (1 Kings 6:38); the inner sanctuary and the olive-wood cherubim (6:19, 6:23); the poles that were not to be removed (Exodus 25:15); the two tablets in the ark (Deuteronomy 10:5; Hebrews 9:4); the tent of meeting and David’s tent (2 Samuel 6:17); the cloud and the glory that filled the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34–35); the thick darkness of Sinai (Exodus 20:21; Deuteronomy 4:11); the place made for his dwelling (Exodus 15:17); the place for the name (Deuteronomy 12:5, 11); Nathan’s word and David’s wish (2 Samuel 7:2, 5, 12); the loins of Jacob (Genesis 35:11); “there is none like you” (2 Samuel 7:22); “the covenant and the kindness” (Deuteronomy 7:9); David’s deathbed “if” (1 Kings 2:4; 6:12); the heaven of heavens (Deuteronomy 10:14; Acts 7:47–48); Moses’ spread hands (Exodus 9:33); the court’s verbs (Deuteronomy 25:1); the confession of Leviticus 26, the sky of iron and bronze (Leviticus 26:19; Deuteronomy 28:22–23); the foreigner from a far land (Deuteronomy 29:21–23); the heart that Jehovah sees (1 Samuel 16:7); the heart brought back and the compassion on captives (Deuteronomy 4:39; 30:1–3); the iron furnace and the inheritance (Deuteronomy 4:20); the people set apart (Leviticus 20:24); not one word fallen (Joshua 21:45; 23:14); “he will not forsake you” (Deuteronomy 31:6, 8); “Jehovah, he is God” (Deuteronomy 4:35, 39); the house dedicated (Deuteronomy 20:5); the Feast of Booths and its eighth day (Leviticus 23:34, 36); and the goodness done to David (2 Samuel 7).

Echoes planted. Jehovah’s answer to this prayer, “I have heard your prayer and your supplication… I have hallowed this house”, with its condition and its warning that every passer-by will ask, “Why has Jehovah done this to this land and to this house?” (1 Kings 9:3–9, now on these pages); the heart “not whole” of Solomon (1 Kings 11:4); the bronze altar that Ahaz moves (2 Kings 16:14); the people sent “to your tents, O Israel” at the division (1 Kings 12:16); the people’s “Jehovah, he is God” at Carmel (1 Kings 18:39); and the Chronicler’s version of the prayer and the fire that falls (2 Chronicles 6–7), none of the others translated here yet.

Next in this book: 1 Kings 9 — Jehovah appears to Solomon a second time, as he had at Gibeon, and answers: “I have heard your prayer and your supplication”; the house is hallowed, with a condition and a warning; then twenty years end, Hiram is given twenty cities in Galilee and calls them Cabul, the levy and the forced labor are counted, Pharaoh’s daughter goes up to her house, Solomon offers three times a year, and a fleet at Ezion-geber brings four hundred and twenty talents of gold from Ophir.
