The wilderness and the dry land are glad and blossom like the chavatselet, the glory of Lebanon given to them; the slack hands and stumbling knees are told to be strong, and the hasty of heart to fear not, for God comes with vengeance and recompense and saves; the eyes of the blind open, the lame leap, the tongue of the mute cries out, and waters burst out of the wilderness, turning the burning sand to a pool and the jackals’ abode to reed; a highway, the Way of Holiness, on which no unclean passes and no lion goes, where the redeemed walk and the ransomed of Jehovah come to Zion with singing, gladness overtaking them and sorrow and sighing fleeing.
Undated in the book; it stands in the first half of Isaiah, after the desolation of chapter 34 (the same ‘abode of jackals’ is there) and before the Hezekiah chapters. ⚠ Nothing in the chapter names a place or a king, and where the redeemed return from is not said.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Isaiah 35:1–2 The wilderness is glad — it blossoms — the glory of Lebanon is given to it — they will see the glory of Jehovah

⚠ An extra letter. The first word, yesusum, “will be glad”, ends in -um, “them”, and the next word, midbar, “wilderness”, begins with the same m. The shelf splits on it: the KJV has “The wilderness and the solitary place shall be glad for them”, with the “them” printed, and the ASV “The wilderness and the dry land shall be glad”, the NIV “The desert and the parched land will be glad” and the NWT 1984 “The wilderness and the waterless region will exult” leave it out. This translation leaves it out, and says here that the Hebrew has it.

⭐ The chavatselet. The flower’s name stands in two verses, here and Song of Solomon 2:1, where the beloved calls herself chavatselet haSharon, and what it is has never been settled. The KJV and the ASV print “blossom as the rose”, the Geneva “flourish as the rose”, the Douay “shall flourish like the lily”, the NIV “Like the crocus”, the NWT 1984 “blossom as the saffron” and the TLB “the desert will blossom with flowers”; so the word is left as it is.

⭐ Glory given, and seen. “the glory of Lebanon” stands in two verses, here and Isaiah 60:13 (now on these pages), and the glory is given, nittan, to the dry land: the KJV has “the glory of Lebanon shall be given unto it, the excellency of Carmel and Sharon” and the NIV “The glory of Lebanon will be given to it, the splendor of Carmel and Sharon”. “the splendor of our God” stands in this one verse: the ASV has “they shall see the glory of Jehovah, the excellency of our God” and the Douay “they shall see the glory of the Lord, and the beauty of our God”. The seeing is Isaiah 40:5’s, “all flesh together shall see it” (now on these pages). The word for joy at verse 2 is ranen, and it will come back at verses 6 and 10: the KJV has “rejoice even with joy and singing” and the NIV “it will rejoice greatly and shout for joy”.

Verses 3–4 · Isaiah 35:3–4 Strengthen the slack hands — say to the hasty of heart — behold your God — vengeance, and the recompense of God

⭐ Hands and knees. “slack hands” stands in two verses, here and Job 4:3, and “knees that stumble” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Strengthen ye the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees”, the NIV “Strengthen the feeble hands, steady the knees that give way”, the NWT 1984 “make the knees that are wobbling firm”, the Geneva “comfort the feeble knees” and the Douay “confirm the weak knees”. The line is taken up in the letter to the Hebrews (Hebrews 12:12, now on these pages), and it is said to someone, not to the land: from here the chapter is about people.

⭐ The hasty of heart. “the hasty of heart”, nimharei lev, stands in this one verse, people whose heart races ahead of them. The shelf prints “a fearful heart” (KJV: “Say to them that are of a fearful heart”; NIV: “say to those with fearful hearts”), “anxious at heart” (NWT 1984: “Say to those who are anxious at heart”), and the Douay “Say to the fainthearted”; the TLB has “Encourage those who are afraid”.

⭐ Behold your God. “behold your God” stands in two verses, here and Isaiah 40:9 (now on these pages), where it is said to the cities of Judah. The verse puts vengeance and recompense in apposition to the coming: “vengeance comes” stands in this one verse, and “the recompense of God” stands in this one verse. ⚠ The shelf is not agreed on what they are. The KJV has “your God will come with vengeance, even God with a recompence”, the ASV “your God will come with vengeance, with the recompense of God”, the NIV “with divine retribution he will come to save you”, the NWT 1984 “with vengeance itself, God even with a repayment”, the Douay “your God will bring the revenge of recompense” and the TLB “your God is coming to destroy your enemies”. In every one of them, what follows is the same: he himself comes, and will save you.

Verses 5–7 · Isaiah 35:5–7 The eyes of the blind are opened — the lame leap — waters burst out — the abode of jackals

⭐ Blind, deaf, lame, mute. “the eyes of the blind will be opened” stands in this one verse, and so does “the lame will leap like a deer”, and “the tongue of the mute will cry out”. The KJV has “Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf shall be unstopped” and the Geneva “the eyes of the blind be lightened, and the ears of the deaf be opened”. At verse 6 the KJV has “Then shall the lame man leap as an hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing”, the NIV “Then will the lame leap like a deer, and the mute tongue shout for joy”, the NWT 1984 “the lame one will climb up just as a stag does” and the Douay “the tongue of the dumb shall be free”. The cry is ranan, the root of ranen at verse 2.

⭐ Answered, in the Gospels. When John the Baptist’s men ask whether Jesus is the one, he points to these verses: “the blind receive sight and the lame walk… the deaf hear” (Matthew 11:5, Luke 7:22, with Matthew 11’s note that sets out the Hebrew), and a crowd says of the deaf man and the mute, “he even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak” (Mark 7:37); the man in the temple stands, “walking and leaping and praising God” (Acts 3:8). All four are now on these pages.

⭐ Water in the waste. “waters have burst out in the wilderness” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “in the wilderness shall waters break out, and streams in the desert”, the NIV “Water will gush forth in the wilderness and streams in the desert” and the NWT 1984 “waters will have burst out”. The ground changes name: the sharav, the burning, mirage-ground, becomes a pool (verse 7), and the thirsty land, tsimma’on, springs; the KJV has “the parched ground shall become a pool”, the ASV “the glowing sand shall become a pool”, the NIV “The burning sand will become a pool” and the NWT 1984 “the heat-parched ground will have become as a reedy pool”. “in the abode of jackals, where they lay” stands in this one verse.

⚠ Dragons, or jackals? The animal is tannim. The KJV has “in the habitation of dragons”, the Geneva “in the habitation of dragons” and the Douay “In the dens where dragons dwell”; the ASV has “in the habitation of jackals”, the NIV “In the haunts where jackals once lay”, the NWT 1984 “In the abiding place of jackals” and the TLB “Where desert jackals lived”. This translation prints “jackals”. The place where the animals lay down becomes grass, and the grass is reed and papyrus, which grow only where there is water.

Verses 8–9 · Isaiah 35:8–9 A highway, the Way of Holiness — the unclean do not pass — no lion — the redeemed walk

⭐ The highway. “highway”, maslul, stands in this one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and “the Way of Holiness” stands in this one verse: the verse has two words for road, maslul and derekh, and the name is the second. The KJV has “it shall be called The way of holiness”, the NIV “it will be called the Way of Holiness”, the NWT 1984 “the Way of Holiness it will be called”, the Douay “it shall be called the holy way” and the TLB “it will be named “The Holy Highway.””. The road itself is Isaiah 40:3’s highway for our God and Isaiah 11:16’s for the remnant, both now on these pages. “the unclean will not pass over it” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “the unclean shall not pass over it” and the NIV “The unclean will not journey on it”.

⚠ For whom, and who strays? The end of verse 8, vehu lamo holekh derekh ve’evilim lo yit’u, is difficult, and the shelf shows it. Vehu lamo is “and it is for them” or “and he is for them”: the KJV has “but it shall be for those: the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein”, the ASV “but it shall be for the redeemed: the wayfaring men, yea fools, shall not err therein”, the NIV “it will be for those who walk on that Way”, the Geneva “for he shall be with them, and walk in the way, and the fools shall not err” and the TLB “God will walk there with you; even the most stupid cannot miss the way”. And whether the fools stray or are kept off it: the NIV has “wicked fools will not go about on it” and the NWT 1984 “no foolish ones will wander about”. This translation prints “whoever walks the way; and fools will not stray” and marks that the verse can be read either way.

⭐ No lion. The KJV has “No lion shall be there, nor any ravenous beast shall go up thereon” and the NWT 1984 “the rapacious sort of wild beasts will not come up on it”; the other peace of the wild is Isaiah 11:6–9 (now on these pages), where the beasts are not kept off but changed. The ones who walk are ge’ulim: the KJV has “the redeemed shall walk there”, the NIV “only the redeemed will walk there”, the Douay “they shall walk there that shall be delivered” and the NWT 1984 “the repurchased ones must walk”.

Verse 10 · Isaiah 35:10 The ransomed of Jehovah return to Zion — everlasting joy upon their heads — sorrow and sighing flee

⭐ Redeemed, ransomed. The verse names them again with another root: pedu’ei Yehovah, from padah, against ge’ulim from ga’al in verse 9. The KJV has “the ransomed of the Lord shall return”, the ASV “the ransomed of Jehovah shall return”, the Geneva “the redeemed of the Lord shall return”, the NIV “those the Lord has rescued will return” and the NWT 1984 “the very ones redeemed by Jehovah will return”; the NWT 1984 prints “redeemed” here and “repurchased” at verse 9. The two roots stand side by side in Jeremiah 31:11, “ransomed Jacob and redeemed him” (now on these pages). The Name is written twice in the chapter, at verses 2 and 10.

⭐ Coming to Zion. They come berinnah, with the cry of joy that is the third rnn of the chapter (verses 2, 6, 10): the KJV has “come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads”, the ASV “come with singing unto Zion”, the NIV “They will enter Zion with singing; everlasting joy will crown their heads”, the NWT 1984 “come to Zion with a joyful cry” and the Geneva “come to Zion with praise”. “everlasting joy upon their heads” stands in two verses, this and Isaiah 51:11, which repeats the rest of the verse with small differences (yeshuvun there, yassigun there, and no “and” before nasu); and Isaiah 61:7 has it a third time (now on these pages).

⭐ One pursues, one flees. The last line has the two verbs the other way round from what the English suggests: gladness and joy overtake them (yassigu), and sorrow and sighing flee (nasu). The KJV has “they shall obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing shall flee away”, the NIV “Gladness and joy will overtake them, and sorrow and sighing will flee away” and the NWT 1984 “To exultation and rejoicing they will attain, and grief and sighing must flee away”; the Geneva and the Douay have “sorrow and mourning shall flee away”, and the TLB “all sorrow and all sighing will be gone forever”. The last line is taken up at the end of the New Testament: “neither will there be mourning nor crying nor pain any more” (Revelation 21:4, with Revelation 7:17, both now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “wilderness” for midbar and “desert plain” for aravah at verses 1 and 6, with “dry land” for tsiyyah at verse 1; “glory” for kavod and “splendor” for hadar at verse 2; the chavatselet left as a word; “vengeance” and “recompense” for naqam and gemul at verse 4; “jackals” for tannim; “redeemed” for ge’ulim (verse 9) and “ransomed” for pedu’ei (verse 10); the cry of joy, rnn, at verses 2, 6 and 10. Jehovah is written twice, at verses 2 and 10, and Elohim at verses 2 and 4.

Echoes paid. Isaiah 40:3, 40:5 and 40:9; Isaiah 11:6–9 and 11:16; Isaiah 61:7; Hebrews 12:12; Matthew 11:5; Luke 7:22; Mark 7:37; Acts 3:8; Jeremiah 31:11; Revelation 7:17 and 21:4.

Echoes planted. Isaiah 51:11; Isaiah 60:13 (now on these pages); Job 4:3; Song of Solomon 2:1. The others are yet to come in this translation.

⚠ A note on order: Isaiah stands on these pages at chapters 1, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14, 26, 35, 40, 41, 43, 49, 53, 54, 55, 58, 60 and 61, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Isaiah 2.
