Abijam reigns three years in Judah and Asa forty-one. Asa puts out the consecrated men and the idols and removes Maacah as queen mother, though the high places stay. When Baasha of Israel builds Ramah, Asa sends the temple’s silver and gold to Ben-hadad of Damascus, who strikes the north, and Judah carries Ramah away. In Israel, Nadab reigns two years; Baasha kills him at Gibbethon and destroys the house of Jeroboam.
From the eighteenth year of Jeroboam (1 Kings 15:1) to the third year of Asa for Baasha (15:33), with Asa’s forty-one years told ahead of time.

1
וּבִשְׁנַת שְׁמֹנֶה עֶשְׂרֵה, לַמֶּלֶךְ יָרָבְעָם בֶּן-נְבָט, מָלַךְ אֲבִיָּם, עַל-יְהוּדָה.
And in the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam son of Nebat, Abijam reigned over Judah.note
Y en el año dieciocho del rey Jeroboam hijo de Nabat, Abiam reinó sobre Judá.
2
שָׁלֹשׁ שָׁנִים, מָלַךְ בִּירוּשָׁלִָם; וְשֵׁם אִמּוֹ, מַעֲכָה בַּת-אֲבִישָׁלוֹם.
Three years he reigned in Jerusalem; and his mother’s name was Maacah daughter of Abishalom.note
Tres años reinó en Jerusalén; y el nombre de su madre era Maaca hija de Abisalom.
3
וַיֵּלֶךְ, בְּכָל-חַטֹּאות אָבִיו אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה לְפָנָיו; וְלֹא-הָיָה לְבָבוֹ שָׁלֵם עִם-יְהוָה אֱלֹהָיו, כִּלְבַב דָּוִד אָבִיו.
And he walked in all the sins of his father which he had done before him; and his heart was not whole with Jehovah his God, like the heart of David his father.note
Y anduvo en todos los pecados de su padre que había hecho antes de él; y su corazón no fue íntegro con Jehová su Dios, como el corazón de David su padre.
4
כִּי לְמַעַן דָּוִד, נָתַן יְהוָה אֱלֹהָיו לוֹ נִיר בִּירוּשָׁלִָם, לְהָקִים אֶת-בְּנוֹ אַחֲרָיו, וּלְהַעֲמִיד אֶת-יְרוּשָׁלִָם.
But for David’s sake Jehovah his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem, to raise up his son after him and to establish Jerusalem;note
Pero por amor a David, Jehová su Dios le dio una lámpara en Jerusalén, para levantar a su hijo después de él y establecer a Jerusalén;
5
אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה דָוִד אֶת-הַיָּשָׁר, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה; וְלֹא-סָר מִכֹּל אֲשֶׁר-צִוָּהוּ, כֹּל יְמֵי חַיָּיו--רַק, בִּדְבַר אוּרִיָּה הַחִתִּי.
because David did what was right in the eyes of Jehovah, and did not turn aside from anything that he commanded him all the days of his life, only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite.note
porque David hizo lo recto a los ojos de Jehová, y no se apartó de nada de lo que le mandó todos los días de su vida, solo en el asunto de Urías el hitita.
6
וּמִלְחָמָה הָיְתָה בֵין-רְחַבְעָם וּבֵין יָרָבְעָם, כָּל-יְמֵי חַיָּיו.
And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days of his life.note
Y hubo guerra entre Roboam y Jeroboam todos los días de su vida.
7
וְיֶתֶר דִּבְרֵי אֲבִיָּם, וְכָל-אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה--הֲלוֹא-הֵם כְּתוּבִים עַל-סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים, לְמַלְכֵי יְהוּדָה; וּמִלְחָמָה הָיְתָה בֵּין אֲבִיָּם, וּבֵין יָרָבְעָם.
And the rest of the acts of Abijam, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the acts of the days of the kings of Judah? And there was war between Abijam and Jeroboam.note
Y lo demás de los hechos de Abiam, y todo lo que hizo, ¿no está escrito en el libro de los hechos de los días de los reyes de Judá? Y hubo guerra entre Abiam y Jeroboam.
8
וַיִּשְׁכַּב אֲבִיָּם עִם-אֲבֹתָיו, וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אֹתוֹ בְּעִיר דָּוִד; וַיִּמְלֹךְ אָסָא בְנוֹ, תַּחְתָּיו.  {פ}
And Abijam lay down with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David; and Asa his son reigned in his place.note
Y Abiam se acostó con sus padres, y lo sepultaron en la ciudad de David; y Asa su hijo reinó en su lugar.
9
וּבִשְׁנַת עֶשְׂרִים, לְיָרָבְעָם מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, מָלַךְ אָסָא, מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה.
And in the twentieth year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Asa king of Judah reigned.note
Y en el año veinte de Jeroboam rey de Israel, reinó Asa rey de Judá.
10
וְאַרְבָּעִים וְאַחַת שָׁנָה, מָלַךְ בִּירוּשָׁלִָם; וְשֵׁם אִמּוֹ, מַעֲכָה בַּת-אֲבִישָׁלוֹם.
And forty-one years he reigned in Jerusalem; and his mother’s name was Maacah daughter of Abishalom.note
Y cuarenta y un años reinó en Jerusalén; y el nombre de su madre era Maaca hija de Abisalom.
11
וַיַּעַשׂ אָסָא הַיָּשָׁר, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה, כְּדָוִד, אָבִיו.
And Asa did what was right in the eyes of Jehovah, like David his father.note
Y Asa hizo lo recto a los ojos de Jehová, como David su padre.
12
וַיַּעֲבֵר הַקְּדֵשִׁים, מִן-הָאָרֶץ; וַיָּסַר, אֶת-כָּל-הַגִּלֻּלִים, אֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ, אֲבֹתָיו.
And he put the consecrated men out of the land, and removed all the idols that his fathers had made.note
Y sacó de la tierra a los hombres consagrados, y quitó todos los ídolos que sus padres habían hecho.
13
וְגַם אֶת-מַעֲכָה אִמּוֹ, וַיְסִרֶהָ מִגְּבִירָה, אֲשֶׁר-עָשְׂתָה מִפְלֶצֶת, לָאֲשֵׁרָה; וַיִּכְרֹת אָסָא אֶת-מִפְלַצְתָּהּ, וַיִּשְׂרֹף בְּנַחַל קִדְרוֹן.
And even Maacah his mother: he removed her from being queen mother, because she had made a horror for the Asherah; and Asa cut down her horror and burned it at the brook Kidron.note
Y aun a Maaca su madre la quitó de ser reina madre, porque había hecho un horror para la Aserá; y Asa cortó su horror y lo quemó junto al torrente Cedrón.
14
וְהַבָּמוֹת, לֹא-סָרוּ; רַק לְבַב-אָסָא, הָיָה שָׁלֵם עִם-יְהוָה--כָּל-יָמָיו.  {ס}
But the high places were not removed; only the heart of Asa was whole with Jehovah all his days.note
Pero los lugares altos no fueron quitados; solo el corazón de Asa fue íntegro con Jehová todos sus días.
15
וַיָּבֵא אֶת-קָדְשֵׁי אָבִיו, וקדשו וְקָדְשֵׁי בֵּית יְהוָה, כֶּסֶף וְזָהָב, וְכֵלִים.
And he brought into the house of Jehovah the holy things of his father and his own holy things: silver and gold and vessels.note
Y llevó a la casa de Jehová las cosas santas de su padre y sus propias cosas santas: plata y oro y utensilios.
16
וּמִלְחָמָה הָיְתָה בֵּין אָסָא, וּבֵין בַּעְשָׁא מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל--כָּל-יְמֵיהֶם.
And there was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days.note
Y hubo guerra entre Asa y Baasa rey de Israel todos sus días.
17
וַיַּעַל בַּעְשָׁא מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל עַל-יְהוּדָה, וַיִּבֶן אֶת-הָרָמָה--לְבִלְתִּי, תֵּת יֹצֵא וָבָא, לְאָסָא, מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה.
And Baasha king of Israel went up against Judah and built Ramah, so as to let no one go out or come in to Asa king of Judah.note
Y Baasa rey de Israel subió contra Judá y edificó Ramá, para no dejar salir ni entrar a nadie hacia Asa rey de Judá.
18
וַיִּקַּח אָסָא אֶת-כָּל-הַכֶּסֶף וְהַזָּהָב הַנּוֹתָרִים בְּאוֹצְרוֹת בֵּית-יְהוָה, וְאֶת-אוֹצְרוֹת בֵּית מלך הַמֶּלֶךְ, וַיִּתְּנֵם, בְּיַד-עֲבָדָיו; וַיִּשְׁלָחֵם הַמֶּלֶךְ אָסָא, אֶל-בֶּן-הֲדַד בֶּן-טַבְרִמֹּן בֶּן-חֶזְיוֹן מֶלֶךְ אֲרָם, הַיֹּשֵׁב בְּדַמֶּשֶׂק, לֵאמֹר.
And Asa took all the silver and the gold that were left in the treasures of the house of Jehovah and the treasures of the king’s house, and put them in the hand of his servants; and King Asa sent them to Ben-hadad son of Tabrimmon son of Hezion, king of Aram, who lived in Damascus, saying:note
Y Asa tomó toda la plata y el oro que habían quedado en los tesoros de la casa de Jehová y en los tesoros de la casa del rey, y los puso en mano de sus siervos; y el rey Asa los envió a Ben-adad hijo de Tabrimón hijo de Hezión, rey de Aram, que habitaba en Damasco, diciendo:
19
בְּרִית בֵּינִי וּבֵינֶךָ, בֵּין אָבִי וּבֵין אָבִיךָ; הִנֵּה שָׁלַחְתִּי לְךָ שֹׁחַד, כֶּסֶף וְזָהָב--לֵךְ הָפֵרָה אֶת-בְּרִיתְךָ אֶת-בַּעְשָׁא מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְיַעֲלֶה מֵעָלָי.
“There is a covenant between me and you, between my father and your father. Look, I have sent you a bribe of silver and gold. Go, break your covenant with Baasha king of Israel, that he may go up from me.”note
«Hay un pacto entre yo y tú, entre mi padre y tu padre. Mira, te he enviado un soborno de plata y oro. Ve, rompe tu pacto con Baasa rey de Israel, para que suba de sobre mí».
20
וַיִּשְׁמַע בֶּן-הֲדַד אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ אָסָא, וַיִּשְׁלַח אֶת-שָׂרֵי הַחֲיָלִים אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ עַל-עָרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיַּךְ אֶת-עִיּוֹן וְאֶת-דָּן, וְאֵת אָבֵל בֵּית-מַעֲכָה; וְאֵת, כָּל-כִּנְרוֹת, עַל, כָּל-אֶרֶץ נַפְתָּלִי.
And Ben-hadad listened to King Asa, and sent the chiefs of his armies against the cities of Israel, and struck Ijon and Dan and Abel-beth-maacah and all Chinneroth, with all the land of Naphtali.note
Y Ben-adad escuchó al rey Asa, y envió a los jefes de sus ejércitos contra las ciudades de Israel, e hirió a Ijón y a Dan y a Abel-bet-maaca y a toda Cineret, con toda la tierra de Neftalí.
21
וַיְהִי, כִּשְׁמֹעַ בַּעְשָׁא, וַיֶּחְדַּל, מִבְּנוֹת אֶת-הָרָמָה; וַיֵּשֶׁב, בְּתִרְצָה.
And when Baasha heard, he stopped building Ramah and lived in Tirzah.note
Y cuando Baasa lo oyó, dejó de edificar Ramá y habitó en Tirsa.
22
וְהַמֶּלֶךְ אָסָא הִשְׁמִיעַ אֶת-כָּל-יְהוּדָה, אֵין נָקִי, וַיִּשְׂאוּ אֶת-אַבְנֵי הָרָמָה וְאֶת-עֵצֶיהָ, אֲשֶׁר בָּנָה בַּעְשָׁא; וַיִּבֶן בָּם הַמֶּלֶךְ אָסָא, אֶת-גֶּבַע בִּנְיָמִן וְאֶת-הַמִּצְפָּה.
And King Asa summoned all Judah, no one exempt; and they carried away the stones of Ramah and its timber, with which Baasha had built; and King Asa built with them Geba of Benjamin and Mizpah.note
Y el rey Asa convocó a todo Judá, sin nadie exento; y se llevaron las piedras de Ramá y su madera, con que Baasa había edificado; y el rey Asa edificó con ellas Gueba de Benjamín y Mizpa.
23
וְיֶתֶר כָּל-דִּבְרֵי-אָסָא וְכָל-גְּבוּרָתוֹ וְכָל-אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה, וְהֶעָרִים אֲשֶׁר בָּנָה--הֲלֹא-הֵמָּה כְתוּבִים עַל-סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים, לְמַלְכֵי יְהוּדָה; רַק לְעֵת זִקְנָתוֹ, חָלָה אֶת-רַגְלָיו.
And the rest of all the acts of Asa, and all his might, and all that he did, and the cities that he built, are they not written in the book of the acts of the days of the kings of Judah? Only, in the time of his old age, he fell sick in his feet.note
Y lo demás de todos los hechos de Asa, y todo su poderío, y todo lo que hizo, y las ciudades que edificó, ¿no está escrito en el libro de los hechos de los días de los reyes de Judá? Solo que, en el tiempo de su vejez, enfermó de los pies.
24
וַיִּשְׁכַּב אָסָא, עִם-אֲבֹתָיו, וַיִּקָּבֵר עִם-אֲבֹתָיו, בְּעִיר דָּוִד אָבִיו; וַיִּמְלֹךְ יְהוֹשָׁפָט בְּנוֹ, תַּחְתָּיו.  {פ}
And Asa lay down with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David his father; and Jehoshaphat his son reigned in his place.note
Y Asa se acostó con sus padres, y fue sepultado con sus padres en la ciudad de David su padre; y Josafat su hijo reinó en su lugar.
25
וְנָדָב בֶּן-יָרָבְעָם, מָלַךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, בִּשְׁנַת שְׁתַּיִם, לְאָסָא מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה; וַיִּמְלֹךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, שְׁנָתָיִם.
And Nadab son of Jeroboam reigned over Israel in the second year of Asa king of Judah, and he reigned over Israel two years.note
Y Nadab hijo de Jeroboam reinó sobre Israel en el año segundo de Asa rey de Judá, y reinó sobre Israel dos años.
26
וַיַּעַשׂ הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה; וַיֵּלֶךְ, בְּדֶרֶךְ אָבִיו, וּבְחַטָּאתוֹ, אֲשֶׁר הֶחֱטִיא אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And he did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, and walked in the way of his father and in his sin which he made Israel sin.note
Y hizo lo malo a los ojos de Jehová, y anduvo en el camino de su padre y en su pecado que hizo pecar a Israel.
27
וַיִּקְשֹׁר עָלָיו בַּעְשָׁא בֶן-אֲחִיָּה, לְבֵית יִשָּׂשכָר, וַיַּכֵּהוּ בַעְשָׁא, בְּגִבְּתוֹן אֲשֶׁר לַפְּלִשְׁתִּים; וְנָדָב, וְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, צָרִים, עַל-גִּבְּתוֹן.
And Baasha son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, conspired against him, and Baasha struck him at Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines, while Nadab and all Israel were besieging Gibbethon.note
Y Baasa hijo de Ahías, de la casa de Isacar, conspiró contra él, y Baasa lo hirió en Gibetón, que era de los filisteos, mientras Nadab y todo Israel sitiaban Gibetón.
28
וַיְמִתֵהוּ בַעְשָׁא--בִּשְׁנַת שָׁלֹשׁ, לְאָסָא מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה; וַיִּמְלֹךְ, תַּחְתָּיו.
And Baasha killed him in the third year of Asa king of Judah, and reigned in his place.note
Y Baasa lo mató en el año tercero de Asa rey de Judá, y reinó en su lugar.
29
וַיְהִי כְמָלְכוֹ, הִכָּה אֶת-כָּל-בֵּית יָרָבְעָם--לֹא-הִשְׁאִיר כָּל-נְשָׁמָה לְיָרָבְעָם, עַד-הִשְׁמִדוֹ:  כִּדְבַר יְהוָה--אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר, בְּיַד-עַבְדּוֹ אֲחִיָּה הַשִּׁילֹנִי.
And as soon as he reigned, he struck all the house of Jeroboam; he left to Jeroboam none that breathed, until he had destroyed him, according to the word of Jehovah which he spoke by the hand of his servant Ahijah the Shilonite,note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–8 · 1 Kings 15:1–8 Abijam of Judah — a lamp in Jerusalem, “only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite”, and a verse that repeats itself

Two clocks. “In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam”: from here on Kings dates each king by the reign of the king across the border, and the two kingdoms that will not share a temple share a calendar. Abijam gets eight verses, and three of them are about David. His mother’s name comes up in the second verse and has a problem of its own, taken up at verse 13. The NWT 1984 prints “the granddaughter of” Abishalom, where the Hebrew has bat, “daughter”. The ASV and the KJV have “the daughter of”. The spelling Abishalom stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and verse 10. Chronicles has Maacah daughter of Absalom (2 Chronicles 11:20), and then, for the same king’s mother, Micaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah (2 Chronicles 13:2).

The lamp. “His heart was not whole with Jehovah his God, like the heart of David his father”: that is the sentence on Solomon (1 Kings 11:4, now on these pages), and the word is shalem. “Jehovah his God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem”: nir, the promise of 1 Kings 11:36 (now on these pages). A bad king stays on the throne on his great-grandfather’s account, and the verse says so. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “a remnant” here. The ASV, the KJV and the NIV have “a lamp”, and the Geneva “a light”.

“Only in the matter of Uriah the Hittite”. The clause stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. It is the one exception Kings allows in David’s record, and it names the man and not the woman (2 Samuel 11:3, now on these pages). The census of 2 Samuel 24 (now on these pages) goes unmentioned. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition stops at “all the days of his life” and has no exception at all. The readings differ. The ASV and the KJV have “save only in the matter of”, and the NWT 1984 “only in the matter of”, which this page follows.

A verse out of place. “And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days of his life”: that is 1 Kings 14:30 (now on these pages) over again, in the middle of Abijam’s reign, and the reader does not know whose life is meant. The next verse says it properly, “between Abijam and Jeroboam”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has no verse 6. The NIV prints “between Abijah and Jeroboam”, a reading reported from some manuscripts, and not the Hebrew printed here. This page keeps “Rehoboam”, with the ASV and the KJV. Kings gives Abijam’s war one clause. Chronicles gives it most of a chapter, with four hundred thousand men against eight hundred thousand (2 Chronicles 13).

Verses 9–15 · 1 Kings 15:9–15 Asa and Maacah — the “consecrated men”, the queen mother and her “horror”, and why his mother is also his father’s mother

“Like David his father”. Asa is the first king since David to be told he did what was right. “In the twentieth year of Jeroboam”: a king who began in the eighteenth and reigned three years ended in the twentieth, so parts of years are counted as years. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has the twenty-fourth. Matthew’s list spells the name “Asaph” (see the note at Matthew 1:7, now on these pages).

⚠ The consecrated men. “He put the consecrated men out of the land”: these are the qedeshim, plural of the “consecrated man” of 1 Kings 14:24 (now on these pages). The verb means he made them pass out. The TLB prints “He executed the male prostitutes”, and there is no execution in the Hebrew. The KJV, the ASV and the Geneva have “sodomites”, the NIV “male shrine prostitutes”, the NWT 1984 “male temple prostitutes”, and the Douay “the effeminate”. The word “sodomites” has been read as a condemnation of men who have sex with men, as the note at 1 Kings 14:24 says. The Hebrew says that a king sent a class of shrine servants out of his country.

“Idols”. “All the idols that his fathers had made”: this is the first use in Kings of gillulim, the word planted beside the dung of 1 Kings 14:10 (now on these pages). This page has “idols”, as at Leviticus 26:30 and Deuteronomy 29:16 (both now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “dungy idols”, the Douay “the filth of the idols”, and the ASV, the KJV and the NIV “idols”. Taking the word from dung is one explanation, and an old one, but the origin of the word is not certain.

His mother. “And even Maacah his mother”: verse 8 makes Asa the son of Abijam, verse 2 makes Maacah the mother of Abijam, and verse 10 makes her the mother of Asa. There are three ways through. She may be his grandmother, keeping her office into a second reign, and the NIV and the TLB print “his grandmother”, as does the NWT 1984; the Hebrew in all these verses has immo, “his mother”. Abijam and Asa may have been brothers. Or a name may have been copied from one notice to the other, and the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition does have a different mother for Asa, Ana. The ASV and the KJV keep “his mother”, and so does this page.

The queen mother and her “horror”. “He removed her from being queen mother”: gevirah, the word for Tahpenes “the queen” at 1 Kings 11:19 (now on these pages). It was an office, since it could be taken away. “She had made a horror for the Asherah”: the noun mifletset stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in the parallel (2 Chronicles 15:16), and it comes from a verb meaning to shudder. The verse does not say what it looked like, and that has left the versions room. The KJV has “an idol in a grove”, the ASV “an abominable image”, the NIV “a repulsive image”, and the NWT 1984 “a horrible idol”. The Douay, following the Latin, makes it “the sacrifices of Priapus”, a Greek garden god the Hebrew has never heard of. Asa burns it at the brook Kidron, where Josiah will burn the Asherah (2 Kings 23:6).

“Were not removed”. “But the high places were not removed”: the phrase stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible, and this is the first. The verb is sur, and the chapter has been using it: David “did not turn aside”, Asa “removed” the idols and “removed” Maacah. The one thing the good king does not remove is the thing the book cares about most. Chronicles says both things, that he took them away (2 Chronicles 14:2) and that they were not taken away (2 Chronicles 15:17). “Whole with Jehovah” is the word of verse 3, turned around. The ASV and the KJV have “perfect”, the NWT 1984 “complete”, and the NIV “fully committed”.

Gifts brought in. “The holy things of his father and his own holy things”: the scroll writes qodashav, “his holy things”, and the reading is qodshei, “the holy things of”. The shelf and this page follow the scroll. He brings silver and gold into the house of Jehovah. In three verses he will take them out.

Verses 16–22 · 1 Kings 15:16–22 Asa’s bribe to Ben-hadad of Damascus — the word the shelf softens, Ramah, and Baasha’s stones

Ramah. “And built Ramah, so as to let no one go out or come in”: Ramah is a few miles north of Jerusalem, on the road. A fort there is a hand on the throat of Judah. “War all their days” is the Kings account. Chronicles says there was no war until the thirty-fifth year of Asa and sets this campaign in the thirty-sixth (2 Chronicles 15:19; 16:1), but Kings has Baasha dead and his son on the throne by the twenty-sixth (1 Kings 16:8, now on these pages). The numbers do not agree as they stand, and the proposed solutions are proposals.

“That were left”. “All the silver and the gold that were left”: Shishak “took it all” (1 Kings 14:26, now on these pages), and the phrase admits that it was not quite all. What is left may be what Asa had just brought in; the text does not say. So the temple is stripped a second time, and by a good king. It will happen again under Ahaz and under Hezekiah (2 Kings 16:8; 18:15). “The king’s house”: the scroll writes “house of king” and the reading adds the article.

“A bribe”. “Look, I have sent you a bribe of silver and gold”: the word is shochad, and this page keeps it where the shelf does not. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 have “a present”, the NIV “a gift”, and the Douay “presents”. It is the word of the law, “You shall not accept a bribe” (Exodus 23:8, now on these pages), and of Jehovah, who “does not take a bribe” (Deuteronomy 10:17, now on these pages). It is also the plain word for paying a king to break his word, which is what Asa is doing, and Ahaz’s payment to Assyria has the same name (2 Kings 16:8). Kings passes no comment. Chronicles does: a seer called Hanani rebukes Asa and is put in prison for it (2 Chronicles 16:7–10).

Ben-hadad and the road north. “Ben-hadad son of Tabrimmon son of Hezion, king of Aram, who lived in Damascus”: three generations of a foreign king. He is the first of the name, “son of Hadad”, the storm god. Whether Hezion is the Rezon of 1 Kings 11:23 (now on these pages) has been argued and cannot be shown. The ASV has “Syria” for “Aram”, and the NIV has “Aram”. “Break your covenant”: the man who has just brought the holy things into the house asks a king of Damascus to break a treaty, and pays him to do it. Dan falls (1 Kings 12:29, now on these pages), the town of the calf, and Abel-beth-maacah, and “all Chinneroth”. It works. Ijon, Abel-beth-maacah and Naphtali are what Assyria will take (2 Kings 15:29), and a good deal of the rest of this book is wars with Damascus.

No one exempt. “No one exempt”: the two words ein naqi stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Naqi is the word for the new husband who is “free for his house” for a year (Deuteronomy 24:5, now on these pages). This time everyone goes. All Judah carries off the stones and timber of Baasha’s fort, and Asa builds two of his own with them, Geba and Mizpah. The materials were free. The pit he made at Mizpah “for fear of Baasha” comes back in Jeremiah (Jeremiah 41:9).

Verses 23–24 · 1 Kings 15:23–24 Asa’s disease of the feet — “only”, and Jehoshaphat

“Only”. “Only, in the time of his old age, he fell sick in his feet”: the word is raq, and it is the third of the chapter. David was right, “only in the matter of Uriah”. The high places stayed, “only the heart of Asa was whole”. Now a long, good reign, “only” this. The phrase chalah et raglav stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and the text does not say what the disease was. Every diagnosis offered since is a guess. Chronicles dates it to his thirty-ninth year and blames him for going to physicians and not to Jehovah (2 Chronicles 16:12). The verb is the verb of the sick boy (1 Kings 14:1, now on these pages), and a chapter after that boy’s mother was undone by her feet, the king of Judah ends on his.

Forty-one years, and a step back. “And Jehoshaphat his son reigned in his place”: he has to wait for his own notice (1 Kings 22:41). The next verse goes back to the second year of Asa. Kings finishes one king before it picks up the other, so the reader has now buried Asa and will go on seeing his years in the dates of the next chapter.

Verses 25–30 · 1 Kings 15:25–30 Nadab, Baasha and the end of the house of Jeroboam — the conspiracy at Gibbethon, and “none that breathed”

The first conspiracy. “Baasha son of Ahijah, of the house of Issachar, conspired against him”: the verb is qashar, to bind, and the noun is qesher. His father’s name is the prophet’s name, and the text does not connect them; this Baasha is from another tribe. “Reigned over Israel two years” runs from Asa’s second year to his third, as verse 28 shows, so the years are being counted as in verse 9. The first dynasty of the north ends after one son.

Gibbethon. “Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines”: the town was allotted to Dan and given to the Levites (Joshua 19:44; 21:23, now on these pages), and it is in the wrong hands. The army that sees its king killed here will still be camped against the same town at the next coup (1 Kings 16:15, now on these pages).

“None that breathed”. “He left to Jeroboam none that breathed, until he had destroyed him”: kol neshamah stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible. The other three are the ban on Canaan and the taking of Hazor (Deuteronomy 20:16; Joshua 11:11, 14, now on these pages). The house of the king of Israel is treated as the book treated the cities of Canaan. The ASV and the KJV have “any that breathed”, and the NIV “anyone that breathed”. “According to the word of Jehovah”: this is the sentence of 1 Kings 14:10–14 (now on these pages), and the man who carried it out was not obeying it. Jehovah said it, Baasha did it for reasons of his own, and he will be condemned for that too (1 Kings 16:7, now on these pages). The text leaves those two facts side by side.

Verses 31–34 · 1 Kings 15:31–34 Baasha in Tirzah — the verdict repeated, and the way of Jeroboam

A repeated line. “And there was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days”: this is verse 16 word for word, and “all their days” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, those two. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has no verse 32. In the Hebrew it divides the end of one king from the start of another.

The way of Jeroboam. “And walked in the way of Jeroboam”: Baasha wiped out every breathing member of Jeroboam’s house, and two verses later the book gives him Jeroboam’s verdict. He has the house and Tirzah for twenty-four years, “over all Israel”. The sentence that fell on Jeroboam’s line is about to be passed on his own (1 Kings 16:3, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “whole with Jehovah” for shalem, as in 1 Kings 11:4; “a lamp” for nir, as in 1 Kings 11:36; “only” for raq, all three times; “the consecrated men” for qedeshim; “idols” for gillulim; “queen mother” for gevirah; “a horror” for mifletset; “removed” for sur; “a bribe” for shochad, as in Exodus 23:8; “covenant” for berit, in a treaty between kings; “no one exempt” for ein naqi; “conspired” for qashar; “none that breathed” for kol neshamah; and “his mother”, as the Hebrew has it, for Maacah.

Echoes paid. The heart not whole (1 Kings 11:4); the lamp (1 Kings 11:36); Uriah the Hittite (2 Samuel 11:3); war all the days (1 Kings 14:30); the consecrated man (1 Kings 14:24); the dung and the idols (1 Kings 14:10); Tahpenes the gevirah (1 Kings 11:19); “he took it all” (1 Kings 14:26); the law of the bribe (Exodus 23:8; Deuteronomy 10:17); Dan and the calf (1 Kings 12:29); the man free for his house (Deuteronomy 24:5); the sick boy (1 Kings 14:1); Nadab and the sentence of Ahijah (1 Kings 14:10–14, 20); Gibbethon (Joshua 19:44; 21:23); and “none that breathed” (Deuteronomy 20:16; Joshua 11:11, 14).

Echoes planted. The word against Baasha, and Gibbethon again (1 Kings 16:1–4, 7, 15, now on these pages); Jehoshaphat (1 Kings 22:41); Maacah in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 11:20; 13:2; 15:16); Abijam’s war (2 Chronicles 13); Hanani and the physicians (2 Chronicles 16:7–12); Ahaz’s bribe and Hezekiah’s gold (2 Kings 16:8; 18:15); Assyria in Naphtali (2 Kings 15:29); Josiah at the Kidron (2 Kings 23:6); and Asa’s pit at Mizpah (Jeremiah 41:9). The rest are yet to come in this translation.
