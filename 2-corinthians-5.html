The same letter, written from Macedonia. Paul speaks of the earthly tent and the house from God, of groaning and the down payment of the Spirit, and of the judgment seat of Christ. He gives the aim of being pleasing to God, the love of Christ that compels, the new creation, and the ministry of reconciliation, and calls the apostles ambassadors for Christ.
Undated by the text: the chapter names no year. It continues the letter of chapters 2 to 4 without a break.

1
Οἴδαμεν γὰρ ὅτι ἐὰν ἡ ἐπίγειος ἡμῶν οἰκία τοῦ σκήνους καταλυθῇ, οἰκοδομὴν ἐκ θεοῦ ἔχομεν οἰκίαν ἀχειροποίητον αἰώνιον ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς.
For we know that if our earthly house, this tent, is taken down, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal, in the heavens.note
Porque sabemos que, si nuestra casa terrenal, esta tienda, se desmantela, tenemos un edificio de Dios, una casa no hecha de manos, eterna, en los cielos.
2
καὶ γὰρ ἐν τούτῳ στενάζομεν, τὸ οἰκητήριον ἡμῶν τὸ ἐξ οὐρανοῦ ἐπενδύσασθαι ἐπιποθοῦντες,
For in this we groan, longing to put on over it our dwelling from heaven,note
Pues en esta gemimos, deseando ardientemente ponernos encima nuestra morada celestial,
3
εἴ γε καὶ ἐνδυσάμενοι οὐ γυμνοὶ εὑρεθησόμεθα.
since, when we have put it on, we shall not be found naked.note
para que, si nos la ponemos, no se nos halle desnudos.
4
καὶ γὰρ οἱ ὄντες ἐν τῷ σκήνει στενάζομεν βαρούμενοι ἐφʼ ᾧ οὐ θέλομεν ἐκδύσασθαι ἀλλʼ ἐπενδύσασθαι, ἵνα καταποθῇ τὸ θνητὸν ὑπὸ τῆς ζωῆς.
For we who are in the tent groan, weighed down; not because we want to take it off, but to put on over it, so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life.note
Porque los que estamos en la tienda gemimos, agobiados, no porque queramos quitárnosla, sino porque queremos ponernos encima la otra, para que lo mortal sea absorbido por la vida.
5
ὁ δὲ κατεργασάμενος ἡμᾶς εἰς αὐτὸ τοῦτο θεός, ὁ δοὺς ἡμῖν τὸν ἀρραβῶνα τοῦ πνεύματος.
Now the one who prepared us for this very thing is God, who gave us the down payment of the Spirit.note
El que nos preparó precisamente para esto es Dios, que nos dio como garantía el espíritu.
6
Θαρροῦντες οὖν πάντοτε καὶ εἰδότες ὅτι ἐνδημοῦντες ἐν τῷ σώματι ἐκδημοῦμεν ἀπὸ τοῦ κυρίου,
So we are always full of courage, knowing that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord,note
Así que siempre estamos confiados, sabiendo que, mientras estamos en el cuerpo, estamos lejos del Señor;
7
διὰ πίστεως γὰρ περιπατοῦμεν, οὐ διὰ εἴδους—
for we walk by faith, not by sight.note
porque andamos por fe, no por vista.
8
θαρροῦμεν δὲ καὶ εὐδοκοῦμεν μᾶλλον ἐκδημῆσαι ἐκ τοῦ σώματος καὶ ἐνδημῆσαι πρὸς τὸν κύριον·
We are full of courage, and we prefer rather to be away from the body and at home with the Lord.note
Estamos confiados, digo, y preferimos estar lejos del cuerpo y estar en casa con el Señor.
9
διὸ καὶ φιλοτιμούμεθα, εἴτε ἐνδημοῦντες εἴτε ἐκδημοῦντες, εὐάρεστοι αὐτῷ εἶναι.
So whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to be pleasing to him.note
Por eso también nos esforzamos, sea que estemos en casa o lejos, por agradarle.
10
τοὺς γὰρ πάντας ἡμᾶς φανερωθῆναι δεῖ ἔμπροσθεν τοῦ βήματος τοῦ Χριστοῦ, ἵνα κομίσηται ἕκαστος τὰ διὰ τοῦ σώματος πρὸς ἃ ἔπραξεν, εἴτε ἀγαθὸν εἴτε φαῦλον
For we must all be made manifest before the judgment seat of the Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he did through the body, whether good or worthless.note
Porque todos nosotros tenemos que ser puestos de manifiesto ante el tribunal de Cristo, para que cada uno reciba lo que corresponde por lo hecho mediante el cuerpo, sea bueno o sea malo.
11
Εἰδότες οὖν τὸν φόβον τοῦ κυρίου ἀνθρώπους πείθομεν, θεῷ δὲ πεφανερώμεθα· ἐλπίζω δὲ καὶ ἐν ταῖς συνειδήσεσιν ὑμῶν πεφανερῶσθαι.
Knowing, then, the fear of the Lord, we persuade people, but we are made manifest to God; and I hope we are made manifest also in your consciences.note
Así que, conociendo el temor del Señor, persuadimos a los hombres, pero ante Dios estamos bien manifiestos; y espero que también estemos manifiestos en las conciencias de ustedes.
12
οὐ πάλιν ἑαυτοὺς συνιστάνομεν ὑμῖν, ἀλλὰ ἀφορμὴν διδόντες ὑμῖν καυχήματος ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν, ἵνα ἔχητε πρὸς τοὺς ἐν προσώπῳ καυχωμένους καὶ μὴ ἐν καρδίᾳ.
We are not commending ourselves to you again, but giving you an occasion to boast about us, so that you have an answer for those who boast in appearance and not in heart.note
No nos recomendamos otra vez a ustedes, sino que les damos ocasión de gloriarse por nosotros, para que tengan con qué responder a los que se glorían en la apariencia y no en el corazón.
13
εἴτε γὰρ ἐξέστημεν, θεῷ· εἴτε σωφρονοῦμεν, ὑμῖν.
For if we are beside ourselves, it is for God; if we are sober-minded, it is for you.note
Porque si estamos fuera de nosotros, es para Dios; si estamos en nuestro juicio, es para ustedes.
14
ἡ γὰρ ἀγάπη τοῦ Χριστοῦ συνέχει ἡμᾶς, κρίναντας τοῦτο ὅτι εἷς ὑπὲρ πάντων ἀπέθανεν· ἄρα οἱ πάντες ἀπέθανον·
For the love of the Christ compels us, because we have concluded this: that one died for all; therefore all died.note
Pues el amor de Cristo nos constriñe, porque hemos juzgado esto: que uno murió por todos; por lo tanto, todos murieron.
15
καὶ ὑπὲρ πάντων ἀπέθανεν ἵνα οἱ ζῶντες μηκέτι ἑαυτοῖς ζῶσιν ἀλλὰ τῷ ὑπὲρ αὐτῶν ἀποθανόντι καὶ ἐγερθέντι.
And he died for all, so that those who live should no longer live for themselves, but for him who died for them and was raised.note
Y murió por todos para que los que viven ya no vivan para sí mismos, sino para el que murió por ellos y fue levantado.
16
Ὥστε ἡμεῖς ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν οὐδένα οἴδαμεν κατὰ σάρκα· εἰ καὶ ἐγνώκαμεν κατὰ σάρκα Χριστόν, ἀλλὰ νῦν οὐκέτι γινώσκομεν.
So from now on we know no one according to the flesh. Even if we once knew Christ according to the flesh, now we no longer know him that way.note
Así que de ahora en adelante no conocemos a nadie según la carne. Aunque conocimos a Cristo según la carne, ahora ya no lo conocemos así.
17
ὥστε εἴ τις ἐν Χριστῷ, καινὴ κτίσις· τὰ ἀρχαῖα παρῆλθεν, ἰδοὺ γέγονεν καινά
So if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old things passed away; look, new things have come.note
Así que, si alguien está en Cristo, es una creación nueva; las cosas viejas pasaron; ¡miren!, han llegado a ser nuevas.
18
τὰ δὲ πάντα ἐκ τοῦ θεοῦ τοῦ καταλλάξαντος ἡμᾶς ἑαυτῷ διὰ Χριστοῦ καὶ δόντος ἡμῖν τὴν διακονίαν τῆς καταλλαγῆς,
But all things are from God, who reconciled us to himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation.note
Pero todas las cosas son de Dios, que nos reconcilió consigo mismo por medio de Cristo y nos dio el ministerio de la reconciliación;
19
ὡς ὅτι θεὸς ἦν ἐν Χριστῷ κόσμον καταλλάσσων ἑαυτῷ, μὴ λογιζόμενος αὐτοῖς τὰ παραπτώματα αὐτῶν, καὶ θέμενος ἐν ἡμῖν τὸν λόγον τῆς καταλλαγῆς.
That is, God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, not counting their trespasses against them, and he entrusted to us the word of reconciliation.note
es decir, que Dios estaba en Cristo reconciliando el mundo consigo mismo, sin contar sus transgresiones contra ellos, y nos encomendó la palabra de la reconciliación.
20
ὑπὲρ Χριστοῦ οὖν πρεσβεύομεν ὡς τοῦ θεοῦ παρακαλοῦντος διʼ ἡμῶν· δεόμεθα ὑπὲρ Χριστοῦ, καταλλάγητε τῷ θεῷ.
So we are ambassadors on behalf of Christ, as though God were appealing through us; we beg on behalf of Christ: be reconciled to God.note
Así que somos embajadores en nombre de Cristo, como si Dios los exhortara por medio de nosotros; les rogamos en nombre de Cristo: reconcíliense con Dios.
21
τὸν μὴ γνόντα ἁμαρτίαν ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν ἁμαρτίαν ἐποίησεν, ἵνα ἡμεῖς γενώμεθα δικαιοσύνη θεοῦ ἐν αὐτῷ.
He who knew no sin, he made to be sin on our behalf, so that in him we might become God’s righteousness.note
Al que no conoció pecado, lo hizo pecado por nosotros, para que en él llegáramos a ser justicia de Dios.
Verses 1–4 · the earthly house and the tent, the groaning to put on over, and the not being found naked
“Our earthly house, this tent.” hē epigeios hēmōn oikia tou skēnous, our earthly house, the tent. The body is called a house and a tent in one phrase, and the verb katalyō, to take down, is the verb used for dismantling a building. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “if our earthly house, this tent, should be torn down, we are to have a building from God.” The KJV “if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God.”

“Groan, and put on over.” stenazomen, we groan, and ependysasthai, to put on over. The verb is the one for putting a garment on over another, and the sentence says the dwelling from heaven is put on over the present one, not in its place. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “we do indeed groan, earnestly desiring to put on the one for us from heaven.” The ASV “we groan, longing to be clothed upon with our habitation which is from heaven.”

“Not found naked.” ei ge kai endysamenoi ou gymnoi heurethēsometha, if indeed, having put it on, we shall not be found naked. The conditional does not say whether the putting on has already happened. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “so that when we do put it on, we will not be found naked.” The ASV “if so be that being clothed we shall not be found naked.”

Verses 5–8 · the down payment of the Spirit, at home in the body and away from the Lord, and walking by faith
“The down payment of the Spirit.” ho dous hēmin ton arrabōna tou pneumatos, who gave us the down payment of the Spirit. Arrabōn is a deposit paid in advance, the first installment that binds the rest; Paul uses the same word of the Spirit at 1:22. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “who gave us the spirit as a token of what is to come.” The KJV “who also hath given unto us the earnest of the Spirit.”

“At home in the body, away from the Lord.” endēmountes en tō sōmati ekdēmoumen apo tou kyriou. The two verbs are a pair, being at home and being away from home, and the sentence makes the body the home where the Lord is absent. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “while we have our home in the body, we are absent from the Lord.” The ASV “whilst we are at home in the body, we are absent from the Lord.”

“By faith, not by sight.” dia pisteōs gar peripatoumen, ou dia eidous, for we walk by faith, not by sight. Eidos is sight or appearance; the sentence sets it against faith and does not name what is seen. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “for we are walking by faith, not by sight.” The KJV “For we walk by faith, not by sight”.

Verses 9–10 · the aim of being pleasing, the judgment seat of the Christ, and good or worthless
“Our aim, whether at home or away.” philotimoumetha, we make it our aim. Euarestos, pleasing, is the word for what is pleasing to God. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “whether at home with him or absent from him, we make it our aim to be acceptable to him.” The ASV “whether at home or absent, to be well-pleasing unto him.”

“The judgment seat of the Christ.” tou bēmatos tou Christou. Bēma is the platform where a magistrate sits to judge; the same word is used for the seat of Gallio at Acts 18:12. The phrase keeps the article before “Christ,” and the sentence makes Christ the judge. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “we must all appear before the judgment seat of the Christ.” The KJV “we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ.”

“Good or worthless.” eite agathon eite phaulon, whether good or worthless. Phaulon is what is of no account, and the NWT 2013 and the KJV both render it “bad,” which is plainer than the Greek. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “whether good or bad.” The ASV “whether it be good or bad.”

Verses 11–13 · the fear of the Lord that persuades, and beside ourselves or sober
“Knowing the fear of the Lord.” eidotes oun ton phobon tou kyriou. The fear of the Lord is the motive for persuading people: Paul persuades because of what the Lord will judge, and he is made manifest to God. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Therefore, since we know the fear of the Lord, we keep persuading men.” The KJV “Knowing therefore the terror of the Lord, we persuade men.”

“Beside ourselves, or sober.” The two verbs are the words for losing one’s mind and for being sound of mind, and the sentence gives the first to God and the second to them. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “if we were out of our mind, it was for God; if we are sound in mind, it is for you.” The ASV “whether we are beside ourselves, it is unto God; or whether we are of sober mind, it is unto you.”

Verses 14–15 · the love of Christ that compels, one who died for all, and living no longer for themselves
“The love of Christ compels us.” hē agapē tou Christou synechei hēmas. The verb synechō is to hold together or press on, and it is used of crowds pressing around Jesus at Luke 8:45. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For the love the Christ has compels us, because this is what we have concluded, that one man died for all.” The ASV “For the love of Christ constraineth us.”

“One died for all; so all had died.” eis hyper pantōn apethanen; ara hoi pantes apethanon. The argument runs from one to all: one died for all, therefore all died. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “one man died for all; so, then, all had died.” The KJV “if one died for all, then were all dead.”

“No longer for themselves.” The purpose of the death is that the living should no longer live for themselves but for him who died and was raised. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “so that those who live should live no longer for themselves, but for him who died for them and was raised up.”

Verses 16–17 · knowing no one according to the flesh, and the new creation
“Know no one according to the flesh.” hōste hēmeis apo tou nyn oudena oidamen kata sarka. The phrase kata sarka, according to the flesh, is used of Christ in the next clause, and Paul says he once knew Christ that way and no longer does. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “So from now on we know no man from a fleshly viewpoint. Even if we once knew Christ according to the flesh, we certainly no longer know him in that way.” The KJV “Wherefore henceforth know we no man after the flesh.”

“A new creation.” kainē ktisis, new creation. The sentence has no verb: “if anyone is in Christ,” then the new creation and the passing of the old. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “if anyone is in union with Christ, he is a new creation; the old things passed away; look! new things have come into existence.” The ASV “he is a new creature: the old things are passed away; behold, they are become new.”

Verses 18–21 · reconciliation, the word entrusted, ambassadors on Christ’s behalf, and made to be sin
“Reconciled us to himself.” katallassō, to reconcile, and katallagē, reconciliation, occur three times in these verses. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “who reconciled us to himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of the reconciliation.” The KJV “who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation.”

“God was reconciling a world.” theos ēn en Christō kosmon katallassōn heautō, God was in Christ reconciling a world to himself. The NWT 2013 and the ASV both keep the trespasses uncounted, and the Greek word is paraptōma. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “God was by means of Christ reconciling a world to himself, not counting their offenses against them.” The ASV “God was in Christ reconciling the world unto himself, not reckoning unto them their trespasses.”

“Ambassadors on Christ’s behalf.” hyper Christou oun presbeuomen, we are ambassadors on behalf of Christ, and katallagēte tō theō, be reconciled to God. The verb presbeuō is to serve as an envoy, and the sentence makes the apostles envoys of God, who is appealing through them. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “we are ambassadors substituting for Christ, as though God were making an appeal through us.” and “Become reconciled to God.”

“Made to be sin.” ton mē gnonta hamartian hyper hēmōn hamartian epoiēsen, the one who did not know sin he made sin on our behalf. The clause has a verb of making and a noun of sin with no article. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “The one who did not know sin, he made to be sin for us, so that by means of him we might become God’s righteousness.” The KJV “For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “house” for oikia at 5:1 and “dwelling” for oikētērion at 5:2, kept apart as the Greek keeps them; “tent” for skēnos; “judgment seat” for bēma at 5:10; “ambassadors” for presbeuō at 5:20.

Echoes paid. The tent of 4:7 and 4:16–18 returns as the tent of 5:1–4; the eternal of 4:17–18 returns as the eternal house of 5:1; the ministry of 4:1 returns as the ministry of reconciliation (5:18).

Echoes planted. The appeal of 5:20 is the one 6:1 takes up, and the judgment seat of 5:10 is the place where each one receives what is due. Both carry on in the next chapter of this book.
