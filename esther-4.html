Mordecai in sackcloth and ashes at the king’s gate; fasting and mourning in every province; the queen shaken, sending clothes and then Hatach; the exact sum of the money and the copy of the written law; the one law of the inner court and thirty days uncalled; “do not imagine”, relief and deliverance from another place, and “who knows whether for a time like this”; and a three-day fast before she goes in against the law.
Undated in the chapter itself; the book’s own sequence puts it after the decree of the thirteenth day of the first month (Esther 3:12) and before Esther goes in, in the twelfth year of Xerxes by the book’s reckoning (about 474 BC). ⚠ The date rests on the book’s chronology as the site has it for Esther 1 (the third year, 483 BC, and the seventh year, 479); this chapter names no year of its own.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Esther 4:1–3 Mordecai in sackcloth and ashes — the king’s gate — mourning in every province

⭐ Sackcloth, ashes, and a cry. “And Mordecai tore” stands in this one verse, and “a great and bitter cry” stands in this one verse. “Sackcloth and ashes” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: Esther 4:1 and 4:3, Isaiah 58:5 (now on these pages, where it is the fast Jehovah asks about) and Daniel 9:3 (now on these pages); the words are saq and efer. The KJV has “put on sackcloth with ashes”, the NIV “put on sackcloth and ashes, and went out into the city, wailing loudly and bitterly”, the NWT 1984 “rip his garments apart and put on sackcloth and ashes”, the TLB “crying with a loud and bitter wail” and the Douay “shewing the anguish of his mind”.

⭐ The king’s gate. “For none could come into the king’s gate” stands in this one verse: Mordecai gets as far as the gate, because sackcloth cannot go in. The KJV has “for none might enter into the king’s gate clothed with sackcloth”, the ASV “for none might enter within the king’s gate clothed with sackcloth” and the Geneva “but he might not enter within the king’s gate, being clothed with sackcloth”.

⭐ In every province. The verse says it twice over, province and province (medinah), wherever the king’s word and his law reached: great mourning among the Jews, and four things done, fasting, weeping, lamentation and the sackcloth and ashes spread as a bed for the many. None of the four is a prayer. The KJV has “great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing”, the NIV “with fasting, weeping and wailing”, the Geneva “great sorrow among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and mourning” and the Douay “many using sackcloth and ashes for their bed”.

Verses 4–9 · Esther 4:4–9 the queen writhes — Hatach goes out — the money and the copy of the law

⭐ The queen writhes. “And she writhed” (vattitchalchal) stands in this one verse; the versions take it as distress. The KJV has “exceedingly grieved”, the NIV “she was in great distress”, the TLB “she was deeply distressed”, the NWT 1984 “the queen was very much pained” and the Douay “she was in a consternation”. She sends clothes to take the sackcloth off him, and “he did not accept” stands in this one verse. ⚠ At the start of this verse the Hebrew prints the maids’ coming twice, written tavo’ina and read tavo’na, a ketiv/qere pair kept in the Hebrew column.

⭐ Hatach. “Esther called Hatach” stands in this one verse. The name stands in four verses, 4:5, 4:6, 4:9 and 4:10, all in this chapter, and he is one of the king’s eunuchs (saris) set to attend her. The spelling is not settled: the KJV has “Hatach”, the ASV and the TLB “Hathach”, the NIV “Hathak” and the Douay “Athach”.

⭐ The money, and the copy. “The exact statement of the money” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “the sum of the money”, the ASV “the exact sum of the money”, the NIV “the exact amount of money”, the NWT 1984 “the exact statement of the money”, the Geneva “the sum of the silver” and the TLB “$20,000,000”, which is a modern figure the Hebrew does not give. The copy is patshegen, a word taken from Persian, and it stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, the other two being Esther 3:14 and Esther 8:13. It is a copy of the written law (dat) that went out from Shushan. The KJV has “the copy of the writing of the decree”, the NIV “a copy of the text of the edict”, the NWT 1984 “a copy of the writing of the law”, the TLB “a copy of the king’s decree” and the Douay “a copy of the edict which was hanging up in Susan”. ⚠ The same pair of written and read forms comes at the end of verse 7, written biyehudiyyim and read bayehudim, “the Jews”.

⭐ To plead for her people. Hatach is to show Esther the copy and tell her, and charge her to go in to the king lehitchannen lo, to plead with him, and to make request before him for her people. The KJV has “to make supplication unto him, and to make request before him for her people”, the NIV “to beg for mercy and plead with him for her people” and the TLB “that she should go to the king to plead for her people”.

Verses 10–12 · Esther 4:10–12 one law — the golden scepter — not called these thirty days

⭐ One law. “One law of his” stands in this one verse: whoever goes in to the king in the inner court (chatzer) without being called is to be put to death, except the one to whom he holds out the golden scepter, which stands in two verses, here and Esther 5:2. The KJV has “there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live”, the ASV “there is one law for him, that he be put to death”, the NIV “the king has but one law: that they be put to death unless the king extends the gold scepter to them and spares their lives”, the NWT 1984 “only in case the king holds out to him the golden scepter” and the TLB “is doomed to die unless the king holds out his gold scepter”.

⭐ Thirty days. “I have not been called to come in to the king” stands in this one verse, zeh sheloshim yom, “these thirty days”. This is her reason, and it is the one fact in her answer that Mordecai’s message did not have. The KJV has “but I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days”, the NIV “But thirty days have passed since I was called to go to the king”, the NWT 1984 “I have not been called to come in to the king now for thirty days”, the TLB “the king has not called for me to come to him in more than a month” and the Douay “who for these thirty days now have not been called unto him”.

Verses 13–14 · Esther 4:13–14 “do not imagine” — relief from another place — “for a time like this”

⭐ “Do not imagine.” “Do not imagine in your soul” stands in this one verse: the king’s house is no shelter. The KJV has “Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king’s house, more than all the Jews”, the NIV “Do not think that because you are in the king’s house you alone of all the Jews will escape”, the NWT 1984 “Do not imagine within your own soul that the king’s household will escape any more than all the other Jews” and the TLB “Do you think you will escape there in the palace when all other Jews are killed?”.

⭐ Silent, and relief from another place. “If you are altogether silent” stands in this one verse, and then “relief and deliverance” stands in this one verse: revach is room or breathing space, the word for the gap Jacob orders between his droves at Genesis 32:17 (now on these pages). “From another place” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place”, the ASV “then will relief and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place”, the Geneva “comfort and deliverance shall appear to the Jews out of another place”, the NWT 1984 “relief and deliverance themselves will stand up for the Jews from another place” and the Douay “the Jews shall be delivered by some other occasion”.

⚠ The word that is not there. The Hebrew does not say who or what the other place is, and a search of the whole book finds neither the Name nor elohim in any verse of Esther. The TLB supplies the word twice in this verse: “God will deliver the Jews from some other source” and “who can say but that God has brought you into the palace for just such a time as this”. Neither “God” is in the Hebrew. In later Jewish usage the word for place, makom, became a name for God; that is a later reading of the word, not something the verse says, and this translation keeps “another place”.

⭐ “For a time like this.” “For a time like this” stands in this one verse, and “you have come to the kingdom” stands in this one verse. The question is put with umi yode’a: “And who knows” stands in three verses, Ecclesiastes 2:19, Ecclesiastes 8:1 and this one, and “who knows” stands in ten, among them David at 2 Samuel 12:22 and Psalm 90:11 (both now on these pages), Joel 2:14 (now on these pages) and Jonah 3:9 (not yet). It is a question and not a statement: the verse does not say she was brought there for this. The KJV has “who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this?”, the NIV “And who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this?”, the NWT 1984 “who is there knowing whether it is for a time like this that you have attained to royal dignity?”, the Geneva “who knoweth whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time?” and the Douay “who knoweth whether thou art not therefore come to the kingdom, that thou mightest be ready in such a time as this?”.

Verses 15–17 · Esther 4:15–17 fast for me — “if I perish, I perish” — Mordecai passed on

⭐ Fast for me. “Three days, night and day” stands in this one verse, and “and do not eat and do not drink” stands in this one verse: the fast is without water. The verb is the plain tsumu, “fast” (tsom). The KJV has “fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day”, the NIV “fast for me. Do not eat or drink for three days, night or day”, the NWT 1984 “fast in my behalf and neither eat nor drink for three days, night and day” and the TLB “fast for me”. ⚠ The Douay has “pray ye for me. Neither eat nor drink for three days and three nights”, which puts a prayer where the Hebrew has a fast; the Hebrew gives no prayer here, as it gave none in verse 3.

⭐ “Which is not according to the law.” “Which is not according to the law” stands in this one verse, the dat of verse 11, now the thing she will break; and “if I perish, I perish” stands in this one verse, the same shape as Jacob’s “if I am bereaved, I am bereaved” at Genesis 43:14 (now on these pages). The KJV has “and if I perish, I perish”, the NIV “And if I perish, I perish”, the NWT 1984 “in case I must perish, I must perish” and the Douay “expose myself to death and to danger”.

⭐ Mordecai passed on. “And Mordecai passed on” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “So Mordecai went his way”, the NWT 1984 “passed along and proceeded to do according to all that Esther had laid in command upon him”. The order has turned: in chapter 2 Esther does what Mordecai commands (Esther 2:10 and 2:20), and here Mordecai does “according to all that Esther had commanded him”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “fast” for tsom; “sackcloth and ashes” for saq va-efer; “the law” for dat in verses 3, 8, 11 and 16; “relief” for revach and “deliverance” for hatsalah; “another place” for makom acher; “who knows whether” for umi yode’a im; “perish” for avad in verses 14 and 16. The Hebrew has two written and read pairs in this chapter, at verse 4 and verse 7, kept as it is printed. No word for God is added, because none is written.

Echoes paid. Isaiah 58:5 (the fast with sackcloth and ashes); Genesis 32:17 (revach) and 43:14 (“if I am bereaved”); 2 Samuel 12:22 and Psalm 90:11 (“who knows”).

Echoes planted. Esther 3:14, 5:2 and 8:13 (the copy and the golden scepter), Esther 2:10 and 2:20, Daniel 9:3, Ecclesiastes 2:19 and 8:1, and Jonah 3:9. None of these is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Esther stands on these pages at chapters 1 and 4, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Esther 2.
