🕰
Moses recites the Ten Words a second time, to the generation that did not stand at Horeb — the same covenant document as Exodus 20, restated with real wording differences (the sabbath's motive clause, the fifth Word's added promise, the ninth Word's vocabulary, the tenth Word's reordering). The chapter closes with the people's own request, remembered in their own voice, for a mediator, and Jehovah's approval of that request — the origin of Moses' standing role for the rest of the book.
Same setting as chapter 4 — the plains of Moab, in the fortieth year — but the CONTENT reported is Horeb itself, thirty-eight years earlier: a recitation of the covenant made at the mountain (Exodus 19-20, already on these pages), not a new event in Moab. Chapter 4 closes on its own petuchah (open paragraph break, confirmed against the archived Hebrew text), so the Masoretic text itself marks a real seam here even though Moses' voice runs on without interruption.

Deuteronomy 5 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיִּקְרָא מֹשֶׁה, אֶל-כָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-הַחֻקִּים וְאֶת-הַמִּשְׁפָּטִים, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי דֹּבֵר בְּאָזְנֵיכֶם הַיּוֹם; וּלְמַדְתֶּם אֹתָם, וּשְׁמַרְתֶּם לַעֲשֹׂתָם.
And Moses called to all Israel and said to them: Hear, O Israel, the statutes and the ordinances that I am speaking in your ears today; and you shall learn them, and keep them, to do them.note
Y Moisés llamó a todo Israel, y les dijo: Oye, Israel, los estatutos y las ordenanzas que hoy hablo en tus oídos; apréndelos, y guárdalos, para ponerlos por obra.
2
יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ, כָּרַת עִמָּנוּ בְּרִית--בְּחֹרֵב.
Jehovah our God made a covenant with us at Horeb.note
Jehová nuestro Dios hizo un pacto con nosotros en Horeb.
3
לֹא אֶת-אֲבֹתֵינוּ, כָּרַת יְהוָה אֶת-הַבְּרִית הַזֹּאת: כִּי אִתָּנוּ, אֲנַחְנוּ אֵלֶּה פֹה הַיּוֹם כֻּלָּנוּ חַיִּים.
Jehovah did not make this covenant with our fathers, but with us — we who are here, all of us, alive today.note
Jehová no hizo este pacto con nuestros padres, sino con nosotros —los que estamos aquí, todos nosotros, vivos hoy.
4
פָּנִים בְּפָנִים, דִּבֶּר יְהוָה עִמָּכֶם בָּהָר--מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ.
Jehovah spoke with you face to face at the mountain, out of the midst of the fire —note
Jehová habló con ustedes cara a cara en el monte, de en medio del fuego —
5
אָנֹכִי עֹמֵד בֵּין-יְהוָה וּבֵינֵיכֶם, בָּעֵת הַהִוא, לְהַגִּיד לָכֶם, אֶת-דְּבַר יְהוָה: כִּי יְרֵאתֶם מִפְּנֵי הָאֵשׁ, וְלֹא-עֲלִיתֶם בָּהָר לֵאמֹר. {ס}
I was standing between Jehovah and you at that time, to declare to you the word of Jehovah, for you were afraid because of the fire and did not go up the mountain — saying,note
Yo estuve de pie entre Jehová y ustedes en aquel tiempo, para declararles la palabra de Jehová, porque ustedes tuvieron miedo a causa del fuego y no subieron al monte —diciendo:
6
אָנֹכִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, אֲשֶׁר הוֹצֵאתִיךָ מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם מִבֵּית עֲבָדִים: לֹא-יִהְיֶה לְךָ אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים, עַל-פָּנָי.
EN 6–7 I am Jehovah your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slaves. You shall have no other gods before my face.note
ES 6–7 Yo soy Jehová tu Dios, que te saqué de la tierra de Egipto, de la casa de esclavos. No tendrás otros dioses delante de mi rostro.
7
לֹא-תַעֲשֶׂה לְךָ פֶסֶל, כָּל-תְּמוּנָה, אֲשֶׁר בַּשָּׁמַיִם מִמַּעַל, וַאֲשֶׁר בָּאָרֶץ מִתָּחַת--וַאֲשֶׁר בַּמַּיִם, מִתַּחַת לָאָרֶץ.
EN 8 You shall not make for yourself a carved image, any form of what is in the heavens above, or what is on the earth beneath, or what is in the water under the earth.note
ES 8 No te harás imagen tallada, ninguna forma de lo que hay en los cielos arriba, ni de lo que hay en la tierra abajo, ni de lo que hay en las aguas debajo de la tierra.
8
לֹא-תִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה לָהֶם, וְלֹא תָעָבְדֵם: כִּי אָנֹכִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, אֵל קַנָּא--פֹּקֵד עֲוֺן אָבוֹת עַל-בָּנִים וְעַל-שִׁלֵּשִׁים וְעַל-רִבֵּעִים, לְשֹׂנְאָי.
EN 9 You shall not bow down to them and you shall not serve them, for I, Jehovah your God, am a jealous God, attending to the guilt of fathers upon sons, and upon the third and upon the fourth generation of those who hate me,note
ES 9 No te inclinarás a ellas, ni las servirás; porque yo, Jehová tu Dios, soy un Dios celoso, que atiende a la culpa de los padres sobre los hijos, y sobre la tercera y sobre la cuarta generación de los que me aborrecen,
9
וְעֹשֶׂה חֶסֶד, לַאֲלָפִים--לְאֹהֲבַי, וּלְשֹׁמְרֵי מצותו מִצְוֺתָי. {ס}
EN 10 and doing loyal-love to the thousandth generation of those who love me and keep my commandments.note
ES 10 y hace amor leal hasta la milésima generación de los que me aman y guardan mis mandamientos.
10
לֹא תִשָּׂא אֶת-שֵׁם-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, לַשָּׁוְא: כִּי לֹא יְנַקֶּה יְהוָה, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-יִשָּׂא אֶת-שְׁמוֹ לַשָּׁוְא. {ס}
EN 11 You shall not lift up the name of Jehovah your God for emptiness, for Jehovah will not hold guiltless the one who lifts up his name for emptiness.note
ES 11 No alzarás el nombre de Jehová tu Dios en vacío, porque Jehová no dará por inocente al que alza su nombre en vacío.
11
שָׁמוֹר אֶת-יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, לְקַדְּשׁוֹ, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוְּךָ, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ.
EN 12 Keep the day of the sabbath, to hallow it, just as Jehovah your God commanded you.note
ES 12 Guarda el día del sábado, para santificarlo, tal como Jehová tu Dios te mandó.
12
שֵׁשֶׁת יָמִים תַּעֲבֹד, וְעָשִׂיתָ כָּל-מְלַאכְתֶּךָ.
EN 13 Six days you shall serve, and do all your work;note
ES 13 Seis días servirás, y harás toda tu obra;

13
וְיוֹם, הַשְּׁבִיעִי--שַׁבָּת, לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ: לֹא תַעֲשֶׂה כָל-מְלָאכָה אַתָּה וּבִנְךָ-וּבִתֶּךָ וְעַבְדְּךָ-וַאֲמָתֶךָ וְשׁוֹרְךָ וַחֲמֹרְךָ וְכָל-בְּהֶמְתֶּךָ, וְגֵרְךָ אֲשֶׁר בִּשְׁעָרֶיךָ--לְמַעַן יָנוּחַ עַבְדְּךָ וַאֲמָתְךָ, כָּמוֹךָ.
EN 14 but the seventh day is a sabbath to Jehovah your God. You shall not do any work — you, or your son, or your daughter, or your male slave, or your female slave, or your ox, or your donkey, or any of your cattle, or the resident alien who is inside your gates — so that your male slave and your female slave may rest, as you do.note
ES 14 mas el séptimo día es sábado para Jehová tu Dios: no harás ninguna obra —tú, ni tu hijo, ni tu hija, ni tu esclavo, ni tu esclava, ni tu buey, ni tu asno, ni ninguno de tu ganado, ni el extranjero que está dentro de tus puertas— para que descansen tu esclavo y tu esclava, como tú.
14
וְזָכַרְתָּ, כִּי עֶבֶד הָיִיתָ בְּאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם, וַיֹּצִאֲךָ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ מִשָּׁם, בְּיָד חֲזָקָה וּבִזְרֹעַ נְטוּיָה; עַל-כֵּן, צִוְּךָ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, לַעֲשׂוֹת, אֶת-יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת. {ס}
EN 15 And you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and Jehovah your God brought you out from there with a strong hand and an outstretched arm; therefore Jehovah your God has commanded you to keep the day of the sabbath.note
ES 15 Y te acordarás de que fuiste esclavo en la tierra de Egipto, y Jehová tu Dios te sacó de allí con mano fuerte y brazo extendido; por eso Jehová tu Dios te ha mandado guardar el día del sábado.
15
כַּבֵּד אֶת-אָבִיךָ וְאֶת-אִמֶּךָ, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוְּךָ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ--לְמַעַן יַאֲרִיכֻן יָמֶיךָ, וּלְמַעַן יִיטַב לָךְ, עַל הָאֲדָמָה, אֲשֶׁר-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ נֹתֵן לָךְ. {ס}
EN 16 Honor your father and your mother, just as Jehovah your God commanded you, so that your days may be long, and so that it may go well with you, on the ground that Jehovah your God is giving you.note
ES 16 Honra a tu padre y a tu madre, tal como Jehová tu Dios te mandó, para que se alarguen tus días, y para que te vaya bien, sobre la tierra que Jehová tu Dios te da.
16
לֹא תִרְצָח, {ס} וְלֹא תִנְאָף; {ס} וְלֹא תִגְנֹב, {ס} וְלֹא-תַעֲנֶה בְרֵעֲךָ עֵד שָׁוְא. {ס}
EN 17–20 You shall not murder. Neither shall you commit adultery. Neither shall you steal. Neither shall you answer against your neighbour as a witness of emptiness.note
ES 17–20 No matarás. Ni cometerás adulterio. Ni robarás. Ni responderás contra tu prójimo como testigo en vacío.
17
וְלֹא תַחְמֹד, אֵשֶׁת רֵעֶךָ; {ס} וְלֹא תִתְאַוֶּה בֵּית רֵעֶךָ, שָׂדֵהוּ וְעַבְדּוֹ וַאֲמָתוֹ שׁוֹרוֹ וַחֲמֹרוֹ, וְכֹל, אֲשֶׁר לְרֵעֶךָ. {ס}
EN 21 Neither shall you covet your neighbour’s wife; neither shall you desire your neighbour’s house — his field, or his male slave, or his female slave, his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbour’s.note
ES 21 Ni codiciarás la mujer de tu prójimo; ni desearás la casa de tu prójimo —su campo, o su esclavo, o su esclava, su buey, o su asno, o cualquier cosa que sea de tu prójimo.
18
אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֶל-כָּל-קְהַלְכֶם בָּהָר, מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ הֶעָנָן וְהָעֲרָפֶל--קוֹל גָּדוֹל, וְלֹא יָסָף; וַיִּכְתְּבֵם, עַל-שְׁנֵי לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים, וַיִּתְּנֵם, אֵלָי.
EN 22 These words Jehovah spoke to all your assembly at the mountain, out of the midst of the fire, the cloud, and the thick darkness, with a great voice — and he added nothing more. And he wrote them on two tablets of stone, and gave them to me.note
ES 22 Estas palabras habló Jehová a toda su asamblea de ustedes en el monte, de en medio del fuego, la nube y la densa oscuridad, con una voz grande —y no añadió nada más. Y las escribió sobre dos tablas de piedra, y me las dio.
19
וַיְהִי, כְּשָׁמְעֲכֶם אֶת-הַקּוֹל מִתּוֹךְ הַחֹשֶׁךְ, וְהָהָר, בֹּעֵר בָּאֵשׁ; וַתִּקְרְבוּן אֵלַי, כָּל-רָאשֵׁי שִׁבְטֵיכֶם וְזִקְנֵיכֶם.
EN 23 And when you heard the voice out of the midst of the darkness, while the mountain was burning with fire, you came near to me, all the heads of your tribes, and your elders,note
ES 23 Y sucedió que, cuando ustedes oyeron la voz de en medio de la oscuridad, mientras el monte ardía en fuego, se acercaron a mí todos los jefes de sus tribus y sus ancianos,
20
וַתֹּאמְרוּ, הֵן הֶרְאָנוּ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ אֶת-כְּבֹדוֹ וְאֶת-גָּדְלוֹ, וְאֶת-קֹלוֹ שָׁמַעְנוּ, מִתּוֹךְ הָאֵשׁ; הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה רָאִינוּ, כִּי-יְדַבֵּר אֱלֹהִים אֶת-הָאָדָם וָחָי.
EN 24 and you said, ‘Look — Jehovah our God has shown us his glory and his greatness, and we have heard his voice out of the midst of the fire; today we have seen that God may speak with a human being, and he still lives.note
ES 24 y dijeron: Mira —Jehová nuestro Dios nos ha mostrado su gloria y su grandeza, y hemos oído su voz de en medio del fuego; hoy hemos visto que Dios puede hablar con un ser humano, y este sigue vivo.
21
וְעַתָּה, לָמָּה נָמוּת, כִּי תֹאכְלֵנוּ, הָאֵשׁ הַגְּדֹלָה הַזֹּאת; אִם-יֹסְפִים אֲנַחְנוּ, לִשְׁמֹעַ אֶת-קוֹל יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ עוֹד--וָמָתְנוּ.
EN 25 So now, why should we die? For this great fire will consume us — if we continue to hear the voice of Jehovah our God any longer, we will die.note
ES 25 Y ahora, ¿por qué hemos de morir? Porque este gran fuego nos consumirá —si seguimos oyendo la voz de Jehová nuestro Dios, moriremos.

22
כִּי מִי כָל-בָּשָׂר אֲשֶׁר שָׁמַע קוֹל אֱלֹהִים חַיִּים מְדַבֵּר מִתּוֹךְ-הָאֵשׁ, כָּמֹנוּ--וַיֶּחִי.
EN 26 For who is there of all flesh who has heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as we have, and lived?note
ES 26 Porque ¿quién, de toda carne, ha oído la voz del Dios viviente hablando de en medio del fuego, como nosotros, y ha vivido?
23
קְרַב אַתָּה וּשְׁמָע, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר יֹאמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ; וְאַתְּ תְּדַבֵּר אֵלֵינוּ, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר יְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ אֵלֶיךָ--וְשָׁמַעְנוּ וְעָשִׂינוּ.
EN 27 You go near and hear all that Jehovah our God will say, and you shall speak to us all that Jehovah our God speaks to you, and we will hear it and do it.’note
ES 27 Acércate tú, y oye todo lo que diga Jehová nuestro Dios; y tú nos hablarás todo lo que Jehová nuestro Dios te hable, y nosotros oiremos y lo haremos.
24
וַיִּשְׁמַע יְהוָה אֶת-קוֹל דִּבְרֵיכֶם, בְּדַבֶּרְכֶם אֵלָי; וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֵלַי, שָׁמַעְתִּי אֶת-קוֹל דִּבְרֵי הָעָם הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר דִּבְּרוּ אֵלֶיךָ--הֵיטִיבוּ, כָּל-אֲשֶׁר דִּבֵּרוּ.
EN 28 And Jehovah heard the sound of your words when you spoke to me, and Jehovah said to me, ‘I have heard the sound of the words of this people, which they have spoken to you — they have done well in all that they have spoken.note
ES 28 Y Jehová oyó el sonido de las palabras de ustedes cuando me hablaban, y Jehová me dijo: He oído el sonido de las palabras de este pueblo, que te han hablado —han hecho bien en todo lo que han hablado.
25
מִי-יִתֵּן וְהָיָה לְבָבָם זֶה לָהֶם, לְיִרְאָה אֹתִי וְלִשְׁמֹר אֶת-כָּל-מִצְוֺתַי--כָּל-הַיָּמִים: לְמַעַן יִיטַב לָהֶם וְלִבְנֵיהֶם, לְעֹלָם.
EN 29 Oh, that they had such a heart as this always, to fear me and to keep all my commandments, so that it might go well with them and with their children forever!note
ES 29 ¡Ojalá tuvieran tal corazón como este siempre, para temerme y guardar todos mis mandamientos, para que les vaya bien a ellos y a sus hijos para siempre!
26
לֵךְ, אֱמֹר לָהֶם: שׁוּבוּ לָכֶם, לְאָהֳלֵיכֶם.
EN 30 Go, say to them: Return to your tents.’note
ES 30 Ve, diles: Vuelvan a sus tiendas.
27
וְאַתָּה, פֹּה עֲמֹד עִמָּדִי, וַאֲדַבְּרָה אֵלֶיךָ אֵת כָּל-הַמִּצְוָה וְהַחֻקִּים וְהַמִּשְׁפָּטִים, אֲשֶׁר תְּלַמְּדֵם; וְעָשׂוּ בָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי נֹתֵן לָהֶם לְרִשְׁתָּהּ.
EN 31 But you, stand here with me, and I will speak to you all the commandment, and the statutes, and the ordinances, that you shall teach them, so that they may do them in the land that I am giving them to take possession of it.note

Translator’s Notes

Verse 1 · “Shema Yisrael” — the exact words of the Shema, spoken here first, about a different subject

★ The two words that open this verse’s command — shema Yisrael, ‘Hear, O Israel’ — are the identical two words, in the identical order, that become Judaism’s central declaration one chapter later: “Hear, O Israel: Jehovah our God, Jehovah is one” (Deuteronomy 6:4, not yet on these pages). Deuteronomy 4’s own closing note, checking this chapter’s forward boundary, had already flagged the shema ROOT as building toward that declaration; searching the whole archived Hebrew Bible for the exact two-word phrase turns up only four candidates, and the other three are all later in this same book (9:1; 20:3) or a false match on unrelated grammar (Genesis 35:22, where the same letters mean ‘and Israel heard,’ not an imperative addressed to the nation). So this is the FIRST time the vocative ‘Hear, O Israel’ is spoken in the Bible, in the order this project is translating — one chapter earlier than the verse that made the phrase famous, and here addressed not to a confession of God’s oneness but to a set of statutes and ordinances Moses is about to repeat.

Moses calls “all Israel” together to address them directly, in his own first-person voice (“that I am speaking”) — the frame for the whole recitation that follows, in which Moses himself, not a narrator, reports the Ten Words a second time.

Verses 2–5 · “not with our fathers” — a covenant claimed personally, and Moses named as go-between

★ Moses makes a striking, almost paradoxical claim: the Horeb covenant was made “not with our fathers, but with us — we who are here, all of us, alive today” (v3). Strictly, most of this audience were children or not yet born at Horeb, thirty-eight years earlier; the generation that stood at the mountain has largely died in the wilderness (a fact this book has already reckoned with at length). Moses collapses that gap deliberately — the covenant is not a historical curiosity inherited from dead ancestors, but a live obligation resting on the people standing in front of him right now.

“Made” (v2, karat) is literally ‘cut’ — see the new dictionary entry for why that verb is not decorative. V5 gives Moses his role for the first time in these exact terms: he “stood between Jehovah and you,” because the people themselves were too afraid of the fire to go up the mountain (developed fully at vv23–27 below) — the origin, inside this chapter’s own narrative, of Moses as mediator.

Verses 6–9 · the first two Words repeated — a written ‘his’ read aloud as ‘my’, and jealousy’s third appearance

These four verses restate the first two of the Ten Words almost exactly as Exodus 20:2–6 (already on these pages) gives them — the same self-introduction, the same ban on carved images, the same warning about a jealous God visiting guilt on children while showing loyal-love to thousands. The wording is close enough, verse for verse, that a reader can lay the two chapters side by side and see a covenant document being read back rather than paraphrased.

⚠ The Hebrew of v9 carries a genuine textual oddity Exodus 20’s parallel does not: the text as WRITTEN spells ‘his commandments’ (mitzvato), and a marginal note tells the reader to SAY ‘my commandments’ (mitzvotai) instead — a kethiv/qere pair, confirmed directly against the archived Masoretic text. Exodus 20’s own version of this clause has no such split; the scribal tradition preserved a written slip at exactly the point in the Decalogue’s SECOND recitation where a copyist’s eye might most easily drift from first person to third. Every translation checked, on both shelves, follows the qere and prints ‘my commandments’ without comment.

Qanna, ‘jealous,’ makes its THIRD appearance on these pages at v8 — after Exodus 34:14 and Deuteronomy 4:24 — here inside the very legal text Deuteronomy 4 was paraphrasing when it first quoted the phrase.

Verse 10 · the third Word — “for emptiness,” a word this chapter will use again

The third Word forbids lifting up Jehovah’s name la-shav, ‘for emptiness’ — matching Exodus 20:6–7 (already on these pages) almost letter for letter. What is worth flagging here, before it pays off, is the word itself: shav recurs in this very chapter, at v16, in a place Exodus 20 does NOT use it — see that note for the echo in full.

Verses 11–14 · “Remember” becomes “Keep” — the fourth Word’s two genuinely different reasons

★ This is the sharpest verified difference between the two Decalogues, and the Hebrew makes it visible in the very first word: Exodus 20:7 opens the sabbath command with zakhor, ‘Remember’; this verse opens it with a different verb, shamor, ‘Keep.’ The two verbs are not interchangeable placeholders — the existing zakhor dictionary entry already flagged this exact pair while discussing Exodus 20, and this chapter is where the promise is paid off. This translation renders the difference visibly: ‘Remember the day of the sabbath’ at Exodus 20:7, ‘Keep the day of the sabbath’ here.

The reason clause attached to the command differs too, and not by accident. Exodus 20:11 grounds the sabbath in CREATION — ‘for in six days Jehovah made the heavens and the earth… and he rested on the seventh day.’ This chapter grounds it in the EXODUS instead (v14) — ‘you shall remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt, and Jehovah your God brought you out from there… therefore Jehovah your God has commanded you to keep the day of the sabbath.’ Notice that zakhor, ‘remember,’ has not vanished from this version at all — it has simply been redirected, from remembering the sabbath itself to remembering slavery in Egypt as the sabbath’s reason. Jewish tradition holds that both words, shamor and zakhor, were spoken by God in a single utterance at Sinai — a theological answer to what is, textually, two different verses.

V13’s list of who must rest is also more specific than Exodus 20’s (which names ‘your beast’ in general): here it is ‘your ox, or your donkey, or any of your cattle’, three terms where Exodus has one. And this verse alone adds an explicit social purpose the creation-based version never states — ‘so that your male slave and your female slave may rest, as you do’ — a motive with no equivalent at Exodus 20:10–11.

Checked directly against the fetched shelf (all ten English and Spanish witnesses this project tracks), zero of the ten print ‘Remember’ at this verse — every witness agrees the two Decalogues open the fourth Word with different verbs, even where they render the difference differently. NIV and the NWT’s 2013 revision both contain ‘Observe the Sabbath day’ verbatim, matching this translation’s own choice of a KEEP-family verb over REMEMBER; KJV and the Geneva Bible both read ‘Keep the sabbath day to sanctify it’ — a near-tautology in English (keep…to sanctify) this translation avoids by pairing ‘Keep’ with ‘hallow’ instead; ASV alone reaches for ‘Observe the sabbath day, to keep it holy’ — the reverse pairing from this translation’s own; and Douay-Rheims differs from all of them, ‘Observe the day of the sabbath, to sanctify it’ — the noun phrase reordered (‘day of the sabbath,’ not ‘sabbath day’) on top of a third verb pairing. On the Spanish shelf, RV60 prints ‘Guardarás el día de reposo’ and the RV 1909 the closely related ‘Guardarás el día DEL reposo’ (the definite article added) — both matching this translation’s ‘Keep’ family; the NVI chooses ‘Observa el día sábado’ instead; and only the TNM’s 2019 revision reaches for ‘Celebra el sábado,’ ‘Celebrate the sabbath,’ a genuinely different verb again.

Verse 15 · “and that it may go well with you” — the fifth Word gains a clause Paul later quotes

★ Checked directly against Exodus 20:11’s own wording (already on these pages), this verse adds two clauses to the command to honor father and mother that Exodus does not have: ‘just as Jehovah your God commanded you’ and ‘and so that it may go well with you.’ Exodus gives one motive — long days on the land. Deuteronomy gives two, doubling the promise attached to the single command the New Testament quotes as carrying a promise at all (Ephesians 6:2–3, not yet on these pages).

⚠ That NT citation is itself a small piece of textual evidence worth stating plainly, confirmed directly against the archived Greek: Ephesians 6:3 reads hina eu soi genētai kai esē makrochronios epi tēs gēs — ‘so that it MAY GO WELL WITH YOU, and you will be long-lived on the earth’ — both clauses, in that order. Paul is quoting the DEUTERONOMY form of the fifth Word, with its added ‘go well with you,’ not the shorter Exodus 20 form. A detail invisible unless the two Old Testament chapters are compared first.

Verse 16 · four Words in one verse — and a “witness of emptiness” that answers the third Word

Hebrew compresses the sixth through ninth Words — murder, adultery, theft, false witness — into a single verse, four bare two-word prohibitions in a row; the split into four separate verses is an English (and Spanish) numbering convention, not a feature of the Masoretic text.

★ The ninth Word’s wording is where this chapter quietly out-does Exodus 20 at its own game. Exodus 20:16 (already on these pages) forbids testifying ‘as a false witness,’ using the noun sheqer, ‘falsehood.’ This verse instead uses shav, ‘emptiness’ — the SAME word already used four verses earlier, at v10, for taking Jehovah’s name ‘for emptiness.’ Exodus 20 names misusing the divine name and lying under oath as two separate wrongs, with two separate Hebrew nouns; this chapter, alone on these pages, names them with the identical word. A structural tightness Exodus does not share.

⚠ Fetched directly and compared word for word against Exodus 20:16, not one witness on the shelf marks this Hebrew distinction — but they get there two different ways. KJV, ASV, Douay-Rheims and the Geneva Bible all use ‘bear false witness against thy neighbour’ here, the literal identical clause each of them already used at Exodus 20:16 — the same fixed English phrase covering two different Hebrew nouns. NIV does the same with its own fixed phrase, ‘give false testimony against your neighbor,’ word for word at both verses. The NWT is the one witness that actually varies its wording between the two chapters — at Exodus 20:16 both its editions explicitly name a witness (1984 ‘testify falsely as a witness’; 2013 ‘when you are a witness’), but here the NWT drops that word entirely: ‘testify to a falsehood against your fellowman’ — no mention of a witness at all — even though the Hebrew ed, ‘witness,’ is present in both verses identically. On the Spanish shelf, RV60, the RV 1909 and NVI all keep ‘falso testimonio’ here too (the NVI word for word identical to its own Exodus 20:16); the TNM is the Spanish sibling of the same split — its 1987 edition drops ‘testigo’ the way NWT 1984 drops ‘witness,’ and its own 2019 revision keeps ‘falso testimonio’ but still drops the separate ‘testigo’ clause Exodus 20:16 has.

Verse 17 · the tenth Word reordered — wife before house, and a second verb for wanting

★ The chamad dictionary entry, written when Exodus 20 shipped, already promised this verse as the place where the tenth Word changes shape — and checked directly against the Hebrew of both chapters, it does, in two ways at once. Exodus 20:14 (already on these pages) lists the neighbour’s HOUSE first, then his wife and everything else, all under the SAME verb, chamad, ‘covet,’ used twice. This verse puts the WIFE first, on her own, still under chamad — and then switches to a second, different verb, tit’avveh, ‘desire,’ for the house, the field, and everything else. Two real changes: the order is reversed, and where Exodus uses one verb twice, this chapter uses two.

The taah dictionary entry already tracks where tit’avveh’s own letters lead: spelled identically to the rare surveyor’s verb that marks out the borders of Canaan at Numbers 34:7–10 (already on these pages) — a coincidence of spelling, not a shared root, but a genuinely checkable one on the page. This verse is also the only place in the whole Hebrew Bible where tit’avveh means ‘desire’ and includes ‘his FIELD’ among the neighbour’s property — a term Exodus 20’s list does not have at all.

⚠ Checked directly against the shelf, the two verbs are assigned differently version to version — seven English witnesses, six different treatments of the same two clauses (the NWT alone changing between its own two editions). ASV, NIV and the Geneva Bible all match this translation’s own mapping, ‘covet’ the wife and ‘desire’ the house; KJV reverses it exactly — ‘desire’ the wife, ‘covet’ the house; the NWT’s 1984 edition reaches for a third pairing, ‘desire’ the wife and ‘crave’ the house — its own 2013 revision then drops the distinction its predecessor kept, using ‘desire’ for both; and TLB a fourth pairing again, ‘burn with desire’ for the wife and ‘envy’ for the house. Douay-Rheims drops the second verb’s distinctness altogether, using ‘covet’ once and letting the house, field and the rest simply follow as its object. On the Spanish shelf the picture is tighter: RV60, the RV 1909 and NVI all agree with this translation, ‘codiciar’ the wife and ‘desear’ the house — but the TNM (both editions) uses ‘desear’ for both, adding only an intensifier (‘desear con vehemencia’ / ‘desees egoístamente’) to mark the second clause, rather than a genuinely second verb.

Verse 18 · “and he added nothing more” — the lawgiver keeping the rule he is about to give

★ The Hebrew verb behind ‘added’ here is yasaph — the identical root Deuteronomy 4:2 (already on these pages) used to forbid ISRAEL from adding to Moses’ own words: ‘you shall not add to the word that I am commanding you.’ Here, one chapter later, the same verb describes what JEHOVAH himself did at Horeb when he spoke the Ten Words: he ‘added nothing more.’ The restraint Moses will demand of Israel in chapter 4 is modeled by God himself in chapter 5’s own narrated past — a rule that precedes the command to keep it.

The same root recurs once more, three verses later (v21), and in the opposite direction — the terrified people beg Moses NOT to let them go on hearing God’s voice: ‘if we continue [yosphim, the same root] to hear the voice of Jehovah our God any longer, we will die.’ Between v18 and v21, the same Hebrew root moves from a limit God sets on his own speech to a limit the people beg him to set on theirs.

Verses 19–27 · the people’s fear, and the request that makes Moses a mediator

This is the same scene Exodus 20:18–21 (already on these pages) narrates in the third person — the people trembling at a distance while Moses approaches the thick darkness — retold here in the FIRST person, by Moses himself, to the very generation that was not there to hear it happen. The content agrees closely with Exodus’s account (v20’s ‘we have seen this day that God may speak with a human being, and he still lives’ matches Exodus’s own report of the people’s reaction almost exactly), but the voice has changed: Exodus reports what the people did; this chapter reports what the people SAID, in their own words, as Moses remembers them.

★ V24’s divine verdict on that request is easy to read past: ‘they have done well in all that they have spoken.’ Asking not to hear God’s voice directly — asking, in effect, for a human mediator — is not treated as a failure of faith in this account. It is called RIGHT, and it is the request that installs Moses as the go-between named already at v5.

Verses 26–27 · the people sent home, Moses kept standing

The scene closes with a clean division of labor: everyone else is sent ‘to your tents’ (v26), while Moses alone is told to ‘stand here with me’ (v27) to receive ‘all the commandment, and the statutes, and the ordinances’ — the same three-part legal vocabulary this sermon opened with at v1, now assigned specifically to Moses to teach onward. The mediator role sketched at v5 and confirmed at v24 is formalized here as an ongoing arrangement, not a one-time event at the mountain.

Verses 28–29 · “turn not to the right hand or the left” — the sermon’s closing command

The chapter closes on a formula — ‘you shall not turn aside, to the right hand or to the left’ — that reduces the whole preceding recitation to a single spatial image: obedience as a straight road with a known direction, departure from it named as a turn either way, with no privileged side. The chapter’s own opening promise returns unchanged at its close: keep this ‘so that you may live’ (v29, echoing v1’s own ‘so that you may live, and go in and take possession of the land’ at Deuteronomy 4:1, already on these pages).

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew has 29 verses in this chapter; English and Spanish Bibles both number it through 33, because of two splits the Masoretic text does not make — v6 (‘I am Jehovah your God…no other gods’) into two verses, and v16 (murder/adultery/theft/false witness) into four — checked directly against the fetched shelf text on both shelves, which agree with each other on every verse number from v6 onward. The rendering choices worth flagging are collected in the notes above: ‘Keep’ for shamor at v11 against ‘Remember’ for zakhor at Exodus 20:7; ‘witness of emptiness’ at v16, keeping the same English word ‘emptiness’ used at v10, to preserve an echo the Hebrew itself repeats; and ‘covet’ versus ‘desire’ at v17 for chamad and tit’avveh, the two different verbs of the tenth Word. Established renderings hold throughout: Jehovah for the divine name, ‘carved image’/‘form’ for pesel/temunah, ‘statutes and ordinances’ for chuqqim u-mishpatim.

Library. Two new dictionary entries in both languages: karat, ‘to cut’ — the covenant-making idiom of v2, tied to the literal cutting ceremony of Genesis 15:17–18 — and shav, ‘emptiness,’ the word this chapter uses twice (vv10, 16) where Exodus 20 uses two different nouns. Four existing dictionary entries extended, each in both languages: yasaph (its own promise from Deuteronomy 4:2 paid off at v18 and v21), qanna (its third appearance, at v8), and chamad/taah (both entries already discussed this verse in full when Exodus 20 and Numbers 34 shipped; both are now linked forward as ‘already on these pages’ rather than a bare citation). No new encyclopedia entries — every place named in this chapter (Horeb, Egypt) already has one.

Echoes paid. Deuteronomy 4:1’s own note, predicting the shema root would surface as ‘Hear, O Israel’ by Deuteronomy 6:4 — paid off one chapter early, at v1, the phrase’s first verified appearance in the Bible (n1). The zakhor dictionary entry’s own promise about Deuteronomy 5:12, paid off at v11–14 (n11). Deuteronomy 4:2’s yasaph — ‘you shall not add’ — recurring at v18, where God himself adds nothing, and again at v21, where the people beg him to stop (n18). Genesis 15:17–18’s pieces ceremony, standing behind the ‘cut’ of v2 (n2). Exodus 20:18–21’s third-person account of the people’s fear, retold here in Moses’ own first-person voice (n19). And the chamad/taah dictionary entries’ own forward promises about this chapter’s tenth Word, paid off in full at v17 (n17).

Patterns worth carrying forward. This chapter is, verse for verse, the closest textual match to an already-shipped chapter this project has translated — large stretches of vv6–18 restate Exodus 20 almost word for word, which makes the genuine differences (the sabbath motive clause, the honor-parents addition, the ninth Word’s vocabulary, the tenth Word’s reordering) stand out with unusual clarity precisely because the surrounding text is otherwise identical. Worth testing again if Deuteronomy repeats other Exodus material later in the book: does a near-verbatim repetition reliably mark its OWN differences this legibly, or did this pair simply happen to fall in a heavily-studied, well-charted stretch of text?
