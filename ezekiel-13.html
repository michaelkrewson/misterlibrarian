Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–3 · Ezekiel 13:1–3 against the prophets of Israel — “out of their own heart”

⭐ Prophets against prophets. The prophet is sent to speak against those who prophesy ‘out of their own heart’ and walk after their own spirit. Jeremiah had said of such prophets that they speak a vision of their own heart (Jeremiah 23:16). Deuteronomy gives the case of a prophet who speaks what was not commanded (Deuteronomy 18:20–22).

⚠ Foolish, and seeing nothing. ‘Foolish’ is naval, the adjective of the ‘foolish nation’ of Deuteronomy 32:21. The end of verse 3 is brief and hard, ‘and not they have seen’; the KJV has “and have seen nothing”, and the Douay “and see nothing”.

Verses 4–7 · Ezekiel 13:4–7 foxes among ruins — the breaches — empty vision

⭐ Foxes. The word (shu’alim) is that of the foxes on desolate Zion in Lamentations 5:18. The NIV reads “jackals among ruins”.

⭐ Breach and fence. The prophets have not gone up into the breaches or built a fence (gader). Isaiah 58:12 uses the two roots in ‘repairer of the breach’. Ezekiel 22:30 tells of a search for someone to build the fence and stand in the breach. The day of Jehovah is named at Joel 2:1.

⚠ Emptiness. Shav (shav) is the emptiness of the ‘empty vision’ of 12:24, and the chapter keeps it apart from kazav, ‘lie’. The noun miqsam, ‘divination’, is the one at 12:24 and in verse 7 here. Qesem (qesem) is its kin.

Verses 8–9 · Ezekiel 13:8–9 “I am against you” — council, register, ground

⭐ Against you. The words hineni aleikhem stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, ‘I am against you’, and 36:9, where the same two words are ‘I am for you’, spoken to the mountains of Israel. The preposition takes its sense from what follows.

⭐ Three losses. They will not be in the council (sod) of the people, not written in the register of the house of Israel, and will not come to the ground of Israel. The NIV has “listed in the records of Israel” for the second.

Verses 10–12 · Ezekiel 13:10–12 “Peace” and no peace — a wall daubed — rain and hail

⭐ Peace, and no peace. Verse 16 says it again, ‘a vision of peace … and there is no peace’, and Jeremiah 6:14 and 8:11 have the words too. Compare Jeremiah 23:17, and Paul’s ‘Peace and security’ at 1 Thessalonians 5:3. The doubled ‘because, even because’ (ya’an uv’ya’an) stands in two verses: here and Leviticus 26:43, where the page prints a single ‘because’. 36:3 has a near twin, ya’an be’ya’an, printed ‘Because, even because’. Jeremiah 23:32 also speaks of prophets leading the people astray, with a verb spelled differently.

⚠ Three words for the wall. Gader is the ‘fence’ of verse 5; chayits, in verse 10, is a ‘partition’; and qir, from verse 12, is the ‘wall’. In verse 10 the Hebrew has ‘and he builds’, naming no builder; this translation has ‘one builds’.

⚠ Whitewash. What they daub on is tafel (tafel), also at Ezekiel 22:28. The same letters are in Lamentations 2:14, where the page prints it ‘emptiness’, and the shav beside it ‘falsehood’; this chapter’s ‘emptiness’ is shav. The KJV has “untempered morter”.

⚠ Who falls. Verse 11 turns to the hailstones, ‘and you’, as pointed. The Douay has “I will cause great hailstones to fall violently from above”. Elgavish, the word that makes them hailstones, stands in three verses: 13:11, 13:13 and Ezekiel 38:22.

Verses 13–16 · Ezekiel 13:13–16 the wall torn down — “the wall is no more”

⭐ Storm, rain, hail. What verse 11 announced, verse 13 gives as Jehovah’s own act, in fury and anger. A house that falls to rain, rivers and winds is in Matthew 7:27.

⚠ She will fall. The wall (qir) is masculine, but verse 14 turns feminine: ‘she will fall … in her midst’. The verse names no city. The NIV has “When it falls, you will be destroyed in it”.

⭐ Spending fury. ‘I will spend my fury’ is the verb of 6:12. A whitewashed wall is also Paul’s word to the high priest (Acts 23:3), and Jesus speaks of whitewashed tombs (Matthew 23:27). Neither passage says it has this chapter in mind.

Verses 17–19 · Ezekiel 13:17–19 the daughters who prophesy — bands and veils — barley and bread

⭐ The daughters. These too prophesy out of their own heart. The form hammitnabbot, ‘who prophesy’, stands in this one verse. The pages have named prophetesses before: Miriam (Exodus 15:20) and Deborah (Judges 4:4).

⚠ Bands and veils. Kesatot, ‘bands’, are in two verses, 18 and 20; mispachot, ‘veils’, come in 18 and 21. What they were, the chapter does not say. The NIV calls the bands “magic charms”, the KJV “pillows”.

⚠ My hands. The pointed Hebrew has yadai, ‘my hands’, which this translation keeps. The ASV has “all elbows”. ‘Souls’ is nefashot (nefesh).

⭐ Handfuls of barley. The pay is small: handfuls of barley and morsels of bread. The ‘handfuls’ word is that of 1 Kings 20:10.

Verses 20–23 · Ezekiel 13:20–23 the bands torn away — the righteous grieved, the wicked strengthened

⚠ Like flying things. Lefarchot is obscure: ‘as flying things’, or ‘to make them fly’, as the ASV has it. Verse 20 also has a rare plural, nefashim, and masculine forms among the feminine ones.

⭐ Out of your hand. Twice, at 21 and 23, the people are delivered from the women’s hand. ‘Something hunted’ in verse 21 is metsudah, the noun 12:13 prints as ‘snare’.

⭐ Hands strengthened. Verse 22 is close to Jeremiah 23:14, where the prophets strengthen the hands of evildoers so that no one turns back from his evil. Here the wicked is not turned ‘to keep him alive’, the aim given to the prophet at 3:18, where he is charged to warn the wicked.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “emptiness” at verses 6, 8, 9 and 23, with “empty” at 7, for shav; “whitewash” at verses 10, 11, 14 and 15; “fence” at 5, “partition” at 10 and “wall” from 12; “bands” at 18 and 20; “veils” at 18 and 21; and “falsehood” at 22, apart from “lie”.

Echoes paid. The empty vision of 12:24; the vision out of the prophets’ own heart and the strengthened hands of Jeremiah 23; the foxes of Lamentations 5:18; and the charge to warn the wicked at 3:18.

Echoes planted. The whitewash again at Ezekiel 22:28, and the fence and breach at Ezekiel 22:30. The hailstones again at Ezekiel 38:22.

Ezekiel 14, now on these pages: elders of Israel come to sit before the prophet, and the word speaks of idols set up in the heart.
