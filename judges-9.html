Abimelech, a son of Gideon by a concubine at Shechem, has his seventy half-brothers killed on one stone at Ophrah and is made king by the men of Shechem. Jotham, the one who hid, speaks the parable of the trees from Mount Gerizim and flees to Beer. After three years God sends an evil spirit between the king and Shechem; Gaal son of Ebed leads a revolt, Zebul drives him out, and Abimelech razes the city and sows it with salt. At Thebez a woman drops a millstone on Abimelech's head, and he asks his armour-bearer to kill him.
Within the period of the judges, with no date the chapter gives for itself. Abimelech's rule is given as three years (9:22), and the chapter comes after the forty years of rest (8:28). The early/late question applies (c. 1200 BC on the early reckoning this project foregrounds, c. 1100 BC on the late).

1
וַיֵּלֶךְ אֲבִימֶלֶךְ בֶּן-יְרֻבַּעַל שְׁכֶמָה, אֶל-אֲחֵי אִמּוֹ; וַיְדַבֵּר אֲלֵיהֶם, וְאֶל-כָּל-מִשְׁפַּחַת בֵּית-אֲבִי אִמּוֹ לֵאמֹר.
Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, to his mother’s brothers, and spoke to them and to all the family of his mother’s father’s house, saying:note
Abimelec hijo de Jerubaal fue a Siquem, a los hermanos de su madre, y habló con ellos y con toda la familia de la casa del padre de su madre, diciendo:
2
דַּבְּרוּ-נָא בְּאָזְנֵי כָל-בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, מַה-טּוֹב לָכֶם--הַמְשֹׁל בָּכֶם שִׁבְעִים אִישׁ כֹּל בְּנֵי יְרֻבַּעַל, אִם-מְשֹׁל בָּכֶם אִישׁ אֶחָד; וּזְכַרְתֶּם, כִּי-עַצְמְכֶם וּבְשַׂרְכֶם אָנִי.
Speak, please, in the ears of all the men of Shechem: which is better for you, that all seventy sons of Jerubbaal rule over you, or that one man rules over you? Remember also that I am your bone and your flesh.note
Hablen, por favor, a oídos de todos los señores de Siquem: ¿Qué les conviene más, que gobiernen sobre ustedes los setenta hijos de Jerubaal, o que gobierne sobre ustedes un solo hombre? Recuerden también que yo soy hueso de ustedes y carne de ustedes.
3
וַיְדַבְּרוּ אֲחֵי-אִמּוֹ עָלָיו, בְּאָזְנֵי כָּל-בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, אֵת כָּל-הַדְּבָרִים, הָאֵלֶּה; וַיֵּט לִבָּם אַחֲרֵי אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, כִּי אָמְרוּ אָחִינוּ הוּא.
His mother’s brothers spoke all these words about him in the ears of all the men of Shechem, and their hearts inclined after Abimelech, for they said: He is our brother.note
Los hermanos de su madre hablaron de él a oídos de todos los señores de Siquem todas estas palabras, y su corazón se inclinó tras Abimelec, porque dijeron: Es nuestro hermano.
4
וַיִּתְּנוּ-לוֹ שִׁבְעִים כֶּסֶף, מִבֵּית בַּעַל בְּרִית; וַיִּשְׂכֹּר בָּהֶם אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, אֲנָשִׁים רֵיקִים וּפֹחֲזִים, וַיֵּלְכוּ, אַחֲרָיו.
They gave him seventy pieces of silver out of the house of Baal-berith, with which Abimelech hired empty and reckless men, who went after him.note
Le dieron setenta piezas de plata de la casa de Baal-berit, con las que Abimelec contrató a hombres vanos e insensatos, que fueron tras él.
5
וַיָּבֹא בֵית-אָבִיו, עָפְרָתָה, וַיַּהֲרֹג אֶת-אֶחָיו בְּנֵי-יְרֻבַּעַל שִׁבְעִים אִישׁ, עַל-אֶבֶן אֶחָת; וַיִּוָּתֵר יוֹתָם בֶּן-יְרֻבַּעַל, הַקָּטֹן--כִּי נֶחְבָּא. {ס}
He went to his father’s house at Ophrah and killed his brothers, the seventy sons of Jerubbaal, on one stone; but Jotham the youngest son of Jerubbaal was left, for he hid himself.note
Fue a la casa de su padre en Ofra, y mató a sus hermanos, los setenta hijos de Jerubaal, sobre una piedra; pero quedó Jotam, el hijo menor de Jerubaal, porque se escondió.
6
וַיֵּאָסְפוּ כָּל-בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, וְכָל-בֵּית מִלּוֹא, וַיֵּלְכוּ, וַיַּמְלִיכוּ אֶת-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ לְמֶלֶךְ--עִם-אֵלוֹן מֻצָּב, אֲשֶׁר בִּשְׁכֶם.
All the men of Shechem and all Beth-millo gathered and went and made Abimelech king, by the terebinth of the pillar that is in Shechem.note
Se reunieron todos los señores de Siquem y toda la casa de Milo, y fueron e hicieron rey a Abimelec junto a la encina de la columna que está en Siquem.
7
וַיַּגִּדוּ לְיוֹתָם, וַיֵּלֶךְ וַיַּעֲמֹד בְּרֹאשׁ הַר-גְּרִזִים, וַיִּשָּׂא קוֹלוֹ, וַיִּקְרָא; וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם, שִׁמְעוּ אֵלַי בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, וְיִשְׁמַע אֲלֵיכֶם, אֱלֹהִים.
When they told Jotham, he went and stood on the top of Mount Gerizim, lifted up his voice and cried out: Listen to me, men of Shechem, so that God may listen to you.note
Cuando se lo contaron a Jotam, fue y se puso de pie en la cumbre del monte Garizim, alzó su voz y clamó: Escúchenme, señores de Siquem, para que Dios los escuche a ustedes.
8
הָלוֹךְ הָלְכוּ הָעֵצִים, לִמְשֹׁחַ עֲלֵיהֶם מֶלֶךְ; וַיֹּאמְרוּ לַזַּיִת, מלוכה מָלְכָה עָלֵינוּ.
The trees went out to anoint a king over themselves, and they said to the olive tree: Reign over us.note
Los árboles fueron a ungir un rey sobre sí; y dijeron al olivo: Reina sobre nosotros.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם, הַזַּיִת, הֶחֳדַלְתִּי אֶת-דִּשְׁנִי, אֲשֶׁר-בִּי יְכַבְּדוּ אֱלֹהִים וַאֲנָשִׁים; וְהָלַכְתִּי, לָנוּעַ עַל-הָעֵצִים.
But the olive tree said to them: Shall I give up my richness, by which God and men are honored, and go to sway over the trees?note
Pero el olivo les dijo: ¿Voy a dejar mi aceite, con el cual se honra a Dios y a los hombres, para ir a mecerme sobre los árboles?
10
וַיֹּאמְרוּ הָעֵצִים, לַתְּאֵנָה: לְכִי-אַתְּ, מָלְכִי עָלֵינוּ.
And the trees said to the fig tree: Come you, and reign over us.note
Y los árboles dijeron a la higuera: Ven tú, reina sobre nosotros.
11
וַתֹּאמֶר לָהֶם, הַתְּאֵנָה, הֶחֳדַלְתִּי אֶת-מָתְקִי, וְאֶת-תְּנוּבָתִי הַטּוֹבָה; וְהָלַכְתִּי, לָנוּעַ עַל-הָעֵצִים.
But the fig tree said to them: Shall I give up my sweetness and my good fruit, and go to sway over the trees?note
Pero la higuera les dijo: ¿Voy a dejar mi dulzura y mi buen fruto, para ir a mecerme sobre los árboles?
12
וַיֹּאמְרוּ הָעֵצִים, לַגָּפֶן: לְכִי-אַתְּ, מלוכי מָלְכִי עָלֵינוּ.
And the trees said to the vine: Come you, and reign over us.note
Y los árboles dijeron a la vid: Ven tú, reina sobre nosotros.
13
וַתֹּאמֶר לָהֶם, הַגֶּפֶן, הֶחֳדַלְתִּי אֶת-תִּירוֹשִׁי, הַמְשַׂמֵּחַ אֱלֹהִים וַאֲנָשִׁים; וְהָלַכְתִּי, לָנוּעַ עַל-הָעֵצִים.
But the vine said to them: Shall I give up my wine, which cheers God and men, and go to sway over the trees?note
Pero la vid les dijo: ¿Voy a dejar mi vino, que alegra a Dios y a los hombres, para ir a mecerme sobre los árboles?
14
וַיֹּאמְרוּ כָל-הָעֵצִים, אֶל-הָאָטָד: לֵךְ אַתָּה, מְלָךְ-עָלֵינוּ.
Then all the trees said to the bramble: Come you, and reign over us.note
Entonces todos los árboles dijeron a la zarza: Ven tú, reina sobre nosotros.
15
וַיֹּאמֶר הָאָטָד, אֶל-הָעֵצִים, אִם בֶּאֱמֶת אַתֶּם מֹשְׁחִים אֹתִי לְמֶלֶךְ עֲלֵיכֶם, בֹּאוּ חֲסוּ בְצִלִּי; וְאִם-אַיִן--תֵּצֵא אֵשׁ מִן-הָאָטָד, וְתֹאכַל אֶת-אַרְזֵי הַלְּבָנוֹן.
And the bramble said to the trees: If in truth you anoint me king over you, then come and take refuge in my shadow; but if not, let fire come out of the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon.note
La zarza dijo a los árboles: Si de verdad me ungen a mí por rey sobre ustedes, vengan, refúgiense a la sombra mía; y si no, salga fuego de la zarza y consuma los cedros del Líbano.
16
וְעַתָּה, אִם-בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים עֲשִׂיתֶם, וַתַּמְלִיכוּ, אֶת-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ; וְאִם-טוֹבָה עֲשִׂיתֶם עִם-יְרֻבַּעַל וְעִם-בֵּיתוֹ, וְאִם-כִּגְמוּל יָדָיו עֲשִׂיתֶם לוֹ.
Now then, if you have acted in truth and in integrity in making Abimelech king, and if you have dealt well with Jerubbaal and his house, and have done to him according to what his hands deserved—note
Ahora bien, si con verdad y con integridad han obrado al hacer rey a Abimelec, y si han obrado bien con Jerubaal y con su casa, y le han pagado conforme a lo que merecen sus manos—
17
אֲשֶׁר-נִלְחַם אָבִי, עֲלֵיכֶם; וַיַּשְׁלֵךְ אֶת-נַפְשׁוֹ מִנֶּגֶד, וַיַּצֵּל אֶתְכֶם מִיַּד מִדְיָן.
for my father fought for you, and risked his life, and delivered you out of the hand of Midian;note
porque mi padre peleó por ustedes, y arriesgó su vida, y los libró de la mano de Madián;
18
וְאַתֶּם קַמְתֶּם עַל-בֵּית אָבִי, הַיּוֹם, וַתַּהַרְגוּ אֶת-בָּנָיו שִׁבְעִים אִישׁ, עַל-אֶבֶן אֶחָת; וַתַּמְלִיכוּ אֶת-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ בֶּן-אֲמָתוֹ, עַל-בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, כִּי אֲחִיכֶם, הוּא.
But you have risen up against my father’s house this day, and killed his sons, seventy men, on one stone, and made Abimelech, the son of his maidservant, king over the men of Shechem, because he is your brother.note
Pero ustedes se han levantado hoy contra la casa de mi padre, y han matado a sus hijos, setenta hombres, sobre una piedra; y han hecho rey sobre los señores de Siquem a Abimelec, hijo de su sierva, porque es hermano de ustedes.
19
וְאִם-בֶּאֱמֶת וּבְתָמִים עֲשִׂיתֶם עִם-יְרֻבַּעַל וְעִם-בֵּיתוֹ, הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה--שִׂמְחוּ, בַּאֲבִימֶלֶךְ, וְיִשְׂמַח גַּם-הוּא, בָּכֶם.
If you have acted in truth and integrity with Jerubbaal and his house this day, then rejoice in Abimelech, and let him also rejoice in you.note
Si con verdad y con integridad han obrado hoy con Jerubaal y con su casa, alégrense con Abimelec, y que él también se alegre con ustedes.
20
וְאִם-אַיִן--תֵּצֵא אֵשׁ מֵאֲבִימֶלֶךְ, וְתֹאכַל אֶת-בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם וְאֶת-בֵּית מִלּוֹא; וְתֵצֵא אֵשׁ מִבַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, וּמִבֵּית מִלּוֹא, וְתֹאכַל, אֶת-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ.
But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and devour the men of Shechem and Beth-millo, and let fire come out from the men of Shechem and Beth-millo and devour Abimelech.note
Pero si no, salga fuego de Abimelec y consuma a los señores de Siquem y a la casa de Milo; y salga fuego de los señores de Siquem y de la casa de Milo, y consuma a Abimelec.
21
וַיָּנָס יוֹתָם, וַיִּבְרַח וַיֵּלֶךְ בְּאֵרָה; וַיֵּשֶׁב שָׁם, מִפְּנֵי אֲבִימֶלֶךְ אָחִיו. {פ}
Jotham ran away and fled, and went to Beer and lived there, for fear of his brother Abimelech.note
Jotam huyó, se escapó y se fue a Beer, y vivió allí, por miedo a su hermano Abimelec.
22
וַיָּשַׂר אֲבִימֶלֶךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, שָׁלֹשׁ שָׁנִים.
Abimelech was prince over Israel three years.note
Abimelec gobernó sobre Israel tres años.
23
וַיִּשְׁלַח אֱלֹהִים, רוּחַ רָעָה, בֵּין אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, וּבֵין בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם; וַיִּבְגְּדוּ בַעֲלֵי-שְׁכֶם, בַּאֲבִימֶלֶךְ.
God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and the men of Shechem dealt treacherously with Abimelech.note
Dios envió un espíritu malo entre Abimelec y los señores de Siquem; y los señores de Siquem obraron con traición contra Abimelec.
24
לָבוֹא, חֲמַס שִׁבְעִים בְּנֵי-יְרֻבָּעַל; וְדָמָם, לָשׂוּם עַל-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ אֲחִיהֶם אֲשֶׁר הָרַג אוֹתָם, וְעַל בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם, אֲשֶׁר-חִזְּקוּ אֶת-יָדָיו לַהֲרֹג אֶת-אֶחָיו.
so that the violence done to the seventy sons of Jerubbaal might come, and their blood be laid on Abimelech their brother, who killed them, and on the men of Shechem, who strengthened his hands to kill his brothers.note
para que viniera la violencia contra los setenta hijos de Jerubaal, y su sangre fuera puesta sobre Abimelec su hermano, que los mató, y sobre los señores de Siquem, que le fortalecieron las manos para matar a sus hermanos.
25
וַיָּשִׂימוּ לוֹ בַעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם מְאָרְבִים, עַל רָאשֵׁי הֶהָרִים, וַיִּגְזְלוּ, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר-יַעֲבֹר עֲלֵיהֶם בַּדָּרֶךְ; וַיֻּגַּד, לַאֲבִימֶלֶךְ. {פ}
The men of Shechem set ambushes against him on the tops of the mountains, and they robbed everyone who passed by on the road; and it was told Abimelech.note
Los señores de Siquem pusieron emboscadas contra él en las cumbres de los montes, y robaban a todo el que pasaba por el camino; y se lo dijeron a Abimelec.
26
וַיָּבֹא גַּעַל בֶּן-עֶבֶד, וְאֶחָיו, וַיַּעַבְרוּ, בִּשְׁכֶם; וַיִּבְטְחוּ-בוֹ, בַּעֲלֵי שְׁכֶם.
Gaal son of Ebed came with his brothers and crossed over into Shechem, and the men of Shechem put their trust in him.note
Llegó Gaal hijo de Ebed con sus hermanos, y pasaron a Siquem; y los señores de Siquem pusieron su confianza en él.
27
וַיֵּצְאוּ הַשָּׂדֶה וַיִּבְצְרוּ אֶת-כַּרְמֵיהֶם, וַיִּדְרְכוּ, וַיַּעֲשׂוּ, הִלּוּלִים; וַיָּבֹאוּ, בֵּית אֱלֹהֵיהֶם, וַיֹּאכְלוּ וַיִּשְׁתּוּ, וַיְקַלְלוּ אֶת-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ.
They went out into the field, gathered their vineyards, trod the grapes, and held a festival; and they went into the house of their god, ate and drank, and cursed Abimelech.note
Salieron al campo, vendimiaron sus viñas, pisaron las uvas e hicieron fiesta; y entraron en la casa de su dios, comieron y bebieron, y maldijeron a Abimelec.
28
וַיֹּאמֶר גַּעַל בֶּן-עֶבֶד, מִי-אֲבִימֶלֶךְ וּמִי-שְׁכֶם כִּי נַעַבְדֶנּוּ--הֲלֹא בֶן-יְרֻבַּעַל, וּזְבֻל פְּקִידוֹ; עִבְדוּ, אֶת-אַנְשֵׁי חֲמוֹר אֲבִי שְׁכֶם, וּמַדּוּעַ, נַעַבְדֶנּוּ אֲנָחְנוּ.
Gaal son of Ebed said: Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him? Is he not the son of Jerubbaal, and Zebul his officer? Serve the men of Hamor, the father of Shechem; but why should we serve him?note

Verses 1–3 · Abimelech goes to his mother’s family, Judges 9:1–3: a bid for a throne made by a son of a concubine, in the words of blood kinship

★★★ Abimelech goes to Shechem to his mother’s brothers, not to his father’s sons. The chapter opens by naming his mother’s family three times over. Abimelech is the son of a pilegesh, a concubine, at Shechem (Judges 8:31, now on these pages). His appeal is made through the kin of his mother, not through the house of Gideon.

★★ His argument is one of blood: ‘I am your bone and your flesh’. The Hebrew atsmekhem u-vesarkhem is the phrase of kinship. Abimelech asks whether seventy sons of Jerubbaal should rule or one man, and the appeal turns on who the men of Shechem are to him.

★ The men of Shechem’s hearts ‘inclined after Abimelech’, because they said, ‘He is our brother’. The verb nata, ‘to incline’, is the verb of Joshua 24:23 (already on these pages) for turning the heart to God. The chapter uses the same verb for turning the heart to a man.

Verses 4–6 · The seventy pieces of silver and the killing on one stone, Judges 9:4–6: money from the house of Baal-berith, and a king made by a terebinth

★★★ The money comes from the house of Baal-berith, the god of Judges 8:33. Seventy pieces of silver are given to Abimelech from that house, and he hires ‘empty and reckless’ men. The god of the covenant has become a source of wages for murder.

★★ Abimelech kills his seventy brothers on one stone, and only Jotham escapes. The number seventy is the number of Gideon’s sons (Judges 8:30, now on these pages). The verb nechba, ‘hid himself’, is the only reason Jotham lives.

★ The men of Shechem make Abimelech king by the terebinth of the pillar in Shechem. The elon matzav, ‘the terebinth of the standing stone’, is a sacred tree at Shechem, and the coronation is made beside a place of the nation’s witness; compare Joshua’s stone under the oak (Joshua 24:26, now on these pages).

Verses 6–7 · Jotham on Mount Gerizim, Judges 9:6–7: the one who escaped speaks from the mountain above the town

★★ Jotham is told, and he stands on the top of Mount Gerizim to speak. Gerizim is the mountain of blessing across from Ebal (Joshua 8:33, now on these pages), and the speech is delivered from it. He cries out so that the men below can hear him, and he asks that God may hear them too.

Verses 8–14 · The parable of the trees, Judges 9:8–14: the olive, the fig, the vine, and the bramble, and the refusal of each

★★★ The trees go out to choose a king, and three good trees refuse. The olive is refused because it gives oil that honours God and man; the fig because of its sweetness and good fruit; the vine because its wine cheers God and man. Each refusal names the thing the tree is for, and the king’s office is the only thing none of them wants.

★★ The bramble, a thorn, is the only tree that accepts, and it is a dry bush. The Hebrew atad is a low thorn-shrub, and the parable makes the least useful plant the king. The verb lanu‘a, ‘to sway’, is used of the trees’ kingship, and the chapter gives it to the bramble.

⚠ The parable is a fable, and the book says so by its own form: the trees speak. The translation keeps the fable without a moral attached in the text itself; the moral is Jotham’s, in v15 onward.

Verse 15 · The bramble’s offer, Judges 9:15: take refuge in my shadow, or fire from the thorn devours the cedars of Lebanon

★★★ The bramble offers shelter, and threatens fire. ‘Come and take refuge in my shadow’ is the offer, and the threat is that fire will come out of the bramble and consume the cedars of Lebanon. The cedars are the nation’s symbol of greatness, and the fire is the price of refusing the thorn. The KJV reads ‘put your trust in my shadow’, where the offer is trust rather than refuge, and the ASV ‘take refuge in my shade’, as the Hebrew has it; the TNM 1987 names the bush a cambrón and the TNM 2019 a zarza, and the translation keeps the Hebrew atad in the note.

Verses 16–21 · Jotham’s conditional curse, Judges 9:16–21: if you have dealt in truth and integrity, rejoice; if not, fire from Abimelech and fire from Shechem, and Jotham flees to Beer

★★★ The curse is conditional, and it turns on the word emet, truth. If the men of Shechem acted ‘in truth and in integrity’ in making Abimelech king, they should rejoice; if not, fire will come out from Abimelech and consume them, and from them to consume Abimelech. The pair emet and tamim is the book’s own test of a just act, and the speech uses it twice.

★★ Jotham says his father fought for them and risked his life. The verb shalakh (‘to throw’) is used of the father throwing his life before them, and the chapter reminds the men of Shechem of the debt they owe the house of Gideon (Judges 8:35, now on these pages).

★ Jotham runs to Beer and lives there, away from his brother. The verb nus, ‘to flee’, ends the family’s first scene. Beer is a place to which the youngest son escapes, and the name is not explained further.

Verses 22–25 · Three years, an evil spirit from God, and the ambushes on the mountains, Judges 9:22–25: God sends the spirit that divides the king from the city

★★★ God sends an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem. The phrase ruach ra`ah is the same as in the Saul story (1 Samuel 16:14, now on these pages). The chapter attributes the betrayal to God as its cause, and the men of Shechem betray the king they made.

★★ The betrayal is punishment for the blood of the seventy sons. V24 says that the violence to Jerubbaal’s sons would come on Abimelech and on the men of Shechem who strengthened his hands. The guilt is shared, and the chapter names both.

★ The men of Shechem set ambushes on the mountains and rob travellers. The verb gazal, ‘to rob’, is the book’s word for the men of Shechem’s own crime against the road.

Verses 26–29 · Gaal son of Ebed, Judges 9:26–29: the feast in the house of the god, and a question that is also a challenge

★★ Gaal son of Ebed comes to Shechem with his brothers, and the men of Shechem trust him. The verb batakh, ‘to trust’, is the men’s own: they put their trust in a stranger against their king.

★★★ At the feast in the house of their god, they curse Abimelech. The men of Shechem go to the vineyards, tread the grapes, hold a festival, go into the house of their god, eat, drink, and curse Abimelech. The sanctuary becomes the place of the curse, and the chapter gives that act a single verb.

★★ Gaal asks, ‘Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him?’ The question is an insult: he is the son of Jerubbaal and of Zebul his officer, and Shechem should serve the men of Hamor, the old lords of the city. The reference to Hamor echoes Genesis 34 (already on these pages), where Hamor’s sons are the men of Shechem.

Verses 30–33 · Zebul’s message, Judges 9:30–33: the governor sends a secret warning, and the plan is a night attack at sunrise

★★ Zebul, the ruler of the city, hears Gaal and his anger burns. The verb charah, ‘burn’, is the word for anger that kindles. Zebul is the officer who holds Shechem for Abimelech, and his anger is the king’s protection.

★★ Zebul sends messengers secretly to Abimelech at Tormah. The message is to rise by night, lie in wait in the field, and attack the city at sunrise. The plan is a trap for the men of Shechem as well as for Gaal.

Verses 34–41 · The ambush and the battle at the gate, Judges 9:34–41: the shadows of the mountains as if they were men, and Zebul’s taunt turned on Gaal

★★★ Gaal sees people coming down from the mountains, and Zebul says he is seeing the shadows of the mountains as if they were men. The line is the chapter’s own dark joke: the men Gaal fears are the mountains’ shadows, and the ambush is real.

★★ Zebul uses Gaal’s own mouth against him: ‘Where is your mouth now?’ The question repeats Gaal’s insult of v28, and Zebul sends him out to fight the same people he despised. Gaal is defeated, and Zebul drives him and his brothers out of Shechem.

Verses 42–45 · The city and the field, Judges 9:42–45: three companies, the gate, and the city sown with salt

★★★ Abimelech divides the people into three companies, and strikes the men who come out of the city. The next day the people go out to the field, and Abimelech lies in wait, then rises as they come out. Two companies attack the field and one holds the gate.

★★★ Abimelech takes the city and destroys it, and sows it with salt. The verb zara, ‘to sow’, is used with salt, which makes the ground barren. The sentence is the chapter’s deliberate act of destruction, and the translation keeps the sowing with salt.

Verses 46–49 · The tower of Shechem and the fire, Judges 9:46–49: the house of El-berith, and every man cuts a bough

★★★ The lords of the tower of Shechem flee into the stronghold of the house of El-berith. El-berith is the Baal of the covenant of Judges 8:33 by another name, and the stronghold is the sanctuary of that god. The tower is the place where the lords of Shechem hide, and the name of the god is the one in whose house the king was paid.

★★★ Abimelech climbs Mount Zalmon, cuts a bough, and the people do the same. The verb karat, ‘to cut’, is the axe’s word, and the boughs are carried to the tower and set on fire. About a thousand men and women die.

Verses 50–57 · Thebez and the millstone, Judges 9:50–57: a woman’s stone, and a king who asks his armour-bearer to kill him so that no one will say a woman killed him

★★★ At Thebez, the people flee into a strong tower and climb to its roof, and a woman drops a millstone on Abimelech’s head. The upper millstone is the grinding stone of the household, and the woman who throws it is the one who ends the king. The ASV reads ‘cast an upper millstone’, the KJV ‘a piece of a millstone’; the NWT 1984 says ‘pitched an upper millstone’ and the NWT 2013 ‘dropped’, and the TNM 1987 keeps ‘piedra superior de molino’ where the TNM 2019 gives ‘piedra de molino’. The story is the chapter’s most famous small detail.

★★★ Abimelech asks his armour-bearer to kill him, so that he will not be said to have died by a woman’s hand. The king’s last concern is his reputation, and the young man kills him. The verb daqar, ‘to pierce’, is the armour-bearer’s act.

★★ V56–57 close the story with two repayments: God repays Abimelech for the seventy brothers, and the men of Shechem for their wickedness. The curse of Jotham, the words of v20, comes true on the men of Shechem. The chapter ends with the curse fulfilled and the speaker remembered by name.

Echoes paid. The Baal-berith of Judges 8:33 supplies the seventy pieces of silver (v4) and the house of El-berith where the tower’s men hide (v46). The seventy sons of Gideon (Judges 8:30) are the seventy killed on one stone (v5). Gideon’s refusal of kingship in Judges 8:23 is the background to Abimelech’s bid.

Echoes planted. The question of who should rule over Israel is taken up by Samuel and Saul (1 Samuel 8, now on these pages). The sowing of Shechem with salt (v45) is the chapter’s own act of destruction, and Jotham’s parable is a fable in the book’s own form.

Decisions. The Hebrew emet u-tamim is kept as ‘truth and integrity’, the same pair as the book uses for a just act. The fable is given without a moral in the text. Atad is kept as ‘bramble’, the low thorn-shrub, and the chapter’s trees keep their names in English.

Library. New entries are needed in both languages: the encyclopedia for Jotham, Gaal, Zebul, Jerubbaal and Thebez; the dictionary for atad, gefen, teenah and zayit. Every name and term the notes link must exist before the chapter ships.

⚠ A note on order: Judges is complete on these pages, chapters 1–21.
