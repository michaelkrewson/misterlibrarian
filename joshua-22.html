Joshua sends the men of Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh home across the Jordan, blessing them and telling them to share their spoil. On the far bank they build a great altar; Israel hears of it, gathers at Shiloh for war, and sends Phinehas and ten chiefs. The answer is that the altar is a copy and a witness, not a place of sacrifice, and the war is called off. The chapter ends with the altar named Witness.
Like chapters 13-21, a legal and narrative chapter with no date or duration of its own. It follows the rest Jehovah gave Israel (21:44) and comes before Joshua's farewell (23). The book does not say how long the tribes had been across the river, so the same early/late conquest question applies (c. 1406 BC on the early date this project foregrounds, c. 1230-1210 BC on the late).

¿No cometió Acán hijo de Zera una infidelidad en lo consagrado, y la ira cayó sobre toda la asamblea de Israel? Y aquel hombre no perecía solo por su iniquidad.
21
וַיַּעֲנוּ בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּבְנֵי-גָד, וַחֲצִי שֵׁבֶט הַמְנַשֶּׁה; וַיְדַבְּרוּ, אֶת-רָאשֵׁי אַלְפֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.

Entonces los rubenitas, los gaditas y la media tribu de Manasés respondieron y hablaron a los jefes de los millares de Israel:
22

«¡Dios, Dios Jehová, Dios, Dios Jehová! Él sabe, e Israel mismo sabrá. Si fue en rebelión o en infidelidad contra Jehová, ¡no nos salves hoy!
23

Si nos edificamos un altar para apartarnos de seguir a Jehová, o si para ofrecer en él holocausto u ofrenda de cereal, o si para ofrecer sobre él sacrificios de paz, que Jehová mismo nos pida cuentas.
24

Pero lo hicimos por la preocupación de un asunto, diciendo: Mañana sus hijos podrían decir a nuestros hijos: ¿Qué tienen que ver con Jehová, el Dios de Israel?
25
וּגְבוּל נָתַן-יְהוָה בֵּינֵנוּ וּבֵינֵיכֶם בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּבְנֵי-גָד, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן--אֵין-לָכֶם חֵלֶק, בַּיהוָה; וְהִשְׁבִּיתוּ בְנֵיכֶם אֶת-בָּנֵינוּ, לְבִלְתִּי יְרֹא אֶת-יְהוָה.
For Jehovah has made the Jordan a border between us and you, sons of Reuben and sons of Gad; you have no share in Jehovah. So your sons might make our sons cease from fearing Jehovah.note
Porque Jehová ha puesto el Jordán como límite entre nosotros y ustedes, hijos de Rubén y hijos de Gad; ustedes no tienen parte en Jehová. Así sus hijos podrían hacer que nuestros hijos dejen de temer a Jehová.
26
וַנֹּאמֶר--נַעֲשֶׂה-נָּא לָנוּ, לִבְנוֹת אֶת-הַמִּזְבֵּחַ: לֹא לְעוֹלָה, וְלֹא לְזָבַח.
So we said: Let us now prepare to build ourselves an altar, not for burnt offering, nor for sacrifice;note
Por eso dijimos: Hagamos ahora, por favor, un altar, no para holocausto ni para sacrificio;
27
כִּי עֵד הוּא בֵּינֵינוּ וּבֵינֵיכֶם, וּבֵין דֹּרוֹתֵינוּ אַחֲרֵינוּ, לַעֲבֹד אֶת-עֲבֹדַת יְהוָה לְפָנָיו, בְּעֹלוֹתֵינוּ וּבִזְבָחֵינוּ וּבִשְׁלָמֵינוּ: וְלֹא-יֹאמְרוּ בְנֵיכֶם מָחָר לְבָנֵינוּ, אֵין-לָכֶם חֵלֶק בַּיהוָה.
but it shall be a witness between us and you, and between our generations after us, that we may do the service of Jehovah before him with our burnt offerings, our sacrifices and our peace offerings; so that your sons may not say to our sons in time to come, You have no share in Jehovah.note
sino para que sea un testigo entre nosotros y ustedes, y entre nuestras generaciones después de nosotros, de que servimos a Jehová delante de él con nuestros holocaustos, nuestros sacrificios y nuestros sacrificios de paz; para que sus hijos no digan mañana a nuestros hijos: Ustedes no tienen parte en Jehová.
28
וַנֹּאמֶר--וְהָיָה כִּי-יֹאמְרוּ אֵלֵינוּ וְאֶל-דֹּרֹתֵינוּ, מָחָר; וְאָמַרְנוּ רְאוּ אֶת-תַּבְנִית מִזְבַּח יְהוָה אֲשֶׁר-עָשׂוּ אֲבוֹתֵינוּ, לֹא לְעוֹלָה וְלֹא לְזֶבַח--כִּי-עֵד הוּא, בֵּינֵינוּ וּבֵינֵיכֶם.
So we said: If they say this to us or to our generations in time to come, we shall answer: Look at the copy of the altar of Jehovah that our fathers made, not for burnt offering nor for sacrifice; but it is a witness between us and you.note
Y dijimos: Si ellos nos dicen esto a nosotros o a nuestras generaciones mañana, responderemos: Miren la copia del altar de Jehová que hicieron nuestros padres, no para holocausto ni para sacrificio; sino que es un testigo entre nosotros y ustedes.
29
חָלִילָה לָּנוּ מִמֶּנּוּ לִמְרֹד בַּיהוָה, וְלָשׁוּב הַיּוֹם מֵאַחֲרֵי יְהוָה, לִבְנוֹת מִזְבֵּחַ, לְעֹלָה לְמִנְחָה וּלְזָבַח--מִלְּבַד, מִזְבַּח יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ, אֲשֶׁר, לִפְנֵי מִשְׁכָּנוֹ. {פ}
Far be it from us to rebel against Jehovah, and to turn away this day from following Jehovah, by building an altar for burnt offering, grain offering or sacrifice, besides the altar of Jehovah our God that stands before his tabernacle.”note
¡Lejos de nosotros rebelarnos contra Jehová, y apartarnos hoy de seguir a Jehová edificando un altar para holocausto, ofrenda de cereal o sacrificio, aparte del altar de Jehová nuestro Dios que está delante de su tabernáculo!»
30
וַיִּשְׁמַע פִּינְחָס הַכֹּהֵן, וּנְשִׂיאֵי הָעֵדָה וְרָאשֵׁי אַלְפֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים, אֲשֶׁר דִּבְּרוּ בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּבְנֵי-גָד וּבְנֵי מְנַשֶּׁה; וַיִּיטַב, בְּעֵינֵיהֶם.
When Phinehas the priest, and the princes of the assembly, the heads of the thousands of Israel who were with him, heard the words that the Reubenites, the Gadites and the Manassites spoke, it pleased them.note
Cuando Pinjas el sacerdote, los príncipes de la asamblea y los jefes de los millares de Israel que estaban con él oyeron las palabras que hablaron los hijos de Rubén, de Gad y de Manasés, les agradaron.
31
וַיֹּאמֶר פִּינְחָס בֶּן-אֶלְעָזָר הַכֹּהֵן אֶל-בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וְאֶל-בְּנֵי-גָד וְאֶל-בְּנֵי מְנַשֶּׁה, הַיּוֹם יָדַעְנוּ כִּי-בְתוֹכֵנוּ יְהוָה, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-מְעַלְתֶּם בַּיהוָה, הַמַּעַל הַזֶּה; אָז, הִצַּלְתֶּם אֶת-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--מִיַּד יְהוָה.
Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest said to the Reubenites, to the Gadites and to the Manassites: Today we know that Jehovah is among us, because you have not committed this treachery against Jehovah; now you have rescued the sons of Israel from the hand of Jehovah.note
Pinjas hijo de Eleazar el sacerdote dijo a los hijos de Rubén, a los hijos de Gad y a los hijos de Manasés: Hoy sabemos que Jehová está en medio de nosotros, porque ustedes no han cometido esta infidelidad contra Jehová; ahora han librado a los hijos de Israel de la mano de Jehová.
32
וַיָּשָׁב פִּינְחָס בֶּן-אֶלְעָזָר הַכֹּהֵן וְהַנְּשִׂיאִים מֵאֵת בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּמֵאֵת בְּנֵי-גָד מֵאֶרֶץ הַגִּלְעָד, אֶל-אֶרֶץ כְּנַעַן--אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיָּשִׁבוּ אוֹתָם, דָּבָר.
Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest and the princes returned from the Reubenites and from the Gadites, out of the land of Gilead, to the land of Canaan, to the sons of Israel, and brought them word.note
Pinjas hijo de Eleazar el sacerdote y los príncipes regresaron de los hijos de Rubén y de los hijos de Gad, desde la tierra de Galaad, a la tierra de Canaán, a los hijos de Israel, y les llevaron la respuesta.
33
וַיִּיטַב הַדָּבָר, בְּעֵינֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיְבָרְכוּ אֱלֹהִים, בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וְלֹא אָמְרוּ, לַעֲלוֹת עֲלֵיהֶם לַצָּבָא, לְשַׁחֵת אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּבְנֵי-גָד יֹשְׁבִים בָּהּ.
The thing pleased the sons of Israel, and the sons of Israel blessed God; they spoke no more of going up against them in war, to destroy the land where the Reubenites and the Gadites dwelt.note
La cosa agradó a los hijos de Israel, y los hijos de Israel bendijeron a Dios; y no hablaron más de subir contra ellos a la guerra para destruir la tierra donde habitaban los hijos de Rubén y de Gad.
34
וַיִּקְרְאוּ בְּנֵי-רְאוּבֵן וּבְנֵי-גָד, לַמִּזְבֵּחַ: כִּי עֵד הוּא בֵּינֹתֵינוּ, כִּי יְהוָה הָאֱלֹהִים. {פ}
The Reubenites and the Gadites called the altar Witness: “for it is a witness between us that Jehovah is God.”note
Y los hijos de Rubén y los hijos de Gad dieron nombre al altar: «Porque es testigo entre nosotros de que Jehová es el Dios verdadero».
Verses 1–3 · Joshua calls the two and a half tribes, Joshua 22:1–3: the one speech in the book that praises a people for keeping faith across the river

★★★ The first verse names the three groups in the order the book gives them, and the order is the point. Reuben and Gad come first, then the half-tribe of Manasseh, the same three tribes whose land lay east of the Jordan (Joshua 13:8, already on these pages). Joshua calls them as a body, and the speech that follows is addressed to all three at once.

★★ “These many days, to this day” (v3) is the book’s own count of the war. The Hebrew zeh yamim rabbim, ‘these many days’, is the phrase Joshua uses for the length of the campaign: the three groups did not leave their brothers across the river during it. The verse says they kept the charge of Jehovah, mishmeret, a word for a guard kept at a post.

⚠ Joshua praises before he sends them home, and the praise is an assessment, not a formality. The shelf versions agree on the sense: the NWT 1984 reads ‘you have kept all that Moses the servant of Jehovah commanded you’, the KJV the same words with its capitalised title in place of the name, and the ASV ‘Jehovah’. The Hebrew says eved YHWH in both cases, and this translation follows the ASV in keeping the name.

Verses 4–8 · The rest given, the law restated, and the spoil divided, Joshua 22:4–8: love, walk and cling, and a blessing with a command to share

★★ V4 says Jehovah has given the other tribes rest, and sends these three home to their own land. The rest is the one promised in Joshua 21:44 (already on these pages), where Jehovah gave rest on every side. Joshua 22 now says the rest reached the far side of the river too, and the promise is complete.

★★ Verse 5 is the book’s own summary of the law in four verbs, and the last one is the strongest. The three groups are to love Jehovah, walk in his ways, keep his commandments and dabaq to him, a word that means to cling or to stay stuck, the same word Genesis 2:24 uses for a man cleaving to his wife (Genesis 2, already on these pages). The verb is used of the covenant, not of a feeling.

★ Verse 7 says Manasseh was split across the river, and the two halves got their land on different banks. Moses had given the half-tribe Bashan; Joshua gave the other half land on the west bank. Joshua then sends the whole tribe home, and the blessing in v6 and v7 is given to both halves.

★★ The spoil in v8 is divided with the brothers who stayed in the fight. The list of metals (silver, gold, bronze, iron) matches the Jericho spoil in Joshua 6:19 (already on these pages). The command to divide with the brothers means the eastern tribes were not to keep the war’s reward for themselves.

Verses 9–10 · The eastern tribes go home, and the altar at the Jordan, Joshua 22:9–10: a great altar to look upon

★★★ The word for the region of the Jordan, gelilot, is rare, and it is a place name here. It stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: Joshua 13:2 and 18:17 (already on these pages), this verse and v11, and Joel 4:4. Here it is the Jordan district, and the translation keeps the place sense through the verse rather than turning it into a general word for ‘regions’.

★★ The altar is “great to look upon”, and the Hebrew uses the verb for seeing for its purpose. Lemar’eh means ‘for appearance’, and the sentence turns on it: the altar was built to be seen. The NWT 1984 reads ‘an altar great in conspicuousness’, the KJV ‘a great altar to see to’, and the ASV ‘a great altar to look upon’. The TNM 2019 uses a larger noun phrase, ‘un altar enorme, impresionante’. The point is the same across all of them: visible size.

★ V9 names the route home: Shiloh, then Gilead, and the land they had taken possession of. Shiloh is where the tent stood (already on these pages), so the eastern tribes leave the sanctuary to return across the river. The verse says they went “by the command of Jehovah through Moses”, which ties their settlement to Numbers 32 (already on these pages).

Verses 11–15 · The alarm at Shiloh and the embassy with Phinehas, Joshua 22:11–15: the whole congregation ready for war over an altar

★★ Israel hears of the altar before anyone has offered a sacrifice on it, and gathers for war. V11 has the report: the altar is built “opposite the land of Canaan”, on the side that belongs to the sons of Israel. V12 has the whole congregation at Shiloh, ready to go up against them. The chapter does not say what the report claimed beyond the altar’s place and size.

★★ Phinehas is sent with ten chiefs, one from each tribe, and the embassy is a full assembly. The Hebrew says ten princes, one for each father’s house of each tribe, ‘heads of their fathers’ houses among the thousands’. The count is the whole nation sending a delegation, so the charge in the next verses is a charge from all Israel.

Verses 16–20 · The accusation: Peor, and Achan, Joshua 22:16–20: the two precedents that make a rival altar a national sin

★★★ Peor is the precedent Israel cannot forget, and it is named in the speech of the men who have just come from the sanctuary. The name Peor stands in thirteen verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the accusing men cite the sin of Peor, avon Peor, ‘the iniquity of Peor’, here as the sin that brought a plague on the assembly (v17). Numbers 25 (already on these pages) tells that story, and the plague it refers to is the one this speech remembers.

★★ Achan is the second precedent, and the speech puts the two sins side by side. Joshua 7 (already on these pages) has Achan take the devoted things, and the wrath falls on the whole congregation. The word the speech uses for the devoted thing is herem, and the name Achan stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, in the Achan story and here.

★ The speech goes from a question to a threat in four clauses. “Will you turn away today?” (v18), “if you rebel today, tomorrow he will be angry” (v18), “if the land is unclean, come across” (v19), “do not build a rival altar” (v19). The last offer is the one that lets the speakers keep their land: move across, where the tabernacle is.

Verses 21–29 · The answer, the altar as witness, and “God, God Jehovah”, Joshua 22:21–29: a copy made to say ‘we are yours too’

★★★ The answer opens with a triple name for God, and the Hebrew repeats it twice. V22 says El Elohim YHWH, ‘God, God, Jehovah’, twice over, and the phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Psalm 50:1, which is also a psalm of judgment. The shelf versions split on the doubling: the NWT 1984 reads ‘Divine One, God, Jehovah’, the ASV ‘The Mighty One, God, Jehovah’, and the KJV ‘God of gods’, after its capitalised title for the name. The Hebrew says El twice, so the doubling is kept.

★★ The sons of Reuben swear they did not build for sacrifice, and the altar is a witness, not an altar. V26 says it plainly: not for burnt offering, not for sacrifice. The copy they made (tavnit, a model or copy, the same word Exodus 25:9 uses for the pattern of the tabernacle) is a witness so that later generations can show it to the west bank and say: we are yours too.

★ The word “witness” (ed) stands in the whole argument and in the naming at v34. Ed appears in v27, v28 and v34 of this chapter, and the sense is a monument that testifies. The Hebrew of v34 gives no name, only the clause, and the KJV and ASV print the name Ed; the NWT 1984 says the altar was ‘named’, not that the name was given.

⚠ “Far be it from us” (v29) is the speech’s own pivot, and it is spoken by the people it accuses. The word chalilah stands in seventeen verses of the Hebrew Bible, and here it is the exclamation the eastern tribes use to deny the charge. The formula is strong enough that the chapter ends its defence with it.

Verses 30–34 · The pleasure of Phinehas, the report, and the name of the altar, Joshua 22:30–34: the crisis ends in a blessing, and nobody goes to war

★★★ Phinehas is satisfied, and the chapter says “it pleased them” twice in four verses. V30 says the answer pleased Phinehas and the princes; v33 says the matter pleased the sons of Israel, and they blessed God. The repetition is the chapter’s own sign that a potential war ended in agreement.

★★ Phinehas says “we know Jehovah is among us”, and the chapter then reports that the sons of Israel blessed God, not the men who came to threaten. The verdict is pronounced by the priest (v31), and the report goes back across the Jordan (v32). The question of who speaks for God in this episode is settled by the priest, not by the army.

★★ V34 is the chapter’s last sentence, and it names the altar. The Reubenites and Gadites ‘called’ the altar, and the clause after the verb is the name. The Hebrew gives no word beyond the clause; the KJV and ASV supply Ed. The NWT 1984 says they ‘began to name’ it, which is the closest the English gets to the Hebrew’s own uncertainty about the name.

Echoes paid. Joshua 21:44 promised rest on every side, and v4 says it has come (21:44). Joshua 13 gave the eastern tribes their land; Joshua 22 sends them to it. The spoil of Jericho (Joshua 6) comes back in v8.

Echoes planted. Joshua 23 (now on these pages) is Joshua’s farewell, and it repeats the warning against turning from Jehovah that v16 and v18 make here. Psalm 50 uses the same triple divine name as v22.

Decisions. The Hebrew of v34 has no name, and this translation prints the clause and says so in the note; the KJV’s Ed is given with its source. The NWT 1984 is quoted for the verb ‘began to name’. Gelilot stays a place name in v10. Tavnit is ‘copy’, not ‘pattern’, as the copy in the sentence is a copy of something already made.

Library. The chapter uses terms already in the library: edah, ed, mishkan, herem, mitzvah, torah, dabaq, maal. Any entry missing from either language is added in the same pass as this chapter.

⚠ A note on order: Joshua stands on these pages at chapters 1–24. The book is complete on these pages.
