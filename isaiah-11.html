A shoot comes out of the stump of Jesse and the Spirit of Jehovah rests on him: wisdom, counsel, the fear of Jehovah; he judges the poor with righteousness and strikes the earth with the rod of his mouth. The wolf sojourns with the lamb and a little boy leads them; there is no harm in all the holy mountain. The root of Jesse stands as a banner for the peoples, the Lord gathers the remnant a second time from eight places, Ephraim and Judah stop being rivals, and a highway opens from Assyria.
Undated in the chapter. It follows the felling of the Assyrian forest at the close of Isaiah 10, while Assyria is the threat; Isaiah 6:1 dates his own vision to the year King Uzziah died. ⚠ The chapter names no king and no date for the shoot; it names Jesse, David’s father, and the stump.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Isaiah 11:1–2 a shoot from the stump of Jesse — the Spirit of Jehovah rests on him

⭐ A shoot from a stump. “A shoot out of the stump of Jesse” stands in this one verse. The word for the shoot, choter, is rare: it stands in two verses, here and Proverbs 14:3, where it is the “rod” of pride; and the stump, geza‘, stands in three, here, Isaiah 40:24 (now on these pages) and Job 14:8 (not yet). A stump is what is left when a tree has been cut down. The chapter before ends with the forest of Assyria felled (Isaiah 10:33–34), and this one opens with what is left of another tree, the house of Jesse, the father of David (1 Samuel 16:1 and Ruth 4:17, both now on these pages), putting out a shoot. The KJV has “a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots”, the NIV “A shoot will come up from the stump of Jesse; from his roots a Branch will bear fruit”, the NWT 1984 “there must go forth a twig” and “a sprout will be fruitful”, the TLB “from the stump will grow a Shoot” and the Douay “a rod out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up out of his root”.

⭐ The branch. “And a branch from his roots will bear fruit” stands in this one verse; the word is netser, a green shoot, and many readers hear it behind “Nazarene” in Matthew 2:23 (now on these pages), where Matthew names “the prophets” and no single verse. That is a reading, not a count. The other sprout-words of the line of David are a different Hebrew word, tsemach: Jeremiah 23:5 (now on these pages) and Zechariah 3:8 (now on these pages).

⭐ The Spirit rests. “And the Spirit of Jehovah will rest upon him” stands in this one verse, with the verb that is used when the spirit rests on the seventy elders in Numbers 11:25 (now on these pages). The Hebrew then names the spirit four times in one breath: the Spirit of Jehovah, and then three pairs, of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and might, of knowledge and the fear of Jehovah, six words in all. The KJV has “the spirit of counsel and might”, the NWT 1984 “the spirit of counsel and of mightiness”, the TLB “the Spirit of wisdom, understanding, counsel, and might” and the NIV “the Spirit of the knowledge and fear of the Lord”. ⚠ The Douay ends the list at verse 2 with “godliness” (“the spirit of knowledge, and of godliness”) and opens verse 3 with “he shall be filled with the spirit of the fear of the Lord”, which makes seven where the Hebrew names six.

Verses 3–5 · Isaiah 11:3–5 his delight is the fear of Jehovah — judging the poor — the belt of righteousness

⭐ His delight, or his scent. “And his delight will be in the fear of Jehovah” stands in this one verse. The verb is from reach, a smell, the same verb as the scent of fire in Samson’s bowstrings (Judges 16:9, now on these pages); the word is a smelling, and the versions read it three ways. The KJV has “And shall make him of quick understanding in the fear of the Lord”, the Geneva “And shall make him prudent in the fear of the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “there will be enjoyment by him in the fear of Jehovah”, the TLB “His delight will be obedience to the Lord” and the Douay “he shall be filled with the spirit of the fear of the Lord”. This translation keeps “delight”, and says so here, because the Hebrew is a verb of the nose.

⭐ Not by the eyes and ears. “He will not judge by the sight of his eyes” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “he shall not judge after the sight of his eyes, neither reprove after the hearing of his ears”, the NIV “He will not judge by what he sees with his eyes, or decide by what he hears with his ears” and the TLB “He will not judge by appearance, false evidence, or hearsay”.

⭐ The poor, the meek, and the rod of his mouth. “And with righteousness he will judge the poor” stands in this one verse, dallim, the thin, the low; and the meek of the earth are the anavim, the same word as in Psalm 37:11 (now on these pages). “The rod of his mouth” stands in this one verse, and “and with the breath of his lips he will put the wicked to death” stands in this one verse, the line that Paul takes up at 2 Thessalonians 2:8 (now on these pages) and the sword from the mouth at Revelation 19:15 (now on these pages). The KJV has “with the rod of his mouth, and with the breath of his lips shall he slay the wicked”, the NWT 1984 “with the spirit of his lips he will put the wicked one to death” and the TLB “He will rule against the wicked who oppress them”. The Hebrew word for the breath is the same ruach as the Spirit of verse 2.

⭐ The belt. “The belt of his loins” stands in this one verse, with righteousness (tsedeq) and faithfulness (emunah) as the two belts, the one that holds the garment and the one that holds the man; Ephesians 6:14 (now on these pages) puts truth on the loins and righteousness on the breast. The KJV has “the girdle of his loins” and “the girdle of his reins”, the NWT 1984 “the belt of his hips” and “the belt of his loins” and the NIV “Righteousness will be his belt and faithfulness the sash around his waist”.

Verses 6–9 · Isaiah 11:6–9 the wolf and the lamb — a little boy leads — no harm in all my holy mountain

⭐ The wolf as a guest. “And the wolf will sojourn with the lamb” stands in this one verse. The verb is gur, to stay as a guest in another’s house, the verb of the stranger who is given room; the lamb is the host. Then the animals pair off, namer, the leopard, with gedi, the kid; and the calf (egel), the young lion (kefir) and the fatling (meri, the fed animal of the feast, as at 2 Samuel 6:13, now on these pages) together. The KJV has “The wolf also shall dwell with the lamb” and “the fatling”, the NIV “The wolf will live with the lamb”, the NWT 1984 “the wolf will actually reside for a while with the male lamb” and “the well-fed animal”, the Geneva “the fat beast”, the Douay “the sheep” and the TLB “fat cattle”.

⭐ A little boy. “And a little boy will lead them” stands in this one verse, na‘ar qaton, the boy (na‘ar) and the little, the one with the herder’s verb, nahag, to drive. The KJV has “and a little child shall lead them”, the NIV “and a little child will lead them” and the NWT 1984 “a mere little boy will be leader over them”.

⭐ The cow, the bear and the lion. “The cow and the bear will graze” stands in this one verse, the cow (parah) and the bear (dov), feeding together, and the lion eats straw like the ox. Isaiah 65:25 says it again with the holy mountain and adds the serpent’s dust. The KJV has “the lion shall eat straw like the ox”, the NIV “the lion will eat straw like the ox” and the TLB “lions will eat grass like the cows”.

⭐ The nursing child and the viper’s den. “The den of the viper” stands in this one verse; the cobra is peten, which is also in Psalm 91:13 (now on these pages). The KJV has “the hole of the asp” and “the cockatrice’ den”, the NIV “The infant will play near the cobra’s den, and the young child will put its hand into the viper’s nest”, the NWT 1984 “the sucking child will certainly play upon the hole of the cobra”, the Douay “thrust his hand into the den of the basilisk” and the TLB “Babies will crawl safely among poisonous snakes”.

⭐ No harm in all my holy mountain. “They will not hurt or destroy in all my holy mountain” stands in two verses, here and Isaiah 65:25. The speaker is now God himself, in the first person, my holy mountain. “The earth will be full of the knowledge of Jehovah” stands in this one verse; the line at Habakkuk 2:14 (now on these pages) is close but not the same, with “the glory of” in it. The KJV has “for the earth shall be full of the knowledge of the Lord, as the waters cover the sea”, the NWT 1984 “the earth will certainly be filled with the knowledge of Jehovah as the waters are covering the very sea”, the TLB “for as the waters fill the sea, so shall the earth be full of the knowledge of the Lord” and the Douay “as the covering waters of the sea”. ⚠ Readers have taken the animals as a picture of the nations or as the creatures themselves; the Hebrew gives the animals and does not explain them.

Verses 10–12 · Isaiah 11:10–12 the root of Jesse a banner — the Lord a second time — the four corners of the earth

⭐ The root, and the banner. “Who stands as a banner for the peoples” stands in this one verse, the banner being nes (nes), the pole raised for the people to gather to. The stump of verse 1 has become a root that stands, and “his resting place will be glorious” stands in this one verse. Paul cites the verse at Romans 15:12 (now on these pages) in the Greek form, “he who rises”, where the Hebrew has “stands”; and Revelation calls him the Root of David (5:5 and 22:16, both now on these pages). The KJV has “a root of Jesse, which shall stand for an ensign of the people”, the NIV “the Root of Jesse will stand as a banner for the peoples”, the NWT 1984 “his resting-place must become glorious”, the Geneva “his rest shall be glorious” and the TLB “a banner of salvation to all the world”. ⚠ The Douay has “and his sepulchre shall be glorious”, which is the Latin’s reading of the resting place and not the Hebrew’s.

⭐ A second time, and the Lord. “A second time his hand” stands in this one verse. The verse says a second time and does not say what the first was; verse 16 names the day Israel came up from Egypt. The verb is qanah, to get or to buy, the verb for the people Jehovah acquired in Exodus 15:16 (now on these pages). ⚠ The Hebrew here has Adonai, “the Lord”, and not the Name, so this translation says “the Lord” and keeps “Jehovah” for the verses that have it; the NWT 1984 prints “Jehovah will again offer his hand, a second time”. The KJV has “the Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people”, the NIV “the Lord will reach out his hand a second time to reclaim the surviving remnant of his people” and the TLB “the Lord will bring back a remnant of his people for the second time”.

⭐ Eight places. “And from Pathros and from Cush and from Elam and from Shinar and from Hamath” stands in this one verse, after Assyria and Egypt, and before the coasts of the sea: Cush (Genesis 10:6), Shinar (Genesis 11:2), Elam (Genesis 14:1) and Hamath (Numbers 13:21) are all on these pages already; Pathros is upper Egypt. The KJV ends “and from the islands of the sea”, the NIV “and from the islands of the Mediterranean”, the TLB “and all the distant coastal lands” and the NWT 1984 “and from the islands of the sea”. The banner is raised “for the nations” and the outcasts of Israel (nidchei) and the scattered of Judah (nefutsot) come to it “from the four corners of the earth”, which stands in this one verse, the kanaf that is also the wing and the edge.

Verses 13–16 · Isaiah 11:13–16 Ephraim and Judah — the neighbours — the tongue of the Egyptian sea — a highway

⭐ The two houses. “And the jealousy of Ephraim will depart” stands in this one verse; Ephraim, the north, and Judah, the south, stop being each other’s rivals, and the Hebrew sets them as a pair that cannot mix: Ephraim will not be jealous (yeqanne) of Judah, and Judah will not harass (yatsor) Ephraim. The KJV has “The envy also of Ephraim shall depart”, the NIV “Ephraim’s jealousy will vanish”, the NWT 1984 “the jealousy of Ephraim must depart” and the TLB “Then at last the jealousy between Israel and Judah will end”.

⭐ Together against the neighbours. “And they will swoop on the shoulder of the Philistines” stands in this one verse, toward the sea, which is the west; and the sons of the east, Edom and Moab and Ammon. “Edom and Moab will be the reach of their hand” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “they shall fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines toward the west”, the NIV “They will swoop down on the slopes of Philistia to the west”, the NWT 1984 “they must fly at the shoulder of the Philistines to the west” and the Douay “by the sea”.

⭐ The tongue of the sea. “And Jehovah will devote to destruction” stands in this one verse, the verb of the ban, cherem, here turned on a sea, “the tongue of the Egyptian sea”, which stands in this one verse, and “with the heat of his wind”, whose first word is found in this one verse and is a word the lexicons cannot settle. The KJV has “the tongue of the Egyptian sea” and “his mighty wind”, the NIV “with a scorching wind”, the NWT 1984 “in the glow of his spirit”, the Douay “in the strength of his spirit” and the TLB “sending a mighty wind”. The River, hanahar, is the Euphrates, struck into seven streams “and made to tread in sandals”, which stands in this one verse; the crossing of the sea in Exodus 14:21 (now on these pages) is the picture behind it.

⭐ A highway. “And there will be a highway for the remnant of his people” stands in this one verse; mesillah is the raised, built road, as in Isaiah 40:3 (now on these pages), and the remnant (sheerit) is the word that Isaiah gave to a son in Isaiah 7:3 (now on these pages), Shear-jashub, “a remnant will return”. The KJV has “there shall be an highway for the remnant of his people”, the NIV “There will be a highway for the remnant of his people”, the NWT 1984 “there must come to be a highway out of Assyria”, the Geneva “a path” and the TLB “He will make a highway from Assyria”. The day of verse 16 is the day Israel came up out of Egypt.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “shoot” for choter and “branch” for netser; “the Spirit of Jehovah”; “belt” for ezor; “sojourn” for gur; “banner” for nes; “the Lord” for Adonai at verse 11, and “Jehovah” where the Name is written; “devote to destruction” for cherem; “highway” for mesillah.

Echoes paid. Jesse and David (1 Samuel 16:1, Ruth 4:17); the stump of Isaiah 40:24; the Spirit resting in Numbers 11:25; the tsemach of Jeremiah 23:5 and Zechariah 3:8; Matthew 2:23, Romans 15:12, 2 Thessalonians 2:8, Ephesians 6:14, Revelation 5:5, 19:15 and 22:16; the knowledge of Jehovah in Habakkuk 2:14; the cobra of Psalm 91:13; the fatling of 2 Samuel 6:13; the people acquired in Exodus 15:16; the sea crossed in Exodus 14; the highway of Isaiah 40:3 and the remnant of Isaiah 7:3.

Echoes planted. The felled forest of Isaiah 10:33–34, the remnant that returns (Isaiah 10:21–22) and the repeated holy mountain of Isaiah 65:25; and the other stump (Job 14:8). None of these is translated here yet.
