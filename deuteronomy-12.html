The law code itself begins. Moses commands the destruction of every place the dispossessed nations used for worship — altars pulled down, standing stones smashed, Asherahs burned, carved images hewn down, and their NAME destroyed out of the place — and sets against it a single sanctuary: the place Jehovah will choose to put his own name, named six times in this chapter and never identified. Because one sanctuary would otherwise put ordinary meat out of reach for anyone living far from it, the chapter then permits domestic slaughter and eating in any town, unclean and clean alike, as with game — while withholding the blood, which is to be poured out on the ground like water and never eaten, because the blood is the life. It closes by forbidding Israel to inquire how the nations served their gods, naming child sacrifice as the reason.
Still the plains of Moab in the fortieth year (1:3); no new narrated event — the chapter continues Moses' second address without a break from chapter 11, and is the opening of the legal code that runs to chapter 26. Its own horizon is explicitly LATER than its setting: vv10-11 make crossing the Jordan, settling, and receiving rest from surrounding enemies the precondition of the central-sanctuary command, so the law is written to take effect after a conquest that has not begun. The place it legislates for is never named here; Jerusalem and Zion do not occur anywhere in Deuteronomy, and the sanctuary's own later history — Shiloh first, then elsewhere, then the temple — lies outside this book entirely.

Deuteronomy 12 — The Mister Translation

1
אֵלֶּה הַחֻקִּים וְהַמִּשְׁפָּטִים, אֲשֶׁר תִּשְׁמְרוּן לַעֲשׂוֹת, בָּאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי אֲבֹתֶיךָ לְךָ לְרִשְׁתָּהּ:  כָּל-הַיָּמִים--אֲשֶׁר-אַתֶּם חַיִּים, עַל-הָאֲדָמָה.
These are the statutes and the judgments that you shall keep to do, in the land that Jehovah the God of your fathers has given you to possess it — all the days that you are alive upon the ground.note
Estos son los estatutos y los juicios que ustedes guardarán para ponerlos por obra, en la tierra que Jehová el Dios de tus padres te ha dado para tomarla en posesión — todos los días que ustedes vivan sobre el suelo.
2
אַבֵּד תְּאַבְּדוּן אֶת-כָּל-הַמְּקֹמוֹת אֲשֶׁר עָבְדוּ-שָׁם הַגּוֹיִם, אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם יֹרְשִׁים אֹתָם--אֶת-אֱלֹהֵיהֶם:  עַל-הֶהָרִים הָרָמִים וְעַל-הַגְּבָעוֹת, וְתַחַת כָּל-עֵץ רַעֲנָן.
You shall utterly destroy all the places where the nations you are dispossessing served their gods: on the high mountains, and on the hills, and under every leafy tree.note
Destruirán por completo todos los lugares donde las naciones que ustedes van a desalojar sirvieron a sus dioses: sobre los montes altos, y sobre las colinas, y debajo de todo árbol frondoso.
3
וְנִתַּצְתֶּם אֶת-מִזְבְּחֹתָם, וְשִׁבַּרְתֶּם אֶת-מַצֵּבֹתָם, וַאֲשֵׁרֵיהֶם תִּשְׂרְפוּן בָּאֵשׁ, וּפְסִילֵי אֱלֹהֵיהֶם תְּגַדֵּעוּן; וְאִבַּדְתֶּם אֶת-שְׁמָם, מִן-הַמָּקוֹם הַהוּא.
And you shall pull down their altars, and smash their standing stones, and their Asherahs you shall burn with fire, and the carved images of their gods you shall hew down; and you shall destroy their name out of that place.note
Y derribarán sus altares, y quebrarán sus piedras alzadas, y sus Aserás las quemarán en el fuego, y las imágenes talladas de sus dioses las talarán; y destruirán su nombre de aquel lugar.
4
לֹא-תַעֲשׂוּן כֵּן, לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם.
You shall not do so to Jehovah your God.note
No harán así con Jehová su Dios.
5
כִּי אִם-אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר-יִבְחַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, מִכָּל-שִׁבְטֵיכֶם, לָשׂוּם אֶת-שְׁמוֹ, שָׁם--לְשִׁכְנוֹ תִדְרְשׁוּ, וּבָאתָ שָּׁמָּה.
But to the place that Jehovah your God chooses out of all your tribes, to put his name there — seek out his dwelling, and come there.note
Sino que al lugar que Jehová su Dios escoja de entre todas sus tribus, para poner allí su nombre — busquen su morada, y vendrás allí.
6
וַהֲבֵאתֶם שָׁמָּה, עֹלֹתֵיכֶם וְזִבְחֵיכֶם, וְאֵת מַעְשְׂרֹתֵיכֶם, וְאֵת תְּרוּמַת יֶדְכֶם; וְנִדְרֵיכֶם, וְנִדְבֹתֵיכֶם, וּבְכֹרֹת בְּקַרְכֶם, וְצֹאנְכֶם.
And you shall bring there your burnt-offerings and your sacrifices, and your tithes and the contribution of your hand, and your vows and your freewill-offerings, and the firstborn of your herd and of your flock.note
Y traerán allí sus holocaustos y sus sacrificios, y sus diezmos y la contribución de su mano, y sus votos y sus ofrendas voluntarias, y los primogénitos de su vacada y de su rebaño.
7
וַאֲכַלְתֶּם-שָׁם, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, וּשְׂמַחְתֶּם בְּכֹל מִשְׁלַח יֶדְכֶם, אַתֶּם וּבָתֵּיכֶם--אֲשֶׁר בֵּרַכְךָ, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ.
And you shall eat there before Jehovah your God, and you shall rejoice in everything you put your hand to — you and your households — in which Jehovah your God has blessed you.note
Y comerán allí delante de Jehová su Dios, y se alegrarán en todo aquello a lo que pongan la mano — ustedes y sus casas — en lo que Jehová tu Dios te haya bendecido.
8
לֹא תַעֲשׂוּן--כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר אֲנַחְנוּ עֹשִׂים פֹּה, הַיּוֹם:  אִישׁ, כָּל-הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינָיו.
You shall not do according to all that we are doing here today — each man whatever is right in his own eyes.note
No harán conforme a todo lo que nosotros hacemos aquí hoy — cada hombre lo que es recto a sus propios ojos.
9
כִּי לֹא-בָאתֶם, עַד-עָתָּה--אֶל-הַמְּנוּחָה, וְאֶל-הַנַּחֲלָה, אֲשֶׁר-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, נֹתֵן לָךְ.
For you have not come, until now, to the rest and to the inheritance that Jehovah your God is giving you.note
Porque no han llegado, hasta ahora, al descanso ni a la heredad que Jehová tu Dios te da.
10
וַעֲבַרְתֶּם, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן, וִישַׁבְתֶּם בָּאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם מַנְחִיל אֶתְכֶם; וְהֵנִיחַ לָכֶם מִכָּל-אֹיְבֵיכֶם מִסָּבִיב, וִישַׁבְתֶּם-בֶּטַח.
But you shall cross the Jordan and settle in the land that Jehovah your God is giving you to inherit, and he will give you rest from all your enemies around you, and you shall dwell in safety.note
Pero cruzarán el Jordán y se asentarán en la tierra que Jehová su Dios les da en herencia, y él les dará descanso de todos sus enemigos alrededor, y habitarán seguros.
11
וְהָיָה הַמָּקוֹם, אֲשֶׁר-יִבְחַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם בּוֹ לְשַׁכֵּן שְׁמוֹ שָׁם--שָׁמָּה תָבִיאוּ, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם:  עוֹלֹתֵיכֶם וְזִבְחֵיכֶם, מַעְשְׂרֹתֵיכֶם וּתְרֻמַת יֶדְכֶם, וְכֹל מִבְחַר נִדְרֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר תִּדְּרוּ לַיהוָה.
And it shall be that the place that Jehovah your God chooses to make his name dwell there — there you shall bring everything I am commanding you: your burnt-offerings and your sacrifices, your tithes and the contribution of your hand, and every choice thing of your vows that you vow to Jehovah.note
Y sucederá que el lugar que Jehová su Dios escoja para hacer habitar allí su nombre — allí traerán todo lo que yo les mando: sus holocaustos y sus sacrificios, sus diezmos y la contribución de su mano, y todo lo escogido de sus votos que voten a Jehová.
12
וּשְׂמַחְתֶּם, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם--אַתֶּם וּבְנֵיכֶם וּבְנֹתֵיכֶם, וְעַבְדֵיכֶם וְאַמְהֹתֵיכֶם; וְהַלֵּוִי אֲשֶׁר בְּשַׁעֲרֵיכֶם, כִּי אֵין לוֹ חֵלֶק וְנַחֲלָה אִתְּכֶם.
And you shall rejoice before Jehovah your God — you and your sons and your daughters, and your male slaves and your female slaves, and the Levite who is within your gates, since he has no portion or inheritance with you.note
Y se alegrarán delante de Jehová su Dios — ustedes y sus hijos y sus hijas, y sus esclavos y sus esclavas, y el levita que está dentro de sus puertas, ya que él no tiene porción ni heredad con ustedes.
13
הִשָּׁמֶר לְךָ, פֶּן-תַּעֲלֶה עֹלֹתֶיךָ, בְּכָל-מָקוֹם, אֲשֶׁר תִּרְאֶה.
Take heed to yourself that you not offer up your burnt-offerings in every place that you see.note
Guárdate de ofrecer tus holocaustos en cualquier lugar que veas.
14
כִּי אִם-בַּמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר-יִבְחַר יְהוָה, בְּאַחַד שְׁבָטֶיךָ--שָׁם, תַּעֲלֶה עֹלֹתֶיךָ; וְשָׁם תַּעֲשֶׂה, כֹּל אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּךָּ.
But in the place that Jehovah chooses in one of your tribes — there you shall offer up your burnt-offerings, and there you shall do everything I am commanding you.note
Sino en el lugar que Jehová escoja en una de tus tribus — allí ofrecerás tus holocaustos, y allí harás todo lo que yo te mando.
15
רַק בְּכָל-אַוַּת נַפְשְׁךָ תִּזְבַּח וְאָכַלְתָּ בָשָׂר, כְּבִרְכַּת יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ אֲשֶׁר נָתַן-לְךָ--בְּכָל-שְׁעָרֶיךָ; הַטָּמֵא וְהַטָּהוֹר יֹאכְלֶנּוּ, כַּצְּבִי וְכָאַיָּל.
Only, as much as your soul desires, you may slaughter and eat meat within all your gates, according to the blessing of Jehovah your God that he has given you; the unclean and the clean may eat it, as they eat the gazelle and the deer.note
Solo que, cuanto desee tu alma, podrás degollar y comer carne dentro de todas tus puertas, conforme a la bendición de Jehová tu Dios que él te ha dado; el inmundo y el limpio podrán comerla, como se come la gacela y el ciervo.
16
רַק הַדָּם, לֹא תֹאכֵלוּ:  עַל-הָאָרֶץ תִּשְׁפְּכֶנּוּ, כַּמָּיִם.
Only the blood you shall not eat; you shall pour it out on the earth like water.note
Solo la sangre no la comerán; sobre la tierra la derramarás como agua.
17
לֹא-תוּכַל לֶאֱכֹל בִּשְׁעָרֶיךָ, מַעְשַׂר דְּגָנְךָ וְתִירֹשְׁךָ וְיִצְהָרֶךָ, וּבְכֹרֹת בְּקָרְךָ, וְצֹאנֶךָ; וְכָל-נְדָרֶיךָ אֲשֶׁר תִּדֹּר, וְנִדְבֹתֶיךָ וּתְרוּמַת יָדֶךָ.
You are not permitted to eat within your gates the tithe of your grain, your new wine and your fresh oil, or the firstborn of your herd and of your flock, or any of your vows that you vow, or your freewill-offerings, or the contribution of your hand.note
No te está permitido comer dentro de tus puertas el diezmo de tu grano, tu vino nuevo y tu aceite fresco, ni los primogénitos de tu vacada y de tu rebaño, ni ninguno de tus votos que votes, ni tus ofrendas voluntarias, ni la contribución de tu mano.
18
כִּי אִם-לִפְנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ תֹּאכְלֶנּוּ, בַּמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר יִבְחַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ בּוֹ--אַתָּה וּבִנְךָ וּבִתֶּךָ וְעַבְדְּךָ וַאֲמָתֶךָ, וְהַלֵּוִי אֲשֶׁר בִּשְׁעָרֶיךָ; וְשָׂמַחְתָּ, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, בְּכֹל, מִשְׁלַח יָדֶךָ.
Rather, before Jehovah your God you shall eat them, in the place that Jehovah your God chooses — you and your son and your daughter, and your male slave and your female slave, and the Levite who is within your gates; and you shall rejoice before Jehovah your God in everything you put your hand to.note
Más bien, delante de Jehová tu Dios los comerás, en el lugar que Jehová tu Dios escoja — tú y tu hijo y tu hija, y tu esclavo y tu esclava, y el levita que está dentro de tus puertas; y te alegrarás delante de Jehová tu Dios en todo aquello a lo que pongas la mano.
19
הִשָּׁמֶר לְךָ, פֶּן-תַּעֲזֹב אֶת-הַלֵּוִי, כָּל-יָמֶיךָ, עַל-אַדְמָתֶךָ.  {ס}
Take heed to yourself that you not forsake the Levite, all your days upon your ground.note
Guárdate de desamparar al levita, todos tus días sobre tu suelo.
20
כִּי-יַרְחִיב יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ אֶת-גְּבֻלְךָ, כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר-לָךְ, וְאָמַרְתָּ אֹכְלָה בָשָׂר, כִּי-תְאַוֶּה נַפְשְׁךָ לֶאֱכֹל בָּשָׂר--בְּכָל-אַוַּת נַפְשְׁךָ, תֹּאכַל בָּשָׂר.
When Jehovah your God enlarges your territory, as he promised you, and you say, “Let me eat meat,” because your soul desires to eat meat — as much as your soul desires you may eat meat.note
Cuando Jehová tu Dios ensanche tu territorio, como te lo prometió, y digas: «Voy a comer carne», porque tu alma desea comer carne — cuanto desee tu alma podrás comer carne.
21
כִּי-יִרְחַק מִמְּךָ הַמָּקוֹם, אֲשֶׁר יִבְחַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ לָשׂוּם שְׁמוֹ שָׁם, וְזָבַחְתָּ מִבְּקָרְךָ וּמִצֹּאנְךָ אֲשֶׁר נָתַן יְהוָה לְךָ, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוִּיתִךָ--וְאָכַלְתָּ, בִּשְׁעָרֶיךָ, בְּכֹל, אַוַּת נַפְשֶׁךָ.
If the place that Jehovah your God chooses to put his name there is too far from you, then you may slaughter from your herd and from your flock that Jehovah has given you, as I have commanded you, and you may eat within your gates as much as your soul desires.note
Si el lugar que Jehová tu Dios escoja para poner allí su nombre queda lejos de ti, entonces podrás degollar de tu vacada y de tu rebaño que Jehová te ha dado, como te he mandado, y comerás dentro de tus puertas cuanto desee tu alma.
22
אַךְ, כַּאֲשֶׁר יֵאָכֵל אֶת-הַצְּבִי וְאֶת-הָאַיָּל--כֵּן, תֹּאכְלֶנּוּ:  הַטָּמֵא, וְהַטָּהוֹר, יַחְדָּו, יֹאכְלֶנּוּ.
Only, as the gazelle and the deer are eaten, so you shall eat it; the unclean and the clean alike may eat it.note
Solo que, como se come la gacela y el ciervo, así lo comerás; el inmundo y el limpio por igual podrán comerlo.
23
רַק חֲזַק, לְבִלְתִּי אֲכֹל הַדָּם, כִּי הַדָּם, הוּא הַנָּפֶשׁ; וְלֹא-תֹאכַל הַנֶּפֶשׁ, עִם-הַבָּשָׂר.
Only be strong — do not eat the blood; for the blood is the life, and you shall not eat the life with the flesh.note
Solo sé fuerte — no comas la sangre; porque la sangre es la vida, y no comerás la vida junto con la carne.
24
לֹא, תֹּאכְלֶנּוּ:  עַל-הָאָרֶץ תִּשְׁפְּכֶנּוּ, כַּמָּיִם.
You shall not eat it; you shall pour it out on the earth like water.note
No la comerás; sobre la tierra la derramarás como agua.
25
לֹא, תֹּאכְלֶנּוּ--לְמַעַן יִיטַב לְךָ וּלְבָנֶיךָ אַחֲרֶיךָ, כִּי-תַעֲשֶׂה הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
You shall not eat it, so that it may go well with you and with your children after you, when you do what is right in the eyes of Jehovah.note
No la comerás, para que te vaya bien a ti y a tus hijos después de ti, cuando hagas lo recto a los ojos de Jehová.
26
רַק קָדָשֶׁיךָ אֲשֶׁר-יִהְיוּ לְךָ, וּנְדָרֶיךָ, תִּשָּׂא וּבָאתָ, אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר-יִבְחַר יְהוָה.
Only your holy things that you have, and your vows, you shall carry and come to the place that Jehovah chooses.note
Solo tus cosas santas que tengas, y tus votos, los llevarás y vendrás al lugar que Jehová escoja.
27

Verses 1–4 · “You shall not do so to Jehovah your God” — the sentence that stands exactly twice in the Bible, and both times in this chapter

★ The chapter is bracketed by one sentence, printed once at v4 and once at v31, and it occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. Searched consonantally across the whole text: lo ta’asun ken la-YHVH eloheikhem at v4 returns two verses in all, this one and Numbers 32:23 (already on these pages) — where it is not the same sentence at all but a negated condition, ‘but IF you do not do so’, with no ‘to Jehovah’ after it. The singular form at v31, lo ta’aseh ken la-YHVH elohekha, returns exactly one verse: v31 itself. So the prohibition stands twice, and this chapter owns both.

⚠ And the two are not identical, which is the interesting part: v4 is PLURAL and v31 is SINGULAR. The bracket spans the seam. Chapter 11 counted its own grammatical number switching form by form and found twelve alternating blocks in thirty-two verses; run the same count here and Deuteronomy 12 comes out at the opposite extreme — two blocks. Verses 1–12 are plural, verses 13–31 singular, with one switch between them. What this chapter has instead of alternation is five verses that switch inside themselves: v1, v5, v7 and v9 each run plural and then close on a singular clause, and v16 does the reverse (see the note there).

★ This is the chapter Deuteronomy 11 said to watch, and the hypothesis it planted does not survive. That chapter closed by offering, explicitly as a hypothesis and not a finding, that the stray singulars arrive ‘where the text narrows to one household’s own property.’ Deuteronomy 12 refutes it on its own clearest evidence, and the refutation is its own note: the same household — you, your sons, your daughters, your slaves, and the Levite in your gates — is addressed in the PLURAL at v12 and in the SINGULAR at v18, in what is otherwise the same sentence. The content is identical; only the number moves. Whatever governs the switch, it is not the household.

⚠ An English reader cannot see any of this, and a Spanish reader can. English has one ‘you’; Spanish has ustedes and tú. The Spanish edition of this chapter therefore prints the seam directly — ustedes through v12, tú from v13 — and the four mid-verse switches show up as a change of person inside one sentence. That is not a liberty taken in the Spanish; it is the Hebrew, which Spanish happens to be able to carry and English is not. ★ Five of the seven English witnesses DO carry it, and four of them do it with a spare pronoun. The ASV still had ‘ye’ and ‘thou’ and used them exactly: ‘Ye shall not do so unto Jehovah your God’ at v4 against ‘Thou shalt not do so unto Jehovah thy God’ at v31 — the bracket and its seam, both visible in one 1901 English Bible. KJV and Geneva do the same, and so does the Douay, which puts a plural you at v4 and a singular thou at v31 — for once the Vulgate HELPING, since Latin has tu and vos and had no way to lose the distinction. The NWT 1984 solves it a fifth way, by typography: it prints the plural as a small-capital YOU, so at v5 its ‘to have it reside, YOU will seek, and there you must come’ marks even the mid-verse switch. ⚠ The two that lose it are the NIV and The Living Bible — and so, going backwards, does the NWT’s own 2013 revision, which drops the small capitals its 1984 edition used. This is one of the places where the older English is carrying information the newer has thrown away.

V1 opens with the pair ha-chuqqim ve-ha-mishpatim, the same ‘statutes and judgments’ already fixed at Deuteronomy 11:1, and closes on adamah, the ground, kept distinct from eretz, the land, earlier in the same verse — two Hebrew words, two English words, as elsewhere in this translation.

Verses 2–3 · Altars, standing stones, Asherahs, carved images — the same four objects as Deuteronomy 7, with two of the verbs exchanged

The demolition list here is the one from Deuteronomy 7:5, already on these pages, and the two lists share all four objects and all four verbs. What they do not share is which verb goes with which object. Set side by side, from the Hebrew of both:

★ Deuteronomy 7:5 — altars pulled down, standing stones smashed, Asherahs hewn down (tegade’un), carved images burned with fire (tisrefun ba-esh).
Deuteronomy 12:3 — altars pulled down, standing stones smashed, Asherahs burned with fire, carved images hewn down.

The last two verbs are exchanged between their objects. Both assignments are possible — an asherah is wooden and will burn or chop, and a pesel can be cut or burned — and neither verse is correcting the other. It is worth noticing because this book so often quotes itself word for word (chapter 11 reproduced 6:9 letter for letter), so a place where it repeats a formula and moves a piece inside it is a different kind of repetition from the ones catalogued so far. The English follows Deuteronomy 7’s own established renderings — standing stones, Asherahs, hew down — precisely so the swap is visible rather than hidden behind a fresh set of words.

★ The verb at v2 is doubled, and it is the verb chapter 11 used of Egypt. Abbed te’abbedun is the infinitive absolute reinforcing its own finite verb — the Hebrew way of saying a thing twice for emphasis, which eleven of the thirteen witnesses render with an adverb: KJV ‘utterly destroy’, ASV ‘surely destroy’, Geneva ‘utterly destroy’, NIV ‘Destroy completely’, NWT 1984 ‘absolutely destroy’, and the whole Spanish shelf (RV 1909 «Destruiréis enteramente», RV60 the same, NVI «por completo»). The two that do not are the Douay, which simply reads ‘Destroy all the places’ and lets the doubling go, and The Living Bible, which paraphrases the verse into ‘heathen altars’ and keeps neither. The root is abad, and it is the same root that closed the corresponding sentence one chapter earlier: at Deuteronomy 11:4, of the Egyptian army in the sea, ‘and Jehovah destroyed them, to this day.’ What God did to the army, Israel is now told to do to the shrines. And the same root returns at the end of v3 with a strange object: not the places, not the images, but the NAME — ve-ibbadtem et shemam, ‘you shall destroy their name out of that place.’

⚠ The name is the hinge, and the next note is where it turns. Two verses after ordering their name destroyed out of the place, the chapter orders Israel to the place where he will put HIS name.

Verses 5–6 · The place Jehovah will choose — six times in this chapter, and not once in the whole book is it named

★ The formula ‘the place that Jehovah will choose’ stands six times in this chapter — v5, v11, v14, v18, v21, v26 — and nineteen times in Deuteronomy, of which eighteen are of a place and one, at 17:15, is of a king. It is never filled in. Searched consonantally across the book: Jerusalem occurs zero times in Deuteronomy, and so does Zion. The most consequential piece of legislation in Israelite worship names its own address nowhere.

⚠ Where the tradition puts it, and why this translation does not decide. The obvious later answer is the Jerusalem temple, and Kings reads it that way. The Geneva Bible of 1599, which is not shy, put the honest version of the answer in its margin at v14 and named the sequence instead of the site: the ark stood at Shiloh ‘243 years, or as some write more than 300 years, and in other places till the temple was built.’ That is a 1599 translator declining to read Jerusalem into a verse that does not have it, which is also this project’s policy: where a tradition fills a blank the text leaves open, the blank is reported as a blank. Readers who want the argument that the chapter was written for, or by, a particular later reform will find it made at length elsewhere; it is a reading with a real pedigree and it is not the only one, and neither is voted on here.

★ The name, continued from the previous note. Verse 3 ends ‘you shall destroy their NAME out of that PLACE’; verse 5 sends Israel to the PLACE where he will put his NAME. The same two nouns, shem and maqom, in the same order, two verses apart, doing opposite work. Erase a name from a place; go to the place for a name.

⚠ ‘Seek out his dwelling’ is a genuinely hard phrase, and the Hebrew word is a hapax. Le-shikhno tidreshu — searched across the whole Hebrew Bible, the form le-shikhno occurs exactly once, here. Read as a noun it is ‘to his dwelling you shall seek’, which is what this translation prints; repointed as an infinitive (le-shakkeno, ‘to make it dwell’) it would match v11’s le-shakken shemo sham exactly, and a number of readers have proposed just that. The shelf shows the strain: ASV ‘even unto his habitation shall ye seek’, KJV ‘even unto his habitation shall ye seek’, NWT 1984 ‘to have it reside, YOU will seek’. The uncertainty is real and is left standing rather than resolved by choosing the smoother reading.

★ The verb is darash, and the chapter will forbid it in its last paragraph. Seek his dwelling, v5; do not seek after their gods, v30. Same verb, opened and closed.

Verses 7, 12 and 18 · The same household, the same sentence, twice — once in the plural and once in the singular

★★ This is the cleanest evidence in the book so far for what the grammatical-number switch is tracking, and it is a minimal pair. Verse 12 and verse 18 give the same instruction to the same list of people in the same order, and differ in nothing but number:

V12 — u-smachtem lifnei YHVH eloheiKHEM: atTEM u-veneiKHEM u-venoteiKHEM, ve-avdeiKHEM ve-amhoteiKHEM, ve-ha-levi asher bi-sha’areiKHEM.
V18 — atTAH u-vinKHA u-vitTEKHA ve-avdeKHA va-amateKHA, ve-ha-levi asher bi-sha’areiKHA; ve-samachTA lifnei YHVH elohEKHA.

Six members, identical order, the identical Levite clause, the identical ‘and you shall rejoice before Jehovah your God’ — wholly plural at v12, wholly singular at v18. And v18 is the singular twin of v7 as well: v7’s u-smachtem be-khol mishlach yedKHEM, ‘you shall rejoice in everything you put your hand to’, is v18’s ve-samachta be-khol mishlach yadEKHA, word for word in the other number.

⚠ What that rules out. It rules out the household hypothesis Deuteronomy 11 offered, since the household is what BOTH verses are about. It rules out any account resting on the KIND of content, which chapter 10 had already tested and reported failing. What it leaves standing is the reading chapter 11 reached from the other direction: the number tracks the block, and the block is a unit of composition. Chapter 11 could show that because its source — Deuteronomy 6 — was on these pages to set beside it. This chapter shows it without needing an outside source, because it states its law twice and the two statements are in different numbers: verses 2–12 give the command to destroy the local shrines and bring everything to the chosen place, and then verses 13–19 give it again, in the singular, and add the concession. Told twice, the second time as if for the first.

⚠ Said with its limits. This is a description of what the text does, not a claim about who wrote which half or when. The doubling is plain on the page in any language; the explanation for it is contested and is not settled here.

Verses 8–9 · “Right in his own eyes” — the line the book of Judges ends on, and the rest that has not arrived yet

★ Deuteronomy 12:8 is one of four verses in the Hebrew Bible with the phrase ha-yashar be-einav, ‘the right in his own eyes’, and the other three are worth knowing. Searched consonantally across the whole text, the four are Exodus 15:26, this verse, Judges 17:6 and Judges 21:25.

⚠ Exodus 15:26 is the one that must not be miscounted, and it belongs on the other side of the ledger. There the ‘his’ is GOD’S — ‘and you do the right in HIS eyes’, a command to obey — so the identical three words mean the opposite thing depending on whose eyes they are. Only three verses put the eyes in a human being’s head, and this is the first of them.

★ The other two are the same sentence, twice, and they are the frame of the book of Judges. Judges 17:6 and 21:25 are letter for letter identical — ba-yamim ha-hem ein melekh be-yisrael: ish ha-yashar be-einav ya’aseh, ‘in those days there was no king in Israel; each man would do the right in his own eyes’ — and the second of them is the last verse of the book. So the phrase Moses uses here for the way Israel is worshipping in the wilderness is the phrase Judges will use for the condition of the country, and the diagnosis is the same one. Deuteronomy’s version carries an extra word the Judges refrain does not: kol, ‘each man whatever is right in his own eyes’.

★ Whether a version keeps the EYES here decides whether its reader can see the answer this chapter gives, and the shelf splits almost down the middle. Verses 25 and 28 reply to v8 by moving the eyes — right in the eyes of JEHOVAH — so a version that renders v8 without them has quietly cut the wire between the two halves of the chapter. Counted across all thirteen witnesses fetched for this chapter, six keep the eyes and seven drop them. Keeping: KJV and ASV (‘every man whatsoever is right in his own eyes’), Geneva 1599 (‘whatsoever seemeth him good in his own eyes’), NWT 1984 (‘each one whatever is right in his own eyes’), the NWT’s own 2013 revision, and the TNM 1987 («lo que sea recto a sus propios ojos»). Dropping: NIV (‘as they see fit’), Douay (‘that which seemeth good to himself’), The Living Bible, and — the part worth pausing on — four of the five Spanish witnesses, the two Reina-Valeras, the NVI, and the TNM in its 2019 revision («lo que le parece bien»), which loses what the TNM 1987 had kept.

⚠ So a Spanish reader has, at this verse, exactly one version on the shelf that preserves the link the chapter is built on. This translation keeps the eyes in both languages for that reason.

V9: ‘the rest and the inheritance’ pairs two words this project has already entered separately, and the pairing itself is a one-off: searched across the Hebrew Bible, el ha-menuchah ve-el ha-nachalah occurs in this verse alone.

Verses 10–11 · Rest from your enemies, and safety — the condition attached to the command

The chosen place is not for now. Verse 9 says Israel has not yet arrived at rest, and v10 makes crossing the Jordan, settling, and being given rest from surrounding enemies the precondition of v11’s ‘and it shall be that the place…’. The law is written to begin later than the sermon that contains it, which is worth holding on to when reading the concession at vv15 and 20–22: both halves of this chapter are addressed to a settled country, not to a camp.

Le-shakken shemo sham, ‘to make his name dwell there’, is the causative of shakhan — the root behind the tabernacle’s own name, the mishkan, the dwelling. ASV keeps the causative visible (‘to cause his name to dwell there’); NIV flattens it to ‘as a dwelling for his Name’. The distinction the Hebrew is drawing is between the god and the name: what is placed at the site is the NAME, and the chapter never says the god lives there.

Verses 13–14 · The law stated a second time — and this time in the singular

Verse 13 restarts the chapter. Everything in vv13–14 has already been said in vv5–6 and v11: not everywhere, only at the chosen place, and everything I command you goes there. What has changed is the number — the plural block ends at v12 and the singular begins here and runs to the end of the chapter — and what follows, which is new (see the next note).

Hishamer lekha, ‘take heed to yourself’, opens v13, v19 and v30: three warnings, spaced across the singular half, about the three ways the law can fail — sacrificing anywhere, abandoning the Levite, and copying the neighbours.

Verses 15, 20–22 · Meat without an altar — the concession, the gazelle, and Leviticus 17

★ This is the chapter’s practical consequence, and it is a real change. If every sacrifice must happen at one place, then a family a week’s walk away cannot eat beef or mutton at all, since killing a domestic animal had been a sacrificial act. Verse 15 releases it: slaughter and eat at home, as much as your soul desires, and the ritually unclean may eat with the clean — because this meat is not an offering. The comparison the Hebrew reaches for is the tzvi and the ayyal, the gazelle and the deer: game animals, which could never be sacrificed and so had never needed an altar. Domestic meat is being reclassified as venison.

⚠ Leviticus 17:3–5, already on these pages, says the opposite in as many words — a man who slaughters an ox or a lamb or a goat and does not bring it to the entrance of the tent ‘has shed blood, and that man shall be cut off from among his people’. The two texts are not reconciled here, and this translation does not reconcile them. What is worth reporting is that Deuteronomy gives its own reason, and the reason is distance: v21, ‘if the place… is too far from you.’ Leviticus legislates for a camp with the tent in the middle of it, where nowhere is far; Deuteronomy legislates for a country. Whether that is a development, a harmonisation, or two laws for two situations is exactly the question the readings differ on, and each of those readings has serious defenders.

★ The King James says ‘roebuck’, and the roebuck does not live there. KJV renders tzvi as the European roe deer at both v15 and v22, and it is not alone: Geneva has ‘roebuck’ at v22 too and Douay ‘the roe’. ASV corrected it to ‘gazelle’, and NWT 1984 and NIV both read gazelle. The animal in question does not live in the southern Levant; the three oldest English versions on the shelf are all describing a deer from home. The Spanish shelf runs the identical course two centuries later: the RV 1909 has «corzo», the roe deer again, and the RV60 replaces it with «gacela». Two shelves, the same mistake, the same correction.

⚠ The Douay-Rheims reads ‘the unclean and the clean’ as a statement about the ANIMAL, and it is alone in that. Every other version on both shelves takes it of the person eating: Douay instead expands it as a gloss on the beast — unclean meaning ‘having blemish or defect’, clean meaning ‘sound and without blemish, such as may be offered’. That is the Vulgate showing through, as this translation’s Douay divergences usually are; and it inverts the verse, because the point of v15 is precisely that a person’s ritual state stops mattering once the meal is not a sacrifice.

Nefesh in these three verses means appetite — be-khol avvat nafshekha, ‘as much as your soul desires’, at vv15, 20 (twice) and 21. Hold on to that for v23, which uses the same word for the thing in the blood.

Verses 16 and 24 · The one sentence in the singular half that is plural — and the same sentence twelve verses later in the singular

★ Verse 16 is the only plural intrusion into the whole singular block, and it is the blood prohibition. Rak ha-dam lo tokheLU — ‘only the blood you (plural) shall not eat’ — sitting between v15 and v17, both singular throughout, and switching back inside its own second clause: al ha-aretz tishpekhennu ka-mayim, ‘you (singular) shall pour it out on the earth like water.’

⚠ And v24 is the same sentence entirely in the singular. Verse 24 reads lo tokhelennu; al ha-aretz tishpekhennu ka-mayim — the second clause word for word identical to v16’s, the first clause the singular of v16’s plural. The same prohibition stands twice in one chapter, twelve verses apart, once addressed to a nation and once to a man. It is the tidiest illustration in the chapter of what the v12/v18 note argues: the number is not tracking the content, because here the content is the same words.

⚠ Leviticus 17 disposes of the blood differently, and the difference is not trivial. Leviticus 17:13, already on these pages, tells a hunter to pour out the blood and cover it with dust. Deuteronomy says pour it on the earth like water, twice, and says nothing about covering it. Pouring blood out like water is a way of saying it is not being offered to anything — the same disposal that makes the meat ordinary makes the blood not a libation.

Verse 17 · The list of what may NOT be eaten at home — and the two products that are not grain

The concession of v15 is immediately bounded: ordinary meat may be eaten anywhere, but the sacred dues may not. Tirosh and yitzhar are kept here as new wine and fresh oil rather than the flatter ‘wine’ and ‘oil’ — both words name the product as it comes off the harvest, which is why they belong in a tithe list at all; ASV reads ‘the tithe of thy grain, or of thy new wine, or of thine oil’ and NWT 1984 ‘your grain or of your new wine or of your oil’ — both keeping the first two specific and letting the third go plain. KJV and Geneva read ‘corn’ for the grain, the familiar King James archaism for cereal of any kind.

‘You are not permitted’ renders lo tukhal, which is the verb ‘to be able’ used for permission — not ‘you must not’ but, literally, ‘you cannot’. KJV keeps the older English idiom that did the same work (‘Thou mayest not eat’), which now reads as a straightforward prohibition to a modern ear and so loses the shading.

Verse 19 · Take heed that you not forsake the Levite — the man with no land in a chapter about land

The Levite appears three times in this chapter (v12, v18, v19) and always with the same tag: ‘who is within your gates’, and at v12 the reason — ‘since he has no portion or inheritance with you.’ That is the chapter’s own arithmetic turning on someone. Centralising sacrifice at one place puts every local sanctuary out of use, and the men who served them out of a living; the chapter that closes the local altars is the chapter that says, twice, that the Levite eats at your table, and then a third time that forgetting him is a thing to be warned about. This pays off Deuteronomy 10:9, already on these pages, where Levi is paid in a person instead of land.

Al admatekha closes the verse — ‘upon your GROUND’, adamah, the same word that closed v1 and the word this translation keeps distinct from eretz.

Verse 23 · “Be strong” — a war-word for a dietary rule, and the margin where the Geneva Bible admits it

★ Rak chazaq — ‘only be strong’ — occurs in exactly three verses in the Hebrew Bible, and the other two are the charge to Joshua. Searched consonantally: this verse, Joshua 1:7 and Joshua 1:18, both ‘only be strong and very courageous’, spoken to a man about to invade a country. Here the identical opening is spoken to a household about not eating something. The Hebrew treats abstaining from blood as an act requiring nerve, and that is a real and slightly startling thing for the text to say.

⚠ Most of the shelf turns the war-verb into caution, and the Geneva Bible knew it was doing so. KJV, ASV and Geneva all print ‘be sure that thou eat not the blood’; NIV ‘be sure you do not eat the blood’. The Geneva translators then added a marginal note to their own verse, which reads, in full: ‘Hebrew, be strong or constant.’ They rendered it one way in the text and told the reader the other way in the margin — and their margin is right.

★ The versions that keep the strength are the ones this project’s standing rule predicts. NWT 1984 has ‘be firmly resolved’; the RV 1909 has «te esfuerces», from esfuerzo, effort, and the RV60 «te mantengas firme». Counted across both shelves the split is seven to six — seven turn it into caution, six keep a word of strength or resolve — and it does not run by age. It runs by family: the NWT/TNM line and the Reina-Valera line keep a word of strength or resolve, while the Geneva–King James–ASV–NIV line, and the NVI with it, all say ‘be sure’.

★★ The word for what is in the blood is the same word that has meant ‘appetite’ four times already in this chapter. Nefesh is the craving at v15, v20 (twice) and v21 — ‘as much as your soul desires’ — and here it is what the blood IS. English cannot hold both in one word without absurdity, so this translation prints ‘soul’ for the appetite and ‘life’ for the blood, and says so here rather than pretending the Hebrew made a distinction it did not. The verse is built on the collision: the thing that wants the meat and the thing that must not be eaten with it are named identically.

⚠ Deuteronomy states the blood-life relation differently from Leviticus, and the difference is the direction. Leviticus 17:11, already on these pages, reads ‘the life of the flesh is IN the blood’; 17:14 makes them equal with the LIFE as subject, ‘the life of all flesh is its blood’. Deuteronomy 12:23 puts the BLOOD in the subject position — ki ha-dam hu ha-nafesh, ‘for the blood, it is the life.’ Three statements of one rule, no two of them phrased alike.

★ Four editions, one direction, both languages. At this verse the NWT 1984’s ‘the blood is the SOUL’ becomes ‘the blood is the life’ in the 2013 revision; the TNM 1987’s «la sangre es el alma» becomes «la sangre es la vida» in 2019; and the Reina-Valera makes the same move on its own timetable, «el alma» in 1909 to «la vida» in 1960. Three revision pairs across two languages, all moving from soul to life at the same verse. The Douay, translating the Vulgate, keeps a fourth position entirely: ‘the blood is FOR the soul’.

Verses 25 and 28 · Right in the eyes of JEHOVAH — the answer to verse 8, and the formula the book of Kings will judge every king by

★ The chapter answers its own diagnosis by moving the eyes. V8: each man does what is right in HIS OWN eyes. V25: ‘when you do what is right in the eyes of JEHOVAH’. V28 repeats it and adds a word — ‘what is GOOD and right in the eyes of Jehovah your God’. Same noun, same idiom, different owner, and the two halves of the chapter are stitched together by it.

★★ Only four of the thirteen versions fetched for this chapter keep the eyes at BOTH ends, and the way the other nine fail splits neatly in two. The note at v8 counted who keeps the eyes in the diagnosis; this is the other half of the same count, and putting them together is what shows the damage. Keeping the link whole: ASV, NWT 1984, the NWT’s 2013 revision, and the TNM 1987. That is the shelf behaving exactly as this project’s standing rule predicts it will — the two most literal witnesses in English, and the Spanish member of the same family.

⚠ The two failures are opposite, and the older one is the surprise. KJV and Geneva keep the eyes at v8 — and then lose them here, printing ‘right in the SIGHT’ at both v25 and v28. So the King James reader is given the diagnosis in the chapter’s own idiom and then handed the cure in a different one. Everyone else fails at the near end instead: NIV, the two Reina-Valeras, the NVI and the TNM 2019 all have the eyes here at v25 and dropped them back at v8. The Douay alone loses both ends, and loses ‘right’ with them — ‘that which is pleasing in the sight’, which is a different claim about what God wants.

★ Ha-yashar be-einei YHVH occurs in twenty-three verses in the Hebrew Bible, and the shape of that list is the point. Searched consonantally: four are in Deuteronomy (12:25, 12:28, 13:19, 21:9) and every one of the other nineteen is in Kings or Chronicles — ‘and he did what was right in the eyes of Jehovah’, said of David, Asa, Jehoshaphat, Jehoash, Amaziah, Uzziah, Jotham, Hezekiah and Josiah, and denied of Ahaz. The sentence Deuteronomy coins here is the sentence a later book will use to grade every king in the country, and 2 Chronicles 14:1 even carries this chapter’s longer v28 form, ‘the good and the right’.

⚠ That is a genuine literary connection and it is not, by itself, an argument about dates or authorship. It is worth naming plainly what one reading makes of it, since a reader will meet it elsewhere: 2 Kings 22:2 uses the formula of Josiah, the king whose reform closed the local sanctuaries, and a well-known line of scholarship reads this chapter and that reign as connected. Others read the influence the other way, or as a shared idiom of the same school. The formula is the evidence; the direction is the argument, and this translation reports the first and does not adjudicate the second.

Verses 26–27 · What still has to travel — and the blood that is poured on an altar rather than on the ground

After the concession, the balance sheet. Ordinary meat stays home; holy things and vows still go to the place (v26), and there the blood is treated in the exact opposite way from v16 and v24: not poured on the earth like water but poured on the altar (v27). The same fluid, two disposals, and which one applies depends entirely on whether the animal was an offering. That is the chapter’s whole logic in one pair of verses.

‘The flesh you may eat’ closes v27 — even at the altar, the worshipper eats. The burnt-offering goes up whole; the zevach, the sacrifice proper, is a meal, which is why the chapter can keep saying ‘you shall eat there and rejoice’.

Verses 29–31 · Do not ask how they did it — the verb that opened the chapter, now forbidden, and the practice named without euphemism

★ The chapter closes on the verb it opened with. V5: tidreshu, seek out his dwelling. V30: pen tidrosh le-eloheihem, lest you inquire after their gods. Darash is the ordinary verb for going to a god with a question, and this chapter both commands it and forbids it — the difference is only the object.

‘Snared’ renders tinnaqesh, a passive from a root for trapping. Searched across the Hebrew Bible, these consonants occur in two verses: here, and 1 Samuel 28:9, where the medium at En-dor asks the disguised Saul why he is ‘snaring’ her life — a different stem of the same root, which is the kind of thing a consonantal search returns and a reader has to check rather than tally — a verse about a king consulting exactly the sort of practitioner this passage is warning against, though the connection is one of vocabulary and not of quotation.

⚠ The question the verse puts in Israel’s mouth is a question about method, not about loyalty: ‘How did these nations serve their gods? Let me do the same.’ What is being forbidden is not switching gods but importing a technique — which is why v31 answers it with a practice rather than with a doctrine.

V31 names child sacrifice plainly and this translation does not soften it: ‘they even burn their sons and their daughters in the fire to their gods.’ The verse states it as the reason the imitation is forbidden. Whether the practice was as widespread as the polemic implies is genuinely disputed among historians — the biblical texts, the classical reports about Carthage, and the excavated tophet cemeteries are read very differently by different scholars — and this note reports that dispute rather than settling it. What is not in dispute is what the verse says, and it is the last word of the chapter.

⚠ A versification note. This chapter runs to 31 verses in the Masoretic text, and this translation follows that numbering. Every English and Spanish version has 32 — checked against all thirteen fetched witnesses on both shelves, which agree on 32 without exception. Their 12:32 is the Hebrew’s 13:1: ‘All this word which I command you, that you shall keep to do; you shall not add to it nor take from it.’ So a reader cross-checking any other Bible will find one more verse here than this page has, and will find it at the head of the next chapter instead. ★ It is worth knowing what that extra verse is, because it repeats Deuteronomy 4:2, already on these pages — do not add, do not take away — and where a reader finds it depends entirely on which numbering their Bible inherited.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. ⚠ This chapter runs to 31 verses in the Hebrew and 32 in every English and Spanish Bible — checked against all thirteen fetched witnesses on both shelves, which agree on 32 without exception. This translation follows the Masoretic numbering, so the familiar ‘do not add, do not take away’ that other Bibles print as 12:32 is the Hebrew’s 13:1 and will appear at the head of the next chapter. Nothing is missing; the division falls in a different place. Three renderings depart from most of the shelf and are argued in their notes rather than asserted here: ‘be strong’ for chazaq at v23, where the Geneva–King James–ASV–NIV line all read ‘be sure’ and Geneva’s own margin says the Hebrew means otherwise (n12-23); ‘gazelle’ for tzvi at vv15 and 22, against the roebuck of the three oldest English versions and the 1909 Reina-Valera’s «corzo» (n12-15); and ‘contribution’ for terumah at vv6, 11 and 17, keeping this project’s own rendering fixed at Leviticus 7 rather than the KJV and ASV’s ‘heave offering’. The demolition list at v3 keeps Deuteronomy 7:5’s established standing stones, Asherahs and hew down deliberately, so that the one thing that differs between the two lists — which verb goes with which object — is visible rather than buried under new vocabulary (n12-2). Adamah is kept as ground and eretz as land throughout, and nefesh is printed as soul where it means appetite (vv15, 20, 21) and as life where it names what is in the blood (v23) — one Hebrew word, two English ones, with the collision explained at n12-23 rather than smoothed away.

⚠ Two Masoretic paragraph breaks fall in this chapter, both {ס}, closed breaks, after v19 and after v28 — checked verse by verse rather than assumed. They divide it 1–19, 20–28, 29–31. Worth noticing that neither of them falls at the chapter’s loudest internal seam, the switch from plural to singular address between v12 and v13: the scribal tradition marked this chapter’s units and did not mark that one.

Echoes paid. Deuteronomy 7:5’s four-object demolition list, repeated here with two of its four verbs exchanged between their objects (n12-2). Deuteronomy 11:4’s abad, used of Jehovah destroying Egypt’s army, handed to Israel as the thing IT is to do to the shrines — and then turned on a name (n12-2). Deuteronomy 10:9’s landless Levi, paid off three times over in this chapter’s insistence that he eats at your table (n12-19). Leviticus 17:3–5’s requirement that every domestic slaughter come to the tent, which this chapter releases and gives its own reason for releasing (n12-15), and 17:11–14’s two statements of the blood-and-life rule, to which this chapter adds a third, phrased differently again (n12-23). Deuteronomy 6:9, the letter-for-letter quotation chapter 11 found, standing behind the observation that this book’s repetitions are usually exact (n12-2). And Deuteronomy 4:2, whose ‘do not add, do not take from it’ returns as the verse this chapter loses to the next one by an accident of numbering (n12-29).

Echoes planted. Ha-yashar be-einei YHVH, coined at vv25 and 28, to be paid whenever this project reaches Kings or Chronicles, where nineteen verses use it to grade a king (n12-25). Rak chazaq, whose only two other occurrences are the charge to Joshua at Joshua 1:7 and 1:18, already on these pages and now readable in both directions (n12-23). The place that is chosen six times and named none, waiting on every later chapter that says ‘the place’ (n12-5). And darash, commanded at v5 and forbidden at v30, which the next chapter will take up again.

Patterns worth carrying forward. ★★ The grammatical-number question, open since Deuteronomy 9, gets its cleanest test here, and the hypothesis Deuteronomy 11 left standing does not survive it. Chapter 9 found the number switching mid-verse and asked what governed it; chapter 10 tested content-KIND and reported honestly that it failed; chapter 11 found the seam explicable where a source was visible, and offered one surviving guess — that stray singulars arrive where the text narrows to one household’s property — explicitly as a hypothesis, and asked that it be watched in this chapter. It is refuted, by a minimal pair. Verse 12 and verse 18 address the same six-member household — you, your sons, your daughters, your male and female slaves, and the Levite in your gates — in the same order, with the same Levite clause and the same ‘rejoice before Jehovah your God’, one wholly plural and one wholly singular; and v7 is a second plural twin of v18’s closing clause. The household is what BOTH are about, so the household cannot be what selects the number. That hypothesis can now be closed alongside chapter 10’s.

★ What replaces it is the reading chapter 11 reached from the other side, and this chapter supplies the evidence without needing an outside source. Counted form by form, Deuteronomy 12 falls into two blocks — plural through v12, singular from v13 — against the twelve Deuteronomy 11 counted in itself. (Those are the only two chapters on these pages whose counts have been published, so this compares two measured numbers rather than claiming anything about the rest of the book.) And it earns that shape by stating its law twice: vv2–12 command the destruction of the local shrines and the bringing of everything to the chosen place, and vv13–19 command it again, in the other number, and then add the concession. The number tracks the block; the block is a unit of composition. Said with its limits, as chapter 11 said its own: this describes what the text does and makes no claim about who wrote which half or when.

★ Five verses switch inside themselves, and the fifth is the one that matters. V1, v5, v7 and v9 all run plural and close on a singular clause. V16 runs the other way — and it is the single plural sentence in the whole singular half, and it is the blood prohibition, whose second clause reappears word for word at v24 entirely in the singular. One prohibition, twice in one chapter, once to a nation and once to a man.

★ The 1984/1987-versus-2013/2019 split produced four more instances here, one of them the clearest yet. At v23 the NWT 1984’s ‘the blood is the SOUL’ becomes ‘the life’ in the 2013, and the TNM 1987’s «el alma» becomes «la vida» in 2019 — the same move in both languages, and the Reina-Valera made it independently between 1909 and 1960. At v8 the TNM 2019 drops the ‘eyes’ the 1987 had kept, breaking the link to v25 that the 1987 preserves. At v5 the NWT 1984’s small-capital YOU, which marks the plural, is gone from the 2013, which loses the mid-verse switch with it. And at v17 the 1984’s ‘inside your gates’ becomes ‘within your cities’ in both 2013 and 2019, trading the Hebrew’s gate for its meaning. Running total across the seven chapters that have counted this deliberately: thirty-three places — Deuteronomy 6’s four, 7’s seven, 8’s four, 9’s five, 10’s four, 11’s five, and this chapter’s four.

★ The Reina-Valera gap moves twice, and both times it is a correction rather than a drift. Chapter 10 found it moving four times, chapter 11 once. Here the 1909’s «corzo» becomes «gacela» in 1960 at v15 — the same zoological fix the ASV had made to the King James’s roebuck two centuries earlier — and the 1909’s «el alma» becomes «la vida» at v23. Elsewhere the two are word for word, including at v8, where both lose the eyes.

★ And a shelf pattern this project has not seen before: a version that breaks a link from the far end. The chapter answers v8’s ‘right in his own eyes’ with v25’s ‘right in the eyes of Jehovah’, and only four of thirteen witnesses keep the eyes at both ends — the ASV, the NWT in both its editions, and the TNM 1987. The KJV and Geneva keep them in the diagnosis and lose them in the cure (‘in the SIGHT’); the NIV and the whole modern Spanish shelf do the reverse. The two failures are mirror images, and between them they hide a connection that neither alone would — which is the argument for reading a verse against more than one generation of the same shelf.

Library. Three new dictionary terms in both languages — matzevah, the standing stone this chapter orders smashed and Jacob three times sets up; chazaq, the strength-verb of v23; and sha’ar, the gate that in Deuteronomy means a town. The parity gap therefore moves DOWN on this chapter rather than holding level. Next installment in this book: Deuteronomy 13 — which opens, in the Hebrew, with the verse most Bibles have just printed as the end of this one, and then turns to the hardest question the previous chapter raises. If worship is to happen in one place and no other, who decides? A prophet who works a genuine sign and then says ‘let us go after other gods’; a brother, a son, a wife ‘of your own bosom’; a whole town gone over. The answers are severe, the procedure is spelled out, and the chapter that told Israel not to ask how the nations served their gods now tells it what to do when the question comes from inside the family.
