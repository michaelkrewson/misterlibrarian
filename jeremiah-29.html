Jeremiah 29 — The Mister Translation

1
וְאֵלֶּה דִּבְרֵי הַסֵּפֶר, אֲשֶׁר שָׁלַח יִרְמְיָה הַנָּבִיא מִירוּשָׁלִָם--אֶל-יֶתֶר זִקְנֵי הַגּוֹלָה, וְאֶל-הַכֹּהֲנִים וְאֶל-הַנְּבִיאִים וְאֶל-כָּל-הָעָם, אֲשֶׁר הֶגְלָה נְבוּכַדְנֶאצַּר מִירוּשָׁלִַם, בָּבֶלָה.
And these are the words of the letter that Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem to the rest of the elders of the exile, and to the priests and to the prophets and to all the people whom Nebuchadnezzar had exiled from Jerusalem to Babylonnote
Y estas son las palabras de la carta que el profeta Jeremías envió desde Jerusalén al resto de los ancianos del destierro, y a los sacerdotes, y a los profetas, y a todo el pueblo que Nabucodonosor había deportado de Jerusalén a Babilonia,
2
אַחֲרֵי צֵאת יְכָנְיָה-הַמֶּלֶךְ וְהַגְּבִירָה וְהַסָּרִיסִים שָׂרֵי יְהוּדָה וִירוּשָׁלִַם, וְהֶחָרָשׁ וְהַמַּסְגֵּר--מִירוּשָׁלִָם.
— after Jeconiah the king and the queen mother and the court officials, the princes of Judah and Jerusalem, and the craftsmen and the smiths had gone out from Jerusalem —note
después de haber salido de Jerusalén el rey Jeconías, y la reina madre, y los funcionarios de la corte, los príncipes de Judá y de Jerusalén, y los artesanos y los herreros;
3
בְּיַד אֶלְעָשָׂה בֶן-שָׁפָן, וּגְמַרְיָה בֶּן-חִלְקִיָּה:  אֲשֶׁר שָׁלַח צִדְקִיָּה מֶלֶךְ-יְהוּדָה, אֶל-נְבוּכַדְנֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל--בָּבֶלָה לֵאמֹר.
by the hand of Elasah son of Shaphan and Gemariah son of Hilkiah, whom Zedekiah king of Judah sent to Babylon, to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, saying:note
por mano de Elasa hijo de Safán y de Guemarías hijo de Hilcías, a quienes Sedequías rey de Judá envió a Babilonia, a Nabucodonosor rey de Babilonia. Decía:
4
כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל:  לְכָל-הַגּוֹלָה--אֲשֶׁר-הִגְלֵיתִי מִירוּשָׁלִַם, בָּבֶלָה.
Thus said Jehovah of armies, the God of Israel, to all the exile whom I have exiled from Jerusalem to Babylon:note
Así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de Israel, a todo el destierro que hice deportar de Jerusalén a Babilonia:
5
בְּנוּ בָתִּים, וְשֵׁבוּ; וְנִטְעוּ גַנּוֹת, וְאִכְלוּ אֶת-פִּרְיָן.
Build houses and live in them; plant gardens and eat their fruit.note
Edifiquen casas y habiten en ellas; planten huertos y coman su fruto.
6
קְחוּ נָשִׁים, וְהוֹלִידוּ בָּנִים וּבָנוֹת, וּקְחוּ לִבְנֵיכֶם נָשִׁים וְאֶת-בְּנוֹתֵיכֶם תְּנוּ לַאֲנָשִׁים, וְתֵלַדְנָה בָּנִים וּבָנוֹת; וּרְבוּ-שָׁם, וְאַל-תִּמְעָטוּ.
Take wives and father sons and daughters; and take wives for your sons and give your daughters to husbands, and let them bear sons and daughters. Multiply there and do not become few.note
Tomen esposas y engendren hijos e hijas; y tomen esposas para sus hijos, y den sus hijas a maridos, para que den a luz hijos e hijas; y multípliquense allí y no disminuyan.
7
וְדִרְשׁוּ אֶת-שְׁלוֹם הָעִיר, אֲשֶׁר הִגְלֵיתִי אֶתְכֶם שָׁמָּה, וְהִתְפַּלְלוּ בַעֲדָהּ, אֶל-יְהוָה:  כִּי בִשְׁלוֹמָהּ, יִהְיֶה לָכֶם שָׁלוֹם.
And seek the shalom of the city to which I have exiled you, and pray for it to Jehovah; for in its shalom you will have shalom.note
Y busquen la shalom de la ciudad a la cual los hice deportar, y oren por ella a Jehová, porque en la shalom de ella tendrán ustedes shalom.
cross-refs⤷ 29:11
8
כִּי כֹה אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אַל-יַשִּׁיאוּ לָכֶם נְבִיאֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר-בְּקִרְבְּכֶם, וְקֹסְמֵיכֶם; וְאַל-תִּשְׁמְעוּ, אֶל-חֲלֹמֹתֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם, מַחְלְמִים.
For thus said Jehovah of armies, the God of Israel: Do not let your prophets who are among you, or your diviners, deceive you; and do not listen to your dreams that you are dreaming.note
Porque así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de Israel: No los engañen sus profetas que están en medio de ustedes, ni sus adivinos; ni escuchen los sueños que ustedes se hacen soñar.
9
כִּי בְשֶׁקֶר, הֵם נִבְּאִים לָכֶם בִּשְׁמִי:  לֹא שְׁלַחְתִּים, נְאֻם-יְהוָה.  {ס}
For it is falsehood that they are prophesying to you in my name. I have not sent them — declares Jehovah.note
Porque con falsedad les están profetizando en mi nombre; no los he enviado — declaración de Jehová.
10
כִּי-כֹה, אָמַר יְהוָה, כִּי לְפִי מְלֹאת לְבָבֶל שִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה, אֶפְקֹד אֶתְכֶם; וַהֲקִמֹתִי עֲלֵיכֶם, אֶת-דְּבָרִי הַטּוֹב, לְהָשִׁיב אֶתְכֶם, אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה.
For thus said Jehovah: When seventy years are completed for Babylon I will attend to you, and I will make good toward you my good word, to bring you back to this place.note
Porque así ha dicho Jehová: Cuando se hayan cumplido para Babilonia setenta años, los visitaré y confirmaré sobre ustedes mi buena palabra, haciéndolos volver a este lugar.
cross-refs⤷ 25:11⤷ Exod 20:4⤷ 30:20
11
כִּי אָנֹכִי יָדַעְתִּי אֶת-הַמַּחֲשָׁבֹת, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי חֹשֵׁב עֲלֵיכֶם--נְאֻם-יְהוָה:  מַחְשְׁבוֹת שָׁלוֹם וְלֹא לְרָעָה, לָתֵת לָכֶם אַחֲרִית וְתִקְוָה.
For I myself know the plans that I am planning for you — declares Jehovah — plans of shalom and not for harm, to give you an outcome and a hope.note
Porque yo sé los planes que estoy pensando acerca de ustedes — declaración de Jehová —, planes de shalom y no de mal, para darles un desenlace y una esperanza.
cross-refs⤷ 29:7⤷ 31:16
12
וּקְרָאתֶם אֹתִי וַהֲלַכְתֶּם, וְהִתְפַּלַּלְתֶּם אֵלָי; וְשָׁמַעְתִּי, אֲלֵיכֶם.
And you will call me and come and pray to me, and I will listen to you.note
Y me invocarán, y vendrán y orarán a mí, y yo los escucharé.
13
וּבִקַּשְׁתֶּם אֹתִי, וּמְצָאתֶם:  כִּי תִדְרְשֻׁנִי, בְּכָל-לְבַבְכֶם.
And you will seek me and find me, when you search for me with all your heart.note
Y me buscarán y me hallarán, porque me habrán buscado con todo su corazón.
14
וְנִמְצֵאתִי לָכֶם, נְאֻם-יְהוָה, וְשַׁבְתִּי אֶת-שביתכם
שְׁבוּתְכֶם
וְקִבַּצְתִּי אֶתְכֶם מִכָּל-הַגּוֹיִם וּמִכָּל-הַמְּקוֹמוֹת אֲשֶׁר הִדַּחְתִּי אֶתְכֶם שָׁם, נְאֻם-יְהוָה; וַהֲשִׁבֹתִי אֶתְכֶם--אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם, אֲשֶׁר-הִגְלֵיתִי אֶתְכֶם מִשָּׁם.
And I will be found by you — declares Jehovah — and I will turn back your turning, and gather you from all the nations and from all the places where I have driven you, declares Jehovah; and I will bring you back to the place from which I exiled you.note
Y me dejaré hallar de ustedes — declaración de Jehová —, y haré volver su vuelta, y los recogeré de entre todas las naciones y de todos los lugares adonde los arrojé — declaración de Jehová —, y los haré volver al lugar de donde los hice deportar.
15
כִּי, אֲמַרְתֶּם:  הֵקִים לָנוּ יְהוָה נְבִאִים, בָּבֶלָה.  {ס}
Because you have said, “Jehovah has raised up prophets for us in Babylon” —note
Porque han dicho: Jehová nos ha levantado profetas en Babilonia.
16
כִּי-כֹה אָמַר יְהוָה, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ הַיּוֹשֵׁב אֶל-כִּסֵּא דָוִד, וְאֶל-כָּל-הָעָם, הַיּוֹשֵׁב בָּעִיר הַזֹּאת:  אֲחֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-יָצְאוּ אִתְּכֶם בַּגּוֹלָה.
thus said Jehovah concerning the king who sits on the throne of David, and concerning all the people who dwell in this city, your brothers who did not go out with you into exile:note
Porque así ha dicho Jehová acerca del rey que se sienta en el trono de David, y acerca de todo el pueblo que habita en esta ciudad — los hermanos de ustedes que no salieron con ustedes al destierro —,
17
כֹּה אָמַר, יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, הִנְנִי מְשַׁלֵּחַ בָּם, אֶת-הַחֶרֶב אֶת-הָרָעָב וְאֶת-הַדָּבֶר; וְנָתַתִּי אוֹתָם, כַּתְּאֵנִים הַשֹּׁעָרִים, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-תֵאָכַלְנָה, מֵרֹעַ.
Thus said Jehovah of armies: Look, I am sending against them the sword, the famine and the plague, and I will make them like the split-open figs that are too bad to be eaten.note
así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos: Mira, yo envío contra ellos la espada, el hambre y la peste; y los pondré como higos podridos que de tan malos no se pueden comer.
cross-refs⤷ 24:2
18
וְרָדַפְתִּי, אַחֲרֵיהֶם, בַּחֶרֶב, בָּרָעָב וּבַדָּבֶר; וּנְתַתִּים לזועה
לְזַעֲוָה
לְכֹל מַמְלְכוֹת הָאָרֶץ, לְאָלָה וּלְשַׁמָּה וְלִשְׁרֵקָה וּלְחֶרְפָּה, בְּכָל-הַגּוֹיִם, אֲשֶׁר-הִדַּחְתִּים שָׁם.
And I will pursue them with the sword, with the famine and with the plague, and I will make them a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth — a curse and a desolation and a hissing and a reproach among all the nations where I have driven them,note
Y los perseguiré con espada, con hambre y con peste; y los pondré por espanto para todos los reinos de la tierra, por maldición y por asombro y por silbido y por afrenta entre todas las naciones adonde los habré arrojado,
19
תַּחַת אֲשֶׁר-לֹא-שָׁמְעוּ אֶל-דְּבָרַי, נְאֻם-יְהוָה--אֲשֶׁר שָׁלַחְתִּי אֲלֵיהֶם אֶת-עֲבָדַי הַנְּבִאִים, הַשְׁכֵּם וְשָׁלֹחַ, וְלֹא שְׁמַעְתֶּם, נְאֻם-יְהוָה.
because they did not listen to my words — declares Jehovah — which I sent to them by my servants the prophets, sending early and sending; and you did not listen, declares Jehovah.note
por cuanto no escucharon mis palabras — declaración de Jehová —, que les envié por mis siervos los profetas, madrugando y enviando; pero no escucharon — declaración de Jehová.
20
וְאַתֶּם, שִׁמְעוּ דְבַר-יְהוָה:  כָּל-הַגּוֹלָה--אֲשֶׁר-שִׁלַּחְתִּי מִירוּשָׁלִַם, בָּבֶלָה.  {ס}
And you, hear the word of Jehovah, all you exiles whom I have sent from Jerusalem to Babylon.note
Ustedes, pues, escuchen la palabra de Jehová, todo el destierro que envié de Jerusalén a Babilonia.
21
כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶל-אַחְאָב בֶּן-קוֹלָיָה וְאֶל-צִדְקִיָּהוּ בֶן-מַעֲשֵׂיָה, הַנִּבְּאִים לָכֶם בִּשְׁמִי, שָׁקֶר:  הִנְנִי נֹתֵן אֹתָם, בְּיַד נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ-בָּבֶל, וְהִכָּם, לְעֵינֵיכֶם.
Thus said Jehovah of armies, the God of Israel, concerning Ahab son of Kolaiah and concerning Zedekiah son of Maaseiah, who are prophesying falsehood to you in my name: Look, I am giving them into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, and he will strike them down before your eyes.note
Así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de Israel, acerca de Acab hijo de Colaías y acerca de Sedequías hijo de Maasías, que les profetizan a ustedes falsedad en mi nombre: Mira, yo los entrego en mano de Nabucodonosor rey de Babilonia, y los herirá ante los ojos de ustedes.
22
וְלֻקַּח מֵהֶם, קְלָלָה, לְכֹל גָּלוּת יְהוּדָה, אֲשֶׁר בְּבָבֶל לֵאמֹר:  יְשִׂמְךָ יְהוָה כְּצִדְקִיָּהוּ וּכְאֶחָב, אֲשֶׁר-קָלָם מֶלֶךְ-בָּבֶל בָּאֵשׁ.
And from them a curse will be taken up by all the exiles of Judah who are in Babylon, saying, “May Jehovah make you like Zedekiah and like Ahab, whom the king of Babylon roasted in the fire” —note
Y de ellos se tomará una maldición para todo el destierro de Judá que está en Babilonia, diciendo: ¡Póngate Jehová como a Sedequías y como a Acab, a quienes el rey de Babilonia asó al fuego!,
23
יַעַן אֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ נְבָלָה בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיְנַאֲפוּ אֶת-נְשֵׁי רֵעֵיהֶם, וַיְדַבְּרוּ דָבָר בִּשְׁמִי שֶׁקֶר, אֲשֶׁר לוֹא צִוִּיתִם; וְאָנֹכִי הוידע
הַיּוֹדֵעַ
וָעֵד, נְאֻם-יְהוָה.  {ס}
because they did an outrage in Israel and committed adultery with their neighbours’ wives, and spoke a word in my name, falsely, which I did not command them. And I am the one who knows, and a witness — declares Jehovah.note
por cuanto hicieron una infamia en Israel, y cometieron adulterio con las mujeres de sus prójimos, y hablaron en mi nombre palabra de mentira que no les mandé; y yo lo sé y soy testigo — declaración de Jehová.
24
וְאֶל-שְׁמַעְיָהוּ הַנֶּחֱלָמִי, תֹּאמַר לֵאמֹר.
And to Shemaiah the Nehelamite you shall say:note
Y a Semaya el nehelamita hablarás, diciendo:
25
כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת, אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--לֵאמֹר:  יַעַן אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה שָׁלַחְתָּ בְשִׁמְכָה סְפָרִים, אֶל-כָּל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר בִּירוּשָׁלִַם, וְאֶל-צְפַנְיָה בֶן-מַעֲשֵׂיָה הַכֹּהֵן, וְאֶל כָּל-הַכֹּהֲנִים לֵאמֹר.
Thus said Jehovah of armies, the God of Israel: Because you yourself sent letters in your own name to all the people who are in Jerusalem, and to Zephaniah son of Maaseiah the priest, and to all the priests, saying,note
Así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de Israel: Por cuanto tú enviaste en tu nombre cartas a todo el pueblo que está en Jerusalén, y a Sofonías hijo de Maasías el sacerdote, y a todos los sacerdotes, diciendo:
26
יְהוָה נְתָנְךָ כֹהֵן, תַּחַת יְהוֹיָדָע הַכֹּהֵן, לִהְיוֹת פְּקִדִים בֵּית יְהוָה, לְכָל-אִישׁ מְשֻׁגָּע וּמִתְנַבֵּא; וְנָתַתָּה אֹתוֹ אֶל-הַמַּהְפֶּכֶת, וְאֶל-הַצִּינֹק.
“Jehovah has made you priest in place of Jehoiada the priest, to be overseers of the house of Jehovah for every madman who prophesies, and you shall put him in the stocks and in the collar.note
Jehová te ha puesto por sacerdote en lugar del sacerdote Joiadá, para que haya inspectores en la casa de Jehová sobre todo hombre loco que se hace profeta, y lo pongas en el cepo y en el collar;
27

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · this is a letter, and it went by diplomatic bag

The chapter is a piece of correspondence, and it names the postmen. Elasah son of Shaphan and Gemariah son of Hilkiah were carrying an official embassy from Zedekiah to Nebuchadnezzar; Jeremiah’s letter travelled with them. The Shaphan family turn up repeatedly around Jeremiah as protectors, which is presumably why he could get a letter to Babylon at all. The detail is the sort of thing a forger would not bother with.

Ha-golah — “the exile” — is a collective noun, not a place. It means the deported community itself, and it is used that way throughout the chapter (vv1, 4, 20, 31). This translation keeps it as a collective (“all the exile”) where English versions usually pluralise it to “the exiles,” because the letter is addressed to a body, not to a set of individuals — which turns out to matter a great deal by verse 11.

Verses 5–7 · ⚠ build houses in the country that deported you — and pray for it

The commands are relentlessly ordinary. Build. Live. Plant. Eat. Marry. Have children. Marry the children off. Have grandchildren. Every verb assumes a long stay, and the last one — “multiply there and do not become few” — is the language of the creation blessing and of the promise to Abraham, now issued to people in a prison camp. The instruction is not to endure Babylon. It is to live there.

⚠ And then verse 7 asks for something much harder than patience. Seek the shalom of the city… and pray for it. Not tolerate; not survive; seek — darash, the verb for actively pursuing something, the same verb used two verses from now for searching out God himself (v13). They are told to work for the flourishing of the empire that burned their temple, and to pray for it. The reason given is not moral but practical, and it is stated as a plain fact: “for in its shalom you will have shalom.” Their welfare and their captors’ are now one thing.

The shelf disagrees about how active the verb is. KJV has “seek the peace of the city”; ASV the same; NIV softens it to “seek the peace and prosperity,” adding a second noun the Hebrew does not have; and NWT keeps the bare “seek the peace of the city.” The Spanish RV 1909 is the most active of all — procurad, which is closer to “procure” or “bring about” than to “look for.” On darash that is arguably the tightest reading on either shelf.

⚠ Count the word, because the chapter is built on it. Shalom occurs four times in this chapter and three of them are in verse 7, about Babylon. The fourth is in verse 11. That is the single most important fact for reading the famous verse, and it is invisible in most English Bibles because they render the word differently in the two places — “peace” or “welfare” here, “prosper” there. NWT keeps “peace” in both and is the easiest place on the shelf to see the link; KJV has “peace” at v7 and “peace” at v11 as well, so it also preserves it; NIV splits them (“peace and prosperity”…“prosper you”) and the connection is gone.

Verses 8–9 · the prophets who were telling them what they wanted

The false prophets in Babylon were preaching a short exile. That is the whole quarrel of this chapter, and of chapters 27–28 before it: Hananiah had already announced two years (28:3, not yet on these pages). Jeremiah’s letter exists to say seventy. ⚠ Worth noticing which message is the comforting one. The prophets promising a quick return are the popular ones, and the man telling people to unpack is the one accused of disloyalty in verse 27.

Sheqer — falsehood, the Lie — is the word this library catalogued in Jeremiah 23, and it is doing the same work here: not merely an inaccurate forecast but a whole way of standing in the world with nothing under it.

Verses 10–11 · ⚠ the most-quoted promise on the internet, and what it is attached to

⚠ Read verse 10 before verse 11, because it is the same sentence. The promise of a future is conditioned on a number: seventy years. For adults reading that letter, seventy years is not a delay — it is the rest of their lives. The people commanded in v5 to build houses are being told, in as many words, that they will die in those houses. The good word is real and it is for their grandchildren. Any reading of v11 that detaches it from v10 has removed the cost from the promise.

⚠ Every “you” in verse 11 is PLURAL. Hebrew marks it unmistakably — aleikhem, lakhem, the 2nd-person masculine plural suffix, three times in one verse. The plans are for a people. English cannot show this, which is most of the reason the verse now circulates as a private reassurance printed on a graduation card. ⚠ The one English version that can show it does: NWT 1984 prints plural “you” in small capitals, so its v11 reads “the thoughts that I am thinking toward YOU… to give YOU a future and a hope.” Whatever one thinks of the typography, on this verse it is doing real work that no other English shelf-version attempts.

⚠ And the promise-word is the Babylon-word. Machshevot shalom — “plans of shalom” — is the same noun God used three times four verses earlier of the city they were deported to. He is not promising them an exit; he is telling them that the thing he intends for them is the thing he told them to go and seek in Babylon. “Plans to prosper you” is not wrong, exactly, but it points the sentence at the reader’s circumstances, where the Hebrew points it at a word with a history four verses long.

The last two nouns are less cosy than they sound. Acharit is the after-part — the outcome, the latter end, what a thing finally comes to (and often, in the prophets, one’s posterity). Tikvah is hope, and the concrete noun behind it is a cord — the same word as the scarlet rope Rahab let down her wall. So the pair is nearer “an outcome, and something to hold on to” than it is to optimism. Rendered here “an outcome and a hope”; the traditional “a future and a hope” is fair, and KJV’s “an expected end” is a real attempt at acharit that most modern versions have dropped.

⚠ None of this makes the verse smaller; it makes it harder. It is a promise of a good outcome, in writing, from God, to a defeated people — and it costs seventy years, applies to a nation, and routes their welfare through the flourishing of the empire that took them. The library’s job here is not to take the verse away from anyone. It is to print what it is attached to.

Verses 12–14 · seeking, finding, and a phrase that turns twice

The same verb comes back. Darash — “seek” — was used at v7 of Babylon’s welfare and is used at v13 of God: “when you search for me with all your heart.” Seeking the city’s good and seeking God are given the same verb six verses apart, which is either the chapter’s point or a very economical coincidence.

⚠ And verse 14 turns on shuv twice in one breath: ve-shavti et shevutkhem — literally “I will turn back your turning.” The idiom means restoring someone’s fortunes, and the doubling is a figura etymologica the versions understandably flatten (“I will restore your fortunes”). ⚠ The Masoretes also flag the word itself: the consonantal text (ketiv) reads shevit and the reading tradition (qere) says shevut — one of those places where the scroll and the voice disagree by a letter and the manuscript prints both.

Every version flattens the doubling, and they are right to — but it is worth seeing what is lost. KJV “I will turn away your captivity” at least keeps one “turn”; NIV and NWT both give the sense (“bring you back from captivity” / “gather your captive ones”) and drop the figure entirely. This translation prints the doubling because shuv is a root this library has been tracking since the Psalms — and because the chapter has just spent seven verses on the question of when, if ever, anyone is turning back.

Verses 15–20 · the ones who stayed behind get the worse half

⚠ The chapter reverses the obvious assumption, and does it deliberately. The people still living in Jerusalem — who kept their homes, their city and their temple — are the ones handed sword, famine and plague; the deportees, who lost everything, are the ones with the seventy-year promise. Being spared the catastrophe is not the same as being on the right side of it.

The image is figs. “Like the split-open figs that are too bad to be eaten” is a direct callback to the vision of two baskets in Jeremiah 24 — good figs for the exiles, rotten figs for those who remained — which is already on these pages, and which is the chapter this book’s own running order was pointing at next.

Verses 21–23 · two prophets, and a curse that names how they died

⚠ This is in the same letter as verse 11, and it is worth not skipping. Ahab son of Kolaiah and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah are handed to Nebuchadnezzar and roasted in the fire — qalam ba-esh, the ordinary verb for parching grain. The exiles will use their names as a curse-formula. Burning alive is attested as a Babylonian punishment (it is the penalty in the Daniel 3 furnace story), and the chapter reports it without comment.

The charge is triple and the last item is the point. They committed adultery, they did an nevalah — an outrage, the word used of the worst crimes in Judges — and they spoke a word in my name, falsely. God’s own line about it is bare and slightly chilling: “And I am the one who knows, and a witness.”

Verses 24–32 · a letter answering a letter answering a letter

The chapter ends as correspondence about correspondence. Shemaiah, in Babylon, writes to the temple police in Jerusalem demanding that Jeremiah be put in the stocks — and quotes Jeremiah’s own letter back at him: “It will be long. Build houses and live in them.” That quotation (v28) is verse 5 of this chapter, cited as evidence for the prosecution. Then Zephaniah the priest reads Shemaiah’s letter aloud to Jeremiah, and Jeremiah writes a third letter about it. Three pieces of mail and a public reading, all preserved.

“Every madman who prophesies” is meshugga — and it is the official job description in Shemaiah’s letter, not an insult he invents. The temple had a post for restraining ecstatic prophets, with stocks and a neck-collar to do it, and Shemaiah is complaining that the post-holder is not doing his job. ⚠ Note what the accusation actually is: Jeremiah is not charged with being wrong. He is charged with not being silenced.

And the shelf splits on how rude to be. KJV renders meshugga “every man that is mad, and maketh himself a prophet”; ASV “every man that is mad”; NIV tidies it to “any maniac who acts like a prophet.” The word is genuinely blunt — it is the ordinary Hebrew for insane — and since it appears here inside an official job description rather than as name-calling, blunting it loses the fact that the temple had a formal category for this and a cupboard of restraints to go with it.

Patterns worth carrying forward.

⚠ The one thing to carry out of this chapter. Shalom appears four times here. Three are in verse 7, about the welfare of Babylon — seek it, pray for it, “for in its shalom you will have shalom.” The fourth is the famous verse 11, “plans of shalom.” The most-quoted promise on the internet is made with the word God had just spent four verses attaching to the empire that deported them, and it is addressed in the plural, to a people, with a seventy-year price tag stated in the previous sentence. Every version that renders the word one way at v7 and another at v11 breaks the link without mistranslating anything.

Renderings chosen. Shalom transliterated at vv7 and 11 so the connection is visible on the page. “All the exile” for ha-golah, a collective rather than a set of individuals. “An outcome and a hope” for acharit ve-tikvah — acharit is the after-part, what a thing comes to; tikvah is hope and, concretely, a cord. “I will turn back your turning” at v14, keeping a doubling every version flattens. And “madman” at v26, because the word is blunt and it is sitting inside a job description.

Echoes paid. Sheqer (v9), catalogued at Jeremiah 23 and doing the same work. Shuv (v14), the root this library has been tracking since Psalm 23. And darash (vv7, 13), used of seeking Babylon’s good and of searching for God within six verses.

Echoes planted. The seventy years (v10), which Daniel 9 will read as a text to be interpreted rather than merely waited out. The bad figs of vv17–18, which are the vision of Jeremiah 24, still untranslated here. And tikvah — the cord.

⚠ Left open, and named rather than smoothed. The chapter that contains verse 11 also contains two prophets roasted alive in a fire (vv21–23) and a promise of sword, famine and plague for the people who stayed in Jerusalem (v17). The library prints them in the same letter because they are in the same letter.

⚠ A note on order: chapter 29 was translated ahead of the book’s own sequence — a deliberate jump to the most-searched chapter in the book, not a lost stretch. The two-baskets vision that verses 17–18 here quote stands at Jeremiah 24, already on these pages.
