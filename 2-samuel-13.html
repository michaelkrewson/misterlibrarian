Amnon son of David loves Tamar, Absalom’s beautiful sister; on Jonadab’s advice he feigns illness and asks that she make two heart-cakes before him, and David sends her. He sends everyone out, seizes her, and violates her despite her plea; then he hates her more than he had loved her and has her put out and the door bolted. She puts ashes on her head, tears her long ornamented tunic and goes away crying out; Absalom tells her to be silent and she stays desolate in his house. David is very angry; Absalom says nothing to Amnon for two full years. At the shearing at Baal-hazor Absalom persuades David to send Amnon and the king’s sons, has his servants kill Amnon when his heart is merry with wine, and the rest flee on their mules. The rumor reaches David that all the sons are dead; Jonadab says only Amnon is dead. Absalom flees to Talmai, king of Geshur, and David mourns, and then longs to go out to him.
After 2 Samuel 12; the text gives two full years between the violation of Tamar and the shearing, and three years for Absalom in Geshur.

1
וַיְהִי אַחֲרֵי-כֵן, וּלְאַבְשָׁלוֹם בֶּן-דָּוִד אָחוֹת יָפָה--וּשְׁמָהּ תָּמָר; וַיֶּאֱהָבֶהָ, אַמְנוֹן בֶּן-דָּוִד.
And it happened after this that Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister, whose name was Tamar; and Amnon son of David loved her.note
Y sucedió después de esto que Absalón hijo de David tenía una hermana hermosa, que se llamaba Tamar; y Amnón hijo de David la amó.
2
וַיֵּצֶר לְאַמְנוֹן לְהִתְחַלּוֹת, בַּעֲבוּר תָּמָר אֲחֹתוֹ--כִּי בְתוּלָה, הִיא; וַיִּפָּלֵא בְּעֵינֵי אַמְנוֹן, לַעֲשׂוֹת לָהּ מְאוּמָה.
And Amnon was so distressed that he made himself ill because of Tamar his sister; for she was a virgin, and it was hard in the eyes of Amnon to do anything to her.note
Y estaba Amnón tan angustiado que se enfermó por Tamar su hermana; porque ella era virgen, y a los ojos de Amnón era difícil hacerle cosa alguna.
3
וּלְאַמְנוֹן רֵעַ, וּשְׁמוֹ יוֹנָדָב, בֶּן-שִׁמְעָה, אֲחִי דָוִד; וְיוֹנָדָב, אִישׁ חָכָם מְאֹד.
And Amnon had a friend, whose name was Jonadab, son of Shimeah, David’s brother; and Jonadab was a very wise man.note
Y Amnón tenía un amigo que se llamaba Jonadab, hijo de Simea, hermano de David; y Jonadab era un hombre muy sabio.
4
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, מַדּוּעַ אַתָּה כָּכָה דַּל בֶּן-הַמֶּלֶךְ בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר--הֲלוֹא, תַּגִּיד לִי; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, אַמְנוֹן, אֶת-תָּמָר אֲחוֹת אַבְשָׁלֹם אָחִי, אֲנִי אֹהֵב.
And he said to him: ‘Why are you, the king’s son, so haggard from morning to morning? Will you not tell me?’ And Amnon said to him: ‘I love Tamar, the sister of Absalom my brother.’note
Y le dijo: «¿Por qué tú, hijo del rey, estás así de demacrado de mañana en mañana? ¿No me lo contarás?» Y Amnón le dijo: «Amo a Tamar, la hermana de Absalón mi hermano».
5
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ יְהוֹנָדָב, שְׁכַב עַל-מִשְׁכָּבְךָ וְהִתְחָל; וּבָא אָבִיךָ לִרְאוֹתֶךָ, וְאָמַרְתָּ אֵלָיו תָּבֹא נָא תָמָר אֲחוֹתִי וְתַבְרֵנִי לֶחֶם וְעָשְׂתָה לְעֵינַי אֶת-הַבִּרְיָה, לְמַעַן אֲשֶׁר אֶרְאֶה, וְאָכַלְתִּי מִיָּדָהּ.
And Jonadab said to him: ‘Lie down on your bed, and make yourself ill; and when your father comes to see you, say to him: “Please let my sister Tamar come, and give me bread to eat, and make the food in my sight, so that I may see it, and eat from her hand.”’note
Y Jonadab le dijo: «Acuéstate en tu cama, y hazte el enfermo; y cuando tu padre venga a verte, dile: “Por favor, que venga mi hermana Tamar, y me dé de comer pan, y haga la comida ante mis ojos, para que yo la vea y coma de su mano”».
6
וַיִּשְׁכַּב אַמְנוֹן, וַיִּתְחָל; וַיָּבֹא הַמֶּלֶךְ לִרְאוֹתוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר אַמְנוֹן אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ תָּבוֹא-נָא תָּמָר אֲחֹתִי וּתְלַבֵּב לְעֵינַי שְׁתֵּי לְבִבוֹת, וְאֶבְרֶה, מִיָּדָהּ.
And Amnon lay down, and made himself ill; and the king came to see him; and Amnon said to the king: ‘Please let Tamar my sister come, and make two heart-cakes in my sight, that I may eat from her hand.’note
Y se acostó Amnón, y se hizo el enfermo; y vino el rey a verlo; y Amnón dijo al rey: «Por favor, que venga Tamar mi hermana, y haga ante mis ojos dos tortas de corazón, para que yo coma de su mano».
7
וַיִּשְׁלַח דָּוִד אֶל-תָּמָר, הַבַּיְתָה לֵאמֹר:  לְכִי נָא, בֵּית אַמְנוֹן אָחִיךְ, וַעֲשִׂי-לוֹ, הַבִּרְיָה.
And David sent to Tamar at home, saying: ‘Go to the house of Amnon your brother, and make him food.’note
Y David envió a Tamar a casa, diciendo: «Ve a la casa de Amnón tu hermano, y hazle la comida».
8
וַתֵּלֶךְ תָּמָר, בֵּית אַמְנוֹן אָחִיהָ--וְהוּא שֹׁכֵב; וַתִּקַּח אֶת-הַבָּצֵק ותלוש וַתָּלָשׁ וַתְּלַבֵּב לְעֵינָיו, וַתְּבַשֵּׁל אֶת-הַלְּבִבוֹת.
And Tamar went to the house of Amnon her brother; and he was lying down. And she took dough, and kneaded it, and made the heart-cakes in his sight, and cooked them.note
Y fue Tamar a la casa de Amnón su hermano; y él estaba acostado. Y ella tomó la masa, y la amasó, e hizo las tortas de corazón ante sus ojos, y las coció.
9
וַתִּקַּח אֶת-הַמַּשְׂרֵת וַתִּצֹק לְפָנָיו, וַיְמָאֵן לֶאֱכוֹל; וַיֹּאמֶר אַמְנוֹן, הוֹצִיאוּ כָל-אִישׁ מֵעָלַי, וַיֵּצְאוּ כָל-אִישׁ, מֵעָלָיו.
And she took the pan, and poured them out before him; but he refused to eat. And Amnon said: ‘Have everyone go out from me.’ And every man went out from him.note
Y tomó la sartén, y las vació delante de él; pero él rehusó comer. Y dijo Amnón: «Hagan salir a todos de junto a mí». Y todos salieron de junto a él.
10
וַיֹּאמֶר אַמְנוֹן אֶל-תָּמָר, הָבִיאִי הַבִּרְיָה הַחֶדֶר, וְאֶבְרֶה, מִיָּדֵךְ; וַתִּקַּח תָּמָר, אֶת-הַלְּבִבוֹת אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂתָה, וַתָּבֵא לְאַמְנוֹן אָחִיהָ, הֶחָדְרָה.
And Amnon said to Tamar: ‘Bring the food into the chamber, that I may eat from your hand.’ And Tamar took the heart-cakes which she had made, and brought them into the chamber to Amnon her brother.note
Y dijo Amnón a Tamar: «Trae la comida a la alcoba, para que yo coma de tu mano». Y tomó Tamar las tortas de corazón que había hecho, y las llevó a la alcoba, a Amnón su hermano.
11
וַתַּגֵּשׁ אֵלָיו, לֶאֱכֹל; וַיַּחֲזֶק-בָּהּ וַיֹּאמֶר לָהּ, בּוֹאִי שִׁכְבִי עִמִּי אֲחוֹתִי.
And when she brought them near to him to eat, he seized her, and said to her: ‘Come, lie with me, my sister.’note
Y cuando ella se las acercó para que comiera, él la agarró, y le dijo: «Ven, acuéstate conmigo, hermana mía».
12
וַתֹּאמֶר לוֹ, אַל-אָחִי אַל-תְּעַנֵּנִי--כִּי לֹא-יֵעָשֶׂה כֵן, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל:  אַל-תַּעֲשֵׂה, אֶת-הַנְּבָלָה הַזֹּאת.
And she said to him: ‘No, my brother, do not violate me; for it is not done so in Israel; do not do this outrage.note
Y ella le dijo: «No, hermano mío, no me violes; porque no se hace así en Israel; no hagas esta infamia.
13
וַאֲנִי, אָנָה אוֹלִיךְ אֶת-חֶרְפָּתִי, וְאַתָּה תִּהְיֶה כְּאַחַד הַנְּבָלִים, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וְעַתָּה דַּבֶּר-נָא אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, כִּי לֹא יִמְנָעֵנִי מִמֶּךָּ.
And I, where shall I carry my shame? And you, you will be like one of the fools in Israel. Now speak, please, to the king; for he will not withhold me from you.’note
Y yo, ¿adónde llevaré mi vergüenza? Y tú, serás como uno de los necios en Israel. Ahora, pues, habla, por favor, al rey; porque él no me negará a ti».
14
וְלֹא אָבָה, לִשְׁמֹעַ בְּקוֹלָהּ; וַיֶּחֱזַק מִמֶּנָּה וַיְעַנֶּהָ, וַיִּשְׁכַּב אֹתָהּ.
But he would not listen to her voice; and he was stronger than she, and violated her, and lay with her.note
Pero él no quiso escuchar su voz; y siendo más fuerte que ella, la violó, y se acostó con ella.
15
וַיִּשְׂנָאֶהָ אַמְנוֹן, שִׂנְאָה גְּדוֹלָה מְאֹד--כִּי גְדוֹלָה הַשִּׂנְאָה אֲשֶׁר שְׂנֵאָהּ, מֵאַהֲבָה אֲשֶׁר אֲהֵבָהּ; וַיֹּאמֶר-לָהּ אַמְנוֹן, קוּמִי לֵכִי.
Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred; for the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. And Amnon said to her: ‘Arise, go.’note
Y Amnón la aborreció con un aborrecimiento muy grande; porque el aborrecimiento con que la aborreció era mayor que el amor con que la había amado. Y Amnón le dijo: «Levántate, vete».
16
וַתֹּאמֶר לוֹ, אַל-אוֹדֹת הָרָעָה הַגְּדוֹלָה הַזֹּאת, מֵאַחֶרֶת אֲשֶׁר-עָשִׂיתָ עִמִּי, לְשַׁלְּחֵנִי; וְלֹא אָבָה, לִשְׁמֹעַ לָהּ.
And she said to him: ‘No — for this great evil, sending me away, is worse than the other that you did with me.’ But he would not listen to her.note
Y ella le dijo: «No — porque este gran mal, echarme, es peor que el otro que me hiciste». Pero él no quiso escucharla.
17
וַיִּקְרָא, אֶת-נַעֲרוֹ מְשָׁרְתוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר, שִׁלְחוּ-נָא אֶת-זֹאת מֵעָלַי הַחוּצָה; וּנְעֹל הַדֶּלֶת, אַחֲרֶיהָ.
And he called his servant who attended him, and said: ‘Send this one away from me, outside, and bolt the door after her.’note
Y llamó a su criado que le servía, y dijo: «Echa a esta de junto a mí, afuera, y cierra la puerta con cerrojo tras ella».
18
וְעָלֶיהָ כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים, כִּי כֵן תִּלְבַּשְׁןָ בְנוֹת-הַמֶּלֶךְ הַבְּתוּלֹת מְעִילִים; וַיֹּצֵא אוֹתָהּ מְשָׁרְתוֹ הַחוּץ, וְנָעַל הַדֶּלֶת אַחֲרֶיהָ.
Now she had on a long ornamented tunic; for so the king’s daughters who were virgins were clothed, in robes. And his servant brought her outside, and bolted the door after her.note
Y ella llevaba una túnica larga y ornamentada; porque así se vestían las hijas vírgenes del rey, con mantos. Y su criado la sacó afuera, y cerró la puerta con cerrojo tras ella.
19
וַתִּקַּח תָּמָר אֵפֶר עַל-רֹאשָׁהּ, וּכְתֹנֶת הַפַּסִּים אֲשֶׁר עָלֶיהָ קָרָעָה; וַתָּשֶׂם יָדָהּ עַל-רֹאשָׁהּ, וַתֵּלֶךְ הָלוֹךְ וְזָעָקָה.
And Tamar put ashes on her head, and tore the long ornamented tunic that was on her; and she laid her hand on her head, and went away, going and crying out.note
Y Tamar se echó ceniza sobre la cabeza, y rasgó la túnica larga y ornamentada que llevaba; y puso su mano sobre su cabeza, y se fue andando y gritando.
20
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלֶיהָ אַבְשָׁלוֹם אָחִיהָ, הַאֲמִינוֹן אָחִיךְ הָיָה עִמָּךְ, וְעַתָּה אֲחוֹתִי הַחֲרִישִׁי אָחִיךְ הוּא, אַל-תָּשִׁיתִי אֶת-לִבֵּךְ לַדָּבָר הַזֶּה; וַתֵּשֶׁב תָּמָר וְשֹׁמֵמָה, בֵּית אַבְשָׁלוֹם אָחִיהָ.
And Absalom her brother said to her: ‘Has Amnon your brother been with you? And now, my sister, be silent; he is your brother; do not set your heart on this thing.’ And Tamar stayed, desolate, in the house of Absalom her brother.note
Y Absalón su hermano le dijo: «¿Ha estado contigo Amnón tu hermano? Ahora, hermana mía, calla; es tu hermano; no pongas tu corazón en esta cosa». Y Tamar se quedó, desolada, en casa de Absalón su hermano.
21
וְהַמֶּלֶךְ דָּוִד--שָׁמַע, אֵת כָּל-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה; וַיִּחַר לוֹ, מְאֹד.
And King David heard all these things, and he was very angry.note
Y el rey David oyó todas estas cosas, y se enojó mucho.
22
וְלֹא-דִבֶּר אַבְשָׁלוֹם עִם-אַמְנוֹן, לְמֵרָע וְעַד-טוֹב:  כִּי-שָׂנֵא אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אֶת-אַמְנוֹן, עַל-דְּבַר אֲשֶׁר עִנָּה, אֵת תָּמָר אֲחֹתוֹ.  {פ}
And Absalom did not speak with Amnon either good or bad; for Absalom hated Amnon, because he had violated Tamar his sister.note
Y Absalón no habló con Amnón ni bueno ni malo; porque Absalón aborrecía a Amnón, por haber violado a Tamar su hermana.
23
וַיְהִי, לִשְׁנָתַיִם יָמִים, וַיִּהְיוּ גֹזְזִים לְאַבְשָׁלוֹם, בְּבַעַל חָצוֹר אֲשֶׁר עִם-אֶפְרָיִם; וַיִּקְרָא אַבְשָׁלוֹם, לְכָל-בְּנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ.
And it happened after two full years that Absalom had shearers at Baal-hazor, which is beside Ephraim; and Absalom invited all the king’s sons.note
Y sucedió pasados dos años completos que Absalón tenía esquiladores en Baal-hazor, que está junto a Efraín; y Absalón convidó a todos los hijos del rey.
24
וַיָּבֹא אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה-נָא גֹזְזִים לְעַבְדֶּךָ; יֵלֶךְ-נָא הַמֶּלֶךְ וַעֲבָדָיו, עִם-עַבְדֶּךָ.
And Absalom came to the king and said: ‘Look, your servant has shearers; let the king, please, and his servants, go with your servant.’note
Y vino Absalón al rey, y dijo: «Mira, tu siervo tiene esquiladores; que vaya el rey, por favor, y sus siervos, con tu siervo».
25
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אַל-בְּנִי אַל-נָא נֵלֵךְ כֻּלָּנוּ, וְלֹא נִכְבַּד, עָלֶיךָ; וַיִּפְרָץ-בּוֹ וְלֹא-אָבָה לָלֶכֶת, וַיְבָרְכֵהוּ.
And the king said to Absalom: ‘No, my son, let us not all go, please, lest we be a burden to you.’ And he pressed him; but he would not go, and blessed him.note
Y el rey dijo a Absalón: «No, hijo mío, no vayamos todos, por favor, para no serte una carga». Y él le insistió; pero no quiso ir, y lo bendijo.
26
וַיֹּאמֶר, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וָלֹא, יֵלֶךְ-נָא אִתָּנוּ אַמְנוֹן אָחִי; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ הַמֶּלֶךְ, לָמָּה יֵלֵךְ עִמָּךְ.
And Absalom said: ‘If not, please let Amnon my brother go with us.’ And the king said to him: ‘Why should he go with you?’note
Y dijo Absalón: «Si no, que vaya con nosotros Amnón mi hermano, por favor». Y el rey le dijo: «¿Para qué ha de ir contigo?»
27
וַיִּפְרָץ-בּוֹ, אַבְשָׁלוֹם; וַיִּשְׁלַח אִתּוֹ אֶת-אַמְנוֹן, וְאֵת כָּל-בְּנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ.  {ס}

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–7 · 2 Samuel 13 Amnon and Tamar — a beautiful sister, “hard in the eyes”, Jonadab the very wise, and two heart-cakes

Absalom’s sister, David’s daughter. “Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister, whose name was Tamar; and Amnon son of David loved her”: the chapter names the two men by their father twice in one verse, and calls the woman by her brother, not her father. Amnon is David’s firstborn by Ahinoam and Absalom is the son of Maacah, the daughter of the king of Geshur (2 Samuel 3:2–3, already on these pages), so Tamar, called Absalom’s sister, is at best Amnon’s half-sister. The name Tamar is a date palm; the only Tamar before her on these pages is the wife of Judah’s firstborn (Genesis 38:6, already on these pages). The ASV has “a fair sister”; the KJV “a fair sister”; the Douay “who was very beautiful”; the TLB “her half brother”. The Greek has Thēmar.

Hard in the eyes. “And Amnon was so distressed that he made himself ill because of Tamar his sister; for she was a virgin, and it was hard in the eyes of Amnon to do anything to her”: the verb is vayyippale, of the root that gives “wonderful” and “too hard”, and the eyes are Amnon’s own. The verb for his illness, hitchalot, is the one Jonadab will use in verse 5, “make yourself ill”, and the one used again in verse 6 when Amnon does it. The NIV has “it seemed impossible for him to do anything to her”; the NWT 1984 “it was difficult in the eyes of”; the KJV “Amnon thought it hard for him to do any thing to her”; the TLB “he had no way of talking to her”. The NWT 1984 is closest to the Hebrew in keeping the eyes.

A very wise man. Jonadab, the text says, is “a very wise man” (ish chakham me’od) stands in one verse. Jonadab is the son of Shimeah, David’s brother, and so Amnon’s cousin, and the Hebrew calls him a rea’, a friend. The NWT 1984 has “a very wise man”. The Douay has “a very wise man” too. The NIV has “a very shrewd man”. The KJV has “a very subtil man”. The ASV has “a very subtle man”. The TLB has “very crafty friend”. The word is chakham, wise, and the Hebrew does not say what kind; the Greek has sophos sphodra, “very wise”. Jonadab will be wise again in verse 32, when he is the one who knows what Absalom has been doing for two years.

Heart-cakes. “Please let Tamar my sister come, and make two heart-cakes in my sight, that I may eat from her hand”: the cakes are levivot, from lev, “heart”, and the noun comes in verses 6, 8 and 10. The verb that makes them, telabbev, “make heart-cakes”, stands in two verses, 6 and 8, so that she is to “heart-cake” two heart-cakes. The NWT 1984 has “heart-shaped cakes” for it, and “the bread of consolation” for the food she is to make. The KJV has “a couple of cakes”; the NIV “make some special bread”; the Douay “two little messes”; the TLB “cook a little something”. The shape of a heart-cake is not known, and “heart” may mean only that it was made to put heart into a sick man; the Hebrew does not say, and it is the man’s heart that the story is about. David, who has only been told that his firstborn is ill, sends Tamar “at home” to him.

Verses 8–14 · “Have everyone go out from me” — Amnon seizes Tamar, and her plea: do not violate me, do not do this outrage

Have everyone go out. “And Amnon said: ‘Have everyone go out from me.’”: “have every man go out from me” (hotzi’u kol-ish me’alay) stands in two verses. They are this one and Genesis 45:1 (already on these pages), where Joseph can no longer hold himself in and calls “Send every man out from me!” before he makes himself known to his brothers. The same words shut the door on a reunion and on a rape. The NIV has “Send everyone out of here”; the KJV “Have out all men from me”; the NWT 1984 “HAVE everybody go out from me!”; the Douay “Put out all persons from me”.

He seized her. “He seized her, and said to her: ‘Come, lie with me, my sister’”: the verb is vayyachazek bah, “he took hold of her”; the word “sister” comes back at the end of the demand, and is the last word he says to her before she speaks. The NIV has “he grabbed her”; the KJV “he took hold of her”; the NWT 1984 “he at once grabbed hold of her”.

Do not violate me. Tamar’s answer has four parts, each in the law’s own words. “No, my brother, do not violate me”: the verb is ta’anneni, the one that the translation renders “violated” at Genesis 34:2 and Deuteronomy 22:29 (both already on these pages), and which comes back in verse 14 for what he does. The NIV has “Don’t force me!”; the KJV “do not force me”; the ASV “do not force me”; the NWT 1984 “Do not humiliate me”; the Douay “do not force me”. “For it is not done so in Israel”: the words are lo ye’aseh ken, and they are Laban’s on the wedding night (Genesis 29:26, already on these pages, “It is not done so in our place”) and the sons of Jacob’s at Shechem, who say that “a thing that should not be done” has been done (Genesis 34:7). “Do not do this outrage”: “this outrage” (hanevalah hazot) stands in three verses, here and in the Levite’s host’s plea to the men of Gibeah (Judges 19:23–24, already on these pages). The NIV has “Don’t do this wicked thing”; the KJV “do not thou this folly”; the NWT 1984 “Do not do this disgraceful folly”; the TLB “Don’t be foolish!”.

Where shall I carry my shame. “And I, where shall I carry my shame? And you, you will be like one of the fools in Israel.”: she names her own cost first, and his second, and the word she uses for him is nevalim, “fools”, from the same root as the outrage. The Nabal of 1 Samuel 25:25 (already on these pages) is “a man of worthlessness”, and “folly is with him”. “Now speak, please, to the king; for he will not withhold me from you”: the law forbids what she says the king would allow. The sister is forbidden by Leviticus 18:9 and 20:17 (both already on these pages), and she says it anyway; the text does not say whether she says it to gain time, or only that she will say anything that will stop him. The NIV has “You would be like one of the wicked fools in Israel”; the KJV “thou shalt be as one of the fools in Israel”; the NWT 1984 “like one of the senseless men in Israel”.

And violated her. “But he would not listen to her voice; and he was stronger than she, and violated her, and lay with her”: “and he lay with her” (vayyishkav otah) stands in two verses, here and Shechem’s act on Dinah (Genesis 34:2); both have the verb ‘anah beside it. And the verb for “was stronger” is chazaq, here with mimmennah, “than she”, a construction that stands in this verse alone; Deuteronomy 22:25 has it of the man who overpowers the betrothed girl in the field. The NIV has “he raped her”; the TLB “he forced her”; the KJV “forced her”; the Douay “overpowered her”; the NWT 1984 “humiliated her”. The Greek has etapeinōsen autēn, “he humbled her”, at every place the Hebrew has this verb in the chapter.

Verses 15–19 · Hatred greater than love — “this great evil, sending me away”, the long ornamented tunic and the ashes

Greater than the love. “Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred; for the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her”: “a great hatred” (sin’ah gedolah) stands in one verse, and the sentence takes the love of verse 1 (vayye’ehavehah) and sets it under this hatred. The NIV has “hated her with intense hatred”; the KJV “hated her exceedingly”; the TLB “suddenly his love turned to hate”; the NWT 1984 “a very great hatred”. The text does not explain why; it states what happened and who was its subject.

No — this great evil. “And she said to him: ‘No — for this great evil, sending me away, is worse than the other that you did with me.’ But he would not listen to her”: the Hebrew of her first word is only al, “no”, and the rest of the sentence is difficult, with ‘al odot, “because of”, at its head; the versions differ about what the evil is that she says is greater. The KJV has “There is no cause: this evil in sending me away is greater than the other”; the ASV “Not so, because this great wrong in putting me forth is worse than the other”; the NIV “Sending me away would be a greater wrong than what you have already done to me”; the NWT 1984 “this badness in sending me away is greater than the other”. The law she is reaching for is Deuteronomy 22:29: the man who violated the girl “may not send her away all his days”; the verb in both verses is shalach.

This one. “And he called his servant who attended him, and said: ‘Send this one away from me, outside, and bolt the door after her’”: he does not say her name, or “my sister”, or even “her”: he says zot, “this one”. The NIV has “Get this woman out of my sight”; the TLB “Throw this woman out”.

A long ornamented tunic. “Now she had on a long ornamented tunic; for so the king’s daughters who were virgins were clothed, in robes”: the garment is ketonet passim, the one that Jacob made for Joseph (Genesis 37:3, already on these pages, “a long ornamented tunic”). The tunic stands in five verses: Genesis 37:3, 23 and 32, and verses 18 and 19 here. Joseph is stripped of his, and Tamar tears hers; each is a garment of favour that is then ruined by a brother’s hand. The KJV has “a garment of divers colours”; the ASV “a garment of divers colors”; the NWT 1984 “a striped robe”; the NIV “an ornate robe”; the TLB “a long robe with sleeves”. Tearing the garment is what David and his men do at the news of Saul’s death (2 Samuel 1:11, already on these pages); the ashes and the hand on the head are Tamar’s own, and she does them alone.

Verses 20–22 · Be silent, my sister — Tamar desolate in Absalom’s house, David very angry, and Absalom’s unspoken hatred

Be silent. “Has Amnon your brother been with you? And now, my sister, be silent; he is your brother; do not set your heart on this thing”: Absalom does not use the verb for what was done to her. He calls Amnon “your brother” and calls her “my sister” in the same breath. The KJV has “hold now thy peace, my sister: he is thy brother; regard not this thing”; the NWT 1984 “Do not set your heart on this matter”; the NIV “Don’t take this thing to heart”; the TLB “It’s not anything to worry about!”.

Desolate. “And Tamar stayed, desolate, in the house of Absalom her brother”: the word is shomemah, the root of the “desolation” in Isaiah 1:7 (already on these pages), said of a land. The ASV has “remained desolate in her brother Absalom’s house”; the NWT 1984 “began to dwell, while being kept from association”; the Douay “pining away”. She does not speak again in the book.

Very angry. “And King David heard all these things, and he was very angry”: the Hebrew stops there, and does not say that he did anything. The nearest thing to a verdict on the house is the one Jehovah had already given David, “I am raising up evil against you out of your own house” (2 Samuel 12:11, already on these pages). The ASV has “very wroth”; the NWT 1984 “he became very angry”; the NIV “he was furious”. The Greek and the Douay add that he would not grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him, as his firstborn; this is not in the Hebrew, and is bracketed here, not adopted.

Neither good nor bad. “And Absalom did not speak with Amnon either good or bad; for Absalom hated Amnon, because he had violated Tamar his sister”: the same verb as verse 14, ‘anah, now said by Absalom. The Douay has “he had ravished his sister Thamar”. The KJV has “he had forced his sister Tamar”. The NWT 1984 has “he had humiliated Tamar his sister”. The NIV has “he had disgraced his sister Tamar”.

Verses 23–27 · Two full years, the shearers at Baal-hazor, and the king who says no and then sends Amnon

Two full years. “And it happened after two full years that Absalom had shearers at Baal-hazor, which is beside Ephraim”: “shearers” (gozezim) stands in three verses: 1 Samuel 25:7 and these verses 23 and 24. At Nabal’s shearing David’s men ask for what is owed them; here the shearing is the occasion of Absalom’s feast. The ASV has “after two full years”; the NWT 1984 “after two full years”; the NIV “Two years later”; the Douay “after two years”. The text places Baal-hazor only by “beside Ephraim”.

And he pressed him. “And he pressed him; but he would not go, and blessed him”: “and he pressed him” (vayyifrats bo) stands in three verses: verses 25 and 27 here, and 2 Kings 5:23, where Naaman presses Gehazi. The king’s answer, “let us not all go, please, lest we be a burden to you”, is a fair one, and Absalom answers it by asking for only one son. “Why should he go with you?” is the king’s last word in the chapter before he hears of the killing. The NIV has “Absalom urged him”; the KJV “Absalom pressed him”; the NWT 1984 “began to urge him”.

Like the feast of a king. The Greek and the Douay add to verse 27: “And Absalom made a feast as it were the feast of a king”. It is not in the Hebrew, and is bracketed here; it is the phrase of 1 Samuel 25:36 (already on these pages), where Nabal is drunk at his shearing “like the feast of a king”, and a copyist or translator may have carried it over from there.

Verses 28–33 · “Strike Amnon, then kill him” — men of valor, a heart merry with wine, and the report that all the sons are dead

Be men of valor. “Be strong, and be men of valor”: “and be men of valor” (vihyu livnei chayil) stands in two verses: 2 Samuel 2:7 (already on these pages), in David’s message to Jabesh, and here. David asks it of the men of Jabesh after Saul’s death; Absalom asks it of his servants for the killing of a guest. The KJV has “be courageous, and be valiant”; the NIV “Be strong and brave”; the Douay “be valiant men”; the Geneva “play the men”.

A heart merry with wine. “When Amnon’s heart is merry with wine”: “when the heart is merry” (ketov lev) stands in three verses: Judges 16:25, Esther 1:10 and here. In Judges the Philistines send for Samson when their hearts are merry; in Esther the king calls for the queen. The NIV has “in high spirits from drinking wine”; the TLB “gets drunk”; the NWT 1984 “in a merry mood with wine”; the KJV “merry with wine”. “Strike Amnon, then kill him” is the verb pair of David’s own order about Uriah (2 Samuel 11:15, already on these pages), “struck down and die”.

Mules and rumor. “And each man mounted his mule, and fled”: the king’s sons ride mules, and so will David’s own mule carry Solomon (1 Kings 1:33, already on these pages). The report that comes to David is false and exact at once: “Absalom has struck down all the king’s sons, and not one of them is left”. David does what he did at the news of Saul’s death: “the king arose, and tore his garments, and lay on the ground” (2 Samuel 1:11; 12:16, where he lay on the ground for the boy). The NIV has “tore his clothes and lay down on the ground”; the KJV “tare his garments, and lay on the earth”; the NWT 1984 “ripped his clothes apart and lay upon the earth”.

By the mouth of Absalom. Jonadab, the wise man of verse 3, tells him: “Amnon alone is dead; for by the mouth of Absalom it was set, from the day he violated Tamar his sister”. The Hebrew is al-pi Avshalom hayetah sumah, “at the mouth of Absalom it was set”, from the verb “to set”, and the versions disagree about how strong the word is. The ASV has “by the appointment of Absalom this hath been determined”; the NWT 1984 “at the order of Absalom it has occurred as something appointed”; the Douay “he was appointed by the mouth of Absalom”; the NIV “This has been Absalom’s express intention ever since”. The man who made the plan of verse 5 knows the plan of verse 28, and the text does not say how.

Verses 34–39 · Absalom fled to Geshur — the watchman, Talmai, Ammihud or Ammihur, and the king who mourned and longed

Absalom fled. “And Absalom fled” stands at the head of verse 34, and again in verses 37 and 38: three times in five verses, and the king’s sons come back weeping in between. In verse 34 the NIV has “Horonaim”, which the Masoretic text does not; the Hebrew has only “the road behind him, from the side of the hill”. The NIV has “the man standing watch looked up”; the KJV “the young man that kept the watch lifted up his eyes”; the NWT 1984 “the young man, the watchman, raised his eyes”.

Talmai son of Ammihud. “Absalom fled, and went to Talmai son of Ammihud, king of Geshur”: the king is Absalom’s mother’s father (2 Samuel 3:3, already on these pages), and so he goes to his grandfather. The name of Talmai’s father is a ketiv and a qere: the written text has Ammihur and the reading tradition Ammihud, and the translation follows the reading. The ASV has “Ammihur”; the KJV “Ammihud”; the NIV “Ammihud”; the NWT 1984 “Am·miʹhud”.

He mourned for his son. “And he mourned for his son all the days”: “and he mourned for his son” (vayyit’abbel ‘al beno) stands in two verses: here and Jacob’s mourning for Joseph (Genesis 37:34, already on these pages, “mourned for his son many days”). The verse does not say who mourns; the translation reads David. The KJV has “David mourned for his son every day”; the NWT 1984 “[David] continued to mourn over his son all the days”.

Longed. “And [the soul of] King David longed to go out to Absalom; for he was comforted over Amnon, because he was dead”: the verb vattekhal is feminine and the king masculine, so the translation supplies “the soul of”. The root kalah is “to be finished”, and it can mean “to long” and “to come to an end”; the Greek and the Douay read it as “ceased”. The ASV has “the soul of king David longed to go forth unto Absalom”; the NIV “King David longed to go to Absalom”; the NWT 1984 “[the soul of] David the king longed to go out to Absalom”; the Geneva “desired to go forth unto Absalom”. The three years of Absalom’s stay in Geshur are told in verse 38.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “violated” for ‘anah at verses 12, 14, 22 and 32, as in Genesis 34:2 and Deuteronomy 22:29; “outrage” for nevalah, as in Genesis 34:7 and Judges 19:23; “heart-cakes”; “very wise man”; “this one”; “long ornamented tunic”, as in Genesis 37:3; “men of valor”; “mule”; and the pointed reading “Ammihud”, with the written “Ammihur” in the note.

Echoes paid. Joseph’s “Send every man out” (Genesis 45:1); Laban’s and Shechem’s “not done so” (Genesis 29:26; 34:7); the outrage at Gibeah (Judges 19:23–24); the law against the sister and the girl who is violated (Leviticus 18:9; 20:17; Deuteronomy 22:29); Judah’s Tamar (Genesis 38:6); Nabal and his feast (1 Samuel 25:25, 36); the shearers (1 Samuel 25:7); “men of valor” (2 Samuel 2:7); the merry heart (Judges 16:25; Esther 1:10); the Geshurite king (2 Samuel 3:3); the evil from the house (2 Samuel 12:11); Uriah’s “struck down and die” (2 Samuel 11:15); torn clothes (2 Samuel 1:11; 12:16); Jacob’s mourning (Genesis 37:34); and Isaiah’s desolation (Isaiah 1:7).

Echoes planted. The mule in the terebinth (2 Samuel 18:9, now on these pages); the sword in the battle against Absalom and in Sheba’s killing (2 Samuel 18:8, now on these pages; 20); Naaman pressing Gehazi (2 Kings 5:23); Adonijah’s death for Abishag (1 Kings 2:25); and the sons of David in the Chronicles (1 Chronicles 3:1–2). None of the others is translated here yet.
