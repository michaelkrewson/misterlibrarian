Gideon's pursuit of the kings of Midian across the Jordan: Succoth and Penuel refuse his men bread and are punished, the kings Zebah and Zalmunna are taken at Karkor, and Gideon makes an ephod of the Midianite gold that becomes a snare to Israel. Gideon refuses kingship, dies, and Israel turns to Baal-berith; Abimelech is born to a concubine at Shechem.
Within the period of the judges, with no date the chapter gives for itself. The forty years of rest (8:28) are the book's own unit, and the early/late question applies (c. 1200 BC on the early reckoning this project foregrounds, c. 1100 BC on the late).

1
וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֵלָיו אִישׁ אֶפְרַיִם, מָה-הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה עָשִׂיתָ לָּנוּ, לְבִלְתִּי קְרֹאות לָנוּ, כִּי הָלַכְתָּ לְהִלָּחֵם בְּמִדְיָן; וַיְרִיבוּן אִתּוֹ, בְּחָזְקָה.
And the men of Ephraim said to him: Why have you treated us this way, not calling us when you went to fight against Midian? And they quarrelled with him sharply.note
Y los hombres de Efraín le dijeron: ¿Por qué nos has tratado así, sin llamarnos cuando fuiste a pelear contra Madián? Y contendieron con él con fuerza.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵיהֶם, מֶה-עָשִׂיתִי עַתָּה כָּכֶם: הֲלֹא, טוֹב עֹלְלוֹת אֶפְרַיִם--מִבְצִיר אֲבִיעֶזֶר.
And he said to them: What have I done now compared with you? Is not the gleaning of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer?note
Y él les respondió: ¿Qué he hecho yo ahora comparado con ustedes? ¿No es mejor el rebusco de Efraín que la vendimia de Abiezer?
3
בְּיֶדְכֶם נָתַן אֱלֹהִים אֶת-שָׂרֵי מִדְיָן, אֶת-עֹרֵב וְאֶת-זְאֵב, וּמַה-יָּכֹלְתִּי, עֲשׂוֹת כָּכֶם; אָז, רָפְתָה רוּחָם מֵעָלָיו, בְּדַבְּרוֹ, הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה.
God has given into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb; and what was I able to do compared with you? Then their anger subsided toward him when he had said this.note
En sus manos entregó Dios a los príncipes de Madián, Oreb y Zeeb; ¿y qué pude yo hacer comparado con ustedes? Entonces su enojo se calmó contra él cuando dijo esto.
4
וַיָּבֹא גִדְעוֹן, הַיַּרְדֵּנָה; עֹבֵר הוּא, וּשְׁלֹשׁ-מֵאוֹת הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, עֲיֵפִים, וְרֹדְפִים.
And Gideon came to the Jordan and crossed over, he and the three hundred men with him, faint yet pursuing.note
Y Gedeón llegó al Jordán y lo cruzó, él y los trescientos hombres que estaban con él, cansados pero persiguiendo.
5
וַיֹּאמֶר, לְאַנְשֵׁי סֻכּוֹת, תְּנוּ-נָא כִּכְּרוֹת לֶחֶם, לָעָם אֲשֶׁר בְּרַגְלָי: כִּי-עֲיֵפִים הֵם--וְאָנֹכִי רֹדֵף אַחֲרֵי זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע, מַלְכֵי מִדְיָן.
He said to the men of Succoth: Give, please, loaves of bread to the people who follow me, for they are faint; and I am pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, the kings of Midian.note
Dijo a los hombres de Sucot: Den, por favor, panes al pueblo que me sigue, pues están cansados; y yo voy tras Zebaj y Zalmuna, reyes de Madián.
6
וַיֹּאמֶר, שָׂרֵי סֻכּוֹת, הֲכַף זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע עַתָּה, בְּיָדֶךָ: כִּי-נִתֵּן לִצְבָאֲךָ, לָחֶם.
The princes of Succoth said: Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your power, that we should give bread to your army?note
Los príncipes de Sucot dijeron: ¿Están ya en tu mano las manos de Zebaj y Zalmuna, para que demos pan a tu ejército?
7
וַיֹּאמֶר גִּדְעוֹן--לָכֵן בְּתֵת יְהוָה אֶת-זֶבַח וְאֶת-צַלְמֻנָּע, בְּיָדִי: וְדַשְׁתִּי, אֶת-בְּשַׂרְכֶם, אֶת-קוֹצֵי הַמִּדְבָּר, וְאֶת-הַבַּרְקֳנִים.
Gideon said: Therefore, when Jehovah has given Zebah and Zalmunna into my hand, I will tear your flesh with the thorns of the wilderness and with briers.note
Gedeón dijo: Por tanto, cuando Jehová haya entregado a Zebaj y a Zalmuna en mi mano, trillaré sus carnes con los espinos del desierto y con los abrojos.
8
וַיַּעַל מִשָּׁם פְּנוּאֵל, וַיְדַבֵּר אֲלֵיהֶם כָּזֹאת; וַיַּעֲנוּ אוֹתוֹ אַנְשֵׁי פְנוּאֵל, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָנוּ אַנְשֵׁי סֻכּוֹת.
He went up from there to Penuel and spoke to them in the same way; and the men of Penuel answered him as the men of Succoth had answered.note
Subió de allí a Penuel, y les habló de la misma manera; y los hombres de Penuel le respondieron como habían respondido los de Sucot.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר גַּם-לְאַנְשֵׁי פְנוּאֵל, לֵאמֹר: בְּשׁוּבִי בְשָׁלוֹם, אֶתֹּץ אֶת-הַמִּגְדָּל הַזֶּה. {פ}
He also said to the men of Penuel: When I come back in peace, I will break down this tower.note
También dijo a los hombres de Penuel: Cuando vuelva en paz, derribaré esta torre.
10
וְזֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע בַּקַּרְקֹר, וּמַחֲנֵיהֶם עִמָּם כַּחֲמֵשֶׁת עָשָׂר אֶלֶף--כֹּל הַנּוֹתָרִים, מִכֹּל מַחֲנֵה בְנֵי-קֶדֶם; וְהַנֹּפְלִים, מֵאָה וְעֶשְׂרִים אֶלֶף אִישׁ שֹׁלֵף חָרֶב.
Now Zebah and Zalmunna were in Karkor, with their army about fifteen thousand men, all that were left of the whole army of the people of the east; for a hundred and twenty thousand men who drew the sword had fallen.note
Zebaj y Zalmuna estaban en Carcor, y sus ejércitos con ellos, unos quince mil hombres, todos los que quedaban de todo el ejército de los hijos del oriente; pues habían caído ciento veinte mil hombres que sacaban espada.
11
וַיַּעַל גִּדְעוֹן, דֶּרֶךְ הַשְּׁכוּנֵי בָאֳהָלִים, מִקֶּדֶם לְנֹבַח, וְיָגְבְּהָה; וַיַּךְ, אֶת-הַמַּחֲנֶה, וְהַמַּחֲנֶה, הָיָה בֶטַח.
Gideon went up by the way of those who dwelt in tents, east of Nobah and Jogbehah, and struck the army, for the army was at ease.note
Gedeón subió por el camino de los que habitaban en tiendas, al oriente de Nobaj y de Jogbeja, y derrotó al ejército, pues el ejército estaba confiado.
12
וַיָּנֻסוּ, זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע, וַיִּרְדֹּף, אַחֲרֵיהֶם; וַיִּלְכֹּד אֶת-שְׁנֵי מַלְכֵי מִדְיָן, אֶת-זֶבַח וְאֶת-צַלְמֻנָּע, וְכָל-הַמַּחֲנֶה, הֶחֱרִיד.
Zebah and Zalmunna fled, and he pursued them and took the two kings of Midian, Zebah and Zalmunna, and threw the whole army into panic.note
Huyeron Zebaj y Zalmuna, y él los persiguió; capturó a los dos reyes de Madián, Zebaj y Zalmuna, y puso en pánico a todo el ejército.
13
וַיָּשָׁב גִּדְעוֹן בֶּן-יוֹאָשׁ, מִן-הַמִּלְחָמָה, מִלְמַעֲלֵה, הֶחָרֶס.
Gideon son of Joash returned from the battle by the ascent of Heres.note
Gedeón hijo de Joás volvió de la batalla por la subida de Jeres.
14
וַיִּלְכָּד-נַעַר מֵאַנְשֵׁי סֻכּוֹת, וַיִּשְׁאָלֵהוּ; וַיִּכְתֹּב אֵלָיו אֶת-שָׂרֵי סֻכּוֹת, וְאֶת-זְקֵנֶיהָ--שִׁבְעִים וְשִׁבְעָה, אִישׁ.
He caught a young man of the men of Succoth and questioned him, and the young man wrote down for him the princes of Succoth and its elders, seventy-seven men.note
Tomó a un joven de los hombres de Sucot y le preguntó; y él le escribió los príncipes de Sucot y sus ancianos: setenta y siete hombres.
15
וַיָּבֹא, אֶל-אַנְשֵׁי סֻכּוֹת, וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע: אֲשֶׁר חֵרַפְתֶּם אוֹתִי לֵאמֹר, הֲכַף זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע עַתָּה בְּיָדֶךָ, כִּי נִתֵּן לַאֲנָשֶׁיךָ הַיְּעֵפִים, לָחֶם.
He came to the men of Succoth and said: Here are Zebah and Zalmunna, about whom you taunted me, saying: Are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your power, that we should give bread to your weary men?note
Llegó a los hombres de Sucot y dijo: Aquí están Zebaj y Zalmuna, por quienes me injuriaron, diciendo: ¿Están ya en tu mano las manos de Zebaj y Zalmuna, para que demos pan a tus hombres cansados?
16
וַיִּקַּח אֶת-זִקְנֵי הָעִיר, וְאֶת-קוֹצֵי הַמִּדְבָּר וְאֶת-הַבַּרְקֳנִים; וַיֹּדַע בָּהֶם, אֵת אַנְשֵׁי סֻכּוֹת.
He took the elders of the city, and the thorns of the wilderness and the briers, and with them he taught the men of Succoth.note
Tomó a los ancianos de la ciudad, y los espinos del desierto y los abrojos, y con ellos enseñó a los hombres de Sucot.
17
וְאֶת-מִגְדַּל פְּנוּאֵל, נָתָץ; וַיַּהֲרֹג, אֶת-אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר.
He broke down the tower of Penuel and killed the men of the city.note
Derribó la torre de Penuel y mató a los hombres de la ciudad.
18
וַיֹּאמֶר, אֶל-זֶבַח וְאֶל-צַלְמֻנָּע, אֵיפֹה הָאֲנָשִׁים, אֲשֶׁר הֲרַגְתֶּם בְּתָבוֹר; וַיֹּאמְרוּ, כָּמוֹךָ כְמוֹהֶם, אֶחָד, כְּתֹאַר בְּנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ.
He said to Zebah and Zalmunna: Where are the men you killed at Tabor? They said: As you are, so were they; each one looked like the son of a king.note
Dijo a Zebaj y a Zalmuna: ¿Dónde están los hombres que mataron en Tabor? Ellos respondieron: Como tú, así eran ellos; cada uno parecía hijo de rey.
19
וַיֹּאמַר, אַחַי בְּנֵי-אִמִּי הֵם; חַי-יְהוָה, לוּ הַחֲיִתֶם אוֹתָם--לֹא הָרַגְתִּי, אֶתְכֶם.
He said: They were my brothers, the sons of my mother. As Jehovah lives, if you had let them live, I would not kill you.note
Dijo: Eran mis hermanos, hijos de mi madre. Vive Jehová, que si los hubieran dejado vivir, no los mataría a ustedes.
20
וַיֹּאמֶר לְיֶתֶר בְּכוֹרוֹ, קוּם הֲרֹג אוֹתָם; וְלֹא-שָׁלַף הַנַּעַר חַרְבּוֹ כִּי יָרֵא, כִּי עוֹדֶנּוּ נָעַר.
He said to Jether, his firstborn: Up, kill them. But the youth did not draw his sword, for he was afraid, being still a youth.note
Dijo a Jéter, su primogénito: Levántate, mátalos. Pero el joven no sacó su espada, porque tenía miedo, pues aún era joven.
21
וַיֹּאמֶר זֶבַח וְצַלְמֻנָּע, קוּם אַתָּה וּפְגַע-בָּנוּ--כִּי כָאִישׁ, גְּבוּרָתוֹ; וַיָּקָם גִּדְעוֹן, וַיַּהֲרֹג אֶת-זֶבַח וְאֶת-צַלְמֻנָּע, וַיִּקַּח אֶת-הַשַּׂהֲרֹנִים, אֲשֶׁר בְּצַוְּארֵי גְמַלֵּיהֶם.
Zebah and Zalmunna said: Rise yourself and fall upon us, for as a man is, so is his strength. Gideon rose and killed Zebah and Zalmunna, and took the crescents that were on their camels’ necks.note
Zebaj y Zalmuna dijeron: Levántate tú y cae sobre nosotros, porque como es el hombre, así es su fuerza. Gedeón se levantó, mató a Zebaj y a Zalmuna, y tomó las medias lunas que estaban en los cuellos de sus camellos.
22
וַיֹּאמְרוּ אִישׁ-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶל-גִּדְעוֹן, מְשָׁל-בָּנוּ גַּם-אַתָּה, גַּם-בִּנְךָ גַּם בֶּן-בְּנֶךָ: כִּי הוֹשַׁעְתָּנוּ, מִיַּד מִדְיָן.
The men of Israel said to Gideon: Rule over us, you and your son and your grandson also, for you have saved us from the hand of Midian.note
Los hombres de Israel dijeron a Gedeón: Gobierna sobre nosotros, tú y tu hijo y también tu nieto; porque nos has librado de la mano de Madián.
23
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם, גִּדְעוֹן, לֹא-אֶמְשֹׁל אֲנִי בָּכֶם, וְלֹא-יִמְשֹׁל בְּנִי בָּכֶם: יְהוָה, יִמְשֹׁל בָּכֶם.
Gideon said to them: I will not rule over you, and my son will not rule over you; Jehovah will rule over you.note
Gedeón les dijo: Yo no gobernaré sobre ustedes, ni mi hijo gobernará sobre ustedes; Jehová gobernará sobre ustedes.
24
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם גִּדְעוֹן, אֶשְׁאֲלָה מִכֶּם שְׁאֵלָה, וּתְנוּ-לִי, אִישׁ נֶזֶם שְׁלָלוֹ: כִּי-נִזְמֵי זָהָב לָהֶם, כִּי יִשְׁמְעֵאלִים הֵם.
Gideon said to them: I would ask one request of you: give me each man the earrings of his plunder, for they had golden earrings, because they were Ishmaelites.note
Gedeón les dijo: Quiero pedirles una cosa: que me dé cada uno los pendientes de su botín, pues ellos tenían pendientes de oro, porque eran ismaelitas.
25
וַיֹּאמְרוּ, נָתוֹן נִתֵּן; וַיִּפְרְשׂוּ, אֶת-הַשִּׂמְלָה, וַיַּשְׁלִיכוּ שָׁמָּה, אִישׁ נֶזֶם שְׁלָלוֹ.
They answered: We will gladly give them. They spread out a garment and each man threw into it the earrings of his plunder.note
Ellos respondieron: De buena gana los daremos. Extendieron un manto y cada uno echó allí los pendientes de su botín.
26
וַיְהִי, מִשְׁקַל נִזְמֵי הַזָּהָב אֲשֶׁר שָׁאָל, אֶלֶף וּשְׁבַע-מֵאוֹת, זָהָב--לְבַד מִן-הַשַּׂהֲרֹנִים וְהַנְּטִיפוֹת וּבִגְדֵי הָאַרְגָּמָן, שֶׁעַל מַלְכֵי מִדְיָן, וּלְבַד מִן-הָעֲנָקוֹת, אֲשֶׁר בְּצַוְּארֵי גְמַלֵּיהֶם.
The weight of the golden earrings he asked for was a thousand and seven hundred shekels of gold, apart from the crescents and the pendants and the purple garments on the kings of Midian, and apart from the chains on their camels’ necks.note
El peso de los pendientes de oro que pidió fue de mil setecientos siclos de oro, aparte de las medias lunas, los pendientes colgantes y las vestiduras de púrpura que tenían los reyes de Madián, y aparte de las cadenas de los cuellos de sus camellos.
27
וַיַּעַשׂ אוֹתוֹ גִדְעוֹן לְאֵפוֹד, וַיַּצֵּג אוֹתוֹ בְעִירוֹ בְּעָפְרָה, וַיִּזְנוּ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל אַחֲרָיו, שָׁם; וַיְהִי לְגִדְעוֹן וּלְבֵיתוֹ, לְמוֹקֵשׁ.
Gideon made it into an ephod and set it up in his city, Ophrah; and all Israel went astray after it there, and it became a snare to Gideon and to his house.note
Gedeón hizo de ello un éfod y lo exhibió en su ciudad, Ofra; y todo Israel se prostituyó tras él allí, y fue un lazo para Gedeón y para su casa.
28
וַיִּכָּנַע מִדְיָן, לִפְנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְלֹא יָסְפוּ, לָשֵׂאת רֹאשָׁם; וַתִּשְׁקֹט הָאָרֶץ אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה, בִּימֵי גִדְעוֹן. {פ}
So Midian was subdued before the sons of Israel, and they lifted up their heads no more. And the land had rest forty years in the days of Gideon.note
Madián fue sometido delante de los hijos de Israel, y no volvieron a levantar la cabeza; y la tierra tuvo reposo cuarenta años en los días de Gedeón.
29
וַיֵּלֶךְ יְרֻבַּעַל בֶּן-יוֹאָשׁ, וַיֵּשֶׁב בְּבֵיתוֹ.
Jerubbaal son of Joash went and lived in his own house.note
Jerubaal hijo de Joás se fue y vivió en su casa.
30
וּלְגִדְעוֹן, הָיוּ שִׁבְעִים בָּנִים, יֹצְאֵי, יְרֵכוֹ: כִּי-נָשִׁים רַבּוֹת, הָיוּ לוֹ.
Gideon had seventy sons, his own offspring, for he had many wives.note
Gedeón tuvo setenta hijos, nacidos de sus lomos, pues tenía muchas mujeres.
31
וּפִילַגְשׁוֹ אֲשֶׁר בִּשְׁכֶם, יָלְדָה-לּוֹ גַם-הִיא בֵּן; וַיָּשֶׂם אֶת-שְׁמוֹ, אֲבִימֶלֶךְ.
His concubine who was in Shechem also bore him a son, and he named him Abimelech.note
Su concubina, que estaba en Siquem, también le dio un hijo, y le puso el nombre de Abimelec.
32
וַיָּמָת גִּדְעוֹן בֶּן-יוֹאָשׁ, בְּשֵׂיבָה טוֹבָה; וַיִּקָּבֵר, בְּקֶבֶר יוֹאָשׁ אָבִיו, בְּעָפְרָה, אֲבִי הָעֶזְרִי. {פ}
Gideon son of Joash died in a good old age and was buried in the tomb of his father Joash, at Ophrah of the Abiezrites.note
Murió Gedeón hijo de Joás en buena vejez, y fue sepultado en el sepulcro de su padre Joás, en Ofra de los abiezritas.
33
וַיְהִי, כַּאֲשֶׁר מֵת גִּדְעוֹן, וַיָּשׁוּבוּ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיִּזְנוּ אַחֲרֵי הַבְּעָלִים; וַיָּשִׂימוּ לָהֶם בַּעַל בְּרִית, לֵאלֹהִים.
As soon as Gideon died, the sons of Israel turned again and went astray after the Baals, and made Baal-berith their god.note
Cuando murió Gedeón, los hijos de Israel volvieron a prostituirse tras los Baales, y se hicieron a Baal-berit por dios.
34
וְלֹא זָכְרוּ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶת-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיהֶם, הַמַּצִּיל אוֹתָם מִיַּד כָּל-אֹיְבֵיהֶם, מִסָּבִיב.
The sons of Israel did not remember Jehovah their God, who had rescued them from the hand of all their enemies round about.note
Los hijos de Israel no se acordaron de Jehová su Dios, que los había librado de la mano de todos sus enemigos alrededor.
35
וְלֹא-עָשׂוּ חֶסֶד, עִם-בֵּית יְרֻבַּעַל גִּדְעוֹן: כְּכָל-הַטּוֹבָה--אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה, עִם-יִשְׂרָאֵל. {פ}
Nor did they show kindness to the house of Jerubbaal, that is Gideon, for all the good he had done to Israel.note
Ni mostraron bondad a la casa de Jerubaal, es decir Gedeón, según todo el bien que él había hecho a Israel.
Verses 1–3 · Ephraim’s quarrel and Gideon’s answer, Judges 8:1–3: gleanings are better than the vintage, and the anger subsides

★★★ The quarrel is about a summons Gideon did not send. The men of Ephraim ask why he did not call them when he went to fight Midian, and they press the complaint ‘sharply’. The verb yarav, ‘they contended’, is the verb of a legal dispute.

★★ Gideon’s answer is a proverb of comparison: is not the gleaning of Ephraim better than the vintage of Abiezer? The Hebrew olelot Ephraim is the leftover grapes that Ephraim picks after the harvest, and mivtsar Abi-ezer is the vintage of Gideon’s own clan. The sentence praises Ephraim by calling its work the better part, and the anger goes down with the compliment.

⚠ The verse also names the princes whom God gave into Ephraim’s hands, Oreb and Zeeb, and gives the credit to Ephraim. Gideon says God delivered the princes of Midian into their hand, and that he himself was able to do little compared with them. The text does not say whether the modesty is sincere, and the translation does not decide it.

Verses 4–7 · Succoth refuses bread, and Gideon promises the thorns, Judges 8:4–7: three hundred men, faint and still pursuing

★★ The pursuit crosses the Jordan with three hundred men, and the text says they were faint and still pursuing. The verb rodefim, ‘pursuing’, is the same participle as in the battle narrative: the pursuers are exhausted and do not stop.

★★ The men of Succoth refuse bread to the army because the kings have not been captured. Their question in v6 is a challenge: are the hands of Zebah and Zalmunna now in your power? Gideon answers with a promise of retribution, to tear their flesh with thorns and briers. The verb dash, ‘to thresh’, is the threshing floor’s word, and the sentence turns the town into grain.

★ The thorns and briers of v7 are the same two words Gideon uses as the instrument of punishment in v16. The promise is carried out in the text, not just announced, which is the chapter’s own sign that the threat was meant.

Verses 8–9 · Penuel refuses too, and the tower is promised, Judges 8:8–9: the same answer and the same threat, in a different town

★★ Penuel gives the same answer as Succoth, and Gideon repeats the same threat. V8 says the men of Penuel answered ‘as the men of Succoth had answered’. The repetition is the narrative’s own method: two refusals, one pattern, and a different penalty for each place.

★ V9 promises to break down the tower on his return in peace. The tower (migdal) is the defensive structure of the town, and the promise is to pull it down. The verb atots (‘break down’) is used of the tower again in v17, where it is carried out.

Verses 10–12 · The kings at Karkor, and the attack on the secure camp, Judges 8:10–12: fifteen thousand left out of a hundred and thirty-five thousand

★★ The numbers are given in the book’s own arithmetic: fifteen thousand were left, and a hundred and twenty thousand who drew the sword had fallen. The chapter does not give the sum, but the two figures together make the whole army of the east about a hundred and thirty-five thousand. The count is the narrative’s own, and the translation keeps it.

★★ Gideon attacks by the way of the tent-dwellers east of Nobah and Jogbehah, where the army is at ease. The army is at ease and unready, and the attack is a surprise. The attack is a surprise, and the text names the two places that mark the way.

Verses 13–17 · The return, the list of seventy-seven, and the punishment of Succoth and Penuel, Judges 8:13–17: the threat fulfilled in the text

★★ Gideon returns by the ascent of Heres, and a young man from Succoth writes down the names of the leaders. The young man is taken to inquire, and he writes for Gideon the princes and elders of the town: seventy-seven men. The number is the list’s own, and the chapter does not say how the youth knew it.

★★ Gideon takes the elders and teaches the men of Succoth with thorns and briers. The verb is the chapter’s word for the punishment: he makes them know what their refusal means, and the translation keeps the sense of a lesson, not a mere beating.

★ The tower of Penuel is broken down and the men of the city are killed. V17 is the carrying out of the promise of v9, and the killing is the chapter’s own penalty for the refusal.

Verses 18–21 · Tabor, Jether, and the crescents, Judges 8:18–21: the brothers who were killed, and a youth too afraid to draw

★★ Gideon asks the kings where the men they killed at Tabor are, and they answer that they looked like the sons of a king. The men of Tabor are Gideon’s brothers, ‘sons of my mother’. Gideon swears by Jehovah that he would have spared the kings if they had spared his brothers. The oath is the chapter’s own reason for the execution.

★ Gideon tells his firstborn Jether to kill them, and the youth draws no sword. The verb yare, ‘he feared’, gives the reason: he is still a youth. The kings then ask Gideon himself to strike, since as the man is, so is his strength, and Gideon kills them.

★★ The crescents on the camels’ necks are the sahronim, the ornaments Gideon takes as part of the plunder. The word is rare, and the object it names is not securely identified; the note keeps the Hebrew.

Verses 22–27 · Kingship refused, the earrings, and the ephod that became a snare, Judges 8:22–27: ‘the LORD shall rule over you’, and the gold that undid him

★★★ Israel offers kingship and Gideon refuses it in the name of Jehovah. V23 has him say he will not rule and his son will not rule either; Jehovah is the ruler. The shelf reads this clause differently: the NWT 1984 says ‘I myself shall not rule’, and the TNM 2019 ‘No seré yo el que los gobierne’, while the KJV and ASV keep ‘I will not rule’, and the translation follows the Hebrew.

★★★ The ephod Gideon makes from the gold of the earrings becomes a snare. V27 says he made it into an ephod, set it up in Ophrah, and all Israel went astray after it. The word moqesh, ‘snare’, is the chapter’s word for the trap that catches what it is meant to serve.

★★ The verb for going astray after the ephod is the verb of prostitution, and the NWT and TNM editions disagree on how to render it. The NWT 1984 reads ‘immoral intercourse’ and the NWT 2013 ‘spiritual prostitution’; the TNM 1987 reads ‘ayuntamiento inmoral’ and the TNM 2019 ‘se prostituyó espiritualmente’. The KJV reads ‘went thither a whoring after it’ and the ASV ‘played the harlot after it’. The translation keeps the metaphor of prostitution, and the note lists the revisions.

Verses 28–32 · Forty years of rest, the seventy sons, Abimelech, and the death of Gideon, Judges 8:28–32: the peace that outlived its leader in one clause

★★ Midian is subdued, the land rests forty years, and Gideon dies in a good old age. The forty years is the book’s own formula for a generation of peace under a judge (Judges 3:11, already on these pages), and the rest is credited to Gideon, not to God.

★★ Gideon has seventy sons from his many wives, and a son by a concubine in Shechem, named Abimelech. The chapter ends the Gideon story with the family the judge leaves, and Abimelech is the son who will try to become king in Judges 9 (now on these pages).

Verses 33–35 · Baal-berith, and the forgetting, Judges 8:33–35: the house of Gideon is forgotten along with its God

★★★ As soon as Gideon dies, Israel goes after the Baals and makes Baal-berith its god. Baal-berith is the Baal of the covenant, and the name is the book’s own sign that the people turn to a local lord who holds a bond. The verb ‘went astray’ is the same verb as v27, and the chapter links the ephod to the Baals.

★ The chapter ends on forgetting: Israel did not remember Jehovah, and did not show kindness to the house of Gideon. The verb zakhar, ‘remember’, is the verb the book uses for what the people fail to do. The word chesed, ‘kindness’, is the covenant word, and the people fail to show it to Gideon’s family, as the chapter says they failed to show it to Jehovah.

Echoes paid. The forty years of rest (v28) pays off Judges 3:11 (already on these pages). The gold of the earrings is the gold of Joshua 7:21 (already on these pages), and the ephod is the word Judges 17 uses of Micah’s image.

Echoes planted. Abimelech (v31) becomes the king of Judges 9 (now on these pages), and the forgetting of v34 is the cycle Judges 10 restarts (now on these pages).

Decisions. The Hebrew ephod is kept as the word, with the note on its meaning. The numbers of v10 and v14 are given as the chapter gives them, not as a sum. The crescents of v21 keep the Hebrew sahronim.

Library. New entries are needed in both languages: the encyclopedia for Zebah, Zalmunna, Penuel, Jerubbaal, the Ishmaelites and Baal-berith; the dictionary for ephod and shaharon. Every name and term the notes link must exist before the chapter ships.

⚠ A note on order: Judges stands on these pages at chapters 1–11. Judges 12 and the rest of the book remain open.
