Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–5 · Ezekiel 21:1–5 a fire in the southern forest — ‘a speaker of parables’

⚠ Verse numbers. This translation follows the Hebrew. These five verses are 20:45–49 in English Bibles, and verses 6–37 here are their 21:1–32. The small mark after each verse gives the English number.

⭐ Three words for south. Teiman, darom and negev, one after another; the last is also the name of the dry country south of Judah. ‘Let your words drip’ is literally ‘drip’. The TLB writes “look toward Jerusalem”.

⭐ Green and dry. The two trees of 17:24. Here the fire takes both. ‘Devour’ (verse 3), ‘consume’ (verse 33) and ‘food’ (verse 37) are from the root for eating. ‘I am setting a fire’ and ‘I have kindled it’ are two verbs. ‘Blazing’ is shalhevet, a rare word; compare Job 15:30, and a longer form at Song of Solomon 8:6.

⭐ A speaker of parables. The verb and noun of 17:2, ‘speak a parable’; the noun is the one printed ‘proverb’ at 12:22 and 18:2. The two words together in this form stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The next oracle speaks of a sword in plain words, and from south to north again.

Verses 6–10 · Ezekiel 21:6–10 the sword out of its sheath — righteous and wicked

⭐ The same shape. ‘Set your face’, ‘let your words drip’, ‘prophesy’, as in verse 2, now toward Jerusalem. ‘I am against you’ as at 13:8.

⭐ Sword and sheath. The word for sheath is also the word for a razor (5:1). The book has spoken of a sword before (6:3, 14:17), and compare Leviticus 26:25.

⚠ Righteous and wicked. Both are cut off here. Abraham asked, ‘Will you really sweep away the righteous with the wicked?’ (Genesis 18:23), chapter 18 says that each answers for himself, and in chapter 9 those who sighed were marked and spared (9:4). Readers differ on how to hold these together: some take ‘righteous’ as those who thought themselves so, some as a way of saying everyone, some let the tension stand. The TLB makes it “I will not spare even the righteous”.

⚠ I have cut off. Verse 9 speaks of the cutting off as already done, though verse 8 says it will be.

⭐ All flesh. The words ‘all flesh’ come three times (verses 4, 9, 10). The first oracle’s fire ‘will not be quenched’; this sword ‘will not return’. Divine name: verse 8 has ‘Jehovah’ alone, without ‘Lord’.

Verses 11–12 · Ezekiel 21:11–12 sigh before their eyes

⭐ Sigh. The word of the men marked at 9:4. The prophet is to do it where people can see, until they ask why. ‘Loins’ is the middle of the body, printed ‘waist’ at 9:2. The NIV has “with broken heart and bitter grief”.

⭐ A report. As at 7:26. ‘All hands will hang slack’ and ‘all knees will run with water’ are 7:17 again. Hearts melted at Jericho and at Ai (Joshua 2:11, 7:5).

⭐ Grow faint. Kahah, to grow dim: the word said of what Moses’s eye did not do (Deuteronomy 34:7). The letters k-h-t-h stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, there and here.

Verses 13–18 · Ezekiel 21:13–18 ‘a sword, a sword’ — sharpened and polished

⭐ A sword, a sword. The doubled word stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here (verse 14) and verse 33. Verse 14 has ‘Thus says the Lord’, Adonai alone. Compare the flashing sword of Deuteronomy 32:41.

⚠ The sceptre of my son. Verses 15 and 18 are very hard. ‘Sceptre’ is the word of chapter 19, and ‘rod’ at 20:37. ‘Rejecting’ is feminine, while ‘sceptre’ is masculine and ‘sword’ feminine. The verses do not say who ‘my son’ is. The ASV has “the rod of my son, it contemneth every tree”; the NIV “The sword despises every such stick”; the Douay “thou removest the sceptre of my son”; the NWT 1984 “Is it rejecting the scepter of my own son”.

⭐ Given. ‘It has been given’ (verse 16) is literally ‘one gave it’. ‘Taken up’ is the verb printed ‘caught’ in verses 28 and 29. The ‘hand’ of verses 16 and 29 is kaf, the ‘palm’ of 19 and 22; the killer’s ‘hand’ is another word. The killer is not named.

⭐ Strike your thigh. A gesture of grief, as at Jeremiah 31:19 (31:18 in the Hebrew). The princes of Israel were the subject of chapter 19’s dirge.

Verses 19–22 · Ezekiel 21:19–22 palm against palm

⭐ Strike palm against palm. The prophet claps (verse 19), and then Jehovah says ‘I too’ (verse 22). ‘I will rest my fury’ is the language of 5:13 and 16:42.

⚠ A third time. ‘Let the sword be doubled, a third time’ is obscure. ‘That closes in on them’ is a rare word perhaps from ‘chamber’: the ASV has “which entereth into their chambers”.

⚠ Point and wrapping. The word printed ‘point’ stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and its meaning is uncertain. ‘Wrapped for slaughter’ is likewise unclear: the KJV has “wrapped up”, the ASV “pointed”.

⭐ Right and left. The commands of verse 21 are feminine: they are spoken to the sword.

Verses 23–27 · Ezekiel 21:23–27 two roads — the king of Babylon divines

⭐ Cut out a hand. A signpost at a fork. ‘Cut out’ is bara, and a form of the same letters is ‘created’ in verse 35. The two roads lead to Rabbah of Ammon and to Jerusalem. David took the crown of Rabbah’s king (2 Samuel 12:30).

⭐ The mother of the way. The fork itself. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible.

⭐ Three ways of asking. Shaking arrows, asking the teraphim, looking at a liver. Divination is forbidden at Deuteronomy 18:10, and Samuel names it beside teraphim (1 Samuel 15:23). The verses report what the king does without approving it, and the lot falls on Jerusalem. ‘Asked’ is not the ‘inquire’ of chapter 20. The TLB adds “uncertain whether to attack Jerusalem or Rabbah”.

⭐ Siege. Battering rams, ramp and siege wall are what the prophet built against his brick at 4:2. Battering rams are named twice in verse 27.

Verses 28–29 · Ezekiel 21:28–29 iniquity brought to remembrance

⚠ An empty divination. To ‘them’ the result will seem false. The Hebrew writes one form of ‘divination’ and reads another (ketiv/qere), and both are printed above. ‘They have sworn oaths’ is obscure; some connect it with the oath broken in chapter 17, where the word for oath is a different one.

⚠ He. ‘He is bringing iniquity to remembrance’ names no one. The NWT 1984 has “he is calling error to remembrance”; the NIV “he will remind them of their guilt”; the TLB makes it the king of Babylon.

⭐ Remembrance and caught. ‘Remember’ runs through both verses, and ‘caught’ closes both, the verb of the snare at 12:13 and 17:20. Iniquity, transgressions and sins stand together.

Verses 30–32 · Ezekiel 21:30–32 the turban and the crown — ‘until he comes whose right it is’

⚠ Slain, wicked. Chalal is ‘slain’ as in verses 19 and 34, said here of one still alive; some read ‘profane’: the KJV has “thou, profane wicked prince of Israel”, the ASV “O deadly wounded wicked one”. No name is given; the TLB writes “O King Zedekiah”.

⭐ The time of the iniquity of the end. The words stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, verse 34, and 35:5.

⚠ Turban and crown. ‘The turban’ with the article stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible; the other five speak of the high priest’s (Exodus 28:37, Leviticus 8:9). Some take turban and crown as priesthood and kingship, others as two signs of the one ruler.

⭐ High and low. As at 17:24. ‘This will not be this’ is as short in the Hebrew.

⚠ Until he comes whose right it is. ‘Right’ is mishpat, justice or judgment. Many hear Genesis 49:10, ‘until Shiloh comes’, and read a coming rightful ruler. Others read the one to whom the judgment belongs, who is to carry it out. The verse does not name him. ‘Ruin’ is said three times. The Douay has “to whom judgment belongeth”; the TLB writes “the Man” with a capital.

Verses 33–37 · Ezekiel 21:33–37 the sword and Ammon

⭐ Ammon too. The road not taken in verse 27 is not forgotten. Their reproach is a word of 5:15. ‘Laid open’ is literally ‘opened’.

⭐ Emptiness and a lie. The pair said of false prophets at 13:9.

⚠ Return it to its sheath. The command is short, and readers differ on whose sword is meant and who is judged ‘where you were created’: Babylon’s sword itself, or Ammon’s. ‘Origins’ is the word of 16:3.

⭐ Brutish men. Bo’arim, with the letters of ‘I have kindled’ in verse 4. ‘Destruction’ is the word of 9:6.

⚠ Who is ‘you’? In verses 34 to 37 the ‘you’ is feminine in the Hebrew, as ‘sword’ is; a land or a people can also be addressed so. The command ‘return it’ is masculine.

⭐ Not remembered. ‘Food for the fire’ was said of the vine wood (15:4). Israel’s iniquity is brought to remembrance (verses 28–29); this one will not be remembered.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “sword” and “sheath” through the chapter; “sharpened” at 14, 15 and 16; “polished” at 14, 15, 16 and 33; “all flesh” at 4, 9 and 10; “caught” at 28 and 29; and “in the time of the iniquity of the end” at 30 and 34.

Echoes paid. The knees of 7:17; the siege works of 4:2; high and low from 17:24; and the dim eye of Deuteronomy 34:7.

Echoes planted. Ammon again at Ezekiel 25:2.
