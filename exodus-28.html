Exodus 28 — The Mister Translation

1
וְאַתָּה הַקְרֵב אֵלֶיךָ אֶת-אַהֲרֹן אָחִיךָ וְאֶת-בָּנָיו אִתּוֹ, מִתּוֹךְ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--לְכַהֲנוֹ-לִי: אַהֲרֹן--נָדָב וַאֲבִיהוּא אֶלְעָזָר וְאִיתָמָר, בְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן.
And you, bring near to yourself Aaron your brother, and his sons with him, from among the children of Israel, that he may serve as priest to me — Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar, the sons of Aaron.note
Y tú, acerca a ti a Aarón tu hermano, y a sus hijos con él, de entre los hijos de Israel, para que me sirva como sacerdote — Aarón, Nadab y Abiú, Eleazar e Itamar, los hijos de Aarón.
2
וְעָשִׂיתָ בִגְדֵי-קֹדֶשׁ, לְאַהֲרֹן אָחִיךָ, לְכָבוֹד, וּלְתִפְאָרֶת.
And you shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty.note
Y harás vestiduras sagradas para Aarón tu hermano, para gloria y para hermosura.
3
וְאַתָּה, תְּדַבֵּר אֶל-כָּל-חַכְמֵי-לֵב, אֲשֶׁר מִלֵּאתִיו, רוּחַ חָכְמָה; וְעָשׂוּ אֶת-בִּגְדֵי אַהֲרֹן, לְקַדְּשׁוֹ--לְכַהֲנוֹ-לִי.
And you shall speak to all who are wise of heart, whom I have filled with a spirit of wisdom, that they make Aaron’s garments, to sanctify him, that he may serve as priest to me.note
Y tú hablarás a todos los sabios de corazón, a quienes he llenado de espíritu de sabiduría, para que hagan las vestiduras de Aarón, para santificarlo, para que me sirva como sacerdote.
cross-refs⤷ 31:6⤷ 35:25
4
וְאֵלֶּה הַבְּגָדִים אֲשֶׁר יַעֲשׂוּ, חֹשֶׁן וְאֵפוֹד וּמְעִיל, וּכְתֹנֶת תַּשְׁבֵּץ, מִצְנֶפֶת וְאַבְנֵט; וְעָשׂוּ בִגְדֵי-קֹדֶשׁ לְאַהֲרֹן אָחִיךָ, וּלְבָנָיו--לְכַהֲנוֹ-לִי.
And these are the garments that they shall make: a breastplate, and an ephod, and a robe, and a checkered tunic, a turban, and a sash; and they shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, and for his sons, that he may serve as priest to me.note
Y estas son las vestiduras que harán: un pectoral, y un efod, y un manto, y una túnica de tejido a cuadros, un turbante, y un cinto; y harán vestiduras sagradas para Aarón tu hermano, y para sus hijos, para que me sirva como sacerdote.
5
וְהֵם יִקְחוּ אֶת-הַזָּהָב, וְאֶת-הַתְּכֵלֶת וְאֶת-הָאַרְגָּמָן, וְאֶת-תּוֹלַעַת הַשָּׁנִי, וְאֶת-הַשֵּׁשׁ. {פ}
And they shall take the gold, and the blue, and the purple, and the scarlet, and the fine linen. {פ}note
Y ellos tomarán el oro, y el azul, y la púrpura, y la escarlata, y el lino fino. {פ}
6
וְעָשׂוּ, אֶת-הָאֵפֹד: זָהָב תְּכֵלֶת וְאַרְגָּמָן תּוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי, וְשֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר--מַעֲשֵׂה חֹשֵׁב.
And they shall make the ephod of gold, blue, and purple, scarlet, and fine twined linen, the work of the designer.note
Y harán el efod de oro, azul, y púrpura, escarlata, y lino fino torcido, obra de diseñador.
7
שְׁתֵּי כְתֵפֹת חֹבְרֹת, יִהְיֶה-לּוֹ אֶל-שְׁנֵי קְצוֹתָיו--וְחֻבָּר.
It shall have two joined shoulder-pieces at its two ends, that it may be joined together.note
Tendrá dos piezas de hombro unidas, en sus dos extremos, para que quede unido.
8
וְחֵשֶׁב אֲפֻדָּתוֹ אֲשֶׁר עָלָיו, כְּמַעֲשֵׂהוּ מִמֶּנּוּ יִהְיֶה: זָהָב, תְּכֵלֶת וְאַרְגָּמָן וְתוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי--וְשֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר.
And the woven band on it, for fastening it, shall be of the same workmanship, of one piece with it: gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen.note
Y la banda tejida que está sobre él, para ajustarlo, será de la misma hechura, de una sola pieza con él: oro, azul, y púrpura, y escarlata, y lino fino torcido.
9
וְלָקַחְתָּ, אֶת-שְׁתֵּי אַבְנֵי-שֹׁהַם; וּפִתַּחְתָּ עֲלֵיהֶם, שְׁמוֹת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And you shall take two onyx stones, and engrave on them the names of the children of Israel:note
Y tomarás dos piedras de ónice, y grabarás en ellas los nombres de los hijos de Israel:
10
שִׁשָּׁה, מִשְּׁמֹתָם, עַל, הָאֶבֶן הָאֶחָת; וְאֶת-שְׁמוֹת הַשִּׁשָּׁה הַנּוֹתָרִים, עַל-הָאֶבֶן הַשֵּׁנִית--כְּתוֹלְדֹתָם.
six of their names on the one stone, and the names of the six that remain on the other stone, according to their birth order.note
seis de sus nombres en una piedra, y los nombres de los seis restantes en la otra piedra, según su orden de nacimiento.
11
מַעֲשֵׂה חָרַשׁ, אֶבֶן--פִּתּוּחֵי חֹתָם תְּפַתַּח אֶת-שְׁתֵּי הָאֲבָנִים, עַל-שְׁמֹת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; מֻסַבֹּת מִשְׁבְּצוֹת זָהָב, תַּעֲשֶׂה אֹתָם.
with the work of a stone engraver, like the engravings of a signet, you shall engrave the two stones with the names of the children of Israel; you shall make them enclosed in settings of gold.note
con obra de grabador en piedra, como grabados de sello, grabarás las dos piedras con los nombres de los hijos de Israel; las harás engastadas en engastes de oro.
12
וְשַׂמְתָּ אֶת-שְׁתֵּי הָאֲבָנִים, עַל כִּתְפֹת הָאֵפֹד, אַבְנֵי זִכָּרֹן, לִבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וְנָשָׂא אַהֲרֹן אֶת-שְׁמוֹתָם לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, עַל-שְׁתֵּי כְתֵפָיו--לְזִכָּרֹן. {ס}
And you shall put the two stones on the shoulder-pieces of the ephod, stones of remembrance for the children of Israel; and Aaron shall bear their names before Jehovah on his two shoulders, for a remembrance. {ס}note
Y pondrás las dos piedras sobre las piezas de hombro del efod, piedras de recuerdo para los hijos de Israel; y Aarón llevará sus nombres delante de Jehová, sobre sus dos hombros, para recuerdo. {ס}
13
וְעָשִׂיתָ מִשְׁבְּצֹת, זָהָב.
And you shall make settings of gold,note
Y harás engastes de oro,
14
וּשְׁתֵּי שַׁרְשְׁרֹת זָהָב טָהוֹר, מִגְבָּלֹת תַּעֲשֶׂה אֹתָם מַעֲשֵׂה עֲבֹת; וְנָתַתָּה אֶת-שַׁרְשְׁרֹת הָעֲבֹתֹת, עַל-הַמִּשְׁבְּצֹת. {ס}
and two chains of pure gold; you shall make them of braided cord-work, and put the braided chains on the settings. {ס}note
y dos cadenillas de oro puro; las harás de obra trenzada, como cordón, y pondrás las cadenillas trenzadas sobre los engastes. {ס}
15
וְעָשִׂיתָ חֹשֶׁן מִשְׁפָּט, מַעֲשֵׂה חֹשֵׁב--כְּמַעֲשֵׂה אֵפֹד, תַּעֲשֶׂנּוּ; זָהָב תְּכֵלֶת וְאַרְגָּמָן וְתוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי, וְשֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר--תַּעֲשֶׂה אֹתוֹ.
And you shall make a breastplate of judgment, the work of the designer; you shall make it like the work of the ephod: of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen you shall make it.note
Y harás un pectoral de juicio, obra de diseñador; lo harás como la obra del efod: de oro, azul, y púrpura, y escarlata, y lino fino torcido lo harás.
cross-refs⤷ 21:1
16
רָבוּעַ יִהְיֶה, כָּפוּל; זֶרֶת אָרְכּוֹ, וְזֶרֶת רָחְבּוֹ.
Square it shall be, and doubled; a span its length, and a span its width.note
Cuadrado será, y doble; un palmo su largo, y un palmo su ancho.
17
וּמִלֵּאתָ בוֹ מִלֻּאַת אֶבֶן, אַרְבָּעָה טוּרִים אָבֶן: טוּר, אֹדֶם פִּטְדָה וּבָרֶקֶת--הַטּוּר, הָאֶחָד.
And you shall set in it a setting of stones, four rows of stone: a row of carnelian, topaz, and emerald — the first row.note
Y engastarás en él un engaste de piedras, cuatro hileras de piedra: una hilera de cornalina, topacio, y esmeralda — la primera hilera.
cross-refs⤷ Rev 21:19
18
וְהַטּוּר, הַשֵּׁנִי--נֹפֶךְ סַפִּיר, וְיָהֲלֹם.
And the second row: turquoise, sapphire, and diamond.note
Y la segunda hilera: turquesa, zafiro, y diamante.
19
וְהַטּוּר, הַשְּׁלִישִׁי--לֶשֶׁם שְׁבוֹ, וְאַחְלָמָה.
And the third row: jacinth, agate, and amethyst.note
Y la tercera hilera: jacinto, ágata, y amatista.
20
וְהַטּוּר, הָרְבִיעִי--תַּרְשִׁישׁ וְשֹׁהַם, וְיָשְׁפֵה; מְשֻׁבָּצִים זָהָב יִהְיוּ, בְּמִלּוּאֹתָם.
And the fourth row: beryl, and onyx, and jasper. They shall be enclosed in gold in their settings.note
Y la cuarta hilera: berilo, y ónice, y jaspe. Estarán engastadas en oro en sus engastes.
21
וְהָאֲבָנִים תִּהְיֶיןָ עַל-שְׁמֹת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, שְׁתֵּים עֶשְׂרֵה--עַל-שְׁמֹתָם; פִּתּוּחֵי חוֹתָם, אִישׁ עַל-שְׁמוֹ, תִּהְיֶיןָ, לִשְׁנֵי עָשָׂר שָׁבֶט.
And the stones shall correspond to the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names; like the engravings of a signet, each one according to its name, they shall be for the twelve tribes.note
Y las piedras corresponderán a los nombres de los hijos de Israel, doce, según sus nombres; como grabados de sello, cada una según su nombre, serán para las doce tribus.
22
וְעָשִׂיתָ עַל-הַחֹשֶׁן שַׁרְשֹׁת גַּבְלֻת, מַעֲשֵׂה עֲבֹת, זָהָב, טָהוֹר.
And you shall make on the breastplate braided chains of cord-work, of pure gold.note
Y harás sobre el pectoral cadenillas trenzadas, obra de cordón, de oro puro.
23
וְעָשִׂיתָ, עַל-הַחֹשֶׁן, שְׁתֵּי, טַבְּעוֹת זָהָב; וְנָתַתָּ, אֶת-שְׁתֵּי הַטַּבָּעוֹת, עַל-שְׁנֵי, קְצוֹת הַחֹשֶׁן.
And you shall make on the breastplate two rings of gold, and put the two rings on the two ends of the breastplate.note
Y harás sobre el pectoral dos anillos de oro, y pondrás los dos anillos sobre los dos extremos del pectoral.
24
וְנָתַתָּה, אֶת-שְׁתֵּי עֲבֹתֹת הַזָּהָב, עַל-שְׁתֵּי, הַטַּבָּעֹת--אֶל-קְצוֹת, הַחֹשֶׁן.
And you shall put the two golden cords on the two rings, at the ends of the breastplate.note
Y pondrás los dos cordones de oro sobre los dos anillos, en los extremos del pectoral.
25
וְאֵת שְׁתֵּי קְצוֹת שְׁתֵּי הָעֲבֹתֹת, תִּתֵּן עַל-שְׁתֵּי הַמִּשְׁבְּצוֹת; וְנָתַתָּה עַל-כִּתְפוֹת הָאֵפֹד, אֶל-מוּל פָּנָיו.
And the two other ends of the two cords you shall put on the two settings, and put them on the shoulder-pieces of the ephod, on its front.note
Y los otros dos extremos de los dos cordones los pondrás sobre los dos engastes, y los pondrás sobre las piezas de hombro del efod, por su frente.
26
וְעָשִׂיתָ, שְׁתֵּי טַבְּעוֹת זָהָב, וְשַׂמְתָּ אֹתָם, עַל-שְׁנֵי קְצוֹת הַחֹשֶׁן--עַל-שְׂפָתוֹ, אֲשֶׁר אֶל-עֵבֶר הָאֵפֹד בָּיְתָה.
And you shall make two rings of gold, and put them on the two ends of the breastplate, on its edge that faces the ephod, inward.note
Y harás dos anillos de oro, y los pondrás sobre los dos extremos del pectoral, en su borde que mira hacia el efod, hacia adentro.
27
וְעָשִׂיתָ, שְׁתֵּי טַבְּעוֹת זָהָב, וְנָתַתָּה אֹתָם עַל-שְׁתֵּי כִתְפוֹת הָאֵפוֹד מִלְּמַטָּה מִמּוּל פָּנָיו, לְעֻמַּת מַחְבַּרְתּוֹ--מִמַּעַל, לְחֵשֶׁב הָאֵפוֹד.
And you shall make two rings of gold, and put them on the two shoulder-pieces of the ephod, below, on its front, close to its seam, above the woven band of the ephod.note
Y harás dos anillos de oro, y los pondrás sobre las dos piezas de hombro del efod, abajo, por su frente, cerca de su costura, encima de la banda tejida del efod.
28
וְיִרְכְּסוּ אֶת-הַחֹשֶׁן מִטַּבְּעֹתָו אֶל-טַבְּעֹת הָאֵפוֹד, בִּפְתִיל תְּכֵלֶת, לִהְיוֹת, עַל-חֵשֶׁב הָאֵפוֹד; וְלֹא-יִזַּח הַחֹשֶׁן, מֵעַל הָאֵפוֹד.
And they shall bind the breastplate by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a blue cord, so that it stays on the woven band of the ephod, and the breastplate does not come loose from the ephod.note
Y atarán el pectoral por sus anillos a los anillos del efod, con un cordón azul, para que quede sobre la banda tejida del efod, y el pectoral no se suelte del efod.
29
וְנָשָׂא אַהֲרֹן אֶת-שְׁמוֹת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּחֹשֶׁן הַמִּשְׁפָּט, עַל-לִבּוֹ--בְּבֹאוֹ אֶל-הַקֹּדֶשׁ: לְזִכָּרֹן לִפְנֵי-יְהוָה, תָּמִיד.
And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the breastplate of judgment on his heart, when he goes into the holy place, for a remembrance before Jehovah continually.note
Y Aarón llevará los nombres de los hijos de Israel en el pectoral de juicio, sobre su corazón, cuando entre en el lugar santo, para recuerdo delante de Jehová continuamente.
30
וְנָתַתָּ אֶל-חֹשֶׁן הַמִּשְׁפָּט, אֶת-הָאוּרִים וְאֶת-הַתֻּמִּים, וְהָיוּ עַל-לֵב אַהֲרֹן, בְּבֹאוֹ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה; וְנָשָׂא אַהֲרֹן אֶת-מִשְׁפַּט בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל עַל-לִבּוֹ, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה--תָּמִיד. {ס}
And you shall put in the breastplate of judgment the Urim and the Thummim, and they shall be on Aaron’s heart when he goes in before Jehovah; and Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel on his heart before Jehovah continually. {ס}note
Y pondrás en el pectoral de juicio el Urim y el Tumim, y estarán sobre el corazón de Aarón cuando entre delante de Jehová; y Aarón llevará el juicio de los hijos de Israel sobre su corazón delante de Jehová continuamente. {ס}
31
וְעָשִׂיתָ אֶת-מְעִיל הָאֵפוֹד, כְּלִיל תְּכֵלֶת.
And you shall make the robe of the ephod all of blue.note
Y harás el manto del efod, todo de azul.
32
וְהָיָה פִי-רֹאשׁוֹ, בְּתוֹכוֹ; שָׂפָה יִהְיֶה לְפִיו סָבִיב מַעֲשֵׂה אֹרֵג, כְּפִי תַחְרָא יִהְיֶה-לּוֹ--לֹא יִקָּרֵעַ.
And it shall have an opening for the head in the middle of it; there shall be a binding of woven work around its opening, like the opening of a coat of mail, so that it does not tear.note

Six garments named at once, and a verb repeated three times in five verses

For the first time since the tabernacle instructions began, the text turns from the STRUCTURE to the PEOPLE who will serve inside it. Aaron and his four sons are named individually — Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar — and a single short purpose clause, ‘lekhahano-li,’ ‘that he may serve as priest to me,’ is repeated three times in these five verses (vv1, 3, 4) before a single garment is even described. Six items are listed all at once (v4) as a kind of table of contents — breastplate, ephod, robe, checkered tunic, turban, sash — and the rest of the chapter fills them in, almost exactly in that order.

⚠ The garments are ‘for glory and for beauty’ (v2) — kavod, the same weighty word that names God’s own manifest presence elsewhere in scripture, here applied to CLOTHING. The chapter repeats the exact phrase verbatim at v40, extending it from Aaron alone to his sons. KJV, ASV, and NIV all render it ‘glory and beauty’/‘dignity and honor’ without remarking on the word’s weight; NWT reads ‘dignity and beauty,’ softening the specific gravity the Hebrew carries.

⚠ Watch the root mille, ‘to fill,’ across this whole chapter. It opens here (v3) with craftsmen ‘filled’ with a spirit of wisdom — the same verb-root, and the same wisdom vocabulary, the book uses again for Bezalel himself (Exodus 31:3, already on these pages: ‘I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom’) — and it recurs twice more before the chapter ends, in two entirely different senses (see the notes at vv17 and 41). A single Hebrew root running under three unrelated English words — filled, set, and the ordination idiom at v41 — that this translation keeps visible where the shelf does not.

The ephod, two memorial stones, and a Genesis word doing quiet work

The ephod (vv6-14) is a shoulder-garment woven in the same choshev — two-sided, pattern-on-both-faces — technique already established for the tabernacle’s own most sacred curtains (the work of the designer, Exodus 26:1, already on these pages). That distinction now extends from the BUILDING to the PRIEST himself: the ephod and the breastplate it carries (v15) both get the expensive two-sided weave; the plainer sash at v39 will not.

Two onyx stones are engraved with the names of the twelve tribes, six to a stone, ‘according to their birth order’ (v10) — toldot, the same structural word that organizes the whole book of Genesis into its ten ‘these are the generations of’ headings, here doing ordinary genealogical work rather than literary architecture, but the same word nonetheless. Set on the shoulder-pieces, the stones become ‘stones of remembrance’ (v12): Aaron does not merely represent the twelve tribes symbolically, he physically CARRIES their names on his own body ‘before Jehovah’ every time he serves. ⚠ The same stone, shoham, reappears once more at v20, as the last of the breastplate’s own twelve stones — the shoulder and the heart, bearing the same gem twice over.

A breastplate of JUDGMENT — the Mishpatim’s own word, worn over the heart

‘Breastplate of judgment’ (choshen mishpat, v15) uses the identical root that named the whole law-code seven chapters back — the Mishpatim, Exodus 21–23, already on these pages — and that Exodus 26’s own closing note found meaning ‘plan’ or ‘design’ at 26:30 (already on these pages). Three distinct senses of one root, now tracked across three chapters: case-law, architectural design, and here, worn literally over Aaron’s heart, judicial verdict. By v29-30 the object built here becomes explicitly what it is named for: Aaron ‘bears the mishpat of the children of Israel on his heart before Jehovah continually’ — the same word for the object, the act it performs, and the whole earlier law-code, three uses of one root converging on a single physical device.

⚠ The twelve stones (vv17-20) are the most famous translation crux in the chapter, and this translation does not pretend more certainty than the evidence allows. Real comparison across the shelf, row by row: KJV/ASV/ESV read sardius, topaz, carbuncle for the first row where NIV reads carnelian, chrysolite, beryl and NWT reads ruby, topaz, emerald; the second row splits between emerald, sapphire, diamond (KJV/ASV/ESV) and turquoise, sapphire, jasper (NWT alone renders the third stone ‘jasper’ where every other version reads ‘diamond’ or ‘emerald’); the third row is the most stable — KJV alone keeps the archaic ‘ligure’ where every modern version reads ‘jacinth,’ and NWT alone declines to translate it at all, printing the transliterated ‘leshem’ with a footnote naming amber, hyacinth, opal, and tourmaline as candidates — the honest move, and the one this translation follows in spirit, keeping ‘jacinth’ only as a working gloss, not a claim. The fourth row again splits, KJV/ASV/ESV/NASB ‘beryl’ against NIV ‘topaz’ and NWT ‘chrysolite’ — and NWT alone renders the chapter’s LAST stone ‘jade’ where every other version, ancient and modern, reads ‘jasper.’ Four of the twelve — sapphire, agate, amethyst, onyx — are genuinely stable across every version checked; the other eight are guesses with real pedigree, not settled identifications, and ancient sapir almost certainly named lapis lazuli rather than the crystalline corundum ‘sapphire’ means today, since true sapphire was not workable with ancient tools.

⚠ Thirteen centuries later, a different vision reuses this same list — and does not reproduce it. Revelation 21:19-20 (already on these pages) sets twelve stones into the foundations of a city wall, and its own note already says plainly that the two lists ‘overlap and do not match.’ Checked side by side: carnelian, topaz, emerald, turquoise, sapphire, jacinth, amethyst, beryl, and jasper all appear as candidate renderings in BOTH lists (though never once in the same position, and never certainly the same underlying stone) — while Revelation’s own chalcedony, sardonyx, chrysolite, and chrysoprase have no clean match here at all. A genuine echo of a genuine echo, with the uncertainty of BOTH originals compounding rather than cancelling.

The mechanism that keeps the breastplate from swinging loose, and the mystery inside it

Verses 22-28 are pure engineering — four gold rings, two braided cords, and a blue thread lashing the breastplate to the ephod’s own woven band so that ‘the breastplate does not come loose from the ephod’ (v28), a practical concern the text states outright rather than leaving to the reader’s imagination.

⚠ ‘The Urim and the Thummim’ (v30) are named, placed inside the breastplate, and never once described. No shape, no material, no instructions for their use are given anywhere in this chapter — a genuinely rare silence in a chapter this obsessed with exact specification. Every reputable source agrees on the uncertainty: the words themselves likely mean something like ‘lights’ and ‘perfections’ or ‘truth,’ but even that etymology cannot be pinned down with confidence, and how the objects actually functioned — cast like lots, or read by some other sign — is a question ancient rabbinic sources already confessed they could not answer with certainty. This translation keeps both words transliterated, following KJV, ASV, NIV, and NWT alike — the one point on this page where every version on the shelf agrees, by declining to guess.

A robe built like armor, and the first ‘so that he does not die’

The robe’s neck-opening (v32) is reinforced ‘like the opening of a coat of mail’ — a martial image inside a garment of service, chosen for one stated reason: ‘so that it does not tear.’ Around its hem, gold bells alternate with woven pomegranates, and the chapter states their purpose as bluntly as anywhere in the Torah: the bells sound when Aaron enters and leaves the holy place, ‘so that he does not die’ (v35). Nothing here is decorative. The audible bell is the only warning system a curtained, windowless sanctuary has — a way for whoever waits outside to know the priest is still moving, still alive, inside a space where his own death is the stated alternative to getting the garment right.

‘Holy to Jehovah,’ worn on the forehead, and a priest who carries guilt that is not his own

The gold plate’s inscription, ‘Kodesh la-YHVH,’ is the most direct claim of holiness in the whole tabernacle account — not a place or an object this time, but two words engraved and bound to a living man’s forehead, worn ‘continually’ (v38). ⚠ What follows is a striking claim: Aaron ‘shall bear the iniquity of the holy things that the children of Israel hallow’ — not his own sin, but the ordinary imperfection built into every gift and offering the nation brings, absorbed in advance by a plate of gold on one man’s head so that the gifts themselves may be accepted. The verb is the SAME ‘bear’ (nasa) used twice already in this chapter for the names on Aaron’s shoulders and heart (vv12, 29) — carrying identity there, carrying guilt here.

Two headgear words, not one, and the plainer craft returns

Aaron’s own turban (mitznefet, vv4, 37, 39) and his sons’ headgear (migba’ot, v40) are two distinct Hebrew nouns, not a singular and its plural — the high priest’s own wound-and-wrapped turban, tall enough to carry the gold plate at its front, against a simpler cap for the ordinary priesthood beneath him. The shelf mostly flattens the distinction: KJV and ASV render both ‘bonnet(s)’/‘mitre,’ obscuring that two different words are at work; this translation keeps ‘turban’ for Aaron alone and ‘headgear’ for his sons.

⚠ The sash at v39 is ‘the work of the embroiderer’ (rokem), the plainer, one-sided craft already established for the tabernacle’s own outer, public-facing hangings (Exodus 26:36, already on these pages) — not the two-sided choshev reserved for the ephod and breastplate at vv6 and 15. The same gradient that ran through the building now runs through the priest’s own body: the garments nearest his heart get the expensive weave; the sash at his waist gets the ordinary one. Verse 40 repeats v2’s exact phrase — ‘for glory and for beauty’ — extending it from Aaron alone to his sons for the first time.

‘Fill their hand’ — the one idiom the whole shelf translates away, and a second ‘lest they die’

‘Fill their hand’ (v41, mille et-yadam) is the standard Hebrew idiom for ordination to office — and every major English version checked, without exception, translates it away. KJV and ASV read ‘consecrate them’; NIV, NASB, and ESV all reach for ‘ordain them’ instead. None keeps the image of a hand being FILLED — almost certainly with the priest’s own portion of the very sacrifice that installs him (Exodus 29, already on these pages, uses the same verb four more times at the ordination itself, plus five more as its own noun, ‘the ram OF FILLING’). This translation keeps the hand visible, in the spirit of ‘side’ over ‘rib’ at Genesis 2: a vivid, physical idiom the whole shelf has quietly smoothed away.

⚠ ‘So that they do not bear iniquity, and die’ (v43) is the chapter’s SECOND life-and-death clause, and it is not the same one as v35’s. There, the danger was entering unannounced — a bell, an audible signal, averts it. Here, the danger is exposure itself — linen breeches, not sound, are what stands between the priest and death. And the closing formula, ‘an everlasting statute for him, and for his seed after him’ (chukkat olam LO U-LEZAR’O ACHARAV), is a genuinely DIFFERENT Hebrew phrase from the ‘everlasting statute throughout THEIR GENERATIONS’ (chukkat olam le-dorotam) already tracked twice on these pages (Exodus 12:14, 27:21) — that formula binds a PRACTICE to the whole nation across time; this one binds an OFFICE to one family’s bloodline specifically. Two distinct everlasting-statute formulas, doing two distinct kinds of binding, in the same book.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. ‘Fill their hand’ kept literal for mille et-yadam (v41) rather than the whole shelf’s ‘consecrate’/‘ordain,’ per the note above. ‘Turban’ kept for Aaron’s own mitznefet, distinct from ‘headgear’ for his sons’ migba’ot (v40), against KJV/ASV’s conflating both as ‘bonnet(s).’ The twelve stones (vv17-20) are rendered with the shelf’s most defensible modern identifications where real consensus exists, and flagged as genuine guesses where it does not — see the note at v15. ‘Urim and Thummim’ (v30) left transliterated, matching the whole shelf’s own restraint. Verse numbering follows the Hebrew exactly; Hebrew and English Exodus 28 both run 1–43.

A note on order: Exodus stands on these pages at chapters 1–38 — the tabernacle’s furniture, structure, altar, and courtyard all specified, and now the priesthood’s own garments. Exodus 29, the ordination ceremony itself — where ‘fill their hand’ stops being an idiom and becomes an instruction — Exodus 30, the incense altar and the half-shekel census, and Exodus 31, the naming of Bezalel and Oholiab and the sabbath command that closes the whole instruction, and Exodus 32, the golden calf and Moses’ intercession, and Exodus 33, Moses’ plea to see Jehovah’s glory and his glimpse of God’s back from the cleft of the rock, and Exodus 34, the covenant remade and Moses’ own face left shining, and Exodus 35, the people’s own gifts for the tabernacle at last brought forward, and Exodus 36, the craftsmen at work and the people’s giving finally restrained, and Exodus 37, the ark and the table and the lampstand actually built, and Exodus 38, the bronze altar and the courtyard built at last, are now on these pages too. Exodus 39, the priestly garments finally sewn, remains open, along with the rest of the book.
