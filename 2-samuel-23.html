David speaks his last words: the anointed of the God of Jacob, a ruler like the light of the morning, an eternal covenant with his house, the worthless like thorns thrown away. Then the list of his mighty men: the Three and their deeds, the water of Bethlehem that David pours out to Jehovah, Abishai and Benaiah, and the Thirty, ending with Uriah the Hittite and the sum thirty-seven.
After the song of the last chapter, near the end of David’s reign: his last words, and a list that runs back across the whole reign to Asahel and forward to Uriah.

1
וְאֵלֶּה דִּבְרֵי דָוִד, הָאַחֲרֹנִים:  נְאֻם דָּוִד בֶּן-יִשַׁי, וּנְאֻם הַגֶּבֶר הֻקַם עָל--מְשִׁיחַ אֱלֹהֵי יַעֲקֹב, וּנְעִים זְמִרוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And these are the last words of David: The utterance of David son of Jesse, and the utterance of the man raised on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob, and the pleasant one of the songs of Israel.note
Y estas son las últimas palabras de David: La expresión de David hijo de Isaí, y la expresión del hombre levantado en alto, el ungido del Dios de Jacob, y el dulce de los cánticos de Israel.
2
רוּחַ יְהוָה, דִּבֶּר-בִּי; וּמִלָּתוֹ, עַל-לְשׁוֹנִי.
The spirit of Jehovah spoke by me, and his word was upon my tongue.note
El espíritu de Jehová habló por mí, y su palabra estuvo en mi lengua.
3
אָמַר אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, לִי דִבֶּר צוּר יִשְׂרָאֵל:  מוֹשֵׁל, בָּאָדָם--צַדִּיק, מוֹשֵׁל יִרְאַת אֱלֹהִים.
The God of Israel said, the Rock of Israel spoke to me: A ruler over men, a righteous one, a ruler in the fear of God,note
El Dios de Israel dijo, me habló la Roca de Israel: Un gobernante de los hombres, un justo, un gobernante en el temor de Dios,
4
וּכְאוֹר בֹּקֶר, יִזְרַח-שָׁמֶשׁ; בֹּקֶר לֹא עָבוֹת, מִנֹּגַהּ מִמָּטָר דֶּשֶׁא מֵאָרֶץ.
and like the light of the morning when the sun rises, a morning without clouds; from brightness, from rain, grass from the earth.note
y como la luz de la mañana cuando sale el sol, una mañana sin nubes; del resplandor, de la lluvia, hierba de la tierra.
5
כִּי-לֹא-כֵן בֵּיתִי, עִם-אֵל:  כִּי בְרִית עוֹלָם שָׂם לִי, עֲרוּכָה בַכֹּל וּשְׁמֻרָה--כִּי-כָל-יִשְׁעִי וְכָל-חֵפֶץ, כִּי-לֹא יַצְמִיחַ.
For is my house not so with God? For he has set for me an everlasting covenant, ordered in everything and kept; for all my salvation and all my desire — will he not make it grow?note
Porque ¿no es así mi casa con Dios? Porque ha puesto para mí un pacto eterno, ordenado en todo y guardado; porque toda mi salvación y todo mi deseo — ¿no lo hará brotar?
6
וּבְלִיַּעַל, כְּקוֹץ מֻנָד כֻּלָּהַם:  כִּי-לֹא בְיָד, יִקָּחוּ.
But the worthless are like a thorn cast away, all of them; for they are not taken with the hand.note
Pero los de la nada son como espino arrojado lejos, todos ellos; porque no se toman con la mano.
7
וְאִישׁ יִגַּע בָּהֶם, יִמָּלֵא בַרְזֶל וְעֵץ חֲנִית; וּבָאֵשׁ, שָׂרוֹף יִשָּׂרְפוּ בַּשָּׁבֶת.  {פ}
And the man who touches them is filled with iron and the shaft of a spear; and in the fire they are burned utterly in the place.note
Y el hombre que los toca se llena de hierro y de asta de lanza; y en el fuego son quemados por completo en el lugar.
8
אֵלֶּה שְׁמוֹת הַגִּבֹּרִים, אֲשֶׁר לְדָוִד:  יֹשֵׁב בַּשֶּׁבֶת תַּחְכְּמֹנִי רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁלִשִׁי, הוּא עֲדִינוֹ העצנו הָעֶצְנִי--עַל-שְׁמֹנֶה מֵאוֹת חָלָל, בְּפַעַם אחד אֶחָת.  {ס}
These are the names of the mighty men whom David had: Josheb-basshebeth a Tahchemonite, head of the three — he is Adino the Eznite — over eight hundred slain, at one time.note
Estos son los nombres de los valientes que tuvo David: Joseb-basébet el tacmonita, jefe de los tres — él es Adino el eznita — sobre ochocientos muertos, de una vez.
9
וְאַחֲרָו אֶלְעָזָר בֶּן-דדי דֹּדוֹ, בֶּן-אֲחֹחִי; בִּשְׁלֹשָׁה גברים הַגִּבֹּרִים עִם-דָּוִד, בְּחָרְפָם בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים נֶאֶסְפוּ-שָׁם לַמִּלְחָמָה, וַיַּעֲלוּ, אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And after him was Eleazar son of Dodo son of an Ahohite, among the three mighty men with David, when they taunted the Philistines gathered there for the battle, and the men of Israel went up.note
Y después de él estaba Eleazar hijo de Dodó, hijo de un ahohita, entre los tres valientes con David, cuando injuriaron a los filisteos reunidos allí para la batalla, y los hombres de Israel subieron.
10
הוּא קָם וַיַּךְ בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים עַד כִּי-יָגְעָה יָדוֹ, וַתִּדְבַּק יָדוֹ אֶל-הַחֶרֶב, וַיַּעַשׂ יְהוָה תְּשׁוּעָה גְדוֹלָה, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא; וְהָעָם יָשֻׁבוּ אַחֲרָיו, אַךְ-לְפַשֵּׁט.  {ס}
He arose and struck the Philistines until his hand was weary and his hand clung to the sword; and Jehovah worked a great salvation that day; and the people returned after him — only to strip.note
Él se levantó e hirió a los filisteos hasta que se cansó su mano, y su mano se pegó a la espada; y Jehová hizo una gran salvación aquel día; y el pueblo volvió tras él — solo para despojar.
11
וְאַחֲרָיו שַׁמָּה בֶן-אָגֵא, הָרָרִי; וַיֵּאָסְפוּ פְלִשְׁתִּים לַחַיָּה, וַתְּהִי-שָׁם חֶלְקַת הַשָּׂדֶה מְלֵאָה עֲדָשִׁים, וְהָעָם נָס, מִפְּנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים.
And after him was Shammah son of Agee the Hararite. And the Philistines were gathered into a troop, and there was a plot of the field full of lentils; and the people fled from the Philistines.note
Y después de él estaba Sama hijo de Agué el hararita. Y los filisteos se juntaron en una tropa, y había una porción del campo llena de lentejas; y el pueblo huyó de los filisteos.
12
וַיִּתְיַצֵּב בְּתוֹךְ-הַחֶלְקָה וַיַּצִּילֶהָ, וַיַּךְ אֶת-פְּלִשְׁתִּים; וַיַּעַשׂ יְהוָה, תְּשׁוּעָה גְדוֹלָה.  {ס}
And he took his stand in the midst of the plot, and delivered it, and struck the Philistines; and Jehovah worked a great salvation.note
Y él se plantó en medio de la porción, y la libró, e hirió a los filisteos; y Jehová hizo una gran salvación.
13
וַיֵּרְדוּ שלשים שְׁלֹשָׁה מֵהַשְּׁלֹשִׁים רֹאשׁ, וַיָּבֹאוּ אֶל-קָצִיר אֶל-דָּוִד--אֶל-מְעָרַת, עֲדֻלָּם; וְחַיַּת פְּלִשְׁתִּים, חֹנָה בְּעֵמֶק רְפָאִים.
And three of the thirty chiefs went down, and came in harvest time to David, to the cave of Adullam; and a troop of Philistines was camping in the valley of Rephaim.note
Y tres de los treinta jefes descendieron, y vinieron en tiempo de siega a David, a la cueva de Adulam; y una tropa de filisteos acampaba en el valle de Refaim.
14
וְדָוִד, אָז בַּמְּצוּדָה; וּמַצַּב פְּלִשְׁתִּים, אָז בֵּית לָחֶם.
And David was then in the stronghold; and a garrison of the Philistines was then in Bethlehem.note
Y David estaba entonces en el baluarte; y una guarnición de filisteos estaba entonces en Belén.
15
וַיִּתְאַוֶּה דָוִד, וַיֹּאמַר:  מִי יַשְׁקֵנִי מַיִם, מִבֹּאר בֵּית-לֶחֶם אֲשֶׁר בַּשָּׁעַר.  {ס}
And David longed, and said: ‘Who will give me water to drink from the cistern of Bethlehem, which is at the gate?’note
Y David tuvo deseo, y dijo: «¿Quién me dará a beber agua de la cisterna de Belén, que está junto a la puerta?»
16
וַיִּבְקְעוּ שְׁלֹשֶׁת הַגִּבֹּרִים בְּמַחֲנֵה פְלִשְׁתִּים, וַיִּשְׁאֲבוּ-מַיִם מִבֹּאר בֵּית-לֶחֶם אֲשֶׁר בַּשַּׁעַר, וַיִּשְׂאוּ, וַיָּבִאוּ אֶל-דָּוִד; וְלֹא אָבָה לִשְׁתּוֹתָם, וַיַּסֵּךְ אֹתָם לַיהוָה.
And the three mighty men broke through the camp of the Philistines, and drew water from the cistern of Bethlehem, which was at the gate, and took it, and brought it to David; but he would not drink it, and poured it out to Jehovah.note
Y los tres valientes irrumpieron en el campamento de los filisteos, y sacaron agua de la cisterna de Belén, que estaba junto a la puerta, y la tomaron, y la trajeron a David; pero él no quiso beberla, y la derramó a Jehová.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר חָלִילָה לִּי יְהוָה מֵעֲשֹׂתִי זֹאת, הֲדַם הָאֲנָשִׁים הַהֹלְכִים בְּנַפְשׁוֹתָם, וְלֹא אָבָה, לִשְׁתּוֹתָם; אֵלֶּה עָשׂוּ, שְׁלֹשֶׁת הַגִּבֹּרִים.  {ס}
And he said: ‘Far be it from me, Jehovah, that I should do this! Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?’ And he would not drink it. These things the three mighty men did.note
Y dijo: «¡Lejos esté de mí, Jehová, hacer esto! ¿No es la sangre de los hombres que fueron a riesgo de sus vidas?» Y no quiso beberla. Esto hicieron los tres valientes.
18
וַאֲבִישַׁי אֲחִי יוֹאָב בֶּן-צְרוּיָה, הוּא רֹאשׁ השלשי הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה, וְהוּא עוֹרֵר אֶת-חֲנִיתוֹ, עַל-שְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת חָלָל; וְלוֹ-שֵׁם, בַּשְּׁלֹשָׁה.
And Abishai, brother of Joab, son of Zeruiah, was head of the three; and he brandished his spear over three hundred slain, and had a name among the three.note
Y Abisai, hermano de Joab, hijo de Sarvia, era jefe de los tres; y blandió su lanza sobre trescientos muertos, y tuvo nombre entre los tres.
19
מִן-הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה הֲכִי נִכְבָּד, וַיְהִי לָהֶם לְשָׂר; וְעַד-הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה, לֹא-בָא.  {ס}
He was the most honored of the three, and became their captain; but he did not come to the three.note
Fue el más honrado de los tres, y llegó a ser su capitán; pero no llegó a los tres.
20
וּבְנָיָהוּ בֶן-יְהוֹיָדָע בֶּן-אִישׁ-חי חַיִל רַב-פְּעָלִים, מִקַּבְצְאֵל; הוּא הִכָּה, אֵת שְׁנֵי אֲרִאֵל מוֹאָב, וְהוּא יָרַד וְהִכָּה אֶת-האריה הָאֲרִי בְּתוֹךְ הַבֹּאר, בְּיוֹם הַשָּׁלֶג.
And Benaiah son of Jehoiada, son of a man of valor, great in deeds, from Kabzeel: he struck the two Ariels of Moab; and he went down and struck the lion in the midst of the pit on a day of snow.note
Y Benaía hijo de Joiada, hijo de un hombre valiente, grande en hechos, de Cabseel: él hirió a los dos Ariel de Moab; y descendió e hirió al león en medio del foso en un día de nieve.
21
וְהוּא-הִכָּה אֶת-אִישׁ מִצְרִי אשר אִישׁ מַרְאֶה, וּבְיַד הַמִּצְרִי חֲנִית, וַיֵּרֶד אֵלָיו, בַּשָּׁבֶט; וַיִּגְזֹל אֶת-הַחֲנִית מִיַּד הַמִּצְרִי, וַיַּהַרְגֵהוּ בַּחֲנִיתוֹ.
And he struck an Egyptian, a man of appearance; and in the Egyptian’s hand was a spear; and he went down to him with a rod, and snatched the spear from the Egyptian’s hand, and killed him with his own spear.note
Y hirió a un egipcio, hombre de aspecto; y en la mano del egipcio había una lanza; y descendió a él con una vara, y arrebató la lanza de la mano del egipcio, y lo mató con su propia lanza.
22
אֵלֶּה עָשָׂה, בְּנָיָהוּ בֶּן-יְהוֹיָדָע; וְלוֹ-שֵׁם, בִּשְׁלֹשָׁה הַגִּבֹּרִים.
These things Benaiah son of Jehoiada did; and he had a name among the three mighty men.note
Estas cosas hizo Benaía hijo de Joiada; y tuvo nombre entre los tres valientes.
23
מִן-הַשְּׁלֹשִׁים נִכְבָּד, וְאֶל-הַשְּׁלֹשָׁה לֹא-בָא; וַיְשִׂמֵהוּ דָוִד,  {ר}  אֶל-מִשְׁמַעְתּוֹ.  {ס}
He was more honored than the thirty, but he did not come to the three; and David set him over his guard.note
Fue más honrado que los treinta, pero no llegó a los tres; y David lo puso sobre su guardia.
24
עֲשָׂהאֵל אֲחִי-יוֹאָב, בַּשְּׁלֹשִׁים; אֶלְחָנָן בֶּן-דֹּדוֹ,  {ר}  בֵּית לָחֶם.  {ס}
Asahel, brother of Joab, was among the thirty; Elhanan son of Dodo of Bethlehem;note
Asael, hermano de Joab, estaba entre los treinta; Eljanán hijo de Dodó, de Belén;
25
שַׁמָּה, הַחֲרֹדִי,  {ס}  אֱלִיקָא,  {ר}  הַחֲרֹדִי.  {ס}
Shammah the Harodite, Elika the Harodite;note
Sama el harodita, Elica el harodita;
26
חֶלֶץ, הַפַּלְטִי,  {ס}  עִירָא  {ר}  בֶן-עִקֵּשׁ,  {ס}  הַתְּקוֹעִי.  {ס}
Helez the Paltite, Ira son of Ikkesh the Tekoite;note
Heles el paltita, Ira hijo de Iqués el tecoíta;
27
אֲבִיעֶזֶר,  {ר}  הָעַנְּתֹתִי,  {ס}  מְבֻנַּי, הַחֻשָׁתִי.  {ס}
Abiezer the Anathothite, Mebunnai the Hushathite;note
Abiezer el anatotita, Mebunai el husatita;
28
צַלְמוֹן,  {ר}  הָאֲחֹחִי,  {ס}  מַהְרַי, הַנְּטֹפָתִי.  {ס}
Zalmon the Ahohite, Maharai the Netophathite;note
Zalmón el ahohita, Maharai el netofatita;
29
חֵלֶב בֶּן-בַּעֲנָה,  {ר}  הַנְּטֹפָתִי;  {ס}  אִתַּי, בֶּן-רִיבַי, מִגִּבְעַת, בְּנֵי בִנְיָמִן.  {ס}
Heleb son of Baanah the Netophathite, Ittai son of Ribai of Gibeah of the sons of Benjamin;note
Héleb hijo de Baaná el netofatita, Itai hijo de Ribai, de Guibeá de los hijos de Benjamín;
30
בְּנָיָהוּ,  {ר}  פִּרְעָתֹנִי,  {ס}  הִדַּי, מִנַּחֲלֵי גָעַשׁ.  {ס}
Benaiah a Pirathonite, Hiddai of the torrent-valleys of Gaash;note
Benaía el piratonita, Hidai de los arroyos de Gaas;
31
אֲבִי-עַלְבוֹן,  {ר}  הָעַרְבָתִי,  {ס}  עַזְמָוֶת, הַבַּרְחֻמִי.  {ס}
Abi-albon the Arbathite, Azmaveth the Barhumite;note
Abi-albón el arbatita, Azmávet el barhumita;
32
אֶלְיַחְבָּא,  {ר}  הַשַּׁעַלְבֹנִי,  {ס}  בְּנֵי יָשֵׁן, יְהוֹנָתָן.  {ס}
Eliahba the Shaalbonite, of the sons of Jashen, Jonathan;note
Eliaba el saalbonita, de los hijos de Jasén, Jonatán;
33
שַׁמָּה,  {ר}  הַהֲרָרִי,  {ס}  אֲחִיאָם בֶּן-שָׁרָר, הָארָרִי.  {ס}
Shammah the Hararite, Ahiam son of Sharar the Ararite;note
Sama el hararita, Ahíam hijo de Sarar el ararita;
34

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–7 · 2 Samuel 23 The last words of David — “the man raised on high”, a ruler like the morning light, an everlasting covenant, and the worthless like thorns

The utterance. “And these are the last words of David: The utterance of David son of Jesse, and the utterance of the man raised on high, the anointed of the God of Jacob”: the word is ne’um, the formula of an oracle, and “and the utterance of the man”, unne’um hagever, stands in three verses: Balaam’s two oracles (Numbers 24:3, 15, now on these pages) and this one. The ASV has “David the son of Jesse saith, And the man who was raised on high saith”. The NIV has “The inspired utterance of David son of Jesse” and “the man exalted by the Most High”. The NWT 1984 has “the able-bodied man that was raised up on high”. The TLB has “David, the son of Jesse, speaks”. The Douay has “The man to whom it was appointed concerning the Christ of the God of Jacob”, from the Latin. Rahlfs’ Greek has pistos Dauid, “faithful David”. “Anointed” is meshiach (see mashiach); the last words come after “the words of this song” of 22:1.

The pleasant one of the songs of Israel. The last title is ne’im zemirot Yisra’el, and it can be read either way: the one who sings the songs of Israel, or the one whom they sing. The ASV has “the sweet psalmist of Israel”. The NIV has “the hero of Israel’s songs”. The NWT 1984 has “the pleasant one of the melodies of Israel”. The TLB has “sweet psalmist of Israel”. The Douay has “the excellent psalmist of Israel”. The translation keeps the Hebrew’s adjective and leaves the reading open.

The spirit, the Rock, the ruler. “The spirit of Jehovah spoke by me, and his word was upon my tongue”: the spirit of Jehovah had rushed on David “from that day onward” (1 Samuel 16:13, now on these pages), and “his word was upon my tongue” stands only here. The ASV has “The Spirit of Jehovah spake by me”. The NIV has “The Spirit of the Lord spoke through me”. The NWT 1984 has “The spirit of Jehovah it was that spoke by me”. The Douay has “The spirit of the Lord hath spoken by me”. The Rock of Israel is the tsur of the song (22:32, 47). The saying is a ruler and a comparison, and the Hebrew gives it no verb: the ASV has “One that ruleth over men righteously”, the NIV “When one rules over people in righteousness” and the NWT 1984 “When one ruling over mankind is righteous”; and the ruler is “like the light of the morning when the sun rises” (the ASV has “as the light of the morning, when the sun riseth”, the NIV “like the light of morning at sunrise”).

A covenant, and thorns. “For is my house not so with God? For he has set for me an everlasting covenant, ordered in everything and kept”: the word berit, “covenant”, stands in six verses of 2 Samuel, 3:12, 13, 21; 5:3; 15:24, and here; the promise of chapter 7 does not use it. The ASV has “Verily my house is not so with God; Yet he hath made with me an everlasting covenant”. The NIV has “surely he would not have made with me an everlasting covenant”. The NWT 1984 has “For is not my household like that with God”. The Douay has “Neither is my house so great with God”. The last clause, “will he not make it grow?”, can also be read “though he does not make it grow”. “But the worthless are like a thorn cast away”: uvliyaal (see beliyaal); the ASV has “the ungodly”, the NIV “evil men” and the NWT 1984 “good-for-nothing persons”. Whoever touches them is filled with iron and “the shaft of a spear”, ets chanit, which stands in four verses: Goliath’s (1 Samuel 17:7; 2 Samuel 21:19), this one, and 1 Chronicles 20:5.

Verses 8–12 · 2 Samuel 23 The Three — Josheb-basshebeth, Eleazar and Shammah: eight hundred slain, a hand clinging to the sword, and a field of lentils

Josheb-basshebeth. “These are the names of the mighty men whom David had: Josheb-basshebeth a Tahchemonite, head of the three — he is Adino the Eznite — over eight hundred slain, at one time”: the verse has no verb for the slaying, and the versions supply one. The ASV has “against eight hundred slain at one time”. The NIV has “he raised his spear against eight hundred men, whom he killed in one encounter”. The NWT 1984 has “He was brandishing his spear over eight hundred slain at one time”. The TLB has “He once killed eight hundred men in one battle”. 1 Chronicles 11:11 has Jashobeam “the son of a Hachmonite”, who “lifted up his spear against three hundred”, and Rahlfs’ Greek here names Iebosthe ho Chananaios. “Adino the Eznite” is written ha’etsno and read ha’etsni. “The mighty men” are the gibborim (see gibbor).

Eleazar. “And after him was Eleazar son of Dodo son of an Ahohite… when they taunted the Philistines gathered there for the battle, and the men of Israel went up. He arose and struck the Philistines until his hand was weary and his hand clung to the sword”: “taunted” is charaf, the verb of Goliath’s taunt (1 Samuel 17:10) and of the man of Gath (2 Samuel 21:21), and “clung” is davaq, the verb of Judah’s clinging to its king (2 Samuel 20:2, all now on these pages). The ASV has “when they defied the Philistines” and “his hand clave unto the sword”. The NIV has “when they taunted the Philistines gathered at Pas Dammim”, the place from Chronicles (1 Chronicles 11:13; “Ephes-dammim” is at 1 Samuel 17:1), and “froze to the sword”. The NWT 1984 has “They had gathered themselves there for the battle” and “kept cleaving to the sword”. The TLB has “too tired to hold his sword”, which says the opposite. The name “Dodo” is written Dodai.

A great salvation. “And Jehovah worked a great salvation that day; and the people returned after him — only to strip”: “and Jehovah worked a great salvation” stands in three verses: Jonathan’s word to Saul about David (1 Samuel 19:5, now on these pages), this verse and verse 12 (see teshuah). The ASV has “Jehovah wrought a great victory that day; and the people returned after him only to take spoil”. The NIV has “The Lord brought about a great victory that day” and “only to strip the dead”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah performed a great salvation on that day”. The Douay has “the Lord wrought a great victory”.

The field of lentils. “And the Philistines were gathered into a troop, and there was a plot of the field full of lentils; and the people fled from the Philistines. And he took his stand in the midst of the plot, and delivered it”: lachayyah, “into a troop”, which the NWT 1984 reads as a place; its words are “a tract of the field full of lentils”. The ASV has “into a troop, where was a plot of ground full of lentils”. The NIV has “banded together at a place where there was a field full of lentils”. The Douay has “in a troop: for there was a field full of lentils”. 1 Chronicles 11:13 has barley. The verb “delivered”, vayyatsilehah, is natsal; the ASV and the NIV have “defended it”. Shammah is the third of the Three, and the Three are not named again.

Verses 13–17 · 2 Samuel 23 The water of Bethlehem — the cave of Adullam, a garrison in David’s town, and a drink poured out to Jehovah

Adullam, the stronghold, Bethlehem. “And three of the thirty chiefs went down, and came in harvest time to David, to the cave of Adullam; and a troop of Philistines was camping in the valley of Rephaim. And David was then in the stronghold; and a garrison of the Philistines was then in Bethlehem”: the cave is where David escaped from Gath (1 Samuel 22:1), and “the stronghold” and the valley of Rephaim are in the Philistine wars of 2 Samuel 5:17–18 (all now on these pages); “stronghold” is metsudah (see metsudah). The ASV has “three of the thirty chief men”. The NIV has “three of the thirty chief warriors”. The NWT 1984 has “three of the thirty head ones” and “the place hard to approach”. The Douay has “the three who were princes among the thirty”. “Three of the thirty” is written shloshim and read shelosha. The verse says “then” twice, and does not say which year.

Who will give me water? “And David longed, and said: ‘Who will give me water to drink from the cistern of Bethlehem, which is at the gate?’”: the word is bor, a cistern. The ASV has “Oh that one would give me water to drink of the well of Beth-lehem”. The NIV has “Oh, that someone would get me a drink of water from the well near the gate of Bethlehem!”. The NWT 1984 has “O that I might have a drink of the water from the cistern”. The Douay has “O that some man would get me a drink of the water out of the cistern”. The text does not say that the three heard him, or that he said it to anyone.

Poured out to Jehovah. “And the three mighty men broke through the camp of the Philistines, and drew water from the cistern of Bethlehem… but he would not drink it, and poured it out to Jehovah”: “and they drew water” stands in three verses: Israel at Mizpah, who “drew water and poured it out before Jehovah” (1 Samuel 7:6, now on these pages), this one, and 1 Chronicles 11:18. The ASV has “but poured it out unto Jehovah”. The NIV has “But he refused to drink it”. The NWT 1984 has “he did not consent to drink it, but poured it out to Jehovah”. The Douay has “but offered it to the Lord”.

Far be it from me. “Far be it from me, Jehovah, that I should do this! Is it not the blood of the men who went at the risk of their lives?”: “that I should do this”, me’asoti zot, stands only here, and it is a chalilah (see chalilah) with Jehovah’s name in the middle of it. The ASV has “Be it far from me, O Jehovah, that I should do this: shall I drink the blood of the men that went in jeopardy of their lives?”. The NIV has “Is it not the blood of men who went at the risk of their lives?”. The NWT 1984 has “It is unthinkable on my part, O Jehovah, that I should do this!”. The TLB has “This is the blood of these men who have risked their lives”. The law is that “the blood is the life” (Deuteronomy 12:23, now on these pages); the Hebrew says “at the risk of their lives” as benafshotam, “with their souls”. Rahlfs’ Greek begins hileōs moi kyrie, “be gracious to me, Lord”, the words Joab’s answer to the woman of Abel had in the Greek (20:20).

Verses 18–23 · 2 Samuel 23 Abishai, chief of the three, and Benaiah — a lion in a pit on a day of snow, an Egyptian and his own spear

Abishai. “And Abishai, brother of Joab, son of Zeruiah, was head of the three; and he brandished his spear over three hundred slain, and had a name among the three. He was the most honored of the three, and became their captain; but he did not come to the three”: the verb is orer, “brandished”. The ASV has “he lifted up his spear against three hundred and slew them” and “Was he not most honorable of the three?”. The NIV has “He raised his spear against three hundred men, whom he killed”. The NWT 1984 has “he was the head of the thirty” and “he had a reputation like the three”. The Douay has “the noblest of three, and was their chief, but to the three first he attained not”. The first “three” is written hashloshah and read hashelishi. It is the Abishai who helped David against Ishbi-benob (2 Samuel 21:17, now on these pages).

Benaiah, and the two Ariels. “And Benaiah son of Jehoiada, son of a man of valor, great in deeds, from Kabzeel: he struck the two Ariels of Moab; and he went down and struck the lion in the midst of the pit on a day of snow”: the Hebrew is sheney Ari’el Mo’av, and the text does not say what the two Ariels were. The ASV has “the two sons of Ariel of Moab”. The NIV has “Moab’s two mightiest warriors”. The TLB has “two giants, sons of Ariel of Moab”. The Douay has “the two lions of Moab”. For the pit the ASV has “a lion in the midst of a pit in time of snow” and the NIV “a pit on a snowy day”. “Son of a man of valor” is written ben-ish-chai and read ben-ish-chayil.

The Egyptian and his spear. “And he struck an Egyptian, a man of appearance; and in the Egyptian’s hand was a spear; and he went down to him with a rod, and snatched the spear from the Egyptian’s hand, and killed him with his own spear”: ish mar’eh, “a man of appearance”. The ASV has “a goodly man” and “with a staff”. The NIV has “a huge Egyptian” and “with a club”. The NWT 1984 has “of extraordinary size” and “with a rod”. The TLB has “armed only with a staff”. The Douay has “a man worthy to be a sight”. Rahlfs’ Greek says the Egyptian’s spear was “like a beam of a ladder”, and 1 Chronicles 11:23 says he was five cubits high and the spear “like a weaver’s beam”; the Hebrew here has neither.

Over his guard. “He was more honored than the thirty, but he did not come to the three; and David set him over his guard”: the word is mishma’to. The ASV has “set him over his guard”. The NIV has “put him in charge of his bodyguard”. The NWT 1984 has “appointed him to his own bodyguard”. The TLB has “chief of his bodyguard”. The Douay has “made him of his privy council”. It is the Benaiah who is over the Cherethites and the Pelethites (2 Samuel 8:18; 20:23, both now on these pages).

Verses 24–31 · 2 Samuel 23 The Thirty, first part — Asahel and Elhanan, three men named Ira, two Benaiahs, and an Ittai of Gibeah

Asahel and Elhanan. “Asahel, brother of Joab, was among the thirty; Elhanan son of Dodo of Bethlehem”: Asahel was killed by Abner early in the book (2 Samuel 2:18–23, now on these pages), so the list is of David’s whole reign and not of one day. Elhanan “son of Dodo of Bethlehem” is not the Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim the Bethlehemite who struck down Goliath the Gittite (2 Samuel 21:19) by the Hebrew’s own words, though the page does not say the two are different men. The ASV has “Asahel the brother of Joab was one of the thirty”. The NIV has “Among the Thirty were: Asahel the brother of Joab”. The NWT 1984 has “was among the thirty”.

Ira, Mebunnai. “Helez the Paltite, Ira son of Ikkesh the Tekoite; Abiezer the Anathothite, Mebunnai the Hushathite”: this is the third man called Ira in the book, after Ira the Jairite (2 Samuel 20:26) and before Ira the Ithrite (verse 38). Tekoa is the town of the wise woman (2 Samuel 14:2, now on these pages). “Mebunnai the Hushathite” is the Hebrew; the NIV has “Sibbekai the Hushathite”, the name of the man who struck down Saph (2 Samuel 21:18), and 1 Chronicles 11:29 has Sibbecai the Hushathite. The ASV has “Mebunnai the Hushathite”.

Heleb, Ittai, the two Benaiahs. “Heleb son of Baanah the Netophathite, Ittai son of Ribai of Gibeah of the sons of Benjamin; Benaiah a Pirathonite, Hiddai of the torrent-valleys of Gaash”: the NIV has “Heled” and “Ithai”, the spellings of Chronicles. This Ittai is of Saul’s Gibeah of Benjamin, and is not Ittai the Gittite who crossed with the king (2 Samuel 15:19, now on these pages). This Benaiah is a Pirathonite, and is not the son of Jehoiada. The ASV has “Ittai the son of Ribai of Gibeah of the children of Benjamin” and “Hiddai of the brooks of Gaash”. The NWT 1984 has “Hiddai of the torrent valleys of”.

Where they came from. The names carry their towns and clans: Harod, Palti, Tekoa, Anathoth, Hushah, Ahoah, Netophah, Gibeah, Pirathon, the torrent-valleys of Gaash. The clans and towns are named and the men are not described, and the verses do not say what any of them did. “Azmaveth the Barhumite” and “Abi-albon the Arbathite” (verse 31) end the first part of the list.

Verses 32–39 · 2 Samuel 23 The Thirty, second part — Eliam son of Ahithophel, Zelek the Ammonite, and Uriah the Hittite, “thirty and seven in all”

Jashen, Jonathan. “Eliahba the Shaalbonite, of the sons of Jashen, Jonathan; Shammah the Hararite, Ahiam son of Sharar the Ararite”: the Hebrew has no “son of” between Jonathan and Shammah. The ASV has “the sons of Jashen, Jonathan, Shammah the Hararite”. The NIV has “Jonathan son of Shammah the Hararite”, and 1 Chronicles 11:34 has “Jonathan the son of Shageh the Hararite”. Whether Jashen is a man, and whether Jonathan and Shammah are two men, changes the count.

Eliam son of Ahithophel. “Eliphelet son of Ahasbai, son of the Maacathite, Eliam son of Ahithophel the Gilonite”: Ahithophel the Gilonite was David’s counselor who went over to Absalom (2 Samuel 15:12, now on these pages), and Bathsheba is “daughter of Eliam, wife of Uriah the Hittite” (2 Samuel 11:3). The page says both and does not say that this Eliam is Bathsheba’s father. The ASV has “Eliam the son of Ahithophel the Gilonite”. The NIV has “Eliam son of Ahithophel the Gilonite”. The NWT 1984 has “the son of Ahithophel”.

Hezrai to Gareb. “Hezrai the Carmelite, Paarai the Arbite; Igal son of Nathan of Zobah, Bani the Gadite; Zelek the Ammonite, Naharai the Beerothite, armor-bearer of Joab son of Zeruiah; Ira the Ithrite, Gareb the Ithrite”: “Hezrai” is written Hezro, the NIV has “Hezro”, and “armor-bearer” is written in the plural and read in the singular (the ASV has “armorbearers”). Zobah is Hadadezer’s kingdom (2 Samuel 8:3, now on these pages). For “Bani the Gadite” the NIV has “the son of Hagri”, and 1 Chronicles 11:38 has “Mibhar the son of Hagri”. Two names are of foreigners, Zelek the Ammonite and Uriah the Hittite.

Uriah, and the count. “Uriah the Hittite. Thirty and seven in all”: Uriah is the last name, and he is the man David sent to the front, who “also died” (2 Samuel 11:17, now on these pages) and the man Nathan said David struck with the sword (12:9). The ASV has “Uriah the Hittite: thirty and seven in all”. The NIV has “There were thirty-seven in all”. After verse 23 the names number thirty-one, if Jashen is not a man; with Josheb-basshebeth, Eleazar, Shammah, Abishai and Benaiah that is thirty-six, and with Jashen thirty-seven. The book that began with Saul’s fall ends its roll of heroes on the man David killed.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the last words”; “the utterance of the man”; “raised on high”; “the pleasant one of the songs of Israel”; “his word was upon my tongue”; “a ruler in the fear of God”; “an everlasting covenant”; “the worthless”; “the shaft of a spear”; “the three”; “the thirty”; “taunted”; “clung to the sword”; “a great salvation”; “a plot of the field”; “the stronghold”; “a garrison”; “the cistern of Bethlehem”; “poured it out to Jehovah”; “far be it from me”; “the blood of the men”; “brandished”; “had a name”; “the two Ariels”; “a man of appearance”; “a rod”; “over his guard”; and “thirty and seven in all”; with the written forms in the notes.

Echoes paid. Balaam’s oracle formula (Numbers 24:3, 15); the spirit that rushed on David (1 Samuel 16:13); the song and its rock (2 Samuel 22:1, 32, 47); the beliyaal of the thorns and the torrents (2 Samuel 22:5); Goliath’s spear-shaft and taunt (1 Samuel 17:7, 10; 2 Samuel 21:19, 21); Judah’s clinging to the king (2 Samuel 20:2); Jonathan’s “Jehovah worked a great salvation” (1 Samuel 19:5); Israel’s drawing and pouring water at Mizpah (1 Samuel 7:6); the blood that is the life (Deuteronomy 12:23); the cave of Adullam, the stronghold and the valley of Rephaim (1 Samuel 22:1; 2 Samuel 5:17–18); Abishai’s help and Benaiah’s guard (2 Samuel 21:17; 8:18; 20:23); Asahel’s death (2 Samuel 2:18–23); the three Iras (2 Samuel 20:26); Sibbecai and Elhanan (2 Samuel 21:18, 19); the wise woman’s Tekoa (2 Samuel 14:2); Ittai the Gittite (2 Samuel 15:19); Ahithophel the Gilonite, Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, and Uriah’s death (2 Samuel 15:12; 11:3, 17; 12:9); and Zobah (2 Samuel 8:3).

Echoes planted. The same roll with its other names in Chronicles, Jashobeam, Pas-dammim and barley, Sibbecai, Mibhar the son of Hagri, and the Egyptian five cubits high (1 Chronicles 11:10–47); Ephes-dammim (1 Samuel 17:1, already on these pages); the census of the next chapter and Jehovah’s being entreated for the land (2 Samuel 24:1, 25, now on these pages); Benaiah’s killing of Joab at the altar (1 Kings 2:28–34); and Solomon and the covenant of the house (1 Kings 8; Psalm 89). None of the others is translated here yet.
