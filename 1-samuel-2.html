Hannah's song at Shiloh: the God who breaks the bows of the mighty, makes the barren bear seven, brings down to Sheol and brings up, and lifts the horn of his anointed. Against it, Eli's sons, 'sons of worthlessness', take meat with a fork before the fat is burned and lie with the women at the door of the tent of meeting, while the boy Samuel ministers in a linen ephod. A man of God announces the end of Eli's house: both sons will die in one day, and God will raise up a faithful priest.
Late in the period of the Judges, in Samuel's boyhood at Shiloh.

12

13

Y el derecho de los sacerdotes respecto al pueblo era este: cuando alguno ofrecía un sacrificio, venía el criado del sacerdote mientras se cocía la carne, con el tenedor de tres dientes en la mano,

and he would strike it into the basin, or the kettle, or the cauldron, or the pot; all that the fork brought up the priest would take for himself. So they did to all Israel who came there, to Shiloh.note
y lo clavaba en el caldero, o en la olla, o en la marmita, o en el perol; todo lo que el tenedor sacaba, el sacerdote lo tomaba para sí. Así hacían con todo Israel que venía allí, a Silo.

Y si el hombre le decía: «Que hagan arder primero la grasa, y después toma para ti cuanto desee tu alma», él respondía: «No, me la darás ahora; y si no, la tomaré por la fuerza».
17

18

Pero Samuel servía delante de Jehová, siendo muchacho, ceñido de un efod de lino.

Y su madre le hacía un manto pequeño y se lo traía año tras año, cuando subía con su marido para ofrecer el sacrificio anual.
20

21

Y Elí era muy anciano; y oía todo lo que sus hijos hacían a todo Israel, y cómo se acostaban con las mujeres que servían a la puerta de la tienda de reunión.
23

Y les dijo: «¿Por qué hacen tales cosas? Porque oigo de sus malas obras por boca de todo este pueblo.
24

No, hijos míos; porque no es buena la fama que oigo que el pueblo de Jehová va divulgando.
25

Si un hombre peca contra otro hombre, Dios mediará por él; pero si un hombre peca contra Jehová, ¿quién intercederá por él?». Pero ellos no escuchaban la voz de su padre, porque a Jehová le agradó hacerlos morir.

Y el muchacho Samuel iba creciendo, y era bien visto tanto por Jehová como por los hombres.
27

And a man of God came to Eli and said to him: ‘Thus says Jehovah: Did I not reveal myself to the house of your father when they were in Egypt, belonging to the house of Pharaoh?note
Y vino un hombre de Dios a Elí y le dijo: «Así dice Jehová: ¿No me revelé yo a la casa de tu padre cuando estaban en Egipto, sometidos a la casa del faraón?
28
וּבָחֹר אֹתוֹ מִכָּל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל לִי, לְכֹהֵן, לַעֲלוֹת עַל-מִזְבְּחִי לְהַקְטִיר קְטֹרֶת לָשֵׂאת אֵפוֹד, לְפָנָי; וָאֶתְּנָה לְבֵית אָבִיךָ, אֶת-כָּל-אִשֵּׁי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.

29

¿Por qué dan coces contra mi sacrificio y contra mi ofrenda, que mandé para mi morada, y honras a tus hijos más que a mí, engordándose con lo primero de toda ofrenda de Israel mi pueblo?
30
לָכֵן, נְאֻם-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אָמוֹר אָמַרְתִּי, בֵּיתְךָ וּבֵית אָבִיךָ יִתְהַלְּכוּ לְפָנַי עַד-עוֹלָם; וְעַתָּה נְאֻם-יְהוָה חָלִילָה לִּי, כִּי-מְכַבְּדַי אֲכַבֵּד וּבֹזַי יֵקָלּוּ.
Therefore, declares Jehovah the God of Israel: I did say that your house and the house of your father would walk before me forever; but now, declares Jehovah, far be it from me! For those who honour me I will honour, and those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed.note
Por tanto, dice Jehová el Dios de Israel: Yo dije que tu casa y la casa de tu padre andarían delante de mí para siempre; pero ahora, dice Jehová, ¡lejos de mí! Porque a los que me honran honraré, y los que me desprecian serán tenidos en poco.
31
הִנֵּה, יָמִים בָּאִים, וְגָדַעְתִּי אֶת-זְרֹעֲךָ, וְאֶת-זְרֹעַ בֵּית אָבִיךָ--מִהְיוֹת זָקֵן, בְּבֵיתֶךָ.
Look — days are coming when I will cut off your arm and the arm of your father’s house, so that there will be no old man in your house.note
Mira: vienen días en que cortaré tu brazo y el brazo de la casa de tu padre, de modo que no haya anciano en tu casa.
32
וְהִבַּטְתָּ צַר מָעוֹן, בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר-יֵיטִיב אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל; וְלֹא-יִהְיֶה זָקֵן בְּבֵיתְךָ, כָּל-הַיָּמִים.
And you will look on distress in the dwelling, in all the good that he does for Israel; and there shall not be an old man in your house all the days.note
Y verás angustia en la morada, en todo el bien que él hace a Israel; y no habrá anciano en tu casa todos los días.
33
וְאִישׁ, לֹא-אַכְרִית לְךָ מֵעִם מִזְבְּחִי, לְכַלּוֹת אֶת-עֵינֶיךָ, וְלַאֲדִיב אֶת-נַפְשֶׁךָ; וְכָל-מַרְבִּית בֵּיתְךָ, יָמוּתוּ אֲנָשִׁים.
Yet any man of yours whom I do not cut off from my altar will be left to wear out your eyes and to grieve your soul; and all the increase of your house shall die as grown men.note
Pero el hombre tuyo a quien yo no corte de mi altar quedará para consumir tus ojos y entristecer tu alma; y toda la descendencia de tu casa morirá en la edad adulta.
34
וְזֶה-לְּךָ הָאוֹת, אֲשֶׁר יָבֹא אֶל-שְׁנֵי בָנֶיךָ--אֶל-חָפְנִי, וּפִינְחָס:  בְּיוֹם אֶחָד, יָמוּתוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם.
And this shall be the sign to you, which shall come upon your two sons, on Hophni and Phinehas: in one day the two of them shall die.note
Y esta te será la señal, lo que vendrá sobre tus dos hijos, sobre Ofni y Finees: en un mismo día morirán los dos.
35
וַהֲקִימֹתִי לִי כֹּהֵן נֶאֱמָן, כַּאֲשֶׁר בִּלְבָבִי וּבְנַפְשִׁי יַעֲשֶׂה; וּבָנִיתִי לוֹ בַּיִת נֶאֱמָן, וְהִתְהַלֵּךְ לִפְנֵי-מְשִׁיחִי כָּל-הַיָּמִים.
And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my soul; and I will build him a faithful house, and he shall walk before my anointed all the days.note
Y yo me levantaré un sacerdote fiel, que hará conforme a lo que está en mi corazón y en mi alma; y le edificaré una casa fiel, y andará delante de mi ungido todos los días.
36
וְהָיָה, כָּל-הַנּוֹתָר בְּבֵיתְךָ, יָבוֹא לְהִשְׁתַּחֲוֺת לוֹ, לַאֲגוֹרַת כֶּסֶף וְכִכַּר-לָחֶם; וְאָמַר, סְפָחֵנִי נָא אֶל-אַחַת הַכְּהֻנּוֹת--לֶאֱכֹל פַּת-לָחֶם.  {פ}
And everyone who is left in your house shall come to bow down to him for a piece of silver and a loaf of bread, and shall say: “Please attach me to one of the priest’s offices, that I may eat a morsel of bread.”’note
Y todo el que quede de tu casa vendrá a postrarse ante él por una moneda de plata y un pan, y dirá: “Te ruego que me agregues a alguno de los oficios del sacerdote, para que coma un bocado de pan”».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Hannah’s song — “my horn is lifted high”, “no rock like our God”, and “by him deeds are weighed” or “are not weighed”

“My horn.” Hannah, who left Shiloh in the last chapter with her arms empty, opens with a boast: “my heart exults in Jehovah; my horn is lifted high in Jehovah”. The horn, qeren, is the animal’s strength and its head held up, and the song closes on the same image, “he lifts high the horn of his anointed” (v. 10), so the poem begins and ends with a raised horn. “My horn is lifted high” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “My horn is exalted in Jehovah”; the NIV “in the Lord my horn is lifted high”; the TLB drops the horn, “How he has blessed me!”. Luke’s “horn of salvation” in the song of Zechariah takes up the image (Luke 1:69, now on these pages).

“No rock like our God.” “There is none holy like Jehovah, for there is none besides you; and there is no rock like our God.” Tsur, “rock”, is the name Moses’ song gives God, “The Rock — his work is perfect” (Deuteronomy 32:4, now on these pages), and the two songs share more than that word (v. 6 below). The Greek has no rock here: it reads “none righteous like our God”. The KJV has “neither is there any rock like our God”.

“Weighed” or “not weighed”. “Do not go on talking so proudly, so proudly”: the Hebrew doubles gevohah, “lofty”, as if mimicking the boasters. Then a written-and-read pair: the written text has lo, “not”, and the margin lo, “by him”, two words that sound alike. Read as written, “deeds are not weighed”, which some take as a question, “are not deeds weighed?”; read with the margin, “by him deeds are weighed”, as this translation has it. The ASV has “by him actions are weighed”; the NWT 1984 “by him deeds are rightly estimated”.

Verses 4–8 · The great reversals — “the barren has borne seven”, “Jehovah puts to death and brings to life”, Sheol, and “from the ash heap”

Reversal after reversal. The mighty’s bows break and the stumbling are “girded with strength”, chayil, the word that made Boaz “a mighty man of valour” (chayil); the full hire themselves out for bread and the hungry “have ceased”, a single verb with no object, which the versions fill out: the ASV “have ceased to hunger”, the NWT 1984 “actually cease [to hunger]”. Then Hannah’s own case: “even the barren has borne seven”. Seven is the full number, the number the women of Bethlehem used when they called Ruth “better to you than seven sons” (Ruth 4:15, now on these pages). Hannah will bear six in all (v. 21).

“Jehovah puts to death and brings to life.” “He brings down to Sheol and brings up.” The line is Moses’ song again, “I put to death, and I bring to life; I have wounded, and I heal” (Deuteronomy 32:39, now on these pages). In its setting it is about God’s power over every fortune, low and high, as the next verse spells out: “he brings low, he also lifts up”. The KJV has “bringeth down to the grave, and bringeth up”; the ASV “bringeth down to Sheol”.

“From the ash heap.” “He raises the poor from the dust; from the ash heap he lifts up the needy, to seat them with nobles.” The ashpot is the town’s refuse heap, where the destitute sat. The phrase “from the ash heap” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Psalm 113:7, which repeats this line almost word for word. The verse keeps dal, “poor”, and evyon, “needy”, apart, as this translation does throughout (evyon). It ends with “the pillars of the earth”; the word for “pillars”, spelled as here, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The KJV has “lifteth up the beggar from the dunghill”; the NWT 1984 “From the ashpit he lifts up a poor one”.

Verses 9–10 · “His king” and “his anointed” — a king before Israel has one, the Greek’s “let not the wise boast”, and the Magnificat

“Not by strength.” “The feet of his faithful ones he guards … for not by strength does a man prevail.” The Greek has a different verse here: “granting the vow to the one who vows, and he blessed the years of the righteous”, a line that fits Hannah’s own vow. In verse 10 it adds a whole sentence that is in Jeremiah 9:23–24: “let not the wise boast in his wisdom … but let the one who boasts boast in this, to understand and know the Lord”. Paul quotes the end of it, “Let the one who boasts, boast in Jehovah” (1 Corinthians 1:31, now on these pages).

“Those who contend with him.” The written Hebrew of verse 10 has the singular, “the one who contends with him … against him he thunders”; the margin reads plurals. The NIV reads Elyon, “the Most High”, for alaw, “against him”: “The Most High will thunder from heaven”. The NWT 1984 has “those contending against him will be terrified”, for a verb that means both “be shattered” and “be dismayed”.

“His king … his anointed.” The song ends: “he gives strength to his king, and lifts high the horn of his anointed”. Israel has no king yet; Judges closed on “there was no king in Israel” (Judges 21:25, now on these pages). “His anointed”, meshicho, stands in ten verses of the Hebrew Bible, and this is the first of them in the order of the Hebrew Bible; the next are Samuel’s own words about Saul in 1 Samuel 12, now on these pages. The anointed one here is the king, but the Greek has christou autou and the Douay, from the Latin, “the horn of his Christ”. Mary’s song in Luke takes Hannah’s for its pattern: “He has brought down rulers from thrones and lifted up the lowly; the hungry he has filled with good things” (Luke 1:52–53, now on these pages).

Verses 11–17 · Eli’s sons — “sons of worthlessness”, “the priests’ due”, the three-pronged fork, and the fat before the burning

“Sons of worthlessness.” The boy stays “ministering to Jehovah in the presence of Eli the priest”, and the next line turns on Eli’s own sons: “sons of worthlessness; they did not know Jehovah”. It is the label of the men of Gibeah (Judges 19:22, now on these pages), and Hannah had begged Eli not to take her for “a worthless woman” (1 Samuel 1:16, now on these pages): the word is beliyya’al both times. “Sons of worthlessness” stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible. The KJV keeps the Hebrew, “sons of Belial”; the ASV has “the sons of Eli were base men”; the NWT 1984 “good-for-nothing men”.

“The priests’ due.” The word for the priests’ practice is mishpat, the word the law uses for what the priests were owed: “this shall be the priests’ due from the people, from those who slaughter a sacrifice” — the shoulder, the cheeks and the stomach (Deuteronomy 18:3, now on these pages), and the breast and thigh of the peace offering (Leviticus 7:34, now on these pages). Here the due is whatever a three-pronged fork brings up out of the pot. The NWT 1984 has “the due right of the priests from the people”; the ASV “the custom of the priests”; the KJV “the priests’ custom”.

“Before they turned the fat into smoke.” Worse than the fork: the servant demands raw meat before the fat is burned. The law says “all the fat is Jehovah’s” (Leviticus 3:16) and “the priest shall turn the fat into smoke on the altar” first (Leviticus 7:31, both now on these pages). The worshipper asks only that the fat go first, and the servant says, “No, you shall give it now”. That “No” is the margin’s reading; the written text has “to him”. So “the men treated the offering of Jehovah with contempt”: the ASV has “the men despised the offering of Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “treated the offering of Jehovah with disrespect”.

Verses 18–21 · Samuel in a linen ephod — the little robe, “the loan that was lent to Jehovah”, and “Jehovah visited Hannah”

A linen ephod and a little robe. Between the scenes of the sons, the boy: “But Samuel was ministering before Jehovah, a boy, girded with a linen ephod.” “A linen ephod” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: Samuel here, the priests of Nob (1 Samuel 22:18), and David dancing before the ark (2 Samuel 6:14, and its parallel at 1 Chronicles 15:27), none of those translated here yet. The ephod and the me’il, the “robe”, are both garments of the high priest (Exodus 28:4, now on these pages), and Hannah sews her son a small one every year. “A little robe” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “a little robe”.

“The loan that was lent.” Eli blesses the couple: “May Jehovah give you offspring from this woman in place of the loan that was lent to Jehovah.” The noun and verb are sha’al again, “ask, lend”, which ran through the last chapter. The Hebrew verb is masculine, “which he lent”, read as “one lent”; the Greek has “which you lent”. The Douay, from the Latin, agrees with the Greek, “for the loan thou hast lent”; the NWT 1984 “in place of the thing lent, that was lent to Jehovah”; the ASV “for the petition which was asked of Jehovah”.

“Jehovah visited Hannah.” The verb is paqad, the verb of “Jehovah visited Sarah” (Genesis 21:1, now on these pages): three sons and two daughters follow. Josephus has “sons and three daughters” (Antiquities 5.347, numbered 5.10.3 in Whiston). Then “the boy Samuel grew up with Jehovah” — the line the chapter keeps returning to between the reports on Eli’s house (vv. 11, 18, 21, 26).

Verses 22–26 · The women at the door of the tent of meeting, “who will intercede for him?”, and Samuel growing “in favour”

The women who served at the door. The verb is tsava, “to serve, muster”. Eli hears “how they lay with the women who served at the door of the tent of meeting”. The same phrase names the women whose bronze mirrors made the laver, “the serving women who served at the door of the tent of meeting” (Exodus 38:8, now on these pages). The Greek lacks this clause, and its edition (Discoveries in the Judaean Desert XVII, 2005) reports that the Samuel scroll from Qumran, 4QSama, lacked it too. Josephus has it: the sons abused “the women who came for worship”, some by force and some by gifts (Antiquities 5.339, numbered 5.10.1 in Whiston). The KJV has “the women that assembled at the door of the tabernacle”; the ASV “the women that did service at the door of the tent of meeting”.

“Who will intercede for him?” Eli’s rebuke turns on one verb: “If a man sins against a man, God will mediate for him; but if a man sins against Jehovah, who will intercede for him?” Both are palal, the verb of Hannah’s prayer (“And Hannah prayed”, v. 1). “Will mediate for him”, u-filelo, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The word for the mediator is Elohim, which can mean God or, as some take it here, the judges: the KJV has “the judge shall judge him”, the ASV “God shall judge him”, the NWT 1984 “God will arbitrate for him”.

“Jehovah was pleased to put them to death.” “But they would not listen to the voice of their father” — the words of the Benjaminites who “would not listen to the voice of their brothers” over Gibeah (Judges 20:13, now on these pages). The reason given is blunt: “for Jehovah was pleased to put them to death”. The text sets the sons’ refusal and God’s decision side by side and does not say which comes first. The ASV has “because Jehovah was minded to slay them”; the NWT 1984 “because Jehovah was now pleased to put them to death”.

“In favour with Jehovah and with men.” Against that, “the boy Samuel went on growing, and was in favour both with Jehovah and with men”; the Hebrew says “and good”. Luke says the same of the boy Jesus, “in favour with God and with people” (Luke 2:52, now on these pages). The ASV has “increased in favor both with Jehovah, and also with men”; the NWT 1984 “growing bigger and more likable”.

Verses 27–36 · The man of God against Eli’s house — “why do you kick”, “those who honour me I will honour”, “a faithful priest”, and Abiathar

“Why do you kick?” An unnamed “man of God” brings the sentence. God chose Eli’s ancestor “to go up to my altar, to burn incense, to carry an ephod”, and gave the house “all the offerings by fire”; now: “Why do you kick at my sacrifice and at my offering … fattening yourselves on the first of every offering?” The verb “kick”, ba’at, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Moses’ song, “Jeshurun grew fat and kicked” (Deuteronomy 32:15, now on these pages), where it also follows getting fat. The Greek has “why have you looked on my incense … with a shameless eye”. The ASV has “Wherefore kick ye at my sacrifice”; the NIV “Why do you scorn my sacrifice”.

Heavy and light. “I did say that your house and the house of your father would walk before me forever”: the priesthood was given to Aaron’s sons “as an everlasting statute” (Exodus 29:9, now on these pages). “But now, declares Jehovah, far be it from me! For those who honour me I will honour, and those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed.” “Honour” is kabed, “to be heavy” (kavod), and “lightly esteemed” is qalal, “to be light”: Eli “honoured” his sons above God (v. 29), and his house will be made light. “Those who honour me I will honour” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “them that honor me I will honor, and they that despise me shall be lightly esteemed”.

The sentence on the house. “I will cut off your arm”, the house’s strength (the Greek reads “your seed”, the same Hebrew letters with other vowels); no old man in the house; “you will look on distress in the dwelling”; the one left at the altar will “wear out your eyes”, and the rest “shall die as grown men”, where the Greek has “by the sword of men”. The sign is Hophni and Phinehas: “in one day the two of them shall die” — Phinehas, the name of Aaron’s grandson, to whom God gave “a covenant of everlasting priesthood” (Numbers 25:13, now on these pages). For the “distress”, the KJV has “an enemy in my habitation”, the NWT 1984 “an adversary [in my] dwelling” and the ASV “the affliction of my habitation”; for the deaths the NWT 1984 follows the Greek, “by the sword of men”.

“A faithful priest … a faithful house.” “I will raise up for myself a faithful priest … and I will build him a faithful house, and he shall walk before my anointed all the days.” The adjective is ne’eman both times, from aman. “A faithful house” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, in Abigail’s words to David (1 Samuel 25:28), and in the prophet’s promise to Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:38), none of them translated here yet. The narrator of Kings marks the fulfilment when Solomon removes Abiathar from the priesthood “to fulfil the word of Jehovah which he spoke concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh” and puts Zadok in his place (1 Kings 2:27, 35). The ASV has “I will build him a sure house”; the NWT 1984 “a lasting house”. The last line pictures the survivors begging: “Please attach me to one of the priest’s offices, that I may eat a morsel of bread”; the word for the “piece” of silver, agorah, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “horn” for qeren, kept in verses 1 and 10 rather than turned into “strength”; “rock” for tsur, as in Deuteronomy 32; “Sheol” left untranslated; “poor” for dal and “needy” for evyon; “his anointed” for meshicho; “sons of worthlessness” for benei beliyya’al, as in Deuteronomy 13 and Judges 19–20; “the priests’ due” for mishpat ha-kohanim, as in Deuteronomy 18:3; “turn the fat into smoke” for qatar, as in Leviticus; “lent” for sha’al; “visited” for paqad; “kick” for ba’at, as in Deuteronomy 32:15; “honour” and “lightly esteemed” for kabed and qalal; and “faithful” twice for ne’eman.

Echoes paid. The song the last chapter promised is sung; Hannah’s “seven” answers Ruth’s “better than seven sons”; Moses’ song lends the Rock, “I put to death and I bring to life”, and “grew fat and kicked”; “sons of worthlessness” ties Eli’s sons to Gibeah; and the sha’al play of chapter 1 returns in “the loan that was lent”.

Echoes planted. The death of Hophni and Phinehas “in one day” and the loss of the ark in 1 Samuel 4; the house of Eli at Nob and Abiathar’s removal in 1 Kings 2; “his anointed” on Samuel’s lips in 1 Samuel 12; and “a faithful house” again for David in 1 Samuel 25. Of these, 1 Samuel 4 and 12 are now on these pages.

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 3 — “the word of Jehovah was rare in those days”: Jehovah calls the boy Samuel by name three times in the night, and Eli tells him to answer, “Speak, for your servant is listening”; the word that comes is the end of Eli’s house.
