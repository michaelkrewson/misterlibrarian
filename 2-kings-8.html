Elisha tells the Shunammite to leave before a famine of seven years, and when she comes back and cries to the king for her house and field, he is talking with Gehazi about all the great things Elisha has done, and she is brought in as the woman whose son Elisha brought to life; the king returns all that was hers. Elisha comes to Damascus, where Ben-hadad is sick; Hazael brings forty camels’ burden and asks whether the king will recover. Elisha says he will, and that Jehovah has shown him he will die, holds his face and weeps, and says Jehovah has shown him Hazael as king over Aram. The next day Hazael spreads a wet cloth over the king’s face and reigns in his place. Jehoram of Judah, whose wife is Ahab’s daughter, reigns eight years while Edom and Libnah rebel; his son Ahaziah reigns one year and goes with Joram of Israel to Ramoth-gilead, where Joram is wounded and goes to Jezreel to be healed.
The chapter dates the accession of Jehoram of Judah to the fifth year of Joram of Israel, and of Ahaziah of Judah to the twelfth. It does not date the Shunammite’s return, and it does not say how the seven years of famine relate to the famine of 2 Kings 4:38.

1
וֶאֱלִישָׁע דִּבֶּר אֶל-הָאִשָּׁה אֲשֶׁר-הֶחֱיָה אֶת-בְּנָהּ לֵאמֹר, קוּמִי וּלְכִי אתי אַתְּ וּבֵיתֵךְ, וְגוּרִי, בַּאֲשֶׁר תָּגוּרִי:  כִּי-קָרָא יְהוָה לָרָעָב, וְגַם-בָּא אֶל-הָאָרֶץ שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים.
And Elisha had spoken to the woman whose son he had brought to life, saying: “Rise and go, you and your household, and sojourn wherever you can sojourn; for Jehovah has called for a famine, and it has also come on the land for seven years.”note
Y Eliseo había hablado a la mujer a cuyo hijo había dado vida, diciendo: «Levántate y vete, tú y tu casa, y reside donde puedas residir; porque Jehová ha llamado al hambre, y también ha venido sobre la tierra por siete años».
2
וַתָּקָם, הָאִשָּׁה, וַתַּעַשׂ, כִּדְבַר אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים; וַתֵּלֶךְ הִיא וּבֵיתָהּ, וַתָּגָר בְּאֶרֶץ-פְּלִשְׁתִּים שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים.
And the woman rose and did according to the word of the man of God; and she went, she and her household, and sojourned in the land of the Philistines seven years.note
Y la mujer se levantó e hizo conforme a la palabra del varón de Dios; y se fue, ella y su casa, y residió en la tierra de los filisteos siete años.
3
וַיְהִי, מִקְצֵה שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים, וַתָּשָׁב הָאִשָּׁה, מֵאֶרֶץ פְּלִשְׁתִּים; וַתֵּצֵא לִצְעֹק אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֶל-בֵּיתָהּ וְאֶל-שָׂדָהּ.
And it happened at the end of seven years that the woman returned from the land of the Philistines; and she went out to cry to the king for her house and for her field.note
Y sucedió que al cabo de siete años la mujer volvió de la tierra de los filisteos; y salió a clamar al rey por su casa y por su campo.
4
וְהַמֶּלֶךְ, מְדַבֵּר אֶל-גֵּחֲזִי, נַעַר אִישׁ-הָאֱלֹהִים, לֵאמֹר:  סַפְּרָה-נָּא לִי, אֵת כָּל-הַגְּדֹלוֹת אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה אֱלִישָׁע.  {ס}
And the king was speaking with Gehazi, the young man of the man of God, saying: “Tell me, please, all the great things that Elisha has done.”note
Y el rey hablaba con Giezi, el criado del varón de Dios, diciendo: «Cuéntame, por favor, todas las grandes cosas que ha hecho Eliseo».
5
וַיְהִי הוּא מְסַפֵּר לַמֶּלֶךְ, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-הֶחֱיָה אֶת-הַמֵּת, וְהִנֵּה הָאִשָּׁה אֲשֶׁר-הֶחֱיָה אֶת-בְּנָהּ צֹעֶקֶת אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, עַל-בֵּיתָהּ וְעַל-שָׂדָהּ; וַיֹּאמֶר גֵּחֲזִי, אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ--זֹאת הָאִשָּׁה, וְזֶה-בְּנָהּ אֲשֶׁר-הֶחֱיָה אֱלִישָׁע.
And it happened, as he was telling the king how he had brought the dead to life, that look — the woman whose son he had brought to life was crying to the king for her house and for her field. And Gehazi said: “My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son, whom Elisha brought to life.”note
Y sucedió que, mientras él contaba al rey cómo había dado vida al muerto, mira, la mujer a cuyo hijo había dado vida clamaba al rey por su casa y por su campo. Y Giezi dijo: «Mi señor el rey, esta es la mujer, y este es su hijo, a quien Eliseo dio vida».
6
וַיִּשְׁאַל הַמֶּלֶךְ לָאִשָּׁה, וַתְּסַפֶּר-לוֹ; וַיִּתֶּן-לָהּ הַמֶּלֶךְ סָרִיס אֶחָד לֵאמֹר, הָשֵׁיב אֶת-כָּל-אֲשֶׁר-לָהּ וְאֵת כָּל-תְּבוּאֹת הַשָּׂדֶה, מִיּוֹם עָזְבָה אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, וְעַד-עָתָּה.  {פ}
And the king asked the woman, and she told him. And the king appointed for her a court official, saying: “Restore all that was hers and all the produce of the field from the day she left the land until now.”note
Y el rey preguntó a la mujer, y ella se lo contó. Y el rey le puso un funcionario de la corte, diciendo: «Devuélvele todo lo que era suyo y todo el producto del campo desde el día en que dejó la tierra hasta ahora».
7
וַיָּבֹא אֱלִישָׁע דַּמֶּשֶׂק, וּבֶן-הֲדַד מֶלֶךְ-אֲרָם חֹלֶה; וַיֻּגַּד-לוֹ לֵאמֹר, בָּא אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים עַד-הֵנָּה.
And Elisha came to Damascus; and Ben-hadad king of Aram was sick; and it was told him, saying: “The man of God has come here.”note
Y Eliseo llegó a Damasco; y Ben-adad rey de Aram estaba enfermo; y se le informó, diciendo: «El varón de Dios ha venido aquí».
8
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-חֲזָהאֵל, קַח בְּיָדְךָ מִנְחָה, וְלֵךְ, לִקְרַאת אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים; וְדָרַשְׁתָּ אֶת-יְהוָה מֵאוֹתוֹ לֵאמֹר, הַאֶחְיֶה מֵחֳלִי זֶה.
And the king said to Hazael: “Take a gift in your hand and go to meet the man of God, and inquire of Jehovah through him, saying: Shall I recover from this illness?”note
Y el rey dijo a Hazael: «Toma un regalo en tu mano y ve a encontrar al varón de Dios, y consulta a Jehová por medio de él, diciendo: ¿Sanaré de esta enfermedad?».
9
וַיֵּלֶךְ חֲזָאֵל, לִקְרָאתוֹ, וַיִּקַּח מִנְחָה בְיָדוֹ וְכָל-טוּב דַּמֶּשֶׂק, מַשָּׂא אַרְבָּעִים גָּמָל; וַיָּבֹא, וַיַּעֲמֹד לְפָנָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר בִּנְךָ בֶן-הֲדַד מֶלֶךְ-אֲרָם שְׁלָחַנִי אֵלֶיךָ לֵאמֹר, הַאֶחְיֶה מֵחֳלִי זֶה.
And Hazael went to meet him, taking a gift in his hand, every good thing of Damascus, forty camels’ burden; and he came and stood before him and said: “Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to you, saying: Shall I recover from this illness?”note
Y Hazael fue a su encuentro, llevando un regalo en su mano, todo lo bueno de Damasco, la carga de cuarenta camellos; y llegó y se puso delante de él, y dijo: «Tu hijo Ben-adad rey de Aram me ha enviado a ti, diciendo: ¿Sanaré de esta enfermedad?».
10
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו אֱלִישָׁע, לֵךְ אֱמָר-לא לוֹ חָיֹה תִחְיֶה; וְהִרְאַנִי יְהוָה, כִּי-מוֹת יָמוּת.
And Elisha said to him: “Go, say to him: You shall surely recover; but Jehovah has shown me that he shall surely die.”note
Y Eliseo le dijo: «Ve, dile: Sanarás sin falta; pero Jehová me ha mostrado que sin falta morirá».
11
וַיַּעֲמֵד אֶת-פָּנָיו, וַיָּשֶׂם עַד-בֹּשׁ; וַיֵּבְךְּ, אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים.
And he held his face steady and set it until he was ashamed; and the man of God wept.note
Y mantuvo firme su rostro y lo fijó hasta avergonzarse; y el varón de Dios lloró.
12
וַיֹּאמֶר חֲזָאֵל, מַדּוּעַ אֲדֹנִי בֹכֶה; וַיֹּאמֶר כִּי-יָדַעְתִּי אֵת אֲשֶׁר-תַּעֲשֶׂה לִבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל רָעָה, מִבְצְרֵיהֶם תְּשַׁלַּח בָּאֵשׁ וּבַחֻרֵיהֶם בַּחֶרֶב תַּהֲרֹג, וְעֹלְלֵיהֶם תְּרַטֵּשׁ, וְהָרֹתֵיהֶם תְּבַקֵּעַ.
And Hazael said: “Why does my lord weep?” And he said: “Because I know the evil that you will do to the sons of Israel: their strongholds you will set on fire, and their young men you will kill with the sword, and their little ones you will dash to pieces, and their pregnant women you will rip open.”note
Y Hazael dijo: «¿Por qué llora mi señor?». Y él dijo: «Porque sé el mal que harás a los hijos de Israel: sus fortalezas incendiarás, y a sus jóvenes matarás con la espada, y a sus pequeños estrellarás, y a sus mujeres embarazadas abrirás en canal».
13
וַיֹּאמֶר חֲזָהאֵל--כִּי מָה עַבְדְּךָ הַכֶּלֶב, כִּי יַעֲשֶׂה הַדָּבָר הַגָּדוֹל הַזֶּה; וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלִישָׁע, הִרְאַנִי יְהוָה אֹתְךָ מֶלֶךְ עַל-אֲרָם.
And Hazael said: “For what is your servant, the dog, that he should do this great thing?” And Elisha said: “Jehovah has shown me you as king over Aram.”note
Y Hazael dijo: «¿Pues qué es tu siervo, el perro, para que haga esta gran cosa?». Y Eliseo dijo: «Jehová me ha mostrado que tú serás rey sobre Aram».
14
וַיֵּלֶךְ מֵאֵת אֱלִישָׁע, וַיָּבֹא אֶל-אֲדֹנָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, מָה-אָמַר לְךָ אֱלִישָׁע; וַיֹּאמֶר, אָמַר לִי חָיֹה תִחְיֶה.
And he went from Elisha and came to his lord; and he said to him: “What did Elisha say to you?” And he said: “He told me you would surely recover.”note
Y se fue de Eliseo y vino a su señor; y este le dijo: «¿Qué te dijo Eliseo?». Y él dijo: «Me dijo que sanarías sin falta».
15
וַיְהִי מִמָּחֳרָת, וַיִּקַּח הַמַּכְבֵּר וַיִּטְבֹּל בַּמַּיִם, וַיִּפְרֹשׂ עַל-פָּנָיו, וַיָּמֹת; וַיִּמְלֹךְ חֲזָהאֵל, תַּחְתָּיו.  {פ}
And it happened on the next day that he took the cloth and dipped it in water and spread it over his face, and he died; and Hazael reigned in his place.note
Y sucedió al día siguiente que tomó el paño y lo mojó en agua y lo extendió sobre su rostro, y murió; y Hazael reinó en su lugar.
16
וּבִשְׁנַת חָמֵשׁ, לְיוֹרָם בֶּן-אַחְאָב מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, וִיהוֹשָׁפָט, מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה--מָלַךְ יְהוֹרָם בֶּן-יְהוֹשָׁפָט, מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה.
And in the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel — and Jehoshaphat being king of Judah — Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah began to reign.note
Y en el año quinto de Joram hijo de Acab rey de Israel —y siendo Josafat rey de Judá— comenzó a reinar Jehoram hijo de Josafat rey de Judá.
17
בֶּן-שְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁתַּיִם שָׁנָה, הָיָה בְמָלְכוֹ; וּשְׁמֹנֶה שנה שָׁנִים, מָלַךְ בִּירוּשָׁלִָם.
Thirty-two years old he was when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.note
De treinta y dos años era cuando comenzó a reinar, y reinó ocho años en Jerusalén.
18
וַיֵּלֶךְ בְּדֶרֶךְ מַלְכֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ בֵּית אַחְאָב--כִּי בַּת-אַחְאָב, הָיְתָה-לּוֹ לְאִשָּׁה; וַיַּעַשׂ הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
And he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for the daughter of Ahab was his wife; and he did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah.note
Y anduvo en el camino de los reyes de Israel, como había hecho la casa de Acab, porque la hija de Acab era su mujer; e hizo lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová.
19
וְלֹא-אָבָה יְהוָה לְהַשְׁחִית אֶת-יְהוּדָה, לְמַעַן דָּוִד עַבְדּוֹ:  כַּאֲשֶׁר אָמַר-לוֹ, לָתֵת לוֹ נִיר לְבָנָיו--כָּל-הַיָּמִים.
But Jehovah was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David his servant, as he had said to him, to give him a lamp for his sons all the days.note
Pero Jehová no quiso destruir a Judá, por amor a David su siervo, como le había dicho, darle una lámpara para sus hijos todos los días.
20
בְּיָמָיו פָּשַׁע אֱדוֹם, מִתַּחַת יַד-יְהוּדָה; וַיַּמְלִכוּ עֲלֵיהֶם, מֶלֶךְ.
In his days Edom rebelled from under the hand of Judah and made a king over themselves.note
En sus días Edom se rebeló de debajo de la mano de Judá y puso un rey sobre sí.
21
וַיַּעֲבֹר יוֹרָם צָעִירָה, וְכָל-הָרֶכֶב עִמּוֹ; וַיְהִי-הוּא קָם לַיְלָה, וַיַּכֶּה אֶת-אֱדוֹם הַסֹּבֵיב אֵלָיו וְאֵת שָׂרֵי הָרֶכֶב, וַיָּנָס הָעָם, לְאֹהָלָיו.
And Jehoram crossed over to Zair, and all the chariotry with him; and he rose by night and struck Edom, who were surrounding him, and the commanders of the chariotry; and the people fled to their tents.note
Y Jehoram pasó a Zair, y todos los carros con él; y se levantó de noche e hirió a Edom, que lo rodeaba, y a los jefes de los carros; y el pueblo huyó a sus tiendas.
22
וַיִּפְשַׁע אֱדוֹם, מִתַּחַת יַד-יְהוּדָה, עַד, הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה; אָז תִּפְשַׁע לִבְנָה, בָּעֵת הַהִיא.
So Edom has been in rebellion from under the hand of Judah to this day. Then Libnah rebelled at the same time.note
Y Edom se ha rebelado de debajo de la mano de Judá hasta el día de hoy. Entonces Libna se rebeló en aquel mismo tiempo.
23
וְיֶתֶר דִּבְרֵי יוֹרָם, וְכָל-אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה:  הֲלֹא-הֵם כְּתוּבִים, עַל-סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים--לְמַלְכֵי יְהוּדָה.
And the rest of the acts of Jehoram, and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the acts of the days of the kings of Judah?note
Y lo demás de los hechos de Jehoram, y todo lo que hizo, ¿no está escrito en el libro de los hechos de los días de los reyes de Judá?
24
וַיִּשְׁכַּב יוֹרָם עִם-אֲבֹתָיו, וַיִּקָּבֵר עִם-אֲבֹתָיו בְּעִיר דָּוִד; וַיִּמְלֹךְ אֲחַזְיָהוּ בְנוֹ, תַּחְתָּיו.  {פ}
And Jehoram slept with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David; and Ahaziah his son reigned in his place.note
Y Jehoram durmió con sus padres y fue sepultado con sus padres en la ciudad de David; y Ocozías su hijo reinó en su lugar.
25
בִּשְׁנַת שְׁתֵּים-עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה, לְיוֹרָם בֶּן-אַחְאָב מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל, מָלַךְ אֲחַזְיָהוּ בֶן-יְהוֹרָם, מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה.
In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah began to reign.note
En el año doce de Joram hijo de Acab rey de Israel comenzó a reinar Ocozías hijo de Jehoram rey de Judá.
26
בֶּן-עֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁתַּיִם שָׁנָה, אֲחַזְיָהוּ בְמָלְכוֹ, וְשָׁנָה אַחַת, מָלַךְ בִּירוּשָׁלִָם; וְשֵׁם אִמּוֹ עֲתַלְיָהוּ, בַּת-עָמְרִי מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל.

De veintidós años era Ocozías cuando comenzó a reinar, y reinó un año en Jerusalén; y el nombre de su madre era Atalía, hija de Omri rey de Israel.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · 2 Kings 8:1–6 The woman whose son was brought to life — seven years away, a king who asks for the great things, and her field returned

⭐ “Whose son he had brought to life”. The verb is he’chayah, “made alive”, and it stands four times in these six verses: once in the narrator’s opening and three times in verse 5, twice in the narrator’s voice and once in Gehazi’s. It is the verb the king of Israel used of God at 2 Kings 5:7 (now on these pages): “Am I God, to kill and to make alive?” The ASV, the KJV and the Douay have “restored to life”, the NIV “restored to life” and “brought back to life”, the TLB “brought back to life”, and the NWT 1984 “revived”. “And Elisha had spoken” is in the past of the story: the ASV has “Now Elisha had spoken”, the NIV “Now Elisha had said”, and the KJV “Then spake Elisha”. Rashi says the words were spoken seven years before the scene at the king’s side, which is where the chapter picks the woman up again. The Hebrew line prints two forms for the first “you”, ati as written and at as read, the kind of pair 2 Kings 4 has several of (now on these pages).

“Sojourn wherever you can sojourn”. The famine is not a surprise to Jehovah’s prophet: “Jehovah has called for a famine, and it has also come on the land for seven years.” The NWT 1984 has “reside as an alien wherever you can reside as an alien”, the KJV and the ASV “sojourn wheresoever thou canst sojourn”, the NIV “stay for a while wherever you can”, and the TLB “move to some other country”. The choice of seven years of famine is Gad’s first offer to David (2 Samuel 24:13, now on these pages). She goes to “the land of the Philistines”, where Abraham “sojourned… many days” (Genesis 21:34, now on these pages). The verse does not say why she goes there and not elsewhere, and it does not say that the famine of 2 Kings 4:38 (now on these pages) is this one.

⭐ “All the great things that Elisha has done”. The king is talking with Gehazi, “the young man of the man of God”, and asks to be told; the king is not named, and the chapter does not say which king. The verse does not mention Gehazi’s disease, which 2 Kings 5:27 (now on these pages) said would cling to him and his seed. Rashi and Radak say the four men at the gate in 2 Kings 7:3 (now on these pages) were Gehazi and his sons; the verse here says nothing of when this conversation happens. Gehazi is telling “how he had brought the dead to life” when the woman cries out, “look”, to the king, “for her house and for her field”: her cry is the verb of the widow’s cry to Elisha at 2 Kings 4:1 and of the woman’s to the king at 2 Kings 6:26 (both now on these pages). Gehazi’s words are “this is the woman, and this is her son”. Josephus does not tell this story: his Elisha goes to Damascus right after the siege and the flight of the Arameans (Antiquities 9.87–88).

“A court official”. The king asks her, “and she told him”, and gives her one: the Hebrew word is saris, the title that also means “eunuch”. The KJV and the ASV have “a certain officer”, the NIV “an official”, the NWT 1984 “a court official”, the TLB “one of his officials”, and the Geneva and the Douay “an eunuch”. He is to return “all that was hers and all the produce of the field from the day she left the land until now”: the KJV and the ASV have “the fruits of the field”, the NIV “all the income from her land”, and the Douay “all the revenues of the lands”. The verse does not say who had her house and field in the seven years.

Verses 7–10 · 2 Kings 8:7–10 Elisha in Damascus — “Shall I recover from this illness?”, forty camels, and a message written one way and read another

⭐ “Shall I recover from this illness?” Ben-hadad is sick, and sends Hazael with “a gift in your hand” to “inquire of Jehovah through him”. “Shall I recover from this illness?”, ha-echyeh me-choli zeh, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, verses 8 and 9, both here. It is Ahaziah’s question in 2 Kings 1:2 (now on these pages), put to Baal-zebub, with the question marker ha- where that verse has im, “whether”. There Elijah’s answer was “you shall surely die” (2 Kings 1:4), and “you shall surely die” stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, three of them in 2 Kings 1. The KJV has “Shall I recover of this disease?”, the ASV “Shall I recover of this sickness?”, the NIV “Will I recover from this illness?”, the Douay “Can I recover of this my illness?”, and the NWT 1984 “Shall I revive from this sickness?”. The verb is chayah, “live”, the verb of verses 1 and 5; “you shall surely live”, chayo tichyeh, stands in two verses, here at verses 10 and 14.

“Forty camels’ burden”. The gift is “every good thing of Damascus”, forty camels’ worth. Radak says this is hyperbole, and adds that the Rabbis say it was one jewel worth all the good of Damascus. Josephus has Hazael bring forty camels carrying the finest products of Damascus (Antiquities 9.89). The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “the load of forty camels” too. Whether Elisha accepted it the verse does not say; at 2 Kings 5:16 (now on these pages) he refused a gift from another commander. Hazael stands “before him”, lefanav, and says “Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to you”. The KJV and the ASV have “Thy son”, the NWT 1984 “Your son”, and the NIV “Your son… has sent me to ask”.

⚠ “Say to him: You shall surely recover”. The Hebrew line prints lo twice in a row, written and read: written with an aleph, lo, “not”, and read with a vav, lo, “to him”. Read “to him”, Elisha says “Go, say to him: You shall surely recover”; written, the clause reads “Go, say: Not — you shall surely live”. Radak says it is written with an aleph because Elisha’s mind was that he would not live, and read with a vav because he told Hazael to say that he would live; Rashi reads “you shall surely live” as said of Hazael, who would live to take Ben-hadad’s throne. This page prints the reading, as the English shelf does. The ASV has “Go, say unto him, Thou shalt surely recover”, the KJV “Thou mayest certainly recover”, the NIV “You will certainly recover”, the NWT 1984 “You will positively revive”, and the TLB “Tell him, ‘Yes.’” The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “you shall surely live”. Then comes “but Jehovah has shown me that he shall surely die”: the verb is hir’ani, “has shown me”, which is the reverse of 2 Kings 4:27 (now on these pages), “Jehovah has hidden it from me and has not told me.”

Verses 11–15 · 2 Kings 8:11–15 A face held steady, “your servant, the dog”, and a wet cloth

⚠ “He held his face steady and set it until he was ashamed”. The Hebrew does not say who is looking or who is ashamed. The NIV has “He stared at him with a fixed gaze until Hazael was embarrassed”, the TLB “Elisha stared at Hazael until he became embarrassed”, the Geneva “till Hazael was ashamed”, and the Douay “was troubled so far as to blush”. The KJV and the ASV keep the ambiguity: “until he was ashamed”. Rashi reads it as Elisha holding back his grief, and making himself stand and control himself, so as not to weep before Hazael; Radak has him turn his face aside and put his hands on it, holding back until he could not, and weep. The NWT 1984 has “kept a fixed look and kept it set to the point of embarrassment”. Then “the man of God wept”.

⭐ “The evil that you will do”. Elisha tells Hazael why: “their strongholds you will set on fire, and their young men you will kill with the sword, and their little ones you will dash to pieces, and their pregnant women you will rip open.” Rashi glosses “dash” as splitting, from “bows will split young men” (Isaiah 13:18), and Radak says it may be dashing against a wall or the ground. The ASV has “dash in pieces their little ones, and rip up their women with child”, the NIV “dash their little children to the ground, and rip open their pregnant women”, the TLB “dash their babies against the rocks, and rip open the bellies of the pregnant women”, and the NWT 1984 “their children you will dash to pieces, and their pregnant women you will rip up”. The same deed is told of Ammon at Gilead (Amos 1:13, now on these pages), and Amos’s first oracle names Damascus: “they threshed Gilead with sledges of iron… I will send fire into the house of Hazael” (Amos 1:3–4, now on these pages). Josephus has Elisha say the same of the people’s best men, strongest cities, children and pregnant women (Antiquities 9.91).

⭐ “What is your servant, the dog?” “Your servant, the dog” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. A dog is what David calls himself to Saul, “a dead dog” (1 Samuel 24:14), and Mephibosheth to David (2 Samuel 9:8), all now on these pages. “This great thing” is the “great thing” the servants put to Naaman (2 Kings 5:13, now on these pages). The ASV has “who is but a dog”, the KJV “is thy servant a dog”, the NIV “a mere dog”, and the NWT 1984 “[who is a mere] dog”. Elisha’s answer is the same word as verse 10: “Jehovah has shown me you as king over Aram.” The verse does not say that anyone anointed him; at 1 Kings 19:15 (now on these pages) Jehovah told Elijah to do that.

⚠ “He took the cloth”. Hazael goes to his lord and reports half of what he heard: “He told me you would surely recover.” The word ha-makber stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “the coverlet”, the KJV and the NIV “a thick cloth”, and the Douay “a blanket”. The Targum, in Rashi’s words, has the word for a blanket, and Rashi sets beside it the cloak with which Jael covered Sisera (Judges 4:18, now on these pages). Radak says it is a cushion, and that Hazael told the king it was for relief and did it to cool him and kill him. The Hebrew says only “he took”, and the TLB supplies “Hazael”. Josephus has Hazael throw a wet net over Adar and strangle him (Antiquities 9.92). “And Hazael reigned in his place.”

Verses 16–24 · 2 Kings 8:16–24 Jehoram of Judah — a daughter of Ahab, a lamp for David, and Edom out from under Judah’s hand

⚠ Two kings with one name. The new king of Judah is Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat, and the king of Israel is Joram son of Ahab; the Hebrew prints both names for each of them, Yehoram and Yoram, and this page calls the king of Israel Joram and the king of Judah Jehoram. The verse puts the accession “in the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel — and Jehoshaphat being king of Judah”, an odd clause that the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has too; Radak gives it a long note about how the years are counted. Verse 17 has the chapter’s third pair: shanah as written and shanim as read, in “he reigned eight years”. The ASV and the KJV have “Thirty and two years old was he”, and the NIV “thirty-two years old”. The NWT 1984 calls the king of Israel “Jehoram” too, and the KJV has “Joram” for both kings in verses 21 to 29.

⭐ “The daughter of Ahab was his wife”. “He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for the daughter of Ahab was his wife”: the northern house has reached the Davidic one by marriage. She is named at verse 26 as Athaliah, “daughter of Omri”; the verse does not explain why Ahab’s daughter is Omri’s. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “Gotholia daughter of Ambri”. Josephus has her teach her husband to worship foreign gods (Antiquities 9.96). Josephus also says that he killed his brothers and was buried outside the royal tombs, and has a letter from Elijah; none of that is in this chapter.

⭐ “A lamp for his sons all the days”. “But Jehovah was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David his servant, as he had said to him, to give him a lamp for his sons all the days.” The promise of a lamp for David stands in 1 Kings 11:36, 1 Kings 15:4, this verse and 2 Chronicles 21:7, and the first two are now on these pages (1 Kings 11:36, 15:4). Rashi says the word, nir, means dominion, and the Targum has “his kingdom”; Radak says it is the same as ner, “lamp”, and has the same Targum. The ASV and the NWT 1984 have “a lamp”, the NIV “a lamp”, and the KJV, the Geneva and the Douay “a light”.

⚠ “Edom rebelled… and made a king over themselves”. Edom had “no king… an officer was king” in 1 Kings 22:48, and “the king of Edom” went out with Israel and Judah in 2 Kings 3:9 (both now on these pages); here it makes one. “Jehoram crossed over to Zair, and all the chariotry with him; and he rose by night and struck Edom, who were surrounding him, and the commanders of the chariotry; and the people fled to their tents.” The Hebrew does not say who won: Rashi reads “who were surrounding him” as those near the border, and Radak, as one reading, has the Edomite king strike the Edomites who had not joined the revolt; Radak says Zair is a city of Edom. Josephus has Jehoram go into Edom by night and kill those around his kingdom and go no further, and says it did him no good (Antiquities 9.97–98). The verse says “Edom has been in rebellion… to this day. Then Libnah rebelled at the same time.” The ASV and the KJV have “Edom revolted”, the NIV “Edom rebelled”, and the NWT 1984 “Edom revolted… and then made a king reign over them”. The NIV has Jehoram break through by night and his army flee, and the ASV has him smite the Edomites.

The book of the acts of the days of the kings of Judah. The closing formula is the one used for Ahab (1 Kings 22:39, now on these pages), here for Judah. Jehoram “slept with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the city of David; and Ahaziah his son reigned in his place”.

Verses 25–29 · 2 Kings 8:25–29 Ahaziah of Judah — a son-in-law of Ahab’s house, Ramoth-gilead again, and two sick kings at Jezreel

“Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah”. The new king is twenty-two when he begins and reigns one year, “and his mother’s name was Athaliah”. Radak notes that the Chronicles’ text says forty-two, and that his father lived only forty years in all (thirty-two at the start, eight years), so forty-two cannot stand; the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has twenty-two here, as the Hebrew does. The KJV and the ASV have “Two and twenty years old”, the NIV “twenty-two years old”, and the NWT 1984 “Twenty-two years old”. The KJV, the ASV and the Geneva have “daughter of Omri” for his mother Athaliah, and the Douay “daughter of Amri”. The NIV and the NWT 1984 have “granddaughter of Omri”, and the TLB “granddaughter of King Omri”. Omri was Ahab’s father, and verse 18 gives her husband’s wife as Ahab’s daughter. He is dated “in the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab”; 2 Kings 9:29 (now on these pages) has “the eleventh year”. The NIV has “related by marriage to Ahab’s family” for the Hebrew chatan, “son-in-law”, where the KJV, the ASV and the Douay have “son in law”, and the NWT 1984 “a relative… by marriage”.

⭐ “To war against Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead”. Ahaziah goes with Joram, and “the Arameans struck Joram”. Ramoth-gilead is where Joram’s father Ahab went up with Jehoshaphat and was killed (1 Kings 22:29, now on these pages), and Hazael is now called “king of Aram”. Verse 29 calls the place Ramah; the KJV and the ASV have “Ramah”, the NIV “Ramoth”, and the NWT 1984 “Ramah”, and the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “Remmoth” in both verses. The KJV has “Syria” and the NWT 1984 “Syria”, where the NIV has “Aram”. Josephus has Joram, after Adar’s death, besiege the city, be shot with an arrow, go to Jezreel to be healed, and leave Jehu in command (Antiquities 9.105).

⭐ “To be healed in Jezreel”. The chapter ends at Jezreel, where Ahab’s house has its palace and where Naboth’s vineyard stood (1 Kings 21:1), and where Jehovah had said the dogs would eat Jezebel (1 Kings 21:23), both now on these pages. “Joram the king returned to be healed… of the wounds that the Arameans had inflicted on him”, and “Ahaziah… went down to see Joram… for he was sick”. The Hebrew word for “sick” is choleh, the word with which verse 7 opens the Damascus scene, “Ben-hadad king of Aram was sick”. The KJV and the ASV have “because he was sick”, and the Douay “because he was sick”. The chapter began with a king who asked to hear all the great things Elisha had done, and ends with two kings together in a city with a vineyard.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “brought to life”, one verb at verses 1 and 5, as at 5:7; “cry to the king”, as at 4:1 and 6:26; “Shall I recover from this illness?”, as at 1:2; “Jehovah has shown me”, at verses 10 and 13, against 4:27; “your servant, the dog”; “this great thing”, as at 5:13; “the cloth”; “Joram” for the king of Israel and “Jehoram” for the king of Judah; “a lamp”, as at 1 Kings 11:36; “to this day”; “the book of the acts of the days of the kings of Judah”, as at 1 Kings 22:39; and “sick”, at verses 7 and 29. The written and read Hebrew are recorded at verses 1, 10 and 17.

Echoes paid. “Am I God, to kill and to make alive?” (2 Kings 5:7); the four men at the gate (2 Kings 7:3); Gehazi, and what would cling to him (2 Kings 5:27); the widow’s and the woman’s cries (2 Kings 4:1; 6:26); the woman’s son (2 Kings 4); Ahaziah’s question to Baal-zebub (2 Kings 1:2, 4); “Jehovah has hidden it from me” (2 Kings 4:27); the gift Elisha refused (2 Kings 5:16); Gad’s seven years of famine (2 Samuel 24:13); Abraham in the land of the Philistines (Genesis 21:34); Elijah told to anoint Hazael (1 Kings 19:15); the pregnant women of Gilead and the house of Hazael (Amos 1:13, 3–4); the dead dog (1 Samuel 24:14; 2 Samuel 9:8); Jael’s cloak (Judges 4:18); the lamp for David (1 Kings 11:36; 15:4); the officer who was king in Edom and the king of Edom (1 Kings 22:48; 2 Kings 3:9); Ahab’s book of acts (1 Kings 22:39); Ramoth-gilead, where Ahab died (1 Kings 22:29); and Naboth’s vineyard and Jezebel at Jezreel (1 Kings 21:1, 23).

Echoes planted. The eleventh year of Joram for Ahaziah’s accession (2 Kings 9:29, now on these pages); the wounded king at Jezreel and the king of Judah who has come down to see him (2 Kings 9:14–16, now on these pages); and Athaliah, Ahaziah’s mother (2 Kings 11:1).
