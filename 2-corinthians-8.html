The same letter, written from Macedonia. Paul tells the Corinthians of the generosity of the churches of Macedonia in their poverty, and urges them to finish the gift for the saints that they began a year before. He sends Titus with two brothers, one of them chosen by the churches, to receive the gift and to avoid any blame in administering it.
Undated by the text: the chapter names no year. It continues the letter of chapters 2 to 7 without a break; its one time-marker is the year since the Corinthians began the gift (8:10).

Now we want you to know, brothers, about the grace of God that has been given in the congregations of Macedonia.note
Hermanos, ahora queremos que sepan de la bondad inmerecida de Dios que se les ha mostrado a las congregaciones de Macedonia.

During a great test of affliction, their abundance of joy and their deep poverty overflowed into the riches of their generosity.note
En medio de una prueba severa de aflicción, su gran alegría y su profunda pobreza se desbordaron en riqueza de generosidad.
3
ὅτι κατὰ δύναμιν, μαρτυρῶ, καὶ παρὰ δύναμιν, αὐθαίρετοι

Porque doy fe de que dieron según sus posibilidades, y aun más allá de sus posibilidades, por propia voluntad,
4
μετὰ πολλῆς παρακλήσεως δεόμενοι ἡμῶν, τὴν χάριν καὶ τὴν κοινωνίαν τῆς διακονίας τῆς εἰς τοὺς ἁγίους—
begging us with much urging for the privilege of sharing in the relief for the holy ones.note
rogándonos con insistencia que les concediéramos el privilegio de participar en el socorro para los santos.
5
καὶ οὐ καθὼς ἠλπίσαμεν ἀλλʼ ἑαυτοὺς ἔδωκαν πρῶτον τῷ κυρίῳ καὶ ἡμῖν διὰ θελήματος θεοῦ,
And they did more than we had hoped: they first gave themselves to the Lord and to us, by God’s will.note
Y no hicieron solo lo que esperábamos: primero se dieron a sí mismos al Señor y a nosotros, por la voluntad de Dios.
6
εἰς τὸ παρακαλέσαι ἡμᾶς Τίτον ἵνα καθὼς προενήρξατο οὕτως καὶ ἐπιτελέσῃ εἰς ὑμᾶς καὶ τὴν χάριν ταύτην·
So we encouraged Titus that, just as he had started this work among you, he should also complete this gift on your part.note
Así que animamos a Tito a que, así como había comenzado esta obra entre ustedes, también la completara con este donativo de ustedes.
7
ἀλλʼ ὥσπερ ἐν παντὶ περισσεύετε, πίστει καὶ λόγῳ καὶ γνώσει καὶ πάσῃ σπουδῇ καὶ τῇ ἐξ ἡμῶν ἐν ὑμῖν ἀγάπῃ, ἵνα καὶ ἐν ταύτῃ τῇ χάριτι περισσεύητε.
But as you abound in everything, in faith and word and knowledge and all earnestness and in our love for you, abound in this gift as well.note
Pero, así como ustedes abundan en todo, en fe, palabra, conocimiento, todo empeño y en nuestro amor por ustedes, abunden también en este donativo.
8
Οὐ κατʼ ἐπιταγὴν λέγω ἀλλὰ διὰ τῆς ἑτέρων σπουδῆς καὶ τὸ τῆς ὑμετέρας ἀγάπης γνήσιον δοκιμάζων·
I say this not as a command, but to prove through the earnestness of others the genuineness of your love.note
No digo esto como mandato, sino para poner a prueba, mediante el empeño de otros, la sinceridad de su amor.
9
γινώσκετε γὰρ τὴν χάριν τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ, ὅτι διʼ ὑμᾶς ἐπτώχευσεν πλούσιος ὤν, ἵνα ὑμεῖς τῇ ἐκείνου πτωχείᾳ πλουτήσητε.
For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ: though he was rich, for your sake he became poor, so that you might become rich through his poverty.note
Porque conocen la bondad inmerecida de nuestro Señor Jesucristo: aunque era rico, se hizo pobre por ustedes, para que ustedes llegaran a ser ricos por medio de su pobreza.
10
καὶ γνώμην ἐν τούτῳ δίδωμι· τοῦτο γὰρ ὑμῖν συμφέρει, οἵτινες οὐ μόνον τὸ ποιῆσαι ἀλλὰ καὶ τὸ θέλειν προενήρξασθε ἀπὸ πέρυσι·
And in this I give my opinion: this is for your good, since a year ago you were the first to begin, not only to act but also to want to.note
Y en esto doy mi opinión: esto les conviene, pues hace un año fueron los primeros en comenzar, no solo en actuar sino también en desearlo.
11
νυνὶ δὲ καὶ τὸ ποιῆσαι ἐπιτελέσατε, ὅπως καθάπερ ἡ προθυμία τοῦ θέλειν οὕτως καὶ τὸ ἐπιτελέσαι ἐκ τοῦ ἔχειν.
Now finish the doing as well, so that, as there was readiness to want, there may also be completion from what you have.note
Ahora terminen también lo que hacen, para que, así como hubo prontitud en el deseo, haya también conclusión según lo que tienen.
12
εἰ γὰρ ἡ προθυμία πρόκειται, καθὸ ἐὰν ἔχῃ εὐπρόσδεκτος, οὐ καθὸ οὐκ ἔχει.
For if the readiness is there, it is acceptable according to what a person has, not according to what he does not have.note
Porque si la prontitud está, es aceptable según lo que uno tiene, no según lo que no tiene.
13
οὐ γὰρ ἵνα ἄλλοις ἄνεσις, ὑμῖν θλῖψις· ἀλλʼ ἐξ ἰσότητος
For I do not mean that others should be relieved while you are burdened;note
No digo que otros deban aliviarse mientras ustedes se cargan;
14
ἐν τῷ νῦν καιρῷ τὸ ὑμῶν περίσσευμα εἰς τὸ ἐκείνων ὑστέρημα, ἵνα καὶ τὸ ἐκείνων περίσσευμα γένηται εἰς τὸ ὑμῶν ὑστέρημα, ὅπως γένηται ἰσότης·
rather, by equality, your abundance at the present time should supply their need, so that their abundance may also supply your need, and there may be equality.note
más bien, por igualdad, lo que ustedes tienen de más en este momento supla su necesidad, para que lo que ellos tienen de más supla también la de ustedes, y haya igualdad.

Como está escrito: «El que recogió mucho no tuvo de más, y el que recogió poco no tuvo de menos».

Pero gracias a Dios, que puso el mismo interés sincero por ustedes en el corazón de Tito.

For he accepted our appeal, but being very eager, he is coming to you of his own accord.note
Pues aceptó nuestro ruego, pero, siendo muy entusiasta, viene a verlos por su propia voluntad.

And we are sending with him the brother whose praise in the good news is known through all the congregations.note
Y enviamos con él al hermano cuya alabanza por las buenas noticias es conocida en todas las congregaciones.

And not only that, but he was also appointed by the congregations to travel with us in this gift, which we administer to the glory of the Lord and to show our readiness.note
Y no solo eso, sino que también fue designado por las congregaciones para viajar con nosotros con esta donación, que administramos para la gloria del Señor y para mostrar nuestra buena disposición.

Hacemos esto para que nadie nos censure por esta generosa contribución que administramos.
21

For we take care to do what is honorable, not only in the sight of the Lord but also in the sight of people.note
Porque nos esforzamos por hacer lo honorable, no solo delante del Señor, sino también delante de la gente.

And we are sending with them our brother, whom we have often tested and found earnest in many matters, but now far more earnest because of his great confidence in you.note
Y enviamos con ellos a nuestro hermano, al que hemos probado muchas veces y hemos visto diligente en muchos asuntos, pero ahora mucho más diligente por la gran confianza que tiene en ustedes.

As for Titus, he is my partner and my fellow worker for you; as for our brothers, they are messengers of the congregations and the glory of Christ.note
En cuanto a Tito, es mi compañero y colaborador para con ustedes; en cuanto a nuestros hermanos, son mensajeros de las congregaciones y gloria de Cristo.

Verses 1–5 · the grace given in Macedonia, and the abundance of their poverty
“The grace of God given in the congregations.” tēn charin tou theou tēn dedomenēn en tais ekklēsiais tēs Makedonias, the grace of God given in the congregations of Macedonia. The word charis is the word for a gift and for the kindness behind it, and the same word names the collection. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “the undeserved kindness of God that has been granted to the congregations.” The ASV “the grace of God which hath been given in the churches of Macedonia.”

“Abundance of joy and deep poverty.” hē perisseia tēs charas autōn kai hē kata bathous ptōcheia autōn, their abundance of joy and their deep poverty. The verse puts the two side by side and says the first overflowed into the second. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “their abundance of joy and their deep poverty made the riches of their generosity abound.” The KJV “the abundance of their joy and their deep poverty abounded unto the riches of their liberality.”

Verses 6–8 · Titus to complete the gift, abounding in this grace also, and the commandment that is not a command
“Titus, who had started it.” eis to parakalesai hēmas Titon hina kathōs proenērxato houtō kai epiteleisē eis hymas, so that Titus, as he had started, would also complete among you. The verb proenarchomai is to begin before, and the verb epiteleō is to finish. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “we encouraged Titus that, just as he had initiated this work among you, he should also complete this same kind giving on your part.” The KJV “that we desired Titus, that as he had begun, so he would also finish in you the same grace also.”

“Abound in this grace also.” hōsper en panti perisseuete, as you abound in everything. The sentence sets the Corinthians’ abundance in faith and speech and knowledge against the grace of the gift, and says the gift is the same kind of abundance. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “just as you abound in everything, in faith and word and knowledge and all earnestness.” The ASV “But as ye abound in everything, in faith, and utterance, and knowledge, and in all earnestness.”

“Not as a command.” ou kat’ epitagēn legō, I do not say this as a command. The word epitagē is an order. The sentence says he is proving the genuineness of their love, not commanding it. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “I am saying this, not to command you.” The KJV “I speak not by commandment.”

Verse 9 · the grace of the Lord, rich and poor
“Though he was rich, he became poor.” epteōcheusen plousios ōn, though he was rich he became poor. The sentence names the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the gift the Corinthians are asked to make is set on his. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “although he was rich, he became poor for your sake, so that you might become rich through his poverty.” The ASV “though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might become rich.” The KJV “though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich.”

Verses 10–12 · the opinion given, the completing of the doing, and readiness by what a person has
“A year ago.” apo perysi, from last year. The verse says they were first to begin, and not only to do but also to want to. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “This is for your benefit, seeing that already a year ago you not only initiated the action.” The ASV “for this is expedient for you, who were the first to make a beginning a year ago.”

“Readiness.” hē prothymia, readiness or eagerness, the word of the will that comes before the gift. The verse says the readiness is acceptable by what a person has. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “for if the readiness is there first, it is especially acceptable according to what a person has.” The KJV “For if there be first a willing mind, it is accepted according to that a man hath.”

Verses 13–15 · not relief for others and burden for you, equality, and the manna of the one who gathered
“Not that others should be eased.” ou gar hina allois anesis, hymin thlipsis, for not that others should have relief and you affliction. The sentence says the gift is not a burden set on the Corinthians for others’ ease. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For I do not want to make it easy for others, but difficult for you.” The KJV “For I mean not that other men be eased, and ye burdened.”

“Equality.” isotēs, equality. The verse makes the balance the aim: the surplus of the one supplies the need of the other, and the reverse. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “so that their surplus might also offset your deficiency, that there may be an equalizing.” The ASV “that there may be equality.”

“He that gathered much.” The quotation in verse 15 is from the account of the manna in Exodus 16:18, and the wording follows the Septuagint of that place. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “The person with much did not have too much, and the person with little did not have too little.” The ASV “He that gathered much had nothing over; and he that gathered little had no lack.”

Verses 16–19 · the same earnest care in the heart of Titus, the brother sent with him, and the appointed companion
“The same earnest concern in the heart of Titus.” tēn autēn spoudēn hyper hymōn en tē kardia Titou, the same earnestness for you in the heart of Titus. Paul gives thanks to God for putting it there. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “putting the same earnest concern for you in the heart of Titus.” The KJV “which put the same earnest care into the heart of Titus for you.”

“Being very eager.” spoudaioteros hyparchōn, being more eager, and authairetos, of his own accord. Titus goes to them not because he is sent but because he chooses it. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “being very eager, he is coming to you on his own initiative.” The ASV “he went forth unto you of his own accord.”

Verses 20–24 · not to fault us, care for everything honestly, and show the congregations why we boast
“Care for everything honestly.” pronooumen kala ou monon enōpion kyriou alla kai enōpion anthrōpōn, we care for honorable things, not only in the sight of the Lord but also in the sight of people. The words are close to Proverbs 3:4 in the Septuagint. The NWT renders the Lord as “Jehovah,” in a quotation set off as a citation. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “we ‘care for everything honestly, not only in the sight of Jehovah but also in the sight of men.’” The KJV “Providing for honest things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men.”

“Why we boast about you.” endeiknymenoi eis prosōpon tōn ekklēsiōn, showing it before the faces of the congregations. The verse asks them to show the congregations what the boasting was for. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “show the congregations why we boasted about you.” The ASV “Show ye therefore unto them in the face of the churches the proof of your love.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “grace” for charis at 8:1, 8:4, 8:6, 8:7, 8:9 and 8:19, the same word for the favor of God and the gift; “congregations” for ekklēsiai at 8:1, 8:18, 8:19 and 8:23–24; “earnest” for spoudē at 8:7, 8:8, 8:16 and 8:22.

Echoes paid. The joy of 7:4 and 7:13 returns as the abundance of joy in 8:2; Titus comes back as the man who began the gift (8:6); the boasting of 7:14 returns as the boast the congregations are shown (8:24); and the Macedonians named at 7:5 are the ones who gave beyond their means.

Echoes planted. The gift is to be finished (8:11), and the next chapter takes the same collection up again. The chapter ends on the proof of love shown before the congregations, and the matter is not closed here.
