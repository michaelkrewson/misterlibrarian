Among the exiles: Ezekiel packs an exile's baggage by day, digs through the wall in the evening and carries it out in the dark with his face covered, as a portent of the prince in Jerusalem; he eats and drinks in anxiety; and he answers the sayings that every vision fails and that his visions are for far off.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · Ezekiel 12:1–2 eyes that do not see — the rebellious house

⭐ Eyes and ears. The house has eyes to see and does not see, ears to hear and does not hear. Isaiah was sent to a people to make their ears heavy and shut their eyes (Isaiah 6:9–10, now on these pages), and Deuteronomy said Jehovah had not yet given eyes to see and ears to hear (Deuteronomy 29:4). Psalm 115:5–6 says idols have eyes and do not see. In the Gospel the question is put to disciples (Mark 8:18, now on these pages).

⭐ The rebellious house. The phrase that has run since 2:5 (meri) is here twice in one verse.

Verses 3–7 · Ezekiel 12:3–7 baggage for exile — a hole in the wall — a face covered — a portent

⭐ An acted sign. By day he brings out an exile’s baggage (kelei golah); in the evening he goes out; he digs through the wall, carries on the shoulder in the dark, and covers his face. ‘Before their eyes’ comes seven times in verses 3–7.

⚠ Perhaps. ‘Perhaps they will see, ki they are a rebellious house.’ The word is usually ‘for’ and may here be ‘though’: the ASV has “it may be they will consider, though they are a rebellious house”, and the Douay “if so be they will regard it: for they are a provoking house”.

⚠ Bring out what? In verses 5, 6 and 7 ‘bring out’ has no object in Hebrew; the baggage of verse 4 is meant, and this translation leaves the gap. The darkness is alatah, ‘thick darkness’, a word that stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: Genesis 15:17 (now on these pages) and verses 6, 7 and 12 here. The KJV has “carry it forth in the twilight”.

⭐ A portent. Mofet (mofet), the word the pages print as ‘wonder’ before Pharaoh (Exodus 7:9). It is not the ‘sign’ of 4:3, which is another word. Ezekiel 24:24 (now on these pages) says Ezekiel will be a portent to them.

Verses 8–12 · Ezekiel 12:8–12 “What are you doing?” — this burden is the prince

⭐ The question. In the morning the word asks whether the house has said ‘What are you doing?’, and gives the answer.

⚠ Prince and burden. The Hebrew sets hannasi, ‘the prince’, beside hammassa, ‘the burden’ (massa); both are built on the verb nasa, to lift, which also gives ‘carry’ in verses 6, 7 and 12. How to join the words is open. The ASV has “This burden concerneth the prince in Jerusalem”, the NIV “This prophecy concerns the prince in Jerusalem”. This translation reads ‘this burden is the prince’. The TLB names a king, “King Zedekiah”; the verse names no one.

⭐ As I have done. The prophet is ‘your portent’: what he did will be done to them. Verse 12 repeats the actions one by one for the prince: carrying on the shoulder, the dark, the wall, the covered face. ‘They will dig’ is plural, and the NIV makes it “a hole will be dug in the wall for him to go through”. The clause about not seeing the land begins ‘since’; the ASV has “because he shall not see the land with his eyes”.

Verses 13–16 · Ezekiel 12:13–16 net and snare — to Babylon, which he will not see — a few left over

⭐ Brought, and not seeing. He is brought to Babylon and does not see it, and dies there. The verse does not explain how. 2 Kings 25:7 reports that Zedekiah’s eyes were put out and that he was carried to Babylon, after the fighting men had fled by night (2 Kings 25:4); Jeremiah 52:11 adds that he was kept in prison till his death. “He will be caught in my snare” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Ezekiel 17:20 (now on these pages).

⭐ Scattered. The sword drawn after them is the line of 5:12; “I will draw [the sword] after them” stands in three verses: 5:2, 5:12 and here. Verse 15 has two verbs, ‘disperse’ and ‘scatter’; the page of 36:19 prints the pair the other way round, ‘scattered’ for the first verb and ‘dispersed’ for the second. A few are left over, as at 6:8, to recount their abominations among the nations.

Verses 17–20 · Ezekiel 12:17–20 bread with quaking — water with horror — the land laid waste

⭐ Eating in fear. He is to eat with quaking and drink with trembling and anxiety, and say that Jerusalem will eat with anxiety and drink with horror. The pair of words is that of 4:16; “with anxiety” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, 4:16 and 12:18–19.

⚠ To, or about. Verse 19 says ‘to’ the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and the preposition can mean ‘concerning’: the ASV has “concerning the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and the land of Israel”. The land is ‘her’ land, the city’s, emptied of what fills it because of violence, chamas (chamas).

Verses 21–25 · Ezekiel 12:21–25 a proverb about vision — “the days have drawn near”

⭐ The proverb. ‘The days grow long, and every vision perishes’ (mashal). “This proverb” stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: Isaiah 14:4, where the page prints it ‘this taunt’, verses 22 and 23 here, and Ezekiel 18:2–3 (now on these pages). The answer reverses it: the days have drawn near, and ‘the word of every vision’, by which the ASV understands “the fulfilment of every vision” and the KJV “the effect of every vision”.

⚠ Empty and smooth. No more empty vision or miqsam chalaq, smooth divination (qesem). The ASV has “false vision nor flattering divination”.

⭐ Not delayed. “It will not be delayed” stands in two verses, 25 and 28 here. Habakkuk says of a vision that it ‘will not be late’ (Habakkuk 2:3, now on these pages), with another verb. The complaint that the promised thing does not come returns in 2 Peter 3:4 (now on these pages).

Verses 26–28 · Ezekiel 12:26–28 “for many days from now” — the word will be done

⭐ A second saying. The first proverb said vision fails; this one grants the vision and puts it far off. “For many days” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Daniel 8:26. The answer is the same: no word of mine will be delayed, and what I speak will be done. The verse, and the chapter, end with a paragraph break.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “rebellious house” at verses 2, 3, 9 and 25; “baggage” at verses 3, 4 and 7; “thick darkness” at verses 6, 7 and 12; “portent” at verses 6 and 11; “proverb” at verses 22 and 23; and “delayed” at verses 25 and 28.

Echoes paid. The eyes and ears of Isaiah 6; the anxiety and horror of 4:16; and the sword drawn after them from 5:12.

Echoes planted. The net and snare again at Ezekiel 17:20 (now on these pages). The prophet as a portent again at Ezekiel 24:24 (now on these pages).

Ezekiel 13, now on these pages: a word against the prophets of Israel who prophesy out of their own heart.
