Ahithophel asks Absalom for twelve thousand men to pursue David that night and strike the king alone, and the plan is right in the eyes of Absalom and the elders. Absalom calls Hushai the Archite, who says the counsel is not good this time and advises gathering all Israel from Dan to Beer-sheba with Absalom going into the battle himself; Absalom and every man of Israel say that Hushai’s counsel is better, because Jehovah had commanded to frustrate the good counsel of Ahithophel. Hushai tells Zadok and Abiathar, Jonathan and Ahimaaz carry the word from En-rogel and are hidden in a well at Bahurim, and David and all the people cross the Jordan before morning. Ahithophel, seeing that his counsel was not done, goes home, sets his house in order, strangles himself and is buried in the tomb of his father. David comes to Mahanaim; Absalom crosses the Jordan with Amasa over the army and camps in Gilead; and Shobi, Machir and Barzillai bring David beds, food and drink.
The night after 2 Samuel 16 and the morning that follows, with Absalom in Jerusalem and David east of it; then David at Mahanaim and Absalom’s army in Gilead.

1
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲחִיתֹפֶל, אֶל-אַבְשָׁלוֹם:  אֶבְחֲרָה נָּא, שְׁנֵים-עָשָׂר אֶלֶף אִישׁ, וְאָקוּמָה וְאֶרְדְּפָה אַחֲרֵי-דָוִד, הַלָּיְלָה.
And Ahithophel said to Absalom: ‘Let me choose, please, twelve thousand men, and let me arise and pursue after David tonight.note
Y Ahitofel dijo a Absalón: «Déjame escoger, por favor, doce mil hombres, y déjame levantarme y perseguir a David esta noche.
2
וְאָבוֹא עָלָיו, וְהוּא יָגֵעַ וּרְפֵה יָדַיִם, וְהַחֲרַדְתִּי אֹתוֹ, וְנָס כָּל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ; וְהִכֵּיתִי אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ, לְבַדּוֹ.
And I will come upon him while he is weary and weak of hands, and I will terrify him; and all the people who are with him will flee, and I will strike the king alone.note
Y caeré sobre él mientras está cansado y flojo de manos, y lo aterraré; y todo el pueblo que está con él huirá, y heriré al rey solo.
3
וְאָשִׁיבָה כָל-הָעָם, אֵלֶיךָ:  כְּשׁוּב הַכֹּל--הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה מְבַקֵּשׁ, כָּל-הָעָם יִהְיֶה שָׁלוֹם.
And I will bring back all the people to you — as all return, the man whom you seek — all the people will be at peace.’note
Y haré volver a ti a todo el pueblo, como vuelve el todo — el hombre a quien buscas —; todo el pueblo estará en paz».
4
וַיִּישַׁר הַדָּבָר, בְּעֵינֵי אַבְשָׁלֹם, וּבְעֵינֵי, כָּל-זִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {ס}
And the word was right in the eyes of Absalom, and in the eyes of all the elders of Israel.note
Y la palabra fue recta a los ojos de Absalón y a los ojos de todos los ancianos de Israel.
5
וַיֹּאמֶר, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, קְרָא נָא, גַּם לְחוּשַׁי הָאַרְכִּי; וְנִשְׁמְעָה מַה-בְּפִיו, גַּם-הוּא.
And Absalom said: ‘Call, please, Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear what is in his mouth, his also.’note
Y Absalón dijo: «Llamen, por favor, también a Husai el arquita, y oigamos qué hay en su boca, también en la suya».
6
וַיָּבֹא חוּשַׁי, אֶל-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וַיֹּאמֶר אַבְשָׁלוֹם אֵלָיו לֵאמֹר כַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה דִּבֶּר אֲחִיתֹפֶל, הֲנַעֲשֶׂה אֶת-דְּבָרוֹ; אִם-אַיִן, אַתָּה דַבֵּר.  {ס}
And Hushai came to Absalom, and Absalom said to him: ‘According to this word Ahithophel has spoken; shall we do his word? If not, you speak.’note
Y Husai vino a Absalón, y Absalón le dijo: «Conforme a esta palabra ha hablado Ahitofel; ¿haremos su palabra? Si no, habla tú».
7
וַיֹּאמֶר חוּשַׁי, אֶל-אַבְשָׁלוֹם:  לֹא-טוֹבָה הָעֵצָה אֲשֶׁר-יָעַץ אֲחִיתֹפֶל, בַּפַּעַם הַזֹּאת.
And Hushai said to Absalom: ‘The counsel that Ahithophel has counseled is not good this time.’note
Y Husai dijo a Absalón: «No es bueno el consejo que ha aconsejado Ahitofel esta vez».
8
וַיֹּאמֶר חוּשַׁי, אַתָּה יָדַעְתָּ אֶת-אָבִיךָ וְאֶת-אֲנָשָׁיו כִּי גִבֹּרִים הֵמָּה וּמָרֵי נֶפֶשׁ הֵמָּה, כְּדֹב שַׁכּוּל, בַּשָּׂדֶה; וְאָבִיךָ אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה, וְלֹא יָלִין אֶת-הָעָם.
And Hushai said: ‘You know your father and his men, that they are mighty men, and they are bitter of soul, like a bear bereaved in the field; and your father is a man of war, and he will not lodge with the people.note
Y dijo Husai: «Tú conoces a tu padre y a sus hombres, que son valientes, y están amargados de alma, como una osa privada de sus crías en el campo; y tu padre es hombre de guerra, y no pasará la noche con el pueblo.
9
הִנֵּה עַתָּה הוּא-נֶחְבָּא בְּאַחַת הַפְּחָתִים, אוֹ בְּאַחַד הַמְּקוֹמֹת; וְהָיָה, כִּנְפֹל בָּהֶם בַּתְּחִלָּה, וְשָׁמַע הַשֹּׁמֵעַ וְאָמַר, הָיְתָה מַגֵּפָה בָּעָם אֲשֶׁר אַחֲרֵי אַבְשָׁלֹם.
Look, he is hidden now in one of the pits, or in one of the places; and it will be, when there is a falling among them at the first, that whoever hears will hear, and say: “There has been a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom.”note
Mira, él está ahora escondido en una de las hoyas, o en uno de los lugares; y será que, cuando haya una caída entre ellos al principio, el que lo oiga lo oirá y dirá: “Ha habido una matanza entre el pueblo que sigue a Absalón”.
10
וְהוּא גַם-בֶּן-חַיִל, אֲשֶׁר לִבּוֹ כְּלֵב הָאַרְיֵה--הִמֵּס יִמָּס:  כִּי-יֹדֵעַ כָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל כִּי-גִבּוֹר אָבִיךָ, וּבְנֵי-חַיִל אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ.
And he also, a son of strength, whose heart is as the heart of the lion, will utterly melt; for all Israel knows that your father is a mighty man, and those who are with him are sons of strength.note
Y también él, hijo de fuerza, cuyo corazón es como el corazón del león, se derretirá del todo; porque todo Israel sabe que tu padre es un valiente, y que los que están con él son hijos de fuerza.
11
כִּי יָעַצְתִּי, הֵאָסֹף יֵאָסֵף עָלֶיךָ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל מִדָּן וְעַד-בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, כַּחוֹל אֲשֶׁר-עַל-הַיָּם, לָרֹב; וּפָנֶיךָ הֹלְכִים, בַּקְרָב.
But I counsel: let all Israel be gathered to you, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, like the sand that is by the sea for multitude; and your own face going into the battle.note
Pero yo aconsejo: que todo Israel se reúna a ti, desde Dan hasta Beerseba, como la arena que está junto al mar por su multitud; y tu propio rostro yendo a la batalla.
12
וּבָאנוּ אֵלָיו, באחת בְּאַחַד הַמְּקוֹמֹת אֲשֶׁר נִמְצָא שָׁם, וְנַחְנוּ עָלָיו, כַּאֲשֶׁר יִפֹּל הַטַּל עַל-הָאֲדָמָה; וְלֹא-נוֹתַר בּוֹ וּבְכָל-הָאֲנָשִׁים אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ, גַּם-אֶחָד.
And we will come to him in one of the places where he is found, and we will alight upon him as the dew falls on the ground; and there will not be left of him, or of all the men who are with him, even one.note
Y llegaremos a él en uno de los lugares donde se le halle, y caeremos sobre él como cae el rocío sobre la tierra; y no quedará de él, ni de todos los hombres que están con él, ni uno.
13
וְאִם-אֶל-עִיר, יֵאָסֵף--וְהִשִּׂיאוּ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל-הָעִיר הַהִיא, חֲבָלִים; וְסָחַבְנוּ אֹתוֹ עַד-הַנַּחַל, עַד אֲשֶׁר-לֹא-נִמְצָא שָׁם גַּם-צְרוֹר.  {פ}
And if he is gathered into a city, then all Israel will bring ropes to that city, and we will drag it to the wadi, until not even a pebble is found there.’note
Y si se recoge en una ciudad, todo Israel llevará cuerdas a aquella ciudad, y la arrastraremos hasta el arroyo, hasta que no se halle allí ni una piedrecilla».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וְכָל-אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל, טוֹבָה עֲצַת חוּשַׁי הָאַרְכִּי, מֵעֲצַת אֲחִיתֹפֶל;  {ס}  וַיהוָה צִוָּה, לְהָפֵר אֶת-עֲצַת אֲחִיתֹפֶל הַטּוֹבָה, לְבַעֲבוּר הָבִיא יְהוָה אֶל-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, אֶת-הָרָעָה.  {ס}
And Absalom and every man of Israel said: ‘The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel.’ And Jehovah had commanded to frustrate the good counsel of Ahithophel, in order that Jehovah might bring the evil upon Absalom.note
Y Absalón y todo hombre de Israel dijeron: «El consejo de Husai el arquita es mejor que el consejo de Ahitofel». Y Jehová había ordenado frustrar el buen consejo de Ahitofel, para que Jehová trajera el mal sobre Absalón.
15
וַיֹּאמֶר חוּשַׁי, אֶל-צָדוֹק וְאֶל-אֶבְיָתָר הַכֹּהֲנִים, כָּזֹאת וְכָזֹאת יָעַץ אֲחִיתֹפֶל אֶת-אַבְשָׁלֹם, וְאֵת זִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וְכָזֹאת וְכָזֹאת, יָעַצְתִּי אָנִי.
And Hushai said to Zadok and to Abiathar the priests: ‘Thus and thus Ahithophel counseled Absalom and the elders of Israel; and thus and thus I counseled.note
Y Husai dijo a Sadoc y a Abiatar, los sacerdotes: «Así y así aconsejó Ahitofel a Absalón y a los ancianos de Israel; y así y así aconsejé yo.
16
וְעַתָּה שִׁלְחוּ מְהֵרָה וְהַגִּידוּ לְדָוִד לֵאמֹר, אַל-תָּלֶן הַלַּיְלָה בְּעַרְבוֹת הַמִּדְבָּר, וְגַם, עָבוֹר תַּעֲבוֹר--פֶּן יְבֻלַּע לַמֶּלֶךְ, וּלְכָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ.
And now send quickly and tell David, saying: “Do not lodge tonight in the plains of the wilderness, but also, pass over, passing — lest it be swallowed up for the king and for all the people who are with him.”’note
Y ahora envíen pronto y avisen a David, diciendo: “No pases la noche en las llanuras del desierto, sino pasa, pasa de largo — no sea que sea tragado para el rey y para todo el pueblo que está con él”».
17
וִיהוֹנָתָן וַאֲחִימַעַץ עֹמְדִים בְּעֵין-רֹגֵל, וְהָלְכָה הַשִּׁפְחָה וְהִגִּידָה לָהֶם, וְהֵם יֵלְכוּ, וְהִגִּידוּ לַמֶּלֶךְ דָּוִד:  כִּי לֹא יוּכְלוּ לְהֵרָאוֹת, לָבוֹא הָעִירָה.
And Jonathan and Ahimaaz were standing by En-rogel; and the maidservant used to go and tell them, and they would go and tell King David; for they could not be seen coming into the city.note
Y Jonatán y Ahimaas estaban junto a En-rogel; y la criada iba y les avisaba, y ellos iban y avisaban al rey David; porque no podían ser vistos entrando en la ciudad.
18
וַיַּרְא אֹתָם נַעַר, וַיַּגֵּד לְאַבְשָׁלֹם; וַיֵּלְכוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם מְהֵרָה וַיָּבֹאוּ אֶל-בֵּית-אִישׁ בְּבַחוּרִים, וְלוֹ בְאֵר בַּחֲצֵרוֹ--וַיֵּרְדוּ שָׁם.
And a young man saw them, and told Absalom; and the two of them went quickly and came to the house of a man in Bahurim, and he had a well in his courtyard; and they went down there.note
Y un joven los vio, y avisó a Absalón; y los dos se fueron deprisa y llegaron a la casa de un hombre en Bahurim, que tenía un pozo en su patio; y bajaron allí.
19
וַתִּקַּח הָאִשָּׁה, וַתִּפְרֹשׂ אֶת-הַמָּסָךְ עַל-פְּנֵי הַבְּאֵר, וַתִּשְׁטַח עָלָיו, הָרִפוֹת; וְלֹא נוֹדַע, דָּבָר.
And the woman took and spread the covering over the face of the well, and spread crushed grain upon it; and the matter was not known.note
Y la mujer tomó el cobertor y lo extendió sobre la boca del pozo, y esparció sobre él grano triturado; y no se supo la cosa.
20
וַיָּבֹאוּ עַבְדֵי אַבְשָׁלוֹם אֶל-הָאִשָּׁה הַבַּיְתָה, וַיֹּאמְרוּ אַיֵּה אֲחִימַעַץ וִיהוֹנָתָן, וַתֹּאמֶר לָהֶם הָאִשָּׁה, עָבְרוּ מִיכַל הַמָּיִם; וַיְבַקְשׁוּ וְלֹא מָצָאוּ, וַיָּשֻׁבוּ יְרוּשָׁלִָם.  {ס}
And Absalom’s servants came to the woman at the house, and said: ‘Where are Ahimaaz and Jonathan?’ And the woman said to them: ‘They have crossed over the brook of water.’ And they sought, and did not find, and returned to Jerusalem.note
Y los siervos de Absalón vinieron a la mujer a la casa, y dijeron: «¿Dónde están Ahimaas y Jonatán?» Y la mujer les dijo: «Han pasado el arroyo de agua». Y buscaron, y no hallaron, y volvieron a Jerusalén.
21
וַיְהִי אַחֲרֵי לֶכְתָּם, וַיַּעֲלוּ מֵהַבְּאֵר, וַיֵּלְכוּ, וַיַּגִּדוּ לַמֶּלֶךְ דָּוִד; וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֶל-דָּוִד, קוּמוּ וְעִבְרוּ מְהֵרָה אֶת-הַמַּיִם--כִּי-כָכָה יָעַץ עֲלֵיכֶם, אֲחִיתֹפֶל.
And it was, after they had gone, that they came up out of the well, and went and told King David; and they said to David: ‘Arise, and cross quickly over the water; for thus Ahithophel has counseled against you.’note
Y fue que, después que se fueron, subieron del pozo, y fueron y avisaron al rey David; y dijeron a David: «Levántense, y pasen pronto las aguas; porque así ha aconsejado Ahitofel contra ustedes».
22
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד, וְכָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, וַיַּעַבְרוּ, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן:  עַד-אוֹר הַבֹּקֶר, עַד-אַחַד לֹא נֶעְדָּר, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-עָבַר, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן.
And David arose, and all the people who were with him, and they crossed the Jordan; by the light of the morning not one was missing who had not crossed the Jordan.note
Y se levantó David, y todo el pueblo que estaba con él, y pasaron el Jordán; hasta la luz de la mañana no faltó uno que no hubiera pasado el Jordán.
23
וַאֲחִיתֹפֶל רָאָה, כִּי לֹא נֶעֶשְׂתָה עֲצָתוֹ, וַיַּחֲבֹשׁ אֶת-הַחֲמוֹר וַיָּקָם וַיֵּלֶךְ אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ אֶל-עִירוֹ, וַיְצַו אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ וַיֵּחָנַק; וַיָּמָת, וַיִּקָּבֵר בְּקֶבֶר אָבִיו.  {ס}
And Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not done, and he saddled the donkey, and arose, and went to his house, to his city, and set his house in order, and strangled himself; and he died, and was buried in the tomb of his father.note
Y Ahitofel vio que no se hacía su consejo, y aparejó el asno, y se levantó y se fue a su casa, a su ciudad, y puso en orden su casa, y se estranguló; y murió, y fue sepultado en el sepulcro de su padre.
24
וְדָוִד, בָּא מַחֲנָיְמָה; וְאַבְשָׁלֹם, עָבַר אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן--הוּא, וְכָל-אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל עִמּוֹ.
And David came to Mahanaim; and Absalom crossed the Jordan, he and every man of Israel with him.note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 2 Samuel 17 Ahithophel’s counsel — twelve thousand men tonight, a king struck alone, and “the man whom you seek”

Twelve thousand, tonight. “Let me choose, please, twelve thousand men, and let me arise and pursue after David tonight”: Ahithophel speaks in the first person from start to finish, “I will come upon him”, “I will strike the king alone”, so the plan is his own raid and not a proposal for Absalom to lead. The Hebrew hallaylah is “tonight”. The ASV has “this night”. The NIV has “set out tonight in pursuit of David”. The TLB has “give me twelve thousand men to start out after David tonight”. The NWT 1984 has “Let me choose, please, twelve thousand men and rise up and chase after David tonight”. The last chapter ended by saying his counsel in those days was as when a man inquires of the word of God (2 Samuel 16:23, now on these pages); this chapter puts the best counselor’s best plan on the table and has it weighed.

Weary and weak of hands. “While he is weary and weak of hands”: the Hebrew is yagea ur’fe yadayim. The last chapter said David and the people “came weary” (2 Samuel 16:14, now on these pages), with another word, ayefim, so Ahithophel has read the same exhaustion in his own vocabulary. The ASV has “weary and weak-handed”. The NIV has “weary and weak”. The TLB has “weary and discouraged”. The NWT 1984 has “weary and feeble in both hands”. For the terror he promises, the ASV has “make him afraid” and the NWT 1984 has “drive him into trembling”. The target is stated plainly: the people flee, and “I will strike the king alone”, levaddo. Rahlfs’ Greek has the king “most alone”, monōtaton.

The man whom you seek. “And I will bring back all the people to you — as all return, the man whom you seek — all the people will be at peace”: the Hebrew has no verb “is” and no word for death, keshuv hakkol ha’ish asher attah mevaqqesh, and the translations fill the gap differently. The ASV has “the man whom thou seekest is as if all returned”. The NWT 1984 has “Equivalent to the returning of all is the man whom you are seeking”. The NIV has “The death of the man you seek will mean the return of all”. The Geneva has “the man whom thou seekest being slain”. The Douay has “as if they were but one man: for thou seekest but one man”. Rahlfs’ Greek has “as a bride returns to her husband”, hon tropon epistrephei hē nymphē pros ton andra autēs, and then “only the life of one man you seek”. A page that prints “death” has added a word; the dash here keeps the Hebrew’s open grammar.

Right in the eyes. “And the word was right in the eyes of Absalom, and in the eyes of all the elders of Israel”: vayyishar. The ASV has “pleased Absalom well”. The NIV has “seemed good”. The NWT 1984 has “the word was just right in the eyes of”, and “the older men of Israel” for the elders. The plan is accepted by the prince and by the elders before the next verse brings in the second voice.

Verses 5–10 · 2 Samuel 17 Hushai the Archite — “not good this time”, a bereaved bear, and a lion’s heart that melts

Not good this time. “Call, please, Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear what is in his mouth, his also”: the Hebrew doubles the pronoun, and the NWT 1984 has “what is in his mouth, even his”. Absalom reports the plan and asks whether to do it, “If not, you speak”. David had prayed that Ahithophel’s counsel be turned to foolishness (2 Samuel 15:31, now on these pages) and had sent Hushai back to defeat it (15:34), and Hushai begins with a limit: “not good this time”, bappa‘am hazzot, so that the counselor whose word was as the word of God is faulted for this plan and not for his record. The ASV has “this time”. The NIV has “not good this time”. The TLB has “this time I think Ahithophel has made a mistake”. The NWT 1984 has “not good in this instance”.

A bear bereaved, bitter of soul. “They are bitter of soul, like a bear bereaved in the field”: “bitter of soul”, marei nefesh, stands in four verses: Judges 18:25 and Proverbs 31:6 (both now on these pages), this one, and Job 3:20. “A bereaved bear” stands in three verses: this one, Hosea 13:8 and Proverbs 17:12. The NWT 1984 has “bitter of soul, like a female bear that has lost her cubs”. The ASV has “chafed in their minds, as a bear robbed of her whelps in the field”. The NIV has “as fierce as a wild bear robbed of her cubs”. The TLB has “as upset as a mother bear who has been robbed of her cubs”. The Douay has “a bear raging in the wood when her whelps are taken away”, where the Hebrew has “in the field”. Rahlfs’ Greek adds a second beast, “and like a rough sow in the plain”, kai hōs hys tracheia en tō pediō.

A man of war. “Your father is a man of war, and he will not lodge with the people”: “a man of war”, ish milchamah, stands in eight verses, and one of them is the verse in which one of Saul’s young men describes David to Saul as “a man of war” (1 Samuel 16:18, now on these pages). Hushai gives it as the reason David will not sleep among the people, and then pictures him “hidden now in one of the pits, or in one of the places”. For the first blow the Hebrew has a bare infinitive, “when there is a falling among them”, with no subject, so the page does not say whose men fall. The ASV has “when some of them are fallen at the first”. The NWT 1984 has “just as soon as he falls upon them at the start”. The NIV has “If he should attack your troops first”. The Douay has “when any one shall fall at the first”.

A lion’s heart that will melt. “And he also, a son of strength, whose heart is as the heart of the lion, will utterly melt”: “the heart of the lion” stands only here, and “melting he will melt”, hamos yimmas, only here. The verb is the one used of the Canaanites’ hearts in Joshua 2:11, 5:1 and 7:5 (all now on these pages), and in the last of those it is Israel’s own heart that “melted and turned to water”; Hushai promises the same to Absalom’s bravest. The ASV has “will utterly melt”. The NWT 1984 has “will himself surely soften in weakness”. The NIV has “will melt with fear”.

Verses 11–14 · 2 Samuel 17 Hushai’s counsel — from Dan to Beer-sheba, the dew, ropes to the wadi, and why Jehovah had commanded it

From Dan to Beer-sheba. “Let all Israel be gathered to you, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, like the sand that is by the sea for multitude”: “from Dan to Beer-sheba”, in that order, stands in seven verses, among them Judges 20:1, 1 Samuel 3:20 and 2 Samuel 3:10 (all now on these pages); in the last, Abner says he will set up David’s throne over Israel and Judah “from Dan to Beer-sheba”, and Hushai now tells Absalom to call up the same breadth against him. “Like the sand that is by the sea” stands in two verses: this one and 1 Kings 4:20. The NIV has “as numerous as the sand on the seashore”. The ASV has “as the sand that is by the sea for multitude”.

Your own face going. The Masoretic text ends Hushai’s advice with the thing Ahithophel had not asked of the prince: ufanekha holkhim baqrav, “and your own face going into the battle”. The ASV has “that thou go to battle in thine own person”. The NWT 1984 has “with your own person going into the fight”. The NIV has “with you yourself leading them into battle”. The Douay has “thou shalt be in the midst of them”, and Rahlfs’ Greek has the same, en mesō autōn, where the Masoretic text has “into the battle”. Ahithophel’s plan had no place for Absalom; Hushai’s puts him in front, and the time it takes to gather Israel is the price.

Dew, ropes and a pebble. “In one of the places where he is found”: the written form is be’achat, feminine, and the pointed reading is be’achad, masculine; this translation follows the pointed reading. “We will alight upon him as the dew falls on the ground”: the picture of the dew falling on the ground stands only here. The ASV has “we will light upon him as the dew falleth on the ground”. The NIV has “we will fall on him as dew settles on the ground”. The Douay has “we shall cover him, as the dew falleth upon the ground”. For the city drawn down to the wadi, the NWT 1984 has “until there shall not be found there even a pebble” and the ASV has “one small stone”. The Hebrew tseror is a pebble or small stone.

Jehovah had commanded. “And Absalom and every man of Israel said: ‘The counsel of Hushai the Archite is better than the counsel of Ahithophel’”: then the narrator speaks, “And Jehovah had commanded to frustrate the good counsel of Ahithophel, in order that Jehovah might bring the evil upon Absalom”. The verb is tsivvah, “commanded”. The NWT 1984 has “had given command to frustrate”. The ASV has “had ordained to defeat”. The NIV has “had determined to frustrate”. The TLB has “had arranged to defeat”. The Douay has “by the will of the Lord”. The infinitive lehafer is the verb of David’s charge to Hushai in 15:34: the exact words “to frustrate the counsel of”, hafer et atsat, stand only here. The Hebrew calls Ahithophel’s counsel “good”, hattovah, in the same verse in which the men of Israel call Hushai’s counsel tovah, “better”; it is the narrator, not the men, who says which was good.

Verses 15–22 · 2 Samuel 17 Hushai to the priests — Jonathan and Ahimaaz at En-rogel, a well at Bahurim, and the brook of water

Thus and thus. “Thus and thus Ahithophel counseled Absalom and the elders of Israel; and thus and thus I counseled”: “thus and thus”, kazot vekhazot, stands in four verses: this one, twice, Joshua 7:20 (now on these pages), and 2 Kings 5:4 and 9:12. Hushai gives the priests the two counsels without their content, which the narrator has already given. His message to David: “Do not lodge tonight in the plains of the wilderness, but also, pass over, passing — lest it be swallowed up for the king and for all the people who are with him”. The Hebrew here is ba‘arvot, “in the plains”; in 15:28 the written form was ba‘avrot, “fords”, and the pointed reading “plains” was translated. The ASV has “at the fords of the wilderness”. The NIV has “at the fords in the wilderness”. The NWT 1984 has “in the desert plains of the wilderness”. The Douay has “in the plains of the wilderness”. The Hebrew for the danger is a swallowing, pen yevulla, and the ASV has “lest the king be swallowed up”.

En-rogel. “Jonathan and Ahimaaz were standing by En-rogel”: the sons of the two priests, Ahimaaz for Zadok and Jonathan for Abiathar (2 Samuel 15:36, now on these pages), stay at the spring outside the city because they “could not be seen coming into the city”. En-rogel stands in four verses: Joshua 15:7, 18:16, 1 Kings 1:9 (all now on these pages), and this one. The maidservant’s trips are habitual in the Hebrew, vehalekhah, “and she would go”. The ASV has “used to go and tell them”. The NIV has “was to go and inform them”, an arrangement and not a habit. The NWT 1984 has “went off and told them”, a single trip. The TLB has “a servant girl to carry to them the messages”.

The well at Bahurim. “A young man saw them, and told Absalom”: the na‘ar, a lad. The two go to “the house of a man in Bahurim”, the village where Shimei came out cursing (2 Samuel 16:5, now on these pages), and go down into a well in his courtyard. “And the woman took and spread the covering over the face of the well, and spread crushed grain upon it; and the matter was not known”. The word for crushed grain stands in two verses, here as harifot and in Proverbs 27:22 with a yod, haripot. The ASV has “bruised grain”. The NIV has “scattered grain”. The NWT 1984 has “cracked grain”. The Douay has “as it were to dry sodden barley”. Rahlfs’ Greek does not translate the word and writes Araphōth. A woman of whom the page gives no name hides two men and sends their pursuers the wrong way, as Rahab did, who hid the two spies “among the stalks of flax” while the pursuers went “by the way of the Jordan, to the fords” (Joshua 2:6–7, now on these pages).

The brook of water. “They have crossed over the brook of water”: the phrase mikhal hammayim stands only here, and the noun is unexplained. The ASV has “the brook of water”. The NIV has “the brook”. The NWT 1984 has “to the waters”. The Douay has “after they had tasted a little water”, and Rahlfs’ Greek has “a little of the water”, mikron tou hydatos, so the Latin and the Greek read the word as “a little” where the Hebrew letters have a stream. The answer sends the servants toward the stream and away from the well, and the page gives her no more words. When the servants have gone the two come up and tell David in the plural, “Arise, and cross quickly over the water”, qumu ve‘ivru. The ASV has “Arise ye”. The NWT 1984 has “YOU people, rise up and speedily pass over the waters”. By the morning light David and the people have crossed the Jordan, and the count was complete, ad echad lo ne‘dar.

Verse 23 · 2 Samuel 17 Ahithophel puts his house in order — strangled, not hanged, and buried in the tomb of his father

Strangled himself. “And Ahithophel saw that his counsel was not done, and he saddled the donkey, and arose, and went to his house, to his city, and set his house in order, and strangled himself; and he died, and was buried in the tomb of his father”: the form vayyehanaq stands only in this verse. It is the niphal of chanaq, to choke, said of himself, and it names no rope and no tree. The NWT 1984 has “strangled himself”. The ASV has “hanged himself”. The NIV has “hanged himself”. The TLB has “hanged himself”. The Douay has “hanged himself”. The Greek verb in Rahlfs is apēnxato, and the identical form stands in Matthew 27:5 (now on these pages), where Judas goes away and apēnxato.

He commanded his house. “And set his house in order”: vayetsav el-beito, literally “and he commanded to his house”. The phrase stands only here. The same verb with the same noun is the imperative that Isaiah brings Hezekiah, tsav leveitekha, “Set your house in order” (2 Kings 20:1; Isaiah 38:1). The ASV has “set his house in order”. The NIV has “put his house in order”. The NWT 1984 has “gave commands to his household”. The Douay has “putting his house in order”. The man goes home, to “his city”, which the page named in 15:12 as Giloh (2 Samuel 15:12, now on these pages), and makes his dispositions as a man does who does not mean to come back.

The tomb of his father. “In the tomb of his father” stands in two verses: 2 Samuel 2:32, where Asahel is buried in the tomb of his father at Bethlehem (now on these pages), and here. The man who gave counsel “as when a man inquires of the word of God” ends in a family tomb, and the verse gives one reason, that his counsel was not done, and no further word.

Verses 24–26 · 2 Samuel 17 Mahanaim, Amasa over the army, and Abigal “the daughter of Nahash”

Mahanaim. “And David came to Mahanaim; and Absalom crossed the Jordan”: David arrives at the town where Abner had brought Saul’s son to be king (2 Samuel 2:8, now on these pages). The directional form Machanaymah, “to Mahanaim”, stands in three verses: here, verse 27, and 1 Kings 4:14. The Douay has “David came to the camp”, translating the name where the others print it.

Amasa. “And Absalom set Amasa over the army in place of Joab”: the prince does what a king would, and replaces the commander who had brought him back from Geshur (2 Samuel 14:23, now on these pages). The Hebrew says of Amasa’s father, “a son of a man whose name was Ithra the Israelite, who went in to Abigal the daughter of Nahash, sister of Zeruiah, Joab’s mother”. The ASV has “Ithra the Israelite”. The NIV has “Jether, an Ishmaelite”. The TLB has “Ithra, an Ishmaelite”. The Douay has “Jethra of Jezrael”. 1 Chronicles 2:17 reads “Jether the Ishmaelite”; the two Hebrew words, hayisre’eli and hayishme’eli, are close in letters, and Rahlfs’ Greek has Iothor ho Israēlitēs, with the Masoretic text. This translation prints what Samuel has and does not carry Chronicles’ word back into it.

Abigal, daughter of Nahash. “Who went in to Abigal”: the verb is the ordinary one for coming in to a woman. The ASV has “went in to”. The NWT 1984 has “had relations with”. The NIV has “had married”, which is an interpretation. The name is written without the yod that the Abigail of Nabal has in its usual spelling, as it is in 1 Samuel 25:32 and in the written form of 2 Samuel 3:3. 1 Chronicles 2:16 says of the sons of Jesse, “And their sisters were Zeruiah and Abigail”, so that Chronicles gives these women Jesse for a father, and Samuel gives Abigal Nahash. The page does not choose between them, and says only what is written. Joab, Abishai and Asahel are David’s nephews if the genealogy of Chronicles holds; this verse names only their mother, Zeruiah, and does not call her David’s sister.

Verses 27–29 · 2 Samuel 17 Shobi, Machir and Barzillai at Mahanaim — beds, basins, two measures of parched grain, and cheese of the herd

Three men. “Shobi son of Nahash from Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and Machir son of Ammiel from Lo-debar, and Barzillai the Gileadite from Rogelim”: an Ammonite from the city David took, the man at Lo-debar who had kept Mephibosheth, and a Gileadite from Rogelim. “Machir son of Ammiel” stands in three verses: 2 Samuel 9:4 and 9:5 (now on these pages), where he is the owner of the house at Lo-debar in which Saul’s grandson was living, and this one. Shobi is the son of a Nahash, and the page does not say whether it is the Nahash of Ammon whose son Hanun is the man in chapter 10 (2 Samuel 10:1, now on these pages), or whether it is the Nahash of verse 25; if it is the king’s son, the brother of the man who humiliated David’s envoys is the one who feeds David. That is an inference and not the text. The NIV has “Makir” for Machir. The Douay has “Sobi” and “Berzellai”.

What they brought. “Beds and basins and a potter’s vessel, and wheat and barley and flour and parched grain, and beans and lentils and parched grain”: the list has fourteen items, and the Hebrew qali, “parched grain”, stands twice in verse 28, after the flour and again after the lentils. Translators avoid the second one. The ASV has “parched grain” and then “parched pulse”. The Douay has “parched corn” and then “fried pulse”. The NWT 1984 has “roasted grain” and then “parched grain”. The NIV has “roasted grain” once. Rahlfs’ Greek has “ten beds” and “ten cauldrons”, deka koitas and lebētas deka, which the Hebrew does not have, and no second parched grain.

Curds and cheese of the herd. “And honey and curds and sheep and cheese of the herd”: chem’ah is curds, as in Isaiah 7, and “cheese of the herd”, shefot baqar, stands only here, so that the word is unexplained. The ASV has “butter” and “cheese of the herd”. The NIV has “curds” and “cheese from cows’ milk”. The NWT 1984 has “butter” and “curds of cattle”, which swaps the two words. The Douay has “butter” and “fat calves”. Rahlfs’ Greek does not translate it and writes Saphphōth boōn.

Hungry, weary, thirsty. “For they said: ‘The people are hungry and weary and thirsty in the wilderness’”: the three men read the road as Ahithophel had read it, except that his word for the king’s weariness was yagea, a weakness to strike at, and theirs is ayef, the weariness of Ziba’s wine “for the weary to drink in the wilderness” (2 Samuel 16:2, now on these pages). The ASV has “hungry, and weary, and thirsty, in the wilderness”. The NWT 1984 has “hungry and tired and thirsty in the wilderness”. What Ahithophel counted as a chance to strike, three men at Mahanaim counted as the need for beds.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “weary and weak of hands” (yagea) beside the ayef of the people; “strike the king alone”; the open grammar of “as all return, the man whom you seek”; “right in the eyes”; “bitter of soul” and “a bear bereaved”; “a man of war”; “the heart of the lion” that “will utterly melt”; “from Dan even to Beer-sheba”; “your own face going”; “as the dew falls on the ground”; “not even a pebble”; “Jehovah had commanded”; “thus and thus”; “crushed grain”; “the brook of water”; “strangled himself”; “set his house in order”; “the tomb of his father”; “went in to Abigal”; “parched grain” twice; and “cheese of the herd”; and the pointed reading “in one of the places”, with the written form in the note.

Echoes paid. David’s prayer that Ahithophel’s counsel be turned to foolishness and his charge to Hushai to defeat it (2 Samuel 15:31, 34); the two priests’ sons sent to carry word (2 Samuel 15:27, 36); Ahithophel’s counsel as the word of God (2 Samuel 16:23) and his city Giloh (2 Samuel 15:12); the weary king and the wine for the weary (2 Samuel 16:14, 2); Bahurim and Shimei (2 Samuel 16:5); Rahab’s flax over the spies (Joshua 2:6–7); the melting heart (Joshua 2:11; 5:1; 7:5); the man of war (1 Samuel 16:18); Abner’s “from Dan to Beer-sheba” (2 Samuel 3:10); En-rogel (Joshua 15:7; 18:16; 1 Kings 1:9); Asahel in his father’s tomb (2 Samuel 2:32); Mahanaim, where Abner crowned Ish-bosheth (2 Samuel 2:8); Abigail with the short spelling (1 Samuel 25:32); and Machir at Lo-debar (2 Samuel 9:4–5).

Echoes planted. Judas’s verb in Matthew 27:5, already on these pages; Hezekiah’s “Set your house in order” (2 Kings 20:1; Isaiah 38:1); Jether the Ishmaelite and the sisters of the sons of Jesse (1 Chronicles 2:16–17); the third of the army that the Gittite will lead and the forest in which the battle is fought (2 Samuel 18:2, 6, now on these pages); Absalom’s end (2 Samuel 18:9, 14–15; now on these pages); the runners Ahimaaz and the Cushite (2 Samuel 18:19–27, now on these pages); Amasa taken back and then killed by Joab (2 Samuel 19:14, now on these pages; 20:10, now on these pages); Barzillai’s farewell at the Jordan and Solomon’s kindness to his sons (2 Samuel 19:32–40, now on these pages; 1 Kings 2:7); the king’s count from Dan to Beer-sheba (2 Samuel 24:2); and the lion, the leopard and the bereaved bear in Hosea 13:7–8. Most of the others are yet to come in this translation.

Next in this book: 2 Samuel 18 — David numbers the people and sends them out in three parts under Joab, Abishai and Ittai; the people will not let him go out with them; he charges the three commanders, in the hearing of all the people, to deal gently for his sake with the young man, Absalom; the battle in the forest of Ephraim, where the forest devours more than the sword; Absalom’s head caught in the oak and Joab’s three darts; the great pit, the heap of stones and the pillar; Ahimaaz and the Cushite run, with David sitting between the two gates; and the Cushite’s answer to the king’s question about the young man Absalom.
