Every high priest is taken from among human beings and called by God, like Aaron; so Christ was named high priest by the one who said 'You are my Son' and 'a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek.' In the days of his flesh he prayed with loud crying and tears and learned obedience from what he suffered. Then a rebuke: readers who should be teachers still need milk, not solid food.
Before AD 70, most likely c. AD 60-69, continuing the same letter as chapter 1 (see that note).

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · Every high priest — “able to deal gently,” “surrounded by weakness,” and called, like Aaron

“Every high priest, taken from among human beings, is appointed on behalf of human beings in the things relating to God.” “In the things relating to God” is the phrase of Hebrews 2:17, where Jesus is first called high priest (dictionary). The high priest belongs to the people he serves: “from among human beings” and “on behalf of human beings.” “Gifts and sacrifices for sins” returns at 8:3 and 9:9. The KJV reads “For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God”; the ASV reads “that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins”; the NIV reads “is selected from among the people.”

“He is able to deal gently with the ignorant and those who go astray, since he himself is surrounded by weakness.” Metriopatheō, “to deal gently, to keep one’s feelings in measure,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). “The ignorant and those who go astray” are those whose sins the Law’s offerings covered, as in Numbers 15:28, “the soul that erred, in sinning through error.” “Go astray” is the verb of Hebrews 3:10, “They always go astray in their heart.” Perikeimai, “to be surrounded by, to have around one,” stands in five verses, among them Paul’s “this chain around me” at Acts 28:20: the high priest wears his weakness the way a prisoner wears a chain. The KJV reads “Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way”; the ASV reads “who can bear gently with the ignorant and erring”; the NWT 1984 reads “He is able to deal moderately with the ignorant and erring ones.”

“And because of it he is obliged, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins.” The Law orders exactly this: “make atonement for yourself and for the people” at Leviticus 9:7, and on the Day of Atonement Aaron’s bull for “himself and his household” at Leviticus 16:6. The Byzantine text has “because of this” for “because of it,” and the editions differ over the form of “himself” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins”; the NWT 1984 reads “he is obliged to make offerings for sins as much for himself as for the people”; the NIV reads “This is why he has to offer sacrifices for his own sins.”

“… but only when he is called by God, just as Aaron was.” Aaron’s call is Exodus 28:1, “bring near to yourself Aaron your brother … that he may serve as priest to me” (encyclopedia), and the Law’s own case against taking it is Korah’s company at Numbers 16:3, who said to Moses and Aaron, “you have too much!” The KJV reads “And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron”; the ASV reads “but when he is called of God, even as was Aaron”; the NWT 1984 reads “a man takes this honor, not of his own accord.”

Verses 5–6 · Psalm 2 and Psalm 110 — “You are my Son” and “a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek”

“Christ did not glorify himself to become high priest; the one who glorified him was the one who said to him, ‘You are my Son; today I have begotten you.’” The quotation is Psalm 2:7, in the words of the Greek Psalter (checked), already quoted at Hebrews 1:5. There it set the Son above the angels; here it is the word that made him high priest, so that the Son’s appointment and his priesthood come from one speaker. The KJV reads “Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee”; the NIV reads “today I have become your Father”; the NWT 1984 reads “I, today, I have become your father.”

“As he also says in another place, ‘You are a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek.’” The quotation is Psalm 110:4, the verse after the “Sit at my right hand” of Hebrews 1:13; the Hebrew opens “Jehovah has sworn and will not relent” and ends al-divrati Malki-tsedeq, “after the manner of Melchizedek,” and the Greek Psalter has kata tēn taxin, “according to the order” (both checked; Psalm 110 yet to come in this translation). Melchizedek is the “priest of God Most High” of Genesis 14:18 (encyclopedia). Taxis, “order, rank,” stands in eight verses, among them Zechariah serving “in the order of his division” at Luke 1:8, and five in Hebrews 5–7 (dictionary). The KJV reads “Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec”; the NIV reads “in the order of Melchizedek”; the TLB reads “with the same rank as Melchizedek.”

Verses 7–10 · “With loud crying and tears” — ⚠ “heard because of his reverence,” and “he learned obedience from what he suffered”

“In the days of his flesh he offered up prayers and supplications, with loud crying and tears, to the one who was able to save him from death.” Readers hear in the line the prayer in Gethsemane, “Take this cup away from me” (Mark 14:36), and the cry from the cross (Mark 15:34); the Gospels do not mention tears at either. “Prayers” is deēsis (dictionary); hiketēria, “supplication,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). “Offered up” is prospherō, the verb of verse 1’s gifts and sacrifices: the first thing the letter says Jesus offered is prayer. The KJV reads “with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death”; the NIV reads “with fervent cries and tears”; the TLB reads “praying with tears and agony of soul.”

⚠ “And he was heard because of his reverence.” Eulabeia, “reverence, godly fear,” stands in two verses, here and Hebrews 12:28 (dictionary). The Greek apo tēs eulabeias is read two ways: “heard because of his reverence,” or “heard and set free from his fear.” Readers also differ over how a man who died was “heard”: saved out of death by being raised, or given strength to go through it. This page does not choose. The KJV reads “and was heard in that he feared”; the Geneva reads “and was also heard in that which he feared”; the ASV reads “and having been heard for his godly fear”; the NIV reads “and he was heard because of his reverent submission.”

“Although he was a Son, he learned obedience from what he suffered.” The Greek rhymes, emathen aph’ hōn epathen, “he learned from what he suffered,” a pairing of “learn” and “suffer” that is reported to be proverbial in Greek writers (reported here, not checked). “Although he was a Son” is the surprise: a son learning obedience as a servant would. The KJV reads “Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered”; the Douay reads “And whereas indeed he was the Son of God,” as the Latin Vulgate has it (checked); the TLB reads “he had to learn from experience what it was like to obey when obeying meant suffering.”

“And having been made complete, he became the source of eternal salvation for all who obey him, designated by God a high priest.” “Made complete” is teleioō, as at Hebrews 2:10, “to make the Author of their salvation complete through sufferings” (dictionary). “Source” is aitios, “cause,” a different word from the archēgos of 2:10; this translation keeps them apart, “Author” there and “source” here (dictionary). He learned obedience, hypakoē, and becomes the source of salvation for those who obey, hypakouousin. Prosagoreuō, “to designate, to name,” stands in one verse. The KJV reads “he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him”; the Douay reads “the cause of eternal salvation”; the NWT 1984 reads “he became responsible for everlasting salvation”; the NIV reads “and was designated by God to be high priest.”

Verses 11–14 · “Sluggish in hearing” — ⚠ milk and solid food, infants and the mature

“About this we have much to say, and it is hard to explain, since you have become sluggish in hearing.” “About this” is peri hou, read as “about him,” Melchizedek or Christ, or as “about this matter.” Dysermēneutos, “hard to explain,” stands in one verse. Nōthros, “sluggish,” stands in two verses, here and Hebrews 6:12 (dictionary). The KJV reads “Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered, seeing ye are dull of hearing”; the ASV reads “and hard of interpretation”; the NIV reads “because you no longer try to understand.”

⚠ “You need someone to teach you again the first elements of the oracles of God, and you have come to need milk, not solid food.” The Greek letters can be read two ways, depending on the accent: tina, “someone,” as the printed text, Westcott–Hort and Nestle–Aland 28 have it, or tína, “what are,” as Tregelles and Robinson–Pierpont have it (the apparatus). Stoicheia, “elements,” stands in seven verses, among them “the elements of the world” at Galatians 4:3 (dictionary); logia, “oracles,” stands in four verses, among them “the oracles of God” at Romans 3:2 and the “living oracles” of Acts 7:38 (dictionary). Paul makes the same complaint with the same diet at 1 Corinthians 3:2, “I gave you milk to drink, not solid food.” The KJV reads “ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God”; the ASV reads “ye have need again that some one teach you the rudiments”; the Douay reads “you have need to be taught again what are the first elements of the words of God”; the NIV reads “you need someone to teach you the elementary truths.”

“For everyone who partakes of milk is unacquainted with the word of righteousness, since he is an infant.” “Partakes” is metecheō, the verb of Hebrews 2:14, “he himself likewise partook.” “The word of righteousness” is read as the teaching about righteousness or as speech that is right. Apeiros, “unacquainted, without experience,” stands in one verse. “Infant” is nēpios, as in “infants in Christ” at 1 Corinthians 3:1 (dictionary). The KJV reads “For every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness”; the ASV reads “is without experience of the word of righteousness”; the NIV reads “is not acquainted with the teaching about righteousness.”

“But solid food is for the mature, who through practice have their faculties trained to distinguish good and evil.” “Mature” is teleios, the adjective of the verb of verse 9: the Son was “made complete,” and the readers are called to be complete, “mature” (dictionary), as in “in your thinking be mature” at 1 Corinthians 14:20. Aisthētērion, “faculty, sense,” stands in one verse (dictionary). Gymnazō, “to train,” the athlete’s word, stands in four verses, among them “train yourself rather for godliness” at 1 Timothy 4:7 (dictionary). Diakrisis, “distinguishing,” stands in three verses, among them “the distinguishing of spirits” at 1 Corinthians 12:10 (dictionary). “Good and evil” recalls Deuteronomy 1:39, where the children “who today do not know good from evil” are the ones who will enter the land. The KJV reads “But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age”; the ASV reads “But solid food is for fullgrown men”; the NWT 1984 reads “to those who through use have their perceptive powers trained to distinguish both right and wrong.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “in the things relating to God,” as at 2:17; “deal gently” for metriopatheō; “go astray,” as at 3:10; “surrounded by,” as with Paul’s chain at Acts 28:20; “today I have begotten you,” as at 1:5; “according to the order of Melchizedek”; “heard because of his reverence,” with the other reading in the note; “made complete,” as at 2:10; “source” for aitios, kept apart from “Author”; “sluggish”; “someone,” the printed reading at verse 12; “elements,” as at Galatians 4:3; “oracles,” as at Romans 3:2; “partakes,” as at 2:14; “infant”; “mature” for teleios; and “trained.”

Echoes paid. The high priest of Hebrews 2:17; go astray from Hebrews 3:10; the chain around me of Acts 28:20; atonement for yourself and for the people at Leviticus 9:7; Aaron brought near at Exodus 28:1; You are my Son from Hebrews 1:5; Sit at my right hand from Hebrews 1:13; the priest of God Most High of Genesis 14:18; the cup of Mark 14:36; made complete from Hebrews 2:10; the oracles of Romans 3:2; milk, not solid food from 1 Corinthians 3:2; partook from Hebrews 2:14; mature from 1 Corinthians 14:20; and the children who did not know good from evil at Deuteronomy 1:39.

Echoes planted. “Gifts and sacrifices” at 8:3; Melchizedek himself in chapter 7; and “reverence” at 12:28.

Next in this book: Hebrews 6, already on these pages. “Let us be carried on to maturity,” leaving the beginning teaching; ⚠ the hardest passage in the letter, on those who “have tasted the heavenly gift” and fallen away; better things for the readers; and God’s oath to Abraham, with a hope that is “an anchor of the soul,” entering “inside the curtain” where Jesus has gone ahead.
