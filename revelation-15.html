Seven angels with the seven last plagues; the conquerors of the beast standing by a sea of glass mixed with fire and singing the song of Moses and the song of the Lamb, 'Great and marvelous are your works'; the temple of the tent of the testimony opened in heaven; seven golden bowls full of the fury of God given to the angels; and the temple filled with smoke, closed until the plagues are finished.
c. AD 95, opening the cycle of the seven bowls (chapters 15-16): the last plagues are framed as a new Exodus, the song by the sea sung before the plagues fall rather than after.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · The seven last plagues and the sea of glass mixed with fire — those who come off victorious from the beast · θάλασσαν ὑαλίνην μεμιγμένην πυρί thalassan hyalinēn memigmenēn pyri

“And I saw another sign in heaven, great and marvelous: seven angels having seven plagues, the last ones, because in them the fury of God is finished.” The book counts its signs: “a great sign appeared in heaven” (Revelation 12:1), “another sign” (12:3), and now a third. Plēgē, “plague,” literally a blow, stands in fifteen verses of Revelation. “The last ones” stands after the noun in Greek, as a second thought: after these there are no more. “Is finished” is teleō in the aorist, said of what is about to happen as already done, the verb of “the mystery of God would be finished” (Revelation 10:7); it returns in verse 8. “Fury” is thymos, as in the wine and the winepress of chapter 14 (dictionary). The KJV reads “for in them is filled up the wrath of God”; the ASV reads “seven plagues, which are the last, for in them is finished the wrath of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “by means of them the anger of God is brought to a finish.”

“And I saw as it were a sea of glass mixed with fire, and those who come off victorious from the beast and from its image and from the number of its name, standing on the sea of glass, holding harps of God.” The sea is the one before the throne, “as it were a sea of glass, like crystal” (Revelation 4:6), now with fire in it; hyalinos, “of glass,” stands only in those two verses. “Come off victorious from” keeps the Greek’s odd pairing of nikaō, “conquer,” with ek, “out of,” a victory that is also an escape (dictionary); the image and “the number of its name” are those of Revelation 13:17–18. “On the sea” is epi with the accusative, which can also mean “at” or “beside”; the picture of a people standing by the sea and singing is the Exodus, where Israel crossed and “Moses and the children of Israel sang this song to Jehovah” (Exodus 15:1). The harps are those of Revelation 5:8 and 14:2. The KJV reads “them that had gotten the victory over the beast, and over his image, and over his mark,” adding “his mark,” and “stand on the sea of glass”; the ASV reads “them that come off victorious from the beast … standing by the sea of glass”; the NWT 1984 reads “a glassy sea mingled with fire.”

Verses 3–4 · ⚠ The song of Moses and the song of the Lamb — “King of the ages” or “King of the nations” · ὁ βασιλεὺς τῶν αἰώνων ho basileus tōn aiōnōn

“And they sing the song of Moses the slave of God and the song of the Lamb, saying, ‘Great and marvelous are your works, Jehovah God, the Almighty; righteous and true are your ways.’” Moses sang by the sea (Exodus 15:1), and after the crossing the people “believed in Jehovah and in Moses his servant” (Exodus 14:31); “slave” is doulos, as of John and the prophets in this book (Revelation 10:7). The words sung are not Exodus 15’s. They are gathered from the Psalms and the Law: “Great are the works of Jehovah” (Psalm 111:2), “Wonderful are your works” (Psalm 139:14), and “all his ways are justice … righteous and upright is he” from the other song of Moses (Deuteronomy 32:4). “Jehovah God, the Almighty” is the title of Revelation 4:8 and 11:17; the Greek reads kyrie ho theos ho pantokratōr. The KJV reads “Great and marvellous are thy works, Lord God Almighty; just and true are thy ways”; the NWT 1984 reads “Great and wonderful are your works, Jehovah God, the Almighty.”

⚠ “King of the ages.” The editions divide three ways. Westcott-Hort, followed by the Greek printed here, reads aiōnōn, “of the ages,” the title of “the King of the ages, immortal, invisible” at 1 Timothy 1:17. Tregelles, the 28th Nestle-Aland edition and the Byzantine text read ethnōn, “of the nations,” which suits the next line’s “all the nations” and Jeremiah’s “Who would not fear you, King of the nations?” (Jeremiah 10:7). The printed Received Text has “of the saints.” The ASV reads “thou King of the ages”; the Douay reads “O King of ages”; the NWT 1984 reads “King of eternity”; the NIV reads “King of the nations”; the KJV reads “thou King of saints.”

“Who will not fear, Jehovah, and glorify your name? For you alone are holy; for all the nations will come and worship before you, for your righteous acts have been made manifest.” The question is Jeremiah’s, just quoted, and the promise is a psalm’s, “All the nations you have made shall come and worship before you” (Psalm 86:9). The Greek is kyrie, here the God of Israel, so this translation prints “Jehovah.” “Holy” is hosios, not the usual hagios (dictionary): devout, set apart by faithfulness, the word of “your holy one” in Peter’s quotation of the Psalm (Acts 2:27) and of the high priest “holy, innocent, undefiled” (Hebrews 7:26). Hosios stands in eight verses; the Byzantine text has hagios here. “Righteous acts” is dikaiōmata, deeds or decrees of justice. The Byzantine text adds “you” after “fear.” The KJV reads “Who shall not fear thee, O Lord, and glorify thy name? for thou only art holy”; the ASV reads “for thy righteous acts have been made manifest”; the NWT 1984 reads “because you alone are loyal,” and “your righteous decrees.”

Verses 5–8 · ⚠ The temple of the tent of the testimony — linen or stone, seven golden bowls, and a temple filled with smoke · ὁ ναὸς τῆς σκηνῆς τοῦ μαρτυρίου ho naos tēs skēnēs tou martyriou

“And after these things I saw, and the temple of the tent of the testimony in heaven was opened.” “The tent of the testimony” is the wilderness tabernacle that held the tablets, as Stephen says, “The tent of the testimony was with our fathers in the wilderness” (Acts 7:44). The temple in heaven was opened once before, and the ark was seen in it (Revelation 11:19) (dictionary). The ASV reads “the temple of the tabernacle of the testimony in heaven was opened”; the NWT 1984 reads “the sanctuary of the tent of the witness”; the NIV reads “the tabernacle of the covenant law.”

⚠ “And the seven angels who had the seven plagues came out of the temple, clothed in pure, bright linen, and girded around their chests with golden sashes.” One letter divides the editions: linon, “linen,” in the 28th Nestle-Aland edition and the Byzantine text, printed here, and lithon, “stone,” in Westcott-Hort and Tregelles, angels clothed in “pure, bright stone.” Readers who keep “stone” compare the jewelled covering of Ezekiel 28:13, “every precious stone was your covering.” The golden sash is the one worn by the figure like a son of man, “girded across the chest with a golden sash” (Revelation 1:13). The ASV follows Westcott-Hort: “arrayed with precious stone, pure and bright”; the KJV reads “clothed in pure and white linen”; the NWT 1984 reads “clothed with clean, bright linen.”

“And one of the four living creatures gave the seven angels seven golden bowls full of the fury of God, who lives forever and ever.” Phialē, “bowl,” stands in twelve verses, all in Revelation (dictionary). The first is the elders’ “golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints” (Revelation 5:8); the same golden bowls are now full of fury. “Who lives forever and ever” is the title of Revelation 4:9. The KJV reads “seven golden vials full of the wrath of God”; the ASV reads “seven golden bowls”; the TLB reads “a golden flask.”

“And the temple was filled with smoke from the glory of God and from his power … until the seven plagues of the seven angels were finished.” The smoke and the closed door are those of the tabernacle’s dedication, “Moses was not able to enter the tent of meeting, because the cloud settled on it, and the glory of Jehovah filled the tabernacle” (Exodus 40:35), of the temple’s, where the priests could not stand to minister (1 Kings 8:11), and of Isaiah’s vision, “the house was filled with smoke” (Isaiah 6:4). “Were finished” is teleō again, as in verse 1. The KJV reads “till the seven plagues of the seven angels were fulfilled”; the Douay reads “filled with smoke from the majesty of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “the sanctuary became filled with smoke because of the glory of God.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

What this chapter answers. Chapter 14 announced the cup of God’s fury; this one hands it out. Before a single bowl is poured, the conquerors stand by a sea of glass and sing the song of Moses, so that the last plagues are framed by a new Exodus: as Egypt’s plagues came before the crossing and the song, these come after it. The temple then fills with smoke, and it stays shut until the plagues are finished.

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “the last ones,” standing after the noun; “is finished,” as at 10:7 and again in verse 8; “a sea of glass,” as at 4:6; “come off victorious from”; “the slave of God”; “Jehovah God, the Almighty,” as at 4:8 and 11:17; “King of the ages,” with the other readings in the note; “holy” for hosios; “the tent of the testimony,” as at Acts 7:44; “linen,” with “stone” in the note; and “bowls,” as at 5:8.

Echoes paid. The seven angels with the seven last plagues, promised at the close of Revelation 14; the fury of God of Revelation 14:10; the sea of glass of Revelation 4:6; the beast’s image and number from Revelation 13:17; the harps of Revelation 14:2; the golden bowls of Revelation 5:8; the temple opened of Revelation 11:19; and the golden sash of Revelation 1:13.

Echoes planted. The seven bowls poured out in chapter 16, the third of them with the angel of the waters calling God the Holy One with the same hosios (16:5), and the seventh with a loud voice from the temple, “It has happened” (16:17).

Next in this book: Revelation 16, already on these pages. The seven bowls of the fury of God poured out: sores, a sea and rivers turned to blood, a scorching sun, darkness on the beast’s throne, the Euphrates dried up, the kings gathered at the place called Harmagedon, and a great earthquake.
