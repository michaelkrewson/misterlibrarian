A psalm headed “of David”: thanks with all the heart in front of gods, a bow toward the holy temple for steadfast love and truth, the word magnified above the name; the day of calling answered with boldness; the kings of the earth giving thanks; the high Jehovah who sees the lowly; and, in the midst of trouble, a hand stretched out, a right hand that saves, and a prayer not to be let go of the works of his hands.
Undated. The heading names David and nothing else: no occasion, no king, no battle. ⚠ The psalm speaks of a holy temple (hekhal), which the heading does not place in time, so this page sets it among the monarchy by the name of David and says no more.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Psalm 138:1–2 thanks with all my heart — in front of gods — toward the holy temple

⭐ With all my heart. “I will give you thanks with all my heart” stands in this one verse. The heading, ledavid, “of David”, is part of verse 1 in the Hebrew and is not numbered apart. “With all my heart” stands in four verses, here, Psalm 9:2 (Psalm 9:1 in English numbering), Psalm 119:10 (now on these pages) and Jeremiah 32:41 (not yet), where it is Jehovah who plants his people “with all my heart”. The verb is yadah, to give thanks, to confess, the verb that returns in verse 4 for the kings. The ASV has “I will give thee thanks with my whole heart”, the KJV “I will praise thee with my whole heart”, the NIV “I will praise you, Lord, with all my heart”, the TLB “Lord, with all my heart I thank you” and the NWT 1984 “I shall laud you with all my heart”.

⭐ In front of gods. “In front of gods” stands in this one verse; the neighbouring phrase is “in front of kings”, which stands in this one verse, Psalm 119:46 (now on these pages). The Hebrew word is the plain elohim, and the versions divide over whom it names. The KJV has “before the gods will I sing praise unto thee”, the NWT 1984 “In front of other gods I shall make melody to you”, the NIV “I will sing your praise”, the TLB “before the armies of angels” and the Douay “in the sight of his angels”. ⚠ Whether the word means the gods of the nations, the heavenly court or human judges is what the versions are deciding; this translation says “gods” and leaves the question where the Hebrew leaves it.

⭐ Toward the temple. “I will bow down toward your holy temple” stands in two verses, here and Psalm 5:8 (Psalm 5:7 in English numbering). To pray toward the house is the practice of Solomon’s prayer (1 Kings 8:30, now on these pages) and of Daniel with his windows open (Daniel 6:11, now on these pages, in the Hebrew numbering). The KJV has “I will worship toward thy holy temple”, the NIV “I will bow down toward your holy temple” and the TLB “I face your Temple as I worship”.

⭐ Steadfast love and truth. “For your steadfast love and for your truth” stands in this one verse, chesed and emet, the pair of the name proclaimed at Exodus 34:6 (now on these pages). The KJV has “for thy lovingkindness and for thy truth”, the NIV “for your unfailing love and your faithfulness”, the NWT 1984 “Because of your loving-kindness and because of your trueness” and the TLB “for all your loving-kindness and your faithfulness”.

⚠ Your word above all your name. “You have magnified your word above all your name” stands in this one verse, and it is the hard line of the psalm: the Hebrew order is “you made great, over all your name, your saying”. The KJV has “thou hast magnified thy word above all thy name”, the NWT 1984 “you have magnified your saying even above all your name”, the NIV “you have so exalted your solemn decree that it surpasses your fame”, the Geneva “thou hast magnified thy Name above all things by thy word”, the TLB “your promises are backed by all the honor of your name” and the Douay “thou hast magnified thy holy name above all”. This translation keeps the Hebrew order and does not choose among them.

Verses 3–4 · Psalm 138:3–4 answered and made bold — all the kings give thanks

⭐ The day I called. “In the day I called, you answered me” stands in this one verse, and the answer is strength: “you made me bold” stands in this one verse, tarhiveni, a rare verb of pressing forward. The NWT 1984 has “You began to make me bold in my soul with strength”, the NIV “you greatly emboldened me”, the ASV “Thou didst encourage me with strength in my soul”, the KJV “and strengthenedst me with strength in my soul”, the Geneva “hast increased strength in my soul” and the Douay “thou shall multiply strength in my soul”.

⭐ The kings. The verb of verse 1 is turned on the kings: yodukha, “they will give you thanks”, from the same yadah, so that the one who gave thanks in front of gods is now joined by the kings of the earth who have heard what Jehovah said. The KJV has “All the kings of the earth shall praise thee, O Lord, when they hear the words of thy mouth”, the NIV “May all the kings of the earth praise you, Lord, when they hear what you have decreed”, the NWT 1984 “All the kings of the earth will laud you, O Jehovah, For they will have heard the sayings of your mouth” and the TLB “Every king in all the earth shall give you thanks, O Lord, for all of them shall hear your voice”. ⚠ The NIV reads the verb as a wish (“May”); the Hebrew has the plain future.

Verses 5–6 · Psalm 138:5–6 they will sing of the ways of Jehovah — high, and he sees the lowly

⭐ The ways, and the glory. “And they will sing of the ways of Jehovah” stands in this one verse, and “great is the glory of Jehovah” stands in this one verse. The ways are the ones Jehovah made known to Moses (Psalm 103:7, now on these pages). The KJV has “Yea, they shall sing in the ways of the Lord”, the NIV “May they sing of the ways of the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “And they will sing about the ways of Jehovah” and the TLB “Yes, they shall sing about Jehovah’s glorious ways”.

⭐ High, and he sees the lowly. “Jehovah is high, and he sees the lowly” stands in this one verse: the high one looks down, and the lofty he knows only from far off. The turn is the one of Proverbs 3:34 (now on these pages), which James takes up at James 4:6 (now on these pages), though the Hebrew word for the humble there is a different one, anavim. The KJV has “Though the Lord be high, yet hath he respect unto the lowly: but the proud he knoweth afar off”, the NWT 1984 “For Jehovah is high, and yet the humble one he sees; But the lofty one he knows only from a distance”, the Douay “and looketh on the low: and the high he knoweth afar off” and the TLB “he respects the humble, but proud men must keep their distance”. ⚠ The NIV has “though lofty, he sees them from afar”, which turns the lofty into those who are seen; the Hebrew has Jehovah knowing the lofty from afar.

Verses 7–8 · Psalm 138:7–8 in the midst of trouble you keep me alive — the works of your hands

⭐ In the midst of trouble. “If I walk in the midst of trouble” stands in this one verse, the walk of Psalm 23:4 (now on these pages), with the answer techayyeni, “you keep me alive”. The KJV has “thou wilt revive me”, the NIV “you preserve my life”, the NWT 1984 “you will preserve me alive”, the Douay “thou wilt quicken me” and the TLB “you will bring me safely through them”.

⭐ The hand and the right hand. “And your right hand saves me” stands in this one verse, the right hand of Exodus 15:6 (now on these pages). The KJV has “thou shalt stretch forth thine hand against the wrath of mine enemies, and thy right hand shall save me”, the NIV “You stretch out your hand against the anger of my foes; with your right hand you save me”, the NWT 1984 “Because of the anger of my enemies you will thrust out your hand” and the TLB “You will clench your fist against my angry enemies”.

⭐ Jehovah will complete it. “Jehovah will complete [it] for me” stands in this one verse, from gamar, to finish. The KJV has “The Lord will perfect that which concerneth me”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah himself will complete what is in my behalf”, the NIV “The Lord will vindicate me”, the Geneva “The Lord will perform his work toward me”, the TLB “The Lord will work out his plans for my life” and the Douay “The Lord will repay for me”. “Your steadfast love is forever”, said to him in the second person, stands in this one verse; the refrain in the third person, “his steadfast love is forever”, is at Psalm 118:1 and Psalm 100:5 (both now on these pages).

⭐ The works of your hands. “Do not let go of the works of your hands” stands in this one verse; the verb is raphah, to let drop, to slacken. The works of his hands are the creation of Psalm 8:7 (now on these pages, in the Hebrew numbering), and here the one praying is among them. The KJV has “forsake not the works of thine own hands”, the NIV “do not abandon the works of your hands”, the NWT 1984 “Do not desert the works of your own hands”, the Douay “O despise not the work of thy hands” and the TLB “Don’t abandon me—for you made me”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “give thanks” for yadah in verses 1 and 4; “gods” for elohim at verse 1; “steadfast love” for chesed at verses 2 and 8; “truth” for emet; “Jehovah” for the Name, written in verses 4 to 8; “keep alive” for techayyeni; “complete” for gamar.

Echoes paid. Psalm 119:10 and 119:46; Solomon’s prayer (1 Kings 8:30) and Daniel 6; Exodus 34:6 and 15:6; Psalm 103:7; Proverbs 3:34 and James 4:6; Psalm 23:4; Psalms 118:1 and 100:5; Psalm 8.

Echoes planted. Psalm 9:2 and Jeremiah 32:41 (“with all my heart”) and Psalm 5:8, the bow toward the holy temple. None of these is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Psalms stands on these pages at chapters 1, 8, 19, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 32, 34, 37, 40, 42, 46, 51, 56, 63, 90, 91, 100, 103, 118, 119, 121, 130, 138, 139, 145 and 150, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Psalm 2.
