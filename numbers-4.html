🕰
The Levites' second census, narrower than the first: only men thirty to fifty, the years of full working strength, mustered by service rather than by dedication. Aaron and his sons wrap the ark, table, lampstand, and altars in colored cloth and tachash skins before the Kohathites are ever allowed to approach them, on pain of death for touching or even looking at the uncovered holy things; Eleazar keeps a separate charge over the oil, incense, and grain offering; Gershon and Merari, whose cargo carries no such danger, serve under Ithamar. The count: 2,750 Kohathites, 2,630 Gershonites, 3,200 Merarites — 8,580 total, exactly.
⚠ Undated within the chapter, like the census around it. Still inside the same Sinai window as Numbers 1, 2, and 3 (already on these pages).

Numbers 4 meaning — a third age, and a promise finally kept

⚠ This chapter fixes a THIRD age-threshold for counting Israel, distinct from the two already established: twenty and up for the fighting men of Numbers 1 (already on these pages), one month and up for the Levites' general dedication in Numbers 3 (already on these pages), and now thirty to fifty specifically for the physical labor of carrying the tabernacle. The distinction is deliberate: Levite status was inherited at birth, but the heaviest, most dangerous work — hauling sacred furniture through the wilderness — belonged only to the years of a man's full strength. This is also the chapter Numbers 3's own closing note (already on these pages) already promised was coming.

Wrapped before it is touched — the ark disappears under its own coverings

⚠ Ten verses describe, object by object, a wrapping ritual performed entirely by Aaron and his sons — the Kohathites are not even present yet. Each holy thing gets a specific colored cloth (the ark in blue, the altar of incense in blue, the bronze altar in purple, marking rank by color) and then a layer of tachash, the same genuinely uncertain covering material this project has already flagged at Exodus 25:5 (already on these pages) — KJV still reading ‘badgers’ skins,’ NIV ‘durable leather’ with a ‘sea cow’ footnote, neither guess resolved by this chapter any more than by Exodus. The ark itself — the single most sacred object Israel possesses — is completely invisible by the time anyone is allowed to carry it; what the Kohathites actually lift is a shape under cloth, never the thing itself.

Carry it, but do not touch it — and a separate charge for what fits in no wrapping

V15's warning is stated as flatly as this book ever states anything: touch the holy thing and die. The shelf does not soften it anywhere — ASV reads ‘lest they die,’ NWT ‘or they will die,’ and even The Living Bible, which paraphrases freely elsewhere, keeps the same stated consequence, ‘lest they die’ — a rare case of every version on this shelf agreeing that the danger belongs in the sentence itself, not softened into background color. The Kohathites carry the ark by its permanent poles, already fixed in their rings, precisely so that no hand ever needs to make contact with the thing itself. ⚠ V16 then hands Eleazar a charge that has nothing to do with carrying at all — the lamp oil, the incense, the regular grain offering, the anointing oil — the consumable, ongoing supplies that keep the whole system running day to day, as distinct from the furniture that only moves when the camp does.

‘Do not cut off’ — a warning built into the job description

⚠ V18's ‘do not cut off the tribe of the clans of the Kohathites’ is not a threat from Jehovah; it is an instruction to Moses and Aaron about how to keep the Kohathites alive doing an inherently lethal job. The danger is real enough that the text spells out the safety procedure twice, once in vv5-15 (wrap first, then let them approach) and again here (assign each man his specific task, and never let them see the holy things ‘even for a moment’ while uncovered). Holiness in this book is never depicted as gentle; nearness to it is a controlled risk, managed by exact procedure, not a comfort available casually.

Gershon and Merari: no wrapping instructions, because nothing they carry is holy enough to kill them

The contrast with Kohath is total. Gershon's curtains and Merari's frames get no colored cloths, no tachash coverings, no death warning — just a straightforward list of what to carry, ending in each case with the same line: under the direction of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest. Eleazar supervises the one clan whose cargo can kill; Ithamar supervises the two clans whose cargo cannot. The chapter's own organization already tells the reader which duties were dangerous before a single verse says so directly.

Merari: the same charge repeated from a chapter ago, now with a headcount attached

Vv31-32 repeat, almost word for word, the list of frames, bars, pillars, and sockets this project's own note on Numbers 3:36-37 (already on these pages) already assigned to Merari's clan in general terms; this time the list closes on a detail chapter 3 never mentioned — the vessels of their charge must be counted ‘by name’ (v32), an inventory precise enough to name individual items, not just categories.

2,750 plus 2,630 plus 3,200 — a total that actually adds up

⚠ Unlike Numbers 3's own clan totals (already on these pages), which summed to 300 more than the chapter's own stated total, this chapter's arithmetic is exact: 2,750 Kohathites, plus 2,630 Gershonites, plus 3,200 Merarites, equal precisely the 8,580 stated as the grand total in v48. No gap, no rounding, nothing left for a later interpreter to explain. The difference is simple — this is a headcount of men actually fit for a specific job, ages thirty to fifty, not the broader dedication-census of every male from a month old that produced chapter 3's own harder puzzle.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English Numbers 4 both run 1–49 — verified against the Masoretic text, no versification shift. Five Masoretic paragraphs divide it: open after v16, v20; closed after v28, v37; open after v49. No new dictionary entries this chapter; tachash and mishmeret are both reused from earlier chapters (already on these pages). New encyclopedia entry: Ithamar, Aaron's youngest son, supervisor of Gershon and Merari.

Echoes paid. Numbers 3's own closing promise (already on these pages) that this chapter would detail exactly how the Kohathites must wrap the sanctuary's holiest objects, now fulfilled; the tachash covering already flagged at Exodus 25:5 (already on these pages), reused eight times in a single wrapping procedure; Numbers 3:36-37's own list of Merari's charge (already on these pages), repeated here with an added inventory requirement; and Numbers 3's own 22,300-versus-22,000 discrepancy (already on these pages), thrown into relief by this chapter's own exact, gapless arithmetic.

Echoes planted. Numbers 8:23-26 (already on these pages), which sets a retirement age of fifty for Levite service — matching this chapter's own upper bound — but a lower entry age of twenty-five, not thirty, a genuine unresolved tension with this chapter's own threshold that the traditional harmonization reads as apprenticeship (twenty-five) versus full active duty (thirty).
