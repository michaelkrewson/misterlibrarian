A father's teaching: kindness and faithfulness bound on the neck, “trust in Jehovah with all your heart”, firstfruits, the discipline of Jehovah as a father's reproof, wisdom more precious than corals and a tree of life, the world founded by wisdom, and sayings on dealing with a neighbor, ending “to the humble he gives grace”.
Attributed to Solomon, son of David (reigned c. 970–931 BC), like the rest of the book's opening collection; wisdom literature keeps no calendar of its own.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · “Do not forget my teaching” — kindness and faithfulness bound on the neck and written on the tablet of the heart

My teaching and my commandments. “My son, do not forget my teaching, and let your heart keep my commandments”: torah and mitzvot, the two great words of Deuteronomy, spoken here by a father. Chapter 1 had the son hear “your father’s discipline” and “your mother’s teaching” (Proverbs 1:8, now on these pages), and “teaching” stays the rendering for torah in this book, because what is meant is a parent’s instruction, not a law code. “Length of days and years of life” is the promise Deuteronomy attaches to keeping the commandments, “so that your days may be prolonged” (Deuteronomy 6:2, now on these pages). The NIV has “do not forget my teaching”; the ASV “forget not my law”; the NWT 1984 “my law do not forget”.

Kindness and faithfulness. “Let not kindness and faithfulness forsake you”: chesed and emet, the pair that Abraham’s servant thanks Jehovah for, “his kindness and his faithfulness” (Genesis 24:27), and that the spies promise Rahab (Joshua 2:14, both now on these pages). Here they are almost persons, companions who might walk away. The Greek turns them into deeds, “acts of mercy and faithful acts”. The ASV has “Let not kindness and truth forsake thee”; the KJV “Let not mercy and truth forsake thee”; the NWT 1984 “loving-kindness and trueness”.

On the neck and on the heart. “Bind them around your neck, write them on the tablet of your heart”: the binding is Deuteronomy’s, “you shall bind them for a sign upon your hand” (Deuteronomy 6:8), and the tablet, luach, is the word for the stone tablets of the law (Exodus 24:12). The neck is where chapter 1 hung wisdom’s “pendants for your neck” (1:9). Jeremiah promises the same writing as a new covenant, “I will put my law within them, and write it on their heart” (Jeremiah 31:32), and Paul calls the Corinthians a letter written “on tablets of human hearts” (2 Corinthians 3:3, all now on these pages). The ASV has “Write them upon the tablet of thy heart”; the KJV “upon the table of thine heart”; the NWT 1984 “Tie them about your throat”.

Favor and good insight. “And find favor and good insight in the eyes of God and humankind”: chen, the favor Noah “found in the eyes of Jehovah” (Genesis 6:8), and sekhel tov, from the root of haskel, “insight” (1:3). The boy Samuel “was in favour both with Jehovah and with men” (1 Samuel 2:26) and Jesus “advanced in wisdom and in stature, and in favour with God and with people” (Luke 2:52, all now on these pages). The Greek reads “plan good things before the Lord and men”, and Paul echoes those words twice (Romans 12:17; 2 Corinthians 8:21, both now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “find favor and good insight”; the ASV “find favor and good understanding”; the NIV “a good name”.

Verses 5–8 · “Trust in Jehovah with all your heart” — your own understanding, “know him”, and paths made straight

Trust in Jehovah with all your heart. “Trust in Jehovah with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding”: batach, to trust, set against nish’an, to lean one’s weight on something. What a person must not lean on is binah, “understanding”, the very faculty chapter 1 set out to teach (1:2); the book does not despise it, it refuses to make it the support. The Greek has “do not be lifted up by your own wisdom”. The ASV has “Trust in Jehovah with all thy heart, And lean not upon thine own understanding”; the Douay “Have confidence in the Lord with all thy heart”; the TLB “don’t ever trust yourself”.

Know him. “In all your ways know him”: da’ehu, from yada, the verb behind da’at, “knowledge”. The familiar “acknowledge him” makes it a matter of giving credit; the Hebrew asks for the knowing that the prophets call for, as when Jeremiah says the day will come when none need teach another “Know Jehovah”, “for they will all know me” (Jeremiah 31:33, now on these pages). “And he will make your paths straight”: yeyasher, to make level and right. The Greek has orthotomē, “he will cut your ways straight”, and adds “and your foot will not stumble”; its verb is the one 2 Timothy uses for “rightly handling the word of truth” (2 Timothy 2:15, now on these pages). The ASV has “acknowledge him, And he will direct thy paths”; the NWT 1984 “take notice of him, and he himself will make your paths straight”; the Douay “think on him”.

Not wise in your own eyes. “Do not be wise in your own eyes; fear Jehovah, and turn away from evil”: Paul quotes the first line, “Do not become wise in your own eyes” (Romans 12:16; compare 11:25), and the second is what is said of Job, a man “who feared God and turned away from evil” (Job 1:1, all now on these pages). The ASV has “Be not wise in thine own eyes; Fear Jehovah, and depart from evil”; the Douay “Be not wise in thy own conceit”; the NWT 1984 “Fear Jehovah and turn away from bad”.

Healing to your navel. “It will be healing to your navel and refreshment to your bones”: shor is the navel, or the cord, the place where life first came in; the Greek read a word one letter different and has “your body”. Shiqquy, refreshment, is otherwise “drink”: “my oil and my drink” (Hosea 2:7) and “I have mingled my drink with weeping” (Psalm 102:10). The NWT 1984 has “a healing to your navel and a refreshment to your bones”; the KJV “health to thy navel, and marrow to thy bones”; the NIV “health to your body and nourishment to your bones”.

Verses 9–10 · Firstfruits — “honor Jehovah from your wealth”, and barns filled with plenty

From the first of all your produce. “Honor Jehovah from your wealth, and from the first of all your produce”: kabbed, to honor, is to give weight, the root of kavod, and the chapter will end on that word (v35). “The first” is reshit, the firstfruits of the law, “some of the first of all the fruit of the ground” brought in a basket (Deuteronomy 26:2, now on these pages), and the same word as “the fear of Jehovah is the beginning of knowledge” (1:7). The Greek adds righteousness twice: “from your righteous labors … from your fruits of righteousness”. The ASV has “Honor Jehovah with thy substance, And with the first-fruits of all thine increase”; the NWT 1984 “Honor Jehovah with your valuable things”; the NIV “with your wealth”.

Barns and vats. “And your barns will be filled with plenty, and your vats will burst with new wine”: “your barns” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and the blessing of Deuteronomy 28:8, “Jehovah will command the blessing to be with you in your barns” (now on these pages). Yifrotsu, “burst”, is the verb of breaking out; tirosh is new wine, the must fresh from the press. The ASV has “So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, And thy vats shall overflow with new wine”; the KJV “thy presses shall burst out with new wine”; the NWT 1984 “your stores of supply will be filled with plenty”.

Verses 11–12 · “Do not reject the discipline of Jehovah” — the father who reproves, and the Greek of Hebrews 12

Do not reject, do not loathe. “My son, do not reject the discipline of Jehovah, and do not loathe his reproof”: musar is “discipline”, as everywhere in this book, the correction that forms. Tim’as, “reject”, is the verb of the people who “rejected” Jehovah as king (1 Samuel 8:7), and taqots, “loathe”, is Rebekah’s “I loathe my life” (Genesis 27:46, both now on these pages). This form of the verb stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Deuteronomy had made the same picture: “as a man disciplines his son, Jehovah your God disciplines you” (Deuteronomy 8:5, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “The discipline of Jehovah, O my son, do not reject; and do not abhor his reproof”; the ASV “despise not the chastening of Jehovah; Neither be weary of his reproof”; the Douay “reject not the correction of the Lord”.

As a father the son. “For whom Jehovah loves he reproves, and as a father the son in whom he delights”: yokhiach, from yakach, to argue a case out and set it right. The second line has no verb of its own; it borrows “reproves” from the first, so the father reproves the son he delights in. The Greek read the same letters, u-kh’av, “and as a father”, as a verb, “and he causes pain”, and so prints “and he scourges every son whom he receives”. That is the form Hebrews quotes: “for whom Jehovah loves he disciplines, and he scourges every son whom he receives” (Hebrews 12:5–6, now on these pages), and the risen Christ says “As many as I love, I reprove and discipline” (Revelation 3:19, now on these pages). The ASV has “For whom Jehovah loveth he reproveth; Even as a father the son in whom he delighteth”; the NWT 1984 “even as a father does a son in whom he finds pleasure”; the NIV “the Lord disciplines those he loves”.

Verses 13–18 · “She is a tree of life” — happy is the one who finds wisdom, better than silver and corals

Happy is the human. “Happy is the human who finds wisdom, and the human who draws out discernment”: ashrei, the first word of the Psalter, “Happy is the man” (Psalm 1:1, now on these pages), and here the generic adam, any human being. The passage closes on the same root, “those who hold her fast are made happy” (v18), me’ushar, so the six verses are framed by happiness. Tevunah, “discernment”, is the sister of binah, “understanding” (v5), from the same root and kept distinct in this translation (tevunah). The NWT 1984 has “Happy is the man that has found wisdom, and the man that gets discernment”; the ASV “the man that getteth understanding”; the NIV “Blessed are those who find wisdom”.

Better than silver, more precious than corals. “For her profit is better than the profit of silver, and her yield than fine gold”: sachar is trade, the merchant’s gain. “She is more precious than corals”: peninim, the word that sets the woman of valour “far beyond corals” (Proverbs 31:10, now on these pages); the familiar “rubies” is a guess. The written text has an unusual spelling of the word, read in its ordinary form. The Greek adds two lines: no evil will stand against her, and she is well known to all who come near her. The NWT 1984 has “It is more precious than corals”; the ASV “She is more precious than rubies”; the Geneva “It is more precious than pearls”.

Ways of pleasantness. “Length of days is in her right hand; in her left, riches and honor”: what verse 2 promised from the father’s teaching, wisdom now holds in her hands. “Her ways are ways of pleasantness, and all her paths are peace”: no’am, delight, the word of the psalmist’s wish “to gaze on the beauty of Jehovah” (Psalm 27:4, now on these pages). The ASV has “Her ways are ways of pleasantness, And all her paths are peace”; the NWT 1984 “all its roadways are peace”; the Douay “Her ways are beautiful ways”.

A tree of life. “She is a tree of life to those who take hold of her”: “a tree of life”, without the article, stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in Proverbs (here, 11:30, 13:12 and 15:4). Genesis has “the tree of life”, the one in the middle of the garden that humankind was barred from (Genesis 2:9; 3:24), and Revelation gives it back, “a tree of life … for the healing of the nations” (Revelation 22:2, all now on these pages). In the synagogue, verses 18 and 17 are sung as the scroll of the Torah is returned to the ark, so that the wisdom of this chapter is sung of the Torah itself. The Greek adds “and secure to those who lean on her as on the Lord”. The ASV has “She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her: And happy is every one that retaineth her”; the NWT 1984 “those keeping fast hold of it are to be called happy”; the TLB “to those who eat her fruit”.

Verses 19–26 · Creation by wisdom — the deeps burst open, practical wisdom, sweet sleep, and Jehovah your confidence

By wisdom he founded the earth. “Jehovah by wisdom founded the earth; he established the heavens by discernment. By his knowledge the deeps burst open”: the three words of the father’s teaching, wisdom, discernment and knowledge, are the tools God built the world with. “The deeps”, tehomot, and “burst open”, nivqa’u, are the words of the flood, when “all the springs of the great deep burst open” (Genesis 7:11, now on these pages); here the same bursting waters the earth. Wisdom’s own account of creation, “Jehovah possessed me at the beginning of his way”, comes in Proverbs 8. The ASV has “Jehovah by wisdom founded the earth; By understanding he established the heavens”; the NWT 1984 “He solidly fixed the heavens in discernment”; the KJV “By his knowledge the depths are broken up”.

Do not let them slip. “My son, let them not slip from your eyes; guard practical wisdom and discretion”: yaluzu, from luz, to turn aside, and the same root returns in verse 32 for “the devious”, naloz: the son must not let wisdom turn aside, or he becomes the man who does. Tushiyyah is “practical wisdom”, sound counsel that works; mezimmah is “discretion”, as in 1:4. “Grace to your neck” returns to the neck of verse 3. The NWT 1984 has “Safeguard practical wisdom and thinking ability”; the ASV “Keep sound wisdom and discretion”; the NIV “preserve sound judgment and discretion”.

Walking and sleeping. “Then you will walk your way securely, and you will not strike your foot”: tiggof, the verb of the psalm, “lest you strike your foot against a stone” (Psalm 91:12), which the tempter quotes to Jesus (Matthew 4:6, both now on these pages). “If you lie down, you will feel no dread; … do not be afraid of sudden dread”: pachad, verb and noun, twice in two verses. “The ruin of the wicked” can be the ruin the wicked bring or the ruin that falls on them; the Hebrew allows both. The Greek of verse 25, “you shall not fear terror”, is echoed in 1 Peter 3:6 (now on these pages). The ASV has “thy sleep shall be sweet” and “Be not afraid of sudden fear, Neither of the desolation of the wicked”; the NWT 1984 “nor of the storm upon the wicked ones”.

Jehovah your confidence. “For Jehovah will be your confidence, and will keep your foot from capture”: kesel, confidence, is also the word for the loins, “the fat that is on them, which is by the loins” (Leviticus 3:4, now on these pages), and its letters make kesil, the dull fool of 1:22. “Your confidence” in this form stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. “Capture”, lakhed, is the snare a hunter sets. The ASV has “For Jehovah will be thy confidence, And will keep thy foot from being taken”; the NIV “the Lord will be at your side”; the Douay “the Lord will be at thy side”.

Verses 27–35 · The neighbor sayings — “do not withhold good”, the devious and the upright, and “he gives grace to the humble”

Good to its owners. “Do not withhold good from its owners when it is in the power of your hand to do it”: mi-be’alav, “from its masters”, those to whom the good belongs, such as a worker’s wage or a neighbor’s loan. “In the power of your hand” is the idiom of Laban, “it is in the power of my hand to do you harm” (Genesis 31:29), and of the curse, “it is not in the power of your hand” (Deuteronomy 28:32, both now on these pages). Three words in these verses are written one way and read another: “your hands”, read “your hand” (v27); “your neighbors”, read “your neighbor” (v28); and “quarrel”, written tarov and read tariv (v30). The ASV has “Withhold not good from them to whom it is due, When it is in the power of thy hand to do it”; the Geneva “Withhold not the good from the owners thereof”; the NWT 1984 “from those to whom it is owing”.

Tomorrow I will give. “Do not say to your neighbor, ‘Go, and come back, and tomorrow I will give,’ when you have it with you”: the Greek adds “for you do not know what the next day will bring forth”, a line close to James, “you who do not know what your life will be tomorrow” (James 4:14, now on these pages). “Do not devise evil against your neighbor while he dwells securely beside you”: tacharosh is the verb of plowing and of engraving, and the evil is cut like a furrow. “Securely” is la-vetach, the word of verse 23. The ASV has “Devise not evil against thy neighbor, Seeing he dwelleth securely by thee”; the NWT 1984 “Do not fabricate against your fellowman anything bad”; the NIV “who lives trustfully near you”.

The devious and the upright. “Do not envy a man of violence”: chamas, the violence that filled the earth before the flood (Genesis 6:11, now on these pages). “For the devious is an abomination to Jehovah, but his close counsel is with the upright”: naloz, the one who turns aside, from the root of verse 21; and sod, the inner circle, the word of Jeremiah’s test, “who has stood in the council of Jehovah?” (Jeremiah 23:18, now on these pages). “The curse of Jehovah is in the house of the wicked, but the dwelling of the righteous he blesses”: naveh, the shepherd’s pasture-abode. The ASV has “But his friendship is with the upright”; the NWT 1984 “the devious person is a detestable thing to Jehovah, but His intimacy is with the upright ones”; the KJV “but his secret is with the righteous”.

The scoffers and the humble. “The scoffers he scoffs at, but to the humble he gives grace”: the Hebrew opens with im, “if” or “surely”, and an emphatic “he”, so that the scoffing comes back on the scoffers. They are the letsim of 1:22. The written text has aniyyim, “the afflicted”, read anavim, “the humble”; the two words come from one root. The Greek has “The Lord opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble”, and in that form the line is quoted twice in the New Testament, with “God” for “the Lord” (James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5, both now on these pages). The ASV has “Surely he scoffeth at the scoffers; But he giveth grace unto the lowly”; the NWT 1984 “If it has to do with ridiculers, he himself will deride”; the Douay “to the meek he will give grace”.

Honor and disgrace. “The wise will inherit honor, but fools carry off disgrace”: kavod, honor, is from the root for heavy, the honor given to Jehovah in verse 9; qalon, disgrace, sounds like qal, light. Merim, “carry off”, is the verb of lifting high, which is why the older shelf reads “shame shall be the promotion of fools”: what raises them is their disgrace. The Greek has “the ungodly exalted dishonor”. The ASV has “But shame shall be the promotion of fools”; the NWT 1984 “the stupid ones are exalting dishonor”; the NIV “fools get only shame”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “teaching” for torah, as in 1:8; “discipline” for musar; “understanding” for binah (v5) and “discernment” for tevunah (vv13, 19), with the NWT 1984; “practical wisdom” for tushiyyah; “discretion” for mezimmah, as in 1:4; “kindness and faithfulness” for chesed ve-emet, as in Genesis 24:27 and Joshua 2:14; “know him” for da’ehu; “corals” for peninim, as in 31:10; “a tree of life”; “burst open” for the deeps, as in Genesis 7:11; “your confidence” for kesel; and the Hebrew of 3:11–12 and 3:34 kept where the New Testament quotes the Greek.

Echoes paid. The father’s teaching and the neck of 1:8–9; reshit of 1:7 as firstfruits; the scoffers of 1:22; the tablets of Exodus 24 and the binding of Deuteronomy 6; the deeps of Genesis 7:11; the tree of life of Genesis 2–3; the barns of Deuteronomy 28:8; and the father’s discipline of Deuteronomy 8:5.

Echoes planted. Wisdom at creation (Proverbs 8:22–31); the other trees of life (Proverbs 11:30, 13:12, 15:4); “write them on the tablet of your heart” again (Proverbs 7:3); and “seek her like silver” (Proverbs 2:4). None is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Proverbs stands on these pages at chapters 1, 3, 4, 16, 18, 22 and 31. Next in sequence is Proverbs 2 — “if you seek her like silver, and search for her like hidden treasure”: wisdom as the thing worth digging for.
