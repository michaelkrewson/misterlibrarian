🗺️ Atlas

Siloam approximate

A pool at the southern end of the City of David in Jerusalem, fed by the Gihon Spring through Hezekiah’s tunnel (2 Kings 20:20, not yet on these pages) — its Hebrew name, Shiloah, ‘sent, conducted’ (from shalach, to send), names exactly what the tunnel does, carrying water where it did not naturally reach. Isaiah already calls it ‘the waters of Shiloah that flow gently’ (Isaiah 8:6, not yet on these pages), the language of an established landmark centuries before Jesus. A TOWER standing at or near the pool is named only here in the whole Bible (Luke 13:4) — nothing else in Scripture or in Josephus records the collapse Jesus refers to, and no tower ruin has been identified with confidence; it survives only as the incident these two verses report. ⚠ Two separate finds carry the name. The SILOAM INSCRIPTION, a Hebrew text carved by Hezekiah’s own tunnelers describing the moment the two digging crews met underground, was found inside the tunnel in 1880. A second, later pool — a large stepped, plastered pool from the Second Temple period — came to light beginning in 2004 during utility work near the tunnel’s outlet, and is generally identified with the pool where John’s Gospel places a blind man’s healing (John 9:7: ‘Go, wash in the pool of Siloam’). Whether this Second Temple pool, Isaiah’s older Shiloah, and the tower Jesus names here all stood at exactly one spot is a question archaeology narrows without fully settling.
