🗺️ Atlas

Hebron

Where Abram settles after Lot's departure, among the oaks of Mamre, and builds his third altar (13:18); also called KIRIATH-ARBA, 'town of Arba' (a giant of the Anakim) or 'town of the four.' It becomes central: Sarah dies here and Abraham buys the cave of Machpelah for her tomb (ch. 23), the patriarchs' burial place; and it is David's FIRST royal capital, where he reigns seven years before taking Jerusalem (2 Samuel 5:5). One of the oldest continuously-inhabited towns on earth. ★ In Joshua 10 (now on these pages) its king, Hoham, joins the coalition against Gibeon and dies at Makkedah; later in the same chapter Joshua takes the city and strikes down its king (v37), a second king of Hebron in one chapter. ★ Joshua 14:13 (now on these pages) is where it is actually granted — to Caleb, directly, on the strength of a forty-five-year-old oath, the first of two inheritances in the land division never once called 'by lot' — Joshua’s own city, given after the last lot is thrown (19:49–50), is the second. Judges 1:10 (already on these pages) has Judah take it again after Joshua’s death. ⭐ Joshua 20:7 (now on these pages) puts it, as ‘Kiriath-arba — that is, Hebron,’ in the hill country of Judah among the six cities of refuge; the clause stands in only one other verse, Joshua 15:54.
