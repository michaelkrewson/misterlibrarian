An end: the word to the ground of Israel that the end has come on its four corners. The rod has blossomed; buying and selling lose their meaning; the horn is blown and no one goes out; sword outside, famine and pestilence inside; silver is thrown into the streets; the treasured place is profaned; vision, instruction and counsel fail, and king and prince mourn.
Follows chapter 6. The chapter gives no date; the next date in the book is at 8:1, in the sixth year.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · Ezekiel 7:1–4 “an end” — the four corners — “my eye will not spare”

⭐ An end. The oracle opens with one word, qets (qets). “The end has come” (ba haqqets) stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: verses 2 and 6 here, and Amos 8:2, ‘the end has come upon my people Israel’. Before the flood God had said “The end of all flesh has come before me, for the earth is filled with violence” (Genesis 6:13, now on these pages). This chapter has both: an end, and a city full of violence beside a land full of bloody judgment (verse 23).

⭐ The ground of Israel. The word is admat Yisra’el, as at Ezekiel 36:6; the phrase stands in seventeen verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in Ezekiel. The land that follows, ‘the four corners of the land’, is ha’arets, the word that also means the earth: Isaiah 11:12 (now on these pages) has ‘the four corners of the earth’. Here the context is Israel, so ‘land’. The text we follow prints two spellings of ‘four’ side by side, the written and the read.

⭐ My eye will not spare. The words of 5:11 (now on these pages) return in verse 4 and again in verse 9; spelled this way they stand in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, the other two Ezekiel 8:18 and 9:10 (both now on these pages). Deuteronomy had told a judge or a family that ‘your eye shall not pity’ (chus); here it is God’s eye. The ASV has “mine eye shall not spare thee, neither will I have pity” and the NWT 1984 “my eye will not feel sorry for you”.

⚠ Who is ‘you’. In verses 3, 4, 8 and 9 the ‘you’ is feminine singular, the ground or land of verse 2. ‘You will know’ at the end of verses 4 and 9 is plural, the people. English cannot show it.

Verses 5–9 · Ezekiel 7:5–9 one evil — the end awakens — “the turn” — “Jehovah who strikes”

⭐ One evil. “One evil” (ra’ah achat) stands in this one verse. The versions mostly make it a disaster like no other: the ASV has “An evil, an only evil”, the NIV “Unheard-of disaster!” and the NWT 2013 “a unique calamity”.

⭐ The end wakes. Verse 6 plays on sound: haqqets heqits, ‘the end has awakened’. The ASV has “it awaketh against thee”, the KJV “it watcheth for thee” and the NIV “It has roused itself against you”.

⚠ The turn, the doom, the morning. The noun of verse 7, hatsefirah, comes again in verse 10 with a shorter spelling, and the shelf shows how uncertain it is: the KJV has “The morning is come unto thee”, the ASV “Thy doom is come unto thee”, the Douay “Destruction is come upon thee” and the NWT 1984 “The garland must come to you”. This translation prints ‘the turn’, with the NWT 2013 (“Your turn has come”), and does not claim to know. Mehumah, ‘panic’ (mehumah), stands against a shout on the mountains, the joy that is not there. Verse 7 turns to the masculine ‘inhabitant of the land’.

⭐ Jehovah who strikes. Verses 8–9 repeat much of verses 3–4. Among the differences: verse 8 opens with fury poured out and anger spent where verse 3 has the end and anger sent; verse 9 drops the ‘you’ after ‘spare’; verse 4 says ‘your ways I will bring upon you’ where verse 9 says ‘according to your ways’, and verse 9 ends not ‘I am Jehovah’ but ani Yhwh makkeh; ‘I, Jehovah, am the one who strikes’ stands in this one verse. ‘Spend my anger’ repeats the verb of 6:12. The ASV has “ye shall know that I, Jehovah, do smite”.

Verses 10–11 · Ezekiel 7:10–11 the rod has blossomed — violence into a rod of wickedness

⭐ A rod that blossoms. The words are those of Aaron’s rod, which “had budded: it had put out a bud, and blossomed a blossom” (Numbers 17:23, now on these pages): matteh, rod, with the verbs parach and tsuts. There a blossoming rod ended a dispute about who was chosen. Here what buds is zadon, insolence (zadon). “Insolence has budded” stands in this one verse. The same noun, matteh, also means ‘tribe’. The ASV has “the rod hath blossomed, pride hath budded” and the NIV “the rod has budded, arrogance has blossomed”.

⚠ Verse 11. The second half is very hard. The Hebrew has three short ‘not’ phrases, then a word, noah, that the versions take in different ways. The KJV has “neither shall there be wailing for them”, the ASV “neither shall there be eminency among them”, the Douay “there shall be no rest among them” and the NIV “nothing of value”. This translation follows the ASV and leaves the gap where the Hebrew leaves it: it does not add ‘shall remain’.

Verses 12–14 · Ezekiel 7:12–14 buyer and seller — no return — the horn that calls no one

⭐ Buyer and seller. In the law, land sold in Israel was not gone for good: the seller could redeem it, or at the Jubilee he returned to his possession (Leviticus 25:27–28, now on these pages). Here the seller ‘will not return to what was sold’ (mimkar, the word Leviticus 25 uses), and so the buyer has no cause to rejoice. The verb ‘return’ is used again of the vision, which ‘will not turn back’.

⚠ A hard verse. Verse 13 is difficult from end to end; this translation keeps it close and does not smooth it. The three verses end or turn on nearly one line: charon, ‘burning anger’, on all its multitude (12); chazon, ‘vision’, for all its multitude (13), one letter away; and charoni, ‘my burning anger’ (14). The ASV has “neither shall any strengthen himself in the iniquity of his life” and the NIV “not one of them will preserve their life”. Three words for the same heat surround it: ‘burning anger’ (charon) in verses 12 and 14, kept apart from ‘anger’ (af) and ‘fury’ (chemah).

⭐ The horn. “They have blown the horn” (taq’u vattaqoa) stands in this one verse. Jeremiah 6:1 has the same letters for blowing at the town of Tekoa, a play on its name. Here the call is sounded and nobody goes.

Verses 15–18 · Ezekiel 7:15–18 sword outside, hunger inside — doves of the valleys — slack hands

⭐ Outside and inside. The pattern of “Outside, the sword will bereave; and inside, terror” (Deuteronomy 32:25, now on these pages) and of Lamentations 1:20 (now on these pages), here with pestilence and famine inside. This is 6:12 again: no distance saves.

⭐ Doves. Those who escape (peletim) are on the mountains like doves of the valleys, moaning, each in his iniquity. ‘Moaning’ shares its root with hamon, the ‘multitude’ of verses 12–14. The ASV has “all of them moaning, every one in his iniquity” and the NIV “each for their own sins”.

⭐ Hands and knees. “Hands will hang slack” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Isaiah 13:7, and “will run with water” of knees stands in two, here and Ezekiel 21:12 (now on these pages). Several versions soften the second to weakness: the KJV has “all knees shall be weak as water”; the NIV takes it bodily, “every leg will be wet with urine”. This translation says what the Hebrew says. Sackcloth and shaved heads are mourning (qorchah).

Verses 19–22 · Ezekiel 7:19–22 silver in the streets — no deliverance — the treasured place profaned

⭐ Silver and gold. The words match Zephaniah’s: “Neither their silver nor their gold will be able to save them on the day of Jehovah’s wrath” (Zephaniah 1:18, now on these pages). “On the day of the wrath of” (beyom evrat) stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and that. Their gold becomes niddah, a thing unclean (niddah), the word used of Jerusalem in Lamentations 1:17. Wealth became the stumbling block of their iniquity.

⚠ Verse 20. “The beauty of his ornament” stands in this one verse. The subject changes from ‘he’ to ‘they’, and the verse does not say what the ornament was; the images made of it point to gold and silver. The ASV has “as for the beauty of his ornament, he set it in majesty”, the NIV “They took pride in their beautiful jewelry” and the TLB makes it Temple gold: “I gave you gold to use in decorating the Temple”.

⭐ My treasured place. The word of verse 22, tsefuni, is built on tsafan, to hide or store up. The verse does not name the place; the KJV has “my secret place”, the NIV “the place I treasure” and the NWT 1984 “my concealed place”. The ‘it’ the robbers enter and profane is feminine in Hebrew, which the word for ‘treasured place’ is not, and the verse does not say what is meant. The ‘robbers’ who enter (paritsim) are the word of the ‘den of robbers’ in Jeremiah 7:11. At the end of verse 21 the text we follow prints two forms of ‘they will profane it’, one feminine and one masculine. Verse 22 ends with a paragraph break.

Verses 23–27 · Ezekiel 7:23–27 the chain — the pride of the strong — no vision, no instruction, no counsel

⚠ The chain. “The chain” (harattoq), in this form, stands in this one verse; a plural of the word is the “chains of gold” of 1 Kings 6:21 (now on these pages). Here the Douay reads it “Make a shutting up”, and several versions read a chain, as for captives. The NIV has “Prepare chains!”. ‘Judgment of blood’ stands in this one verse; violence fills the city as it filled the earth in Genesis 6:11.

⭐ Pride and holy places. The covenant warning had said “I will break the pride of your power” (Leviticus 26:19, now on these pages). Here ge’on azim, ‘the pride of the strong’, is brought to an end. The word for their holy places, meqadshehem, is pointed differently from the ‘sanctuaries’ of Leviticus 26:31, and the verb venichalu can be heard as ‘be profaned’ or as ‘possess’: the ASV has “their holy places shall be profaned”, the Douay “they shall possess their sanctuary”.

⭐ Anguish, then silence. “Anguish” (qefadah) stands in this one verse (qippod). Jeremiah’s opponents had counted on “instruction will not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the wise, nor the word from the prophet” (Jeremiah 18:18, now on these pages). Ezekiel says all three go: a vision is sought from a prophet, instruction perishes from the priest, counsel from the elders. King, prince and the people of the land then all fail. God judges them ‘by their judgments’, the phrase turned back on them (compare 5:7). The chapter ends with a paragraph break.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “anger” for af at verses 3 and 8; “fury” for chemah at verse 8; “burning anger” for charon at verses 12 and 14; “wrath” for evrah at verse 19; “turn” for tsefirah at verses 7 and 10; “abominations” at verses 3, 4, 8, 9 and 20; and “a thing unclean” at verses 19 and 20.

Echoes paid. The end and the violence of Genesis 6:13; the returning seller of Leviticus 25; the silver and gold of Zephaniah 1:18; and the eye that will not spare from 5:11.

Echoes planted. The eye that will not spare at 8:18 and at 9:10 (both now on these pages), and the knees running with water at 21:12 (now on these pages).

Ezekiel 8, now on these pages: in the sixth year, with the elders of Judah sitting before him, the hand of the Lord Jehovah falls on the prophet.
