Paul writes to the congregations of Galatia that the Spirit came to them by the hearing of faith, not by works of law, and argues from Abraham, who believed God, that the promise came 430 years before the law and that those who are Christ’s are Abraham’s seed.
Undated by the text: Galatians names no year. Its date depends on which Galatia it was written to and which Jerusalem visit chapter 2 describes, both argued. The chapter’s own number, 430 years from the promise to the law, is a count of the past, not a date for the letter.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–5 · the evil eye, Christ portrayed as crucified, and the Spirit by the hearing of faith

⚠ “Who has bewitched you?” — baskainō, the evil eye. Baskainō stands in one verse of the New Testament, this one. In ordinary Greek it is the evil eye, the envious look that does harm, and this translation keeps the eye because Paul does: the next words are kat’ ophthalmous, “before whose eyes.” The address Ō anoētoi, “O senseless,” is the risen Jesus’ to the two on the road at Luke 24:25, “O senseless ones, and slow of heart to believe,” and comes back in v3. The Byzantine text adds “that you should not obey the truth” after “bewitched” and “among you” after “portrayed,” which is why the KJV reads “O foolish Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that ye should not obey the truth.” The Douay reads “O senseless Galatians”; the NWT 1984 reads “who is it that brought YOU under evil influence”; the TLB reads “What magician has hypnotized you and cast an evil spell upon you?”

“Publicly portrayed as crucified.” Proegraphē is “written up before”: here, set out in public before their eyes; at Romans 15:4 the same form is “whatever was written beforehand.” “Crucified” is a perfect participle, the Christ who was crucified and remains the crucified one, as in “Jesus Christ, and him crucified” at 1 Corinthians 2:2. The NIV reads “clearly portrayed as crucified”; the ASV reads “openly set forth crucified”; the NWT 1984 reads “openly portrayed impaled,” its rendering of stauroō; the TLB reads “as clearly as though I had waved a placard before you.”

⚠ “The hearing of faith” — asked twice. Ex akoēs pisteōs, at v2 and again at v5, set against “works of law” (2:16). Akoē is hearing, and also the thing heard: at Romans 10:17, “faith is from the thing heard.” So the phrase can be the hearing that comes with faith, or the message that calls for it; “the hearing of faith” keeps both. The KJV and the ASV both read “by the hearing of faith”; the NIV reads “by believing what you heard”; the NWT 1984 reads “due to a hearing by faith.”

“Having begun with the Spirit, brought to completion with the flesh?” Enarxamenoi … epiteleisthe: enarchomai, “begin,” stands in two verses of the New Testament, and both pair it with epiteleō, “complete”: here, and Philippians 1:6, “the one who began a good work in you will bring it to completion.” At Philippians God finishes what God began; here the Galatians are asked whether the flesh will finish what the Spirit began. The verb can be middle, “are you finishing,” or passive, “are you being completed.” The ASV reads “are ye now perfected in the flesh?”; the NIV reads “are you now trying to finish by means of the flesh?”; the NWT 1984 reads “are YOU now being completed in flesh?”

“Did you suffer so much in vain?” Tosauta epathete eikē. Paschō is usually “suffer,” but it can mean simply “experience,” and the next verse is about the Spirit and acts of power, so either can be meant. Eikē, “in vain,” is the word of 1 Corinthians 15:2, “unless you believed in vain.” “The one who supplies” is the verb of 2 Corinthians 9:10, “the one who supplies seed to the sower,” and “acts of power” are the dynameis of 2 Corinthians 12:12. The KJV reads “Have ye suffered so many things in vain?”; the NIV reads “Have you experienced so much in vain.”

Verses 6–9 · Abraham believed God, sons of Abraham, and all the nations blessed

⚠ “Abraham believed God” — Genesis 15:6, in Greek. In the Hebrew of Genesis 15:6 Abram “believed Jehovah, and he counted it to him as righteousness”: the divine name, and God doing the counting. The Greek Old Testament reads “Abram believed God, and it was reckoned to him,” and Paul quotes the Greek, here and at Romans 4:3, where this translation also prints “reckoned.” This translation prints “God” because Paul’s Greek says theō. The NWT 1984 prints “put faith in Jehovah,” supplying the name the Hebrew has and the Greek here does not. The KJV reads “Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness”; the ASV reads “it was reckoned unto him for righteousness”; the Douay adds a formula, “As it is written: Abraham believed God,” from the Vulgate’s Sicut scriptum est.

“Those who are of faith — these are sons of Abraham.” Hoi ek pisteōs is built like hoi ek peritomēs, “those of the circumcision,” at 2:12: a party named by what it stands on. Ginōskete can be a command, “know,” or a statement, “you know.” The KJV reads “Know ye therefore that they which are of faith”; the NWT 1984 reads “Surely YOU know that those who adhere to faith.”

“The Scripture … announced the good news beforehand.” Proeuangelizomai stands in one verse of the New Testament, this one, and the one doing it is the Scripture itself, foreseeing. The quotation joins two promises: “all the families of the ground will be blessed” through Abram (Genesis 12:3) and “all the nations of the earth will be blessed” through him (Genesis 18:18); Paul takes “in you” from the first and “all the nations” from the second. “Justifies” is present: God is doing it. The KJV reads “preached before the gospel unto Abraham”; the NIV reads “announced the gospel in advance to Abraham.”

“Abraham the believer.” Tō pistō Abraam: pistos is “faithful” and also “believing,” and this translation prints “the believer” because v6 has just said “Abraham believed.” The KJV reads “blessed with faithful Abraham”; the NWT 1984 reads “blessed together with faithful Abraham”; the NIV reads “along with Abraham, the man of faith.”

Verses 10–14 · under a curse, the righteous will live by faith, and hanging on a tree

⚠ “Cursed is everyone who does not abide” — Deuteronomy 27:26, with “everyone” and “all.” The Hebrew of Deuteronomy 27:26 reads “Cursed is he who does not uphold the words of this law, to do them”: no “everyone,” no “all.” The Greek Old Testament has both, “cursed is every man who does not abide in all the words of this law,” and Paul quotes that, with “all the things written in the book of the law.” Epikataratos, “cursed,” stands in two verses of the New Testament, both in this paragraph: here, and at v13, where Paul uses the same word for a different verse. The KJV reads “Cursed is every one that continueth not in all things which are written in the book of the law to do them”; the NIV reads “Cursed is everyone who does not continue to do everything written in the Book of the Law.”

⚠ “The righteous will live by faith” — Habakkuk 2:4, without a pronoun. In the Hebrew of Habakkuk 2:4 the righteous lives “by his faithfulness,” be’emunato; the Greek Old Testament reads “by my faith,” ek pisteōs mou, God’s. Paul writes neither pronoun, as at Romans 1:17, where this translation prints the same words. Whether “by faith” goes with “the righteous” (the one righteous by faith will live) or with “will live” (will live by faith) the Greek leaves open. The KJV reads “The just shall live by faith”; the NWT 1984 reads “the righteous one will live by reason of faith”; the TLB reads “The man who finds life will find it through trusting God.”

“The one who has done them will live by them” — Leviticus 18:5. The Hebrew of Leviticus 18:5 has ha-adam, “the man,” and the Greek Old Testament anthrōpos; Paul leaves the word out, and the Byzantine text puts it back, which is why the KJV reads “The man that doeth them shall live in them.” At Romans 10:5 Paul keeps it: “the man who has done the righteousness that is out of the law will live by it.” The ASV reads “He that doeth them shall live in them”; the NWT 1984 reads “he that does them shall live by means of them.”

⚠ “Becoming a curse for us” — and Deuteronomy 21:23, adjusted. Exagorazō, “to buy out,” stands in four verses of the New Testament; at 4:5 Christ buys out “those under law.” Christ became not “cursed” but “a curse,” the noun. The quotation is Deuteronomy 21:23, where the body of an executed man hung on a tree must be buried the same day, “for one who is hanged is a curse of God.” The Greek Old Testament reads “cursed by God is everyone who hangs on a tree,” with kekatēramenos; Paul writes epikataratos, the word of Deuteronomy 27:26 in v10, and leaves out “by God.” “On a tree” is the phrase of Peter’s preaching, “hanging him on a tree” (Acts 5:30, Acts 10:39). The KJV reads “Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree”; the NIV reads “Cursed is everyone who is hung on a pole”; the NWT 1984 reads “Accursed is every man hanged upon a stake.” In v14 the KJV reads “through Jesus Christ” and the NWT 1984 “by means of Jesus Christ,” the order Westcott–Hort and Tregelles print; the Greek here reads “Christ Jesus.”

Verses 15–18 · a covenant or a will, seed and not seeds, and four hundred and thirty years

⚠ “Even a man’s covenant” — diathēkē, covenant and will. Diathēkē is the word of the covenants, the “new covenant” of 2 Corinthians 3:6, and in ordinary Greek it is also a will, a last testament (dictionary). The picture here, a document that, once confirmed, cannot be set aside or added to, fits a will; the argument that follows is about God’s covenant with Abraham. Hebrews 9:16–17 is read in both senses. “Confirmed” is kyroō, the verb of 2 Corinthians 2:8, “confirm your love”; “sets aside” is the atheteō of 2:21. The Douay reads “testament,” from the Vulgate’s testamentum; the KJV reads “no man disannulleth, or addeth thereto”; the NWT 1984 reads “attaches additions to it”; the NIV reads “add to a human covenant that has been duly established.”

⚠ “Not ‘seeds,’ as of many, but ‘your seed,’ as of one.” “And to your seed” is the promise to Abram at Genesis 12:7 and again in Genesis 13:15, 17:8 and 24:7. In Hebrew zera and in Greek sperma are collective nouns, singular in form and plural in sense, as English “offspring” is; this translation’s Genesis prints “offspring” at 12:7 and “seed” at Genesis 22:18. Paul reads the singular as one person, Christ, and at v29 he turns the word back into a collective: “you are Abraham’s seed.” This translation prints “seed” throughout the chapter so that the argument can be seen. The KJV and the ASV read “And to seeds, as of many”; the NIV prints “the Seed to whom the promise referred” with a capital at v19; the TLB reads “Now, God gave some promises to Abraham and his Child.”

⚠ “Four hundred and thirty years” — counted from where? In the Hebrew of Exodus 12:40 the 430 years are the time Israel “dwelt in Egypt.” The Greek Old Testament reads “in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan,” so that the count begins with the patriarchs, and Paul’s count, from the promise to Abraham to the law, fits that reading. Genesis 15:13 gives “four hundred years” of affliction. How the numbers fit together is argued, and the verse does not need to settle it: the point is that the law came after the promise. “Make void” is akyroō, the “made void the word of God” of Matthew 15:6; “abolish” is katargeō, as at Romans 4:14, “the promise has been abolished.” The Byzantine text adds “in Christ” after “by God,” which is why the KJV reads “confirmed before of God in Christ”; the NIV reads “The law, introduced 430 years later.”

“God has freely given it.” Kecharistai, from charizomai, the grace-verb, in the perfect: given, and still given. The inheritance is the word of Romans 4:13–14, where Abraham was to be “heir of the world” and “if those who are of law are heirs, faith has been emptied.” The KJV reads “but God gave it to Abraham by promise”; the NWT 1984 reads “God has kindly given it to Abraham through a promise”; the NIV reads “God in his grace gave it to Abraham through a promise.”

Verses 19–22 · why the law, angels and a mediator, “God is one,” and all things shut up under sin

⚠ “Added for the sake of transgressions.” Tōn parabaseōn charin: charin with a genitive is “for the sake of, on account of,” and it does not say how. Readers take it as to restrain transgressions, to show them up as transgressions, or to multiply them, as when “law came in alongside, so that the trespass might increase” (Romans 5:20); this page does not choose. The KJV and the ASV both read “It was added because of transgressions”; the Douay reads “It was set because of transgressions”; the NWT 1984 supplies a purpose, “It was added to make transgressions manifest.”

“Ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator.” Stephen says the same of the law: “you who received the law as ordinances of angels” (Acts 7:53), and Moses was with “the angel who spoke to him on Mount Sinai” (Acts 7:38). The Greek Old Testament of Deuteronomy 33:2 adds angels to the Sinai blessing, “at his right hand angels with him,” where the Hebrew is a disputed phrase about fire. “By the hand of” is the Hebrew idiom of the law itself, which was given “on Mount Sinai, by the hand of Moses” (Leviticus 26:46). Mesitēs, “mediator,” stands in six verses of the New Testament: these two, and 1 Timothy 2:5, and Hebrews 8:6, 9:15 and 12:24 (the last two yet to come in this translation).

⚠ “A mediator is not of one, but God is one” — a famously hard sentence. Ho de mesitēs henos ouk estin, ho de theos heis estin: eight words, and no agreed reading. On one, a mediator goes between two parties, the law was an arrangement with two sides and a go-between, and the promise came from God alone, directly. On another, “God is one” is the confession of Deuteronomy 6:4, “Jehovah is one,” used as at Romans 3:30, “if indeed God is one, who will justify the circumcision out of faith and the uncircumcision through the faith”: one God, one people of Jews and nations together. The Greek is printed here as it stands. The KJV reads “Now a mediator is not a mediator of one”; the NIV reads “A mediator, however, implies more than one party”; the NWT 1984 reads “there is no mediator where only one person is concerned.”

“Shut up all things under sin.” “May it never be!” again, as at 2:17. A law that could “give life” is the verb of 2 Corinthians 3:6, “the Spirit gives life.” Synkleiō, “shut up,” stands in four verses of the New Testament: the nets that enclosed the fish at Luke 5:6, God who “shut up all together into disobedience, so that he might show mercy to all” at Romans 11:32, and vv22 and 23 here (dictionary). Romans and Galatians have the same shape: shut up, so that. Ta panta is neuter, “all things.” “Faith in Jesus Christ” carries the genitive question of 2:16. The KJV reads “hath concluded all under sin”; the NIV reads “locked up everything under the control of sin”; the NWT 1984 reads “delivered up all things together to the custody of sin.”

Verses 23–29 · kept under guard, the child-minder, sons through faith, put on Christ, and no male and female

“Kept under guard under law.” Phroureō is the soldier’s verb, the ethnarch “guarding the city of the Damascenes” at 2 Corinthians 11:32 and the peace of God that “will stand guard over your hearts” at Philippians 4:7: custody and protection at once. “The faith” has the article in vv23 and 25, faith as something that “came.” The ASV reads “we were kept in ward under the law”; the Geneva reads “as under a garrison.”

⚠ “Our child-minder until Christ” — paidagōgos, not a schoolmaster. The paidagōgos was the household slave who walked a boy to school and kept him in order; the teacher was someone else (dictionary). Paidagōgos stands in three verses of the New Testament: these two and 1 Corinthians 4:15, “ten thousand child-minders in Christ,” and this translation prints “child-minder” in all three. Eis Christon can be “until Christ,” matching the “before the faith came” and “now that the faith has come” around it, or “to bring us to Christ”; this translation prints “until.” The KJV reads “our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ”; the ASV reads “our tutor to bring us unto Christ”; the Douay reads “our pedagogue”; the NIV reads “the law was our guardian until Christ came.”

“Sons of God, through faith, in Christ Jesus.” The Greek can be read “through faith in Christ Jesus” or “sons in Christ Jesus, through faith,” and the commas keep both, as the ASV does: “For ye are all sons of God, through faith, in Christ Jesus.” Huioi is “sons,” the heirs of v29, and v28 says who is included. The NIV reads “you are all children of God through faith.” “Baptized into Christ” is the phrase of Romans 6:3, and “put on Christ” is a garment, as at Romans 13:14, “put on the Lord Jesus Christ”; the KJV reads “have put on Christ”; the NIV reads “have clothed yourselves with Christ.”

⚠ “No Jew nor Greek, no slave nor free, no male and female.” Three pairs, and the third changes its joining word: oude … oude … kai, “nor, nor, and.” “Male and female” stands in three verses of the New Testament, all quoting the creation, “male and female he created them” (Genesis 1:27): Matthew 19:4, Mark 10:6 and this one. The verse has been at the center of argument about the place of women, and of slaves, in the church: some read it as a statement about standing before God in Christ, others as a statement with social consequences too. This page reports both and does not vote. “You are all one” uses the masculine heis, “one man, one person.” The KJV reads “there is neither male nor female”; the ASV keeps the change, “there can be no male and female,” and reads “for ye all are one man in Christ Jesus”; the NIV reads “nor is there male and female.”

“Abraham’s seed, heirs according to promise.” The singular seed of v16, who is Christ, is now many people, because they are Christ’s: the collective sense returns through the one. “Heirs” is klēronomoi, as at Romans 8:17, “and if children, then heirs also.” The Byzantine text adds “and,” which is why the KJV reads “and heirs according to the promise.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: the evil eye and the eyes of 3:1; “senseless” for anoētos at 3:1 and 3:3, as at Luke 24:25; “the hearing of faith” at 3:2 and 3:5; “begun” and “brought to completion” at 3:3, as at Philippians 1:6; “reckoned” at 3:6, as at Romans 4:3, and “God” where the Greek has theos; “works of law” without the article; “cursed” for epikataratos at 3:10 and 3:13; “The righteous will live by faith,” as at Romans 1:17; “on a tree,” as at Acts 5:30; “covenant” for diathēkē; “seed” for sperma throughout; “shut up” for synkleiō at 3:22 and 3:23, as at Romans 11:32; “child-minder” for paidagōgos, as at 1 Corinthians 4:15; “put on” Christ, as at Romans 13:14; and “male and female” with the Greek’s “and.”

Echoes paid. Works of law and faith in Jesus Christ from 2:16; May it never be from 2:17; set aside from 2:21; Abraham believed God from Genesis 15:6 and Romans 4:3; the blessing of the nations from Genesis 12:3 and Genesis 18:18; the curse of Deuteronomy 27:26 and the hanged man of Deuteronomy 21:23; the righteous will live by faith from Romans 1:17; the one who has done them from Leviticus 18:5 and Romans 10:5; the 430 years of Exodus 12:40; the angels of Acts 7:53; shut up from Romans 11:32; the child-minders of 1 Corinthians 4:15; and male and female from Genesis 1:27.

Echoes planted. Buying out those under law, and sons who cry “Abba,” at 4:5–6; the heir who is a child under guardians at 4:1–2; Abraham’s two sons and two covenants at 4:21–31; the Spirit against the flesh at 5:16–25; Habakkuk 2:4 at Hebrews 10:38; the will and the covenant at Hebrews 9:16–17; and the one mediator of 1 Timothy 2:5.
