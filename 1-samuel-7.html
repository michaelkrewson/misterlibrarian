The men of Kiriath-jearim bring the ark to the house of Abinadab and consecrate his son Eleazar to guard it. Twenty years later Samuel calls Israel to put away the Baalim and the Ashtaroth and gathers them at Mizpah, where they pour out water, fast and confess. The Philistines attack while Samuel offers a suckling lamb; Jehovah thunders and they are routed as far as Beth-car. Samuel sets up the stone Eben-ezer, the cities from Ekron to Gath return to Israel, and Samuel judges Israel on a yearly circuit from his house at Ramah.
Twenty years after the ark came to Kiriath-jearim (1 Samuel 7:2); Samuel judges for the rest of his life (7:15).

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Kiriath-jearim — the ark in the house of Abinadab “on the hill”, Eleazar consecrated to guard it, twenty years, and Israel “lamented after Jehovah”

The house of Abinadab. The men of Kiriath-jearim answer the call of 6:21 (now on these pages) and bring the ark “into the house of Abinadab on the hill”. On the hill is bagiv‘ah, which can also be read as a place name, Gibeah. The ASV has “into the house of Abinadab in the hill”; the Douay “into the house of Abinadab in Gabaa”. They “consecrated Eleazar his son to guard the ark of Jehovah”: qiddeshu, “they made holy”, and shamar, “guard, keep”. The text does not say what tribe the family was; Josephus calls Abinadab “a Levite by descent” (Antiquities 6.18, numbered 6.1.4 in Whiston). The NWT 1984 has “and El·e·aʹzar his son was the one whom they sanctified”, keeping the Hebrew’s order.

Twenty years, and a lament. “The days grew many, and they came to twenty years”: the twenty years run to Samuel’s assembly at Mizpah, not to the end of the ark’s stay, for the ark is still in the house of Abinadab when David comes for it (2 Samuel 6:3). “All the house of Israel lamented after Jehovah”: the verb is naha, a rare word used elsewhere of wailing over the dead (Ezekiel 32:18; Micah 2:4). The ASV has “all the house of Israel lamented after Jehovah”; the NWT 2013 “all the house of Israel began to seek after Jehovah”; the Douay “all the house of Israel rested following the Lord”. The Greek has a different verb again, that Israel “looked after” the Lord.

Verses 3–6 · Mizpah — “put away the foreign gods”, the Baalim and the Ashtaroth, water poured out before Jehovah, and “Samuel judged the sons of Israel”

“Put away the foreign gods.” “If with all your heart you are returning to Jehovah”, shuv, “put away the foreign gods from among you”: the words of Jacob, “Put away the foreign gods that are among you” (Genesis 35:2), and of Joshua at Shechem (Joshua 24:23, both now on these pages), and what Israel did in the days of Jephthah: “They put away the foreign gods from among them and served Jehovah” (Judges 10:16, now on these pages). Foreign is nekhar. “Fix your heart on Jehovah and serve him alone”, and verse 4 reports that they “served Jehovah alone”. The ASV has “direct your hearts unto Jehovah, and serve him only”; the NWT 1984 “direct YOUR heart unswervingly to Jehovah and serve him alone”.

The Baalim and the Ashtaroth. Both words are plural: the many local Baals and the images of Ashtoreth, the goddess whose plural is ashtarot. Judges uses the same pair for the apostasy that opens the oppressions, “They served the Baalim and the Ashtaroth” (Judges 10:6, now on these pages). The ASV has “did put away the Baalim and the Ashtaroth”; the TLB “your Ashtaroth idols”. The Greek has “the groves” for the Ashtaroth in verse 3 and “the groves of Astaroth” in verse 4.

Water poured out. At Mizpah they “drew water and poured it out before Jehovah, and fasted that day”. No law of the Torah prescribes this rite, and the chapter does not explain it. Lamentations has the nearest image, “pour out your heart like water before the face of the Lord” (Lamentations 2:19, now on these pages). Josephus says they drew water and poured it as a libation to God (Antiquities 6.22, numbered 6.2.1 in Whiston). The Greek adds that they poured it “on the ground”. The confession is short: “We have sinned against Jehovah”, as Israel cried in Judges, “We have sinned against you” (Judges 10:10, now on these pages). The ASV has “drew water, and poured it out before Jehovah, and fasted on that day”; the NWT 1984 “kept a fast on that day”.

“Samuel judged.” “And Samuel judged the sons of Israel at Mizpah”: the verb of the shofetim, the judges Jehovah “raised up” to save Israel (Judges 2:16, now on these pages), and the place where all Israel had gathered “to Jehovah at Mizpah” against Benjamin (Judges 20:1, now on these pages). Verses 15–17 return to it three times. Josephus explains the name Mizpah as meaning a lookout (Antiquities 6.22). The ASV has “Samuel judged the children of Israel in Mizpah”; the NIV “Samuel was serving as leader of Israel”.

Verses 7–11 · Thunder at Mizpah — “do not keep silent”, a suckling lamb as a whole burnt offering, Jehovah thunders “with a great voice”, and the Philistines struck down before Israel

“Do not keep silent.” The lords of the Philistines hear of the gathering and go up against it, and Israel is afraid. “Do not keep silent from us, from crying out to Jehovah our God”: the verb is charash, to be silent, and the cry is za‘aq, the cry of distress. The ASV has “Cease not to cry unto Jehovah our God for us”; the NWT 1984 “Do not keep silent for our sakes from calling to Jehovah our God for aid”.

A suckling lamb, a whole burnt offering. Samuel takes “one suckling lamb”; the phrase teleh chalav stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. He offers it “as a whole burnt offering”, olah kalil, the “whole offering on your altar” of Moses’ blessing on Levi (Deuteronomy 33:10, now on these pages). The written text has “and he offered up”, the margin “and he offered it up”. The Greek adds that he offered it “with all the people”. The ASV has “offered it for a whole burnt-offering unto Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “as a burnt offering, a whole offering”. “Samuel cried out to Jehovah for Israel, and Jehovah answered him.”

Thunder “with a great voice”. While the lamb is still burning the Philistines close in, “and Jehovah thundered with a great voice that day against the Philistines”. The verb is ra‘am, “thunder”, the verb of Hannah’s song, “against them he thunders in the heavens” (1 Samuel 2:10, now on these pages): her son prays, and it happens. Voice is qol, the word Exodus uses for the thunders of the plague of hail (Exodus 9:23, now on these pages). He “threw them into confusion”, hamam, as he threw the Amorite kings “into confusion before Israel” at Gibeon (Joshua 10:10, now on these pages), “and they were struck down before Israel”, the verb of the defeat at Eben-ezer, “Israel was struck down before the Philistines” (1 Samuel 4:2, now on these pages). The ASV has “Jehovah thundered with a great thunder on that day upon the Philistines, and discomfited them”; the NWT 1984 “that he might throw them into confusion”. Josephus adds an earthquake that opened chasms under the Philistines, lightning that seemed to burn their faces, and weapons shaken from their hands (Antiquities 6.27, numbered 6.2.2 in Whiston).

Beth-car. Israel pursues “as far as below Beth-car”, “house of the lamb” or “of pasture”; the name stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and the place is unknown. Josephus names the end of the pursuit a place called Korraia (Antiquities 6.28). The ASV has “until they came under Beth-car”; the NWT 1984 “as far as south of Beth-car”.

Verses 12–14 · Eben-ezer, “stone of help” — “thus far Jehovah has helped us”, Shen or Jeshanah, the Philistines subdued, and the cities from Ekron to Gath

“Thus far Jehovah has helped us.” “Samuel took one stone”, even achat, and named it Eben-ezer, “stone of help”; ‘ezer is the word of the “helper corresponding to him” (Genesis 2:18, now on these pages; ezer kenegdo). The name stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: the camp of the defeat (1 Samuel 4:1), the place the ark was taken from (5:1, both now on these pages), and here, where it is explained; whether this stone stands at the battlefield of chapter 4 the text does not say. “Thus far”, ‘ad-hennah, is “as far as here” and “until now” at once. The ASV has “Hitherto hath Jehovah helped us”; the NWT 1984 “Till now Jehovah has helped us”. Josephus calls the stone “Strong”, a token of the strength God gave them (Antiquities 6.28, numbered 6.2.2 in Whiston).

Shen or Jeshanah. The stone stands “between Mizpah and Shen”; shen is “tooth”, perhaps a crag. The Greek reads “the Old”, the town Jeshanah, and several translations follow it. The TLB has “between Mizpah and Jeshanah”; the NIV “between Mizpah and Shen”; the KJV “between Mizpeh and Shen”. The NWT 1984 has Jeshanah too.

The Philistines subdued. “So the Philistines were subdued” is the formula of Judges, “So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel” (Judges 3:30; compare 8:28 and 11:33, all now on these pages), here without the closing “and the land had rest”. “They did not come again into the territory of Israel … all the days of Samuel”, though Philistine garrisons stand in the land within a few chapters (1 Samuel 10:5, now on these pages, and 13:3, also now on these pages); the verse speaks of Samuel’s days, and the book leaves the two side by side. The ASV has “So the Philistines were subdued, and they came no more within the border of Israel”; the NIV “they stopped invading Israel’s territory”. Josephus says the Philistines “made no more expeditions against the Israelites” after the defeat (Antiquities 6.29, numbered 6.2.3 in Whiston).

From Ekron to Gath. “The cities that the Philistines had taken from Israel returned to Israel, from Ekron as far as Gath”, the two Philistine cities nearest Israel, both of which had held the ark (1 Samuel 5:8–10, now on these pages). The Greek reads “from Ashkelon to Azob”. “There was peace between Israel and the Amorites”, the old name for the peoples of the land, as in “the iniquity of the Amorite is not yet complete” (Genesis 15:16, now on these pages); Josephus says the rest of the Canaanites were friendly to Israel at that time (Antiquities 6.30). The ASV has “from Ekron even unto Gath”; the NIV “there was peace between Israel and the Amorites”.

Verses 15–17 · Samuel the judge — a yearly circuit of Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah, his house at Ramah, and an altar there

The circuit. “Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life”, the last of the judges in the line of the book of Judges. “He went from year to year and made the circuit”, sabab, the verb of the ark “moved around” the Philistine cities (1 Samuel 5:8, now on these pages), now of a judge going round Israel’s own towns. Bethel, where the ark stood in the days of Phinehas (Judges 20:27, now on these pages), Gilgal and Mizpah are all places of earlier assemblies. The ASV has “he went from year to year in circuit to Beth-el and Gilgal, and Mizpah”; the NIV “he went on a circuit”. The Greek ends the verse “in all these consecrated places”.

His house at Ramah. “His return was to Ramah, for his house was there”: the house of Elkanah and Hannah, “their house at Ramah” (1 Samuel 1:19; 2:11, both now on these pages). The Greek calls it Armathaim, the Ramathaim of 1:1. “He built an altar there to Jehovah”, a mizbeach in his own town, while the ark stays at Kiriath-jearim; the narrator does not comment. The ASV has “And his return was to Ramah, for there was his house”; the KJV “and there he built an altar unto”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “lamented after” for naha; “put away the foreign gods”, as in Genesis 35:2, Joshua 24:23 and Judges 10:16; “the Baalim and the Ashtaroth”, as in Judges 10:6; “a whole burnt offering” for olah kalil; “thundered” for ra‘am, as in 1 Samuel 2:10; “threw them into confusion” for hamam, as in Joshua 10:10; “struck down before”, as in 1 Samuel 4:2; “Thus far Jehovah has helped us”; “subdued”, as in Judges 3:30; “made the circuit” for sabab; and “judged”.

Echoes paid. Eben-ezer, used as a name in chapters 4 and 5, is explained; Hannah’s “he thunders in the heavens” (2:10) comes true for her son; “Israel was struck down before the Philistines” (4:2) is turned round; and the ark sent toward Kiriath-jearim (6:21) arrives.

Echoes planted. The Philistine garrisons of 1 Samuel 10:5, now on these pages, and of 13:3, also now on these pages, and David bringing the ark out of the house of Abinadab (2 Samuel 6:3).

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 8 — Samuel grows old and makes his sons judges at Beersheba, but they take bribes; the elders come to Ramah and ask for a king “like all the nations”, and Samuel tells them what a king will take.
