🕰
Two calendars of release nested inside each other. Every seventh year the land itself keeps a sabbath — no sowing, no pruning, whatever grows on its own free for owner, servant, sojourner, and wild animal alike. After seven such cycles, the fiftieth year is proclaimed a jubilee with a trumpet blast on the Day of Atonement: every sold field returns to its original family, every Israelite sold into service goes free. The chapter then works out the legal mechanics in detail — fair pricing keyed to years-until-jubilee, a kinsman's right to redeem sold land, a special exception for houses in walled cities, a ban on interest against a poor brother, and the difference between an Israelite servant (never a slave, freed at the jubilee) and a foreign-born slave (permanent property) — closing on the principle underneath all of it: the land is Jehovah's, and so are Israel's people, on the strength of the Exodus itself.
⚠ Undated within the chapter, like the rest of Leviticus. Its sabbatical-year law (vv1-7) restates Exodus 23:10-11's own one-sentence version at full length; its ban on ruling a fellow Israelite 'with harshness' (vv43, 46, 53) reuses the identical rare word this project's own Exodus 1:13 entry used for Pharaoh's cruelty to enslaved Israel. Still inside the same Sinai window as the rest of this block (Exodus 40:17 / Numbers 1:1, already on these pages).

Leviticus 25 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, בְּהַר סִינַי לֵאמֹר.
And Jehovah spoke to Moses on Mount Sinai, saying:note
Jehová habló a Moisés en el monte Sinaí, diciendo:
2
דַּבֵּר אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵהֶם, כִּי תָבֹאוּ אֶל-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר אֲנִי נֹתֵן לָכֶם--וְשָׁבְתָה הָאָרֶץ, שַׁבָּת לַיהוָה.
Speak to the children of Israel, and say to them: When you come into the land that I am giving you, the land shall keep a sabbath to Jehovah.note
Habla a los hijos de Israel, y diles: Cuando entréis en la tierra que yo os doy, la tierra guardará un sábado para Jehová.
3
שֵׁשׁ שָׁנִים תִּזְרַע שָׂדֶךָ, וְשֵׁשׁ שָׁנִים תִּזְמֹר כַּרְמֶךָ; וְאָסַפְתָּ, אֶת-תְּבוּאָתָהּ.
Six years you shall sow your field, and six years you shall prune your vineyard, and gather in its produce;note
Seis años sembrarás tu campo, y seis años podarás tu viña, y recogerás su fruto;
4
וּבַשָּׁנָה הַשְּׁבִיעִת, שַׁבַּת שַׁבָּתוֹן יִהְיֶה לָאָרֶץ--שַׁבָּת, לַיהוָה: שָׂדְךָ לֹא תִזְרָע, וְכַרְמְךָ לֹא תִזְמֹר.
but in the seventh year there shall be a sabbath of solemn rest for the land, a sabbath to Jehovah: you shall not sow your field, nor prune your vineyard.note
pero en el séptimo año habrá un sabat de reposo solemne para la tierra, un sábado para Jehová: no sembrarás tu campo, ni podarás tu viña.

5
אֵת סְפִיחַ קְצִירְךָ לֹא תִקְצוֹר, וְאֶת-עִנְּבֵי נְזִירֶךָ לֹא תִבְצֹר: שְׁנַת שַׁבָּתוֹן, יִהְיֶה לָאָרֶץ.
What grows of itself from your harvest you shall not reap, and the grapes of your undressed vine you shall not gather: it shall be a year of solemn rest for the land.note
Lo que nazca por sí solo de tu siega no lo segarás, y las uvas de tu viña sin podar no las vendimiarás: año de reposo solemne será para la tierra.
6
וְהָיְתָה שַׁבַּת הָאָרֶץ לָכֶם, לְאָכְלָה--לְךָ, וּלְעַבְדְּךָ וְלַאֲמָתֶךָ; וְלִשְׂכִירְךָ, וּלְתוֹשָׁבְךָ, הַגָּרִים, עִמָּךְ.
And the sabbath of the land shall be food for you — for you, and for your male servant, and for your female servant, and for your hired worker, and for your settler who resides with you;note
Y el sábado de la tierra os será para comer —a ti, a tu siervo y a tu sierva, a tu jornalero, y a tu colono que reside contigo;
7
וְלִבְהֶמְתְּךָ--וְלַחַיָּה, אֲשֶׁר בְּאַרְצֶךָ: תִּהְיֶה כָל-תְּבוּאָתָהּ, לֶאֱכֹל. {ס}
and for your cattle, and for the wild animals that are in your land, shall all its produce be for food. {ס}note
y a tu ganado, y a los animales salvajes que hay en tu tierra: todo su fruto será para comer.
8
וְסָפַרְתָּ לְךָ, שֶׁבַע שַׁבְּתֹת שָׁנִים--שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים, שֶׁבַע פְּעָמִים; וְהָיוּ לְךָ, יְמֵי שֶׁבַע שַׁבְּתֹת הַשָּׁנִים, תֵּשַׁע וְאַרְבָּעִים, שָׁנָה.
And you shall count off seven sabbaths of years for yourself — seven times seven years — and the days of the seven sabbaths of years shall give you forty-nine years.note
Y contarás siete sabats de años —siete veces siete años—, y los días de los siete sabats de años te darán cuarenta y nueve años.
9
וְהַעֲבַרְתָּ שׁוֹפַר תְּרוּעָה, בַּחֹדֶשׁ הַשְּׁבִעִי, בֶּעָשׂוֹר, לַחֹדֶשׁ; בְּיוֹם, הַכִּפֻּרִים, תַּעֲבִירוּ שׁוֹפָר, בְּכָל-אַרְצְכֶם.
Then you shall sound the ram's-horn trumpet abroad, in the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month; on the Day of Atonement you shall sound the trumpet throughout all your land.note
Entonces harás sonar fuerte el cuerno de carnero, en el mes séptimo, a los diez días del mes; en el Día de la Expiación haréis sonar el cuerno por toda vuestra tierra.
10
וְקִדַּשְׁתֶּם, אֵת שְׁנַת הַחֲמִשִּׁים שָׁנָה, וּקְרָאתֶם דְּרוֹר בָּאָרֶץ, לְכָל-יֹשְׁבֶיהָ; יוֹבֵל הִוא, תִּהְיֶה לָכֶם, וְשַׁבְתֶּם אִישׁ אֶל-אֲחֻזָּתוֹ, וְאִישׁ אֶל-מִשְׁפַּחְתּוֹ תָּשֻׁבוּ.
And you shall sanctify the fiftieth year, and proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants: it shall be a jubilee for you, and each of you shall return to his possession, and each of you shall return to his family.note
Y santificaréis el año cincuenta, y proclamaréis libertad en la tierra a todos sus habitantes: será para vosotros un jubileo, y cada uno volverá a su posesión, y cada uno volverá a su familia.
cross-refs⤷ 16:29
11
יוֹבֵל הִוא, שְׁנַת הַחֲמִשִּׁים שָׁנָה--תִּהְיֶה לָכֶם; לֹא תִזְרָעוּ--וְלֹא תִקְצְרוּ אֶת-סְפִיחֶיהָ, וְלֹא תִבְצְרוּ אֶת-נְזִרֶיהָ.
A jubilee shall that fiftieth year be to you: you shall not sow, nor reap what grows of itself, nor gather the grapes of your undressed vines.note
Jubileo será para vosotros ese año cincuenta: no sembraréis, ni segaréis lo que nazca por sí solo, ni vendimiaréis las uvas de vuestras viñas sin podar.
12
כִּי יוֹבֵל הִוא, קֹדֶשׁ תִּהְיֶה לָכֶם; מִן-הַשָּׂדֶה--תֹּאכְלוּ, אֶת-תְּבוּאָתָהּ.
For it is a jubilee; it shall be holy to you: you shall eat its produce out of the field.note
Porque es jubileo; santo será para vosotros: del campo comeréis su fruto.
13
בִּשְׁנַת הַיּוֹבֵל, הַזֹּאת, תָּשֻׁבוּ, אִישׁ אֶל-אֲחֻזָּתוֹ.
In this Year of Jubilee, each of you shall return to his possession.note
En este año de jubileo, cada uno volverá a su posesión.
14
וְכִי-תִמְכְּרוּ מִמְכָּר לַעֲמִיתֶךָ, אוֹ קָנֹה מִיַּד עֲמִיתֶךָ--אַל-תּוֹנוּ, אִישׁ אֶת-אָחִיו.
And if you sell anything to your neighbor, or buy from your neighbor's hand, you shall not wrong one another.note
Y si vendéis algo a vuestro compañero, o compráis de mano de vuestro compañero, no os engañéis el uno al otro.
15
בְּמִסְפַּר שָׁנִים אַחַר הַיּוֹבֵל, תִּקְנֶה מֵאֵת עֲמִיתֶךָ; בְּמִסְפַּר שְׁנֵי-תְבוּאֹת, יִמְכָּר-לָךְ.
According to the number of years after the jubilee you shall buy from your neighbor, and according to the number of years of crops he shall sell to you.note
Conforme al número de años después del jubileo comprarás de tu compañero, y conforme al número de años de cosechas te venderá a ti.

16
לְפִי רֹב הַשָּׁנִים, תַּרְבֶּה מִקְנָתוֹ, וּלְפִי מְעֹט הַשָּׁנִים, תַּמְעִיט מִקְנָתוֹ: כִּי מִסְפַּר תְּבוּאֹת, הוּא מֹכֵר לָךְ.
According to the greater number of years you shall increase its price, and according to the fewer number of years you shall diminish its price; for it is the number of the crops that he is selling to you.note
Conforme al mayor número de años aumentarás el precio, y conforme al menor número de años disminuirás el precio; porque es el número de las cosechas lo que él te vende.
17
וְלֹא תוֹנוּ אִישׁ אֶת-עֲמִיתוֹ, וְיָרֵאתָ מֵאֱלֹהֶיךָ: כִּי אֲנִי יְהוָה, אֱלֹהֵיכֶם.
And you shall not wrong one another, but you shall fear your God: for I am Jehovah your God.note
No os engañéis el uno al otro, sino que temeréis a vuestro Dios: porque yo soy Jehová vuestro Dios.
18
וַעֲשִׂיתֶם, אֶת-חֻקֹּתַי, וְאֶת-מִשְׁפָּטַי תִּשְׁמְרוּ, וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֹתָם--וִישַׁבְתֶּם עַל-הָאָרֶץ, לָבֶטַח.
So you shall do my statutes, and keep my ordinances, and do them; and you shall dwell in the land in safety.note
Así pues, cumpliréis mis estatutos, y guardaréis mis ordenanzas, y las pondréis por obra; y habitaréis en la tierra con seguridad.
19
וְנָתְנָה הָאָרֶץ פִּרְיָהּ, וַאֲכַלְתֶּם לָשֹׂבַע; וִישַׁבְתֶּם לָבֶטַח, עָלֶיהָ.
And the land shall yield its fruit, and you shall eat your fill, and dwell in it in safety.note
Y la tierra dará su fruto, y comeréis hasta saciaros, y habitaréis en ella con seguridad.
20

And if you say, "What shall we eat in the seventh year, since we shall not sow, nor gather in our produce?" —note
Y si decís: «¿Qué comeremos en el séptimo año, si no hemos de sembrar, ni hemos de recoger nuestra cosecha?» —
21

entonces yo mandaré mi bendición sobre vosotros en el sexto año, y producirá fruto para tres años.
22
וּזְרַעְתֶּם, אֵת הַשָּׁנָה הַשְּׁמִינִת, וַאֲכַלְתֶּם, מִן-הַתְּבוּאָה יָשָׁן; עַד הַשָּׁנָה הַתְּשִׁיעִת, עַד-בּוֹא תְּבוּאָתָהּ--תֹּאכְלוּ, יָשָׁן.
And you shall sow in the eighth year, and eat of the old produce until the ninth year; until its produce comes in, you shall eat the old.note
Y sembraréis en el octavo año, y comeréis del fruto viejo; hasta el año noveno, hasta que llegue su fruto, comeréis lo viejo.
23
וְהָאָרֶץ, לֹא תִמָּכֵר לִצְמִתֻת--כִּי-לִי, הָאָרֶץ: כִּי-גֵרִים וְתוֹשָׁבִים אַתֶּם, עִמָּדִי.
And the land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the land is mine: for you are resident aliens and settlers with me.note
Y la tierra no se venderá a perpetuidad, porque la tierra es mía: porque vosotros sois extranjeros residentes y colonos conmigo.
24
וּבְכֹל, אֶרֶץ אֲחֻזַּתְכֶם, גְּאֻלָּה, תִּתְּנוּ לָאָרֶץ. {ס}
And in all the land of your possession you shall grant a redemption for the land. {ס}note
Y en toda la tierra de vuestra posesión concederéis rescate para la tierra.
25

If your brother becomes poor, and sells part of his possession, then his kinsman-redeemer who is nearest to him shall come and redeem what his brother has sold.note
Si tu hermano empobrece, y vende parte de su posesión, vendrá su pariente redentor más cercano, y rescatará lo que su hermano vendió.

And if a man has no one to redeem it, but he himself has grown rich and found sufficient means to redeem it,note
Y si un hombre no tiene quien lo rescate, pero él mismo prospera y halla lo suficiente para su rescate,
27
וְחִשַּׁב, אֶת-שְׁנֵי מִמְכָּרוֹ, וְהֵשִׁיב אֶת-הָעֹדֵף, לָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר מָכַר-לוֹ; וְשָׁב, לַאֲחֻזָּתוֹ.

entonces calculará los años desde que la vendió, y devolverá el sobrante al hombre a quien la vendió; y volverá a su posesión.
28
וְאִם לֹא-מָצְאָה יָדוֹ, דֵּי הָשִׁיב לוֹ--וְהָיָה מִמְכָּרוֹ בְּיַד הַקֹּנֶה אֹתוֹ, עַד שְׁנַת הַיּוֹבֵל; וְיָצָא, בַּיֹּבֵל, וְשָׁב, לַאֲחֻזָּתוֹ. {ס}
But if he has not found sufficient means to restore it to him, then what he sold shall remain in the hand of the one who bought it until the Year of Jubilee: and in the jubilee it shall be released, and he shall return to his possession. {ס}note
Pero si no halla lo suficiente para restituírselo, entonces lo que vendió permanecerá en mano del que lo compró hasta el año del jubileo; y en el jubileo quedará libre, y volverá a su posesión.
29
וְאִישׁ, כִּי-יִמְכֹּר בֵּית-מוֹשַׁב עִיר חוֹמָה--וְהָיְתָה גְּאֻלָּתוֹ, עַד-תֹּם שְׁנַת מִמְכָּרוֹ: יָמִים, תִּהְיֶה גְאֻלָּתוֹ.
And if a man sells a dwelling-house in a walled city, then he may redeem it within a full year after it is sold; for a full year he shall have the right of redemption.note
Y si un hombre vende una casa de vivienda en una ciudad amurallada, podrá rescatarla dentro de un año completo después de venderla; por un año completo tendrá derecho de rescate.
30
וְאִם לֹא-יִגָּאֵל, עַד-מְלֹאת לוֹ שָׁנָה תְמִימָה--וְקָם הַבַּיִת אֲשֶׁר-בָּעִיר אֲשֶׁר-לא לוֹ חֹמָה לַצְּמִיתֻת לַקֹּנֶה אֹתוֹ, לְדֹרֹתָיו: לֹא יֵצֵא, בַּיֹּבֵל.

Pero si no es rescatada dentro del espacio de un año completo, la casa que está en la ciudad amurallada quedará asegurada a perpetuidad para el que la compró, por sus generaciones: no quedará libre en el jubileo.
31
וּבָתֵּי הַחֲצֵרִים, אֲשֶׁר אֵין-לָהֶם חֹמָה סָבִיב--עַל-שְׂדֵה הָאָרֶץ, יֵחָשֵׁב: גְּאֻלָּה, תִּהְיֶה-לּוֹ, וּבַיֹּבֵל, יֵצֵא.

Pero las casas de las aldeas que no tienen muro alrededor se contarán como los campos de la tierra: tendrán derecho de rescate, y quedarán libres en el jubileo.
32

Leviticus 25 meaning — a sabbath for the land, not just the people

The weekly Sabbath (Exodus 20:8-11, already on these pages) rests a person; here the same architecture is extended one level up, to the ground itself. Six years of working the field mirror the six days of labor; the seventh year, left fallow, mirrors the seventh day. This project's own note on Exodus 23:10-11 (already on these pages), the earlier one-sentence statement of this same law, already promised that ‘the full shemitah legislation’ would arrive here — it does, with the enforcement mechanism, the theology, and forty-four more verses Exodus never needed. Whatever the untended land produces on its own belongs to no one in particular: the poor, the sojourner, and even wild animals eat from it on the same terms as its own owner (v6-7) — ownership itself takes a sabbath.

Proclaim liberty throughout the land — the verse on the Liberty Bell

⚠ V10's ‘proclaim liberty throughout the land to all its inhabitants’ is, word for word, the inscription cast onto Pennsylvania's State House bell in 1752 — what the world now calls the Liberty Bell. KJV reads it almost identically, ‘proclaim liberty throughout all the land unto all the inhabitants thereof,’ and that is the exact wording the bell's founders chose to cast in metal, a law for debt-release and land-return in an agricultural society repurposed, two and a half millennia later, as a founding civic slogan for a nation that had not yet ended slavery. The jubilee itself is counted the same way the festival calendar of Leviticus 23 (already on these pages) counts to Pentecost — seven sabbaths of seven, then one more — so the fiftieth year answers structurally to the fiftieth day, the two great ‘counted’ institutions of the Torah's calendar. It opens, distinctively, on the Day of Atonement (already on these pages): liberty is proclaimed on the one day of the year the nation has just been declared clean.

Pricing by the calendar, not by the land

What looks like a sale of land is legally a lease of harvests until the jubilee — vv15-16 make the buyer and seller literally count the crop-years remaining and price accordingly, since the land itself reverts on a fixed schedule no transaction can override. ‘Neighbor’ here is amit (already discussed at Leviticus 19:15, already on these pages), the narrower word this project has already distinguished from the broader rea — the same care for a fellow member of the covenant community this project has already tracked once.

The land is mine — the theological center of the whole chapter

V23 states outright what the rest of the chapter only implies: Israel does not, strictly, own the land at all. It is Jehovah's; Israel holds it as resident aliens and settlers — the identical pair this project's own dictionary entry (already on these pages, first at Genesis 23:4) already defined as legal terms for someone with no underlying title. KJV and ASV both keep the pair distinct here, ‘strangers and sojourners’; NIV collapses it to a single word, ‘foreigners.’ The nation that will one day parcel this land among its tribes is told, at the very moment the parceling law is given, that it holds the deed the same way Abraham held Canaan while owning nothing in it but a grave.

The kinsman-redeemer law, finally arrived

This is the chapter Genesis 48:16's own go'el entry (already on these pages) named by number and promised would come: ‘the word goes on to carry Ruth's Boaz, the “kinsman-redeemer” laws of Leviticus 25.’ The mechanism is exact: sold land is not gone, it is on loan until either a near relative buys it back (vv25-27), the seller himself recovers enough to redeem it (v26), or the jubilee arrives regardless (v28) — three separate paths back to the same possession. Vv29-34 then draw one sharp exception: a house inside a walled city, once a year passes unredeemed, is gone for good and does NOT return at the jubilee — ordinary land always returns, city real estate does not, unless it belongs to the landless Levites, whose houses keep the jubilee right permanently because their ‘possession’ was never fields to begin with (Numbers 35, now on these pages).

Two words for interest, and neither may be charged

Neshekh and tarbit are paired here exactly as they are everywhere else in the Torah (Exodus 22:25, already on these pages; Deuteronomy 23:19-20, not yet on these pages) — not two rates but the two mechanisms ancient lenders used, interest deducted in advance from the loan and interest added on at repayment, both forbidden in one breath. The law is aimed specifically at a brother who has ‘grown poor,’ not at commerce in general: profiting from a fellow Israelite's poverty is what is banned, not lending itself.

The word for Egypt's cruelty, forbidden between brothers

⚠ ‘Harshness’ (vv43, 46, 53, perekh) is the identical rare word this project's own Exodus 1 entry (already on these pages) used for Pharaoh's own treatment of enslaved Israel: ‘they made their lives bitter with hard service, in mortar and brick… with rigor (be-farekh).’ That entry named this exact verse as where the word would return; it does, three times in eight verses, as the one thing an Israelite master is flatly forbidden to become to an Israelite servant. This project's own note on Exodus 21:2 (already on these pages) already flagged the limit a foreign-born slave does not get (vv44-46) — that limit is exactly what stands here, spelled out at last: six years and an ear-piercing ritual bound only a fellow Israelite; the nations around Israel could be bought as permanent property, but Israel's own could not be made to Egypt what Egypt had been to Israel.

A closing frame — servants of no one but Jehovah

The chapter's final unit runs the same law in reverse: an Israelite who sells himself to a wealthy resident alien keeps every jubilee protection a debtor owes a fellow Israelite, priced the identical way (vv50-52) and freed the identical way (v54). ‘For to me the children of Israel are servants’ (v55) closes the chapter by echoing v42 almost word for word — the same claim bracketing the whole unit on servitude: whatever an Israelite owes a human buyer, ownership itself belongs to Jehovah first, on the strength of one historical fact repeated at both ends, ‘whom I brought out of the land of Egypt.’

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English Leviticus 25 both run 1–55 — verified against the Masoretic text, no versification shift. Six Masoretic paragraphs divide it: closed after v7, v24, v28, v34, v38, v46. Three new dictionary entries: yovel, ‘jubilee’; deror, ‘liberty/release’, the Liberty Bell's own word; and neshekh/tarbit, the paired terms for interest. Ger and toshav, paired throughout this chapter (vv23, 35, 47), follow this project's own established Genesis 23:4 rendering, ‘resident alien and settler,’ rather than Leviticus 19's standalone ‘stranger’ for ger alone.

Echoes paid. Exodus 23:10-11's one-sentence sabbatical year (already on these pages), given here its full ritual and legal apparatus; Genesis 48:16's own go'el entry (already on these pages), which named this exact chapter by number; Exodus 1:13-14's perekh (already on these pages), the word for Egypt's own cruelty, now forbidden between Israelites by name; Exodus 21:2's own note on the six-year Hebrew-servant limit (already on these pages), whose promised foreign-slave exception (vv44-46) arrives here; Leviticus 19:15's amit (already on these pages), reused for fair dealing in a land transaction; Leviticus 23's count of seven sabbaths of days to Pentecost (already on these pages), structurally mirrored here as seven sabbaths of years to the jubilee; and the ger/toshav dictionary entry itself (already on these pages, Genesis 23:4), reused for Israel's own legal status before Jehovah (v23).

Echoes planted. Numbers 35 (now on these pages), which sets the Levites' landless cities that this chapter's v32-34 already treats as a special case — forty-eight towns, six of them cities of refuge; Deuteronomy 23:19-20 (not yet on these pages), which will repeat the neshekh/tarbit interest ban; Isaiah 61:1-2 (not yet on these pages), which borrows this chapter's own ‘proclaim liberty’ language for a much larger deliverance; and Ruth (already on these pages), the go'el law turned into a love story.
