Jeremiah audits twenty-three years of an unheeded message, then hands a cup of Jehovah's wrath to nation after nation — the seventy years of Babylonian service prophesied by name for the first time.
The fourth year of Jehoiakim, dated twice over as also 'the first year of Nebuchadrezzar' — 605 BC, the year of Carchemish, the single most precisely fixed date in the book so far.

Jeremiah 25 — The Mister Translation

1
הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר-הָיָה עַל-יִרְמְיָהוּ, עַל-כָּל-עַם יְהוּדָה, בַּשָּׁנָה הָרְבִעִית, לִיהוֹיָקִים בֶּן-יֹאשִׁיָּהוּ מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה:  הִיא, הַשָּׁנָה הָרִאשֹׁנִית, לִנְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר, מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל.
The word that came to Jeremiah concerning all the people of Judah, in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah — that was the first year of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon —note
La palabra que vino a Jeremías acerca de todo el pueblo de Judá, en el año cuarto de Joacim hijo de Josías, rey de Judá — ese fue el primer año de Nabucodonosor rey de Babilonia —
2
אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יִרְמְיָהוּ הַנָּבִיא, עַל-כָּל-עַם יְהוּדָה, וְאֶל כָּל-יֹשְׁבֵי יְרוּשָׁלִַם, לֵאמֹר.
which Jeremiah the prophet spoke to all the people of Judah and to all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying:note
la cual habló el profeta Jeremías a todo el pueblo de Judá y a todos los habitantes de Jerusalén, diciendo:
3
מִן-שְׁלֹשׁ עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה לְיֹאשִׁיָּהוּ בֶן-אָמוֹן מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה וְעַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, זֶה שָׁלֹשׁ וְעֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה, הָיָה דְבַר-יְהוָה, אֵלָי; וָאֲדַבֵּר אֲלֵיכֶם אַשְׁכֵּים וְדַבֵּר, וְלֹא שְׁמַעְתֶּם.
From the thirteenth year of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah, even to this day — these twenty-three years — the word of Jehovah has come to me, and I have spoken to you, rising up early and speaking; but you have not listened.note
Desde el año trece de Josías hijo de Amón, rey de Judá, hasta este día — estos veintitrés años — ha venido a mí la palabra de Jehová, y os he hablado desde temprano y sin cesar, pero no habéis escuchado.
cross-refs⤷ 1:2⤷ 26:5
4
וְשָׁלַח יְהוָה אֲלֵיכֶם אֶת-כָּל-עֲבָדָיו הַנְּבִאִים, הַשְׁכֵּם וְשָׁלֹחַ--וְלֹא שְׁמַעְתֶּם; וְלֹא-הִטִּיתֶם אֶת-אָזְנְכֶם, לִשְׁמֹעַ.
And Jehovah has sent to you all his servants the prophets, rising up early and sending them — but you have not listened, nor inclined your ear to hear —note
Y Jehová os ha enviado a todos sus siervos los profetas, enviándolos desde temprano y sin cesar — pero no habéis escuchado, ni habéis inclinado vuestro oído para oír —
5
לֵאמֹר, שׁוּבוּ-נָא אִישׁ מִדַּרְכּוֹ הָרָעָה וּמֵרֹעַ מַעַלְלֵיכֶם, וּשְׁבוּ עַל-הָאֲדָמָה, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן יְהוָה לָכֶם וְלַאֲבוֹתֵיכֶם--לְמִן-עוֹלָם, וְעַד-עוֹלָם.
saying, ‘Turn now, each of you, from your evil way and from the evil of your deeds, and dwell on the land that Jehovah gave to you and to your fathers, from age to age;note
diciendo: «Volveos ahora, cada uno de su mal camino y de la maldad de vuestras obras, y habitad en la tierra que Jehová os dio a vosotros y a vuestros padres, para siempre;
6
וְאַל-תֵּלְכוּ, אַחֲרֵי אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים, לְעָבְדָם, וּלְהִשְׁתַּחֲוֺת לָהֶם; וְלֹא-תַכְעִיסוּ אוֹתִי בְּמַעֲשֵׂה יְדֵיכֶם, וְלֹא אָרַע לָכֶם.
and do not go after other gods to serve them and bow down to them, and do not provoke me with the work of your hands, and I will do you no harm.’note
y no vayáis en pos de dioses ajenos para servirlos y postraros ante ellos, ni me provoquéis con la obra de vuestras manos, y no os haré daño.»
7
וְלֹא-שְׁמַעְתֶּם אֵלַי, נְאֻם-יְהוָה, לְמַעַן הַכְעִיסֵנִי בְּמַעֲשֵׂה יְדֵיכֶם, לְרַע לָכֶם.  {פ}
Yet you have not listened to me, declares Jehovah, so that you provoked me with the work of your hands, to your own harm.note
Pero no me escuchasteis, declara Jehová, de modo que me provocasteis con la obra de vuestras manos, para vuestro propio mal.
8
לָכֵן, כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת:  יַעַן, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-שְׁמַעְתֶּם אֶת-דְּבָרָי.
Therefore thus says Jehovah of hosts: Because you have not obeyed my words,note
Por tanto, así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos: Por cuanto no habéis obedecido mis palabras,
9
הִנְנִי שֹׁלֵחַ וְלָקַחְתִּי אֶת-כָּל-מִשְׁפְּחוֹת צָפוֹן נְאֻם-יְהוָה, וְאֶל-נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר מֶלֶךְ-בָּבֶל עַבְדִּי, וַהֲבִאֹתִים עַל-הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת וְעַל-יֹשְׁבֶיהָ, וְעַל כָּל-הַגּוֹיִם הָאֵלֶּה סָבִיב; וְהַחֲרַמְתִּים--וְשַׂמְתִּים לְשַׁמָּה וְלִשְׁרֵקָה, וּלְחָרְבוֹת עוֹלָם.
look, I am sending for all the tribes of the north, declares Jehovah, and for Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, my servant, and I will bring them against this land and against its inhabitants and against all these surrounding nations; I will devote them to destruction, and make them a horror and a hissing and ruins forever.note
he aquí que envío a buscar a todas las familias del norte, declara Jehová, y a Nabucodonosor rey de Babilonia, mi siervo, y los traeré contra esta tierra y contra sus habitantes y contra todas estas naciones de alrededor; los entregaré a la destrucción, y los pondré por horror y por burla y por ruinas perpetuas.
cross-refs⤷ 21:9
10
וְהַאֲבַדְתִּי מֵהֶם, קוֹל שָׂשׂוֹן וְקוֹל שִׂמְחָה, קוֹל חָתָן, וְקוֹל כַּלָּה--קוֹל רֵחַיִם, וְאוֹר נֵר.
And I will silence from among them the sound of joy and the sound of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom and the voice of the bride, the sound of the millstones and the light of the lamp.note
Y haré cesar de entre ellos la voz de gozo y la voz de alegría, la voz del novio y la voz de la novia, el sonido de las piedras del molino y la luz de la lámpara.
11
וְהָיְתָה כָּל-הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת, לְחָרְבָּה לְשַׁמָּה; וְעָבְדוּ הַגּוֹיִם הָאֵלֶּה, אֶת-מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל--שִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה.
And this whole land will become a ruin, a horror; and these nations will serve the king of Babylon seventy years.note
Y toda esta tierra será una ruina, un horror; y estas naciones servirán al rey de Babilonia setenta años.
cross-refs⤷ 29:10⤷ Ezra 1:1⤷ Zechariah 1:12
12
וְהָיָה כִמְלֹאות שִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה אֶפְקֹד עַל-מֶלֶךְ-בָּבֶל וְעַל-הַגּוֹי הַהוּא נְאֻם-יְהוָה, אֶת-עֲוֺנָם--וְעַל-אֶרֶץ כַּשְׂדִּים; וְשַׂמְתִּי אֹתוֹ, לְשִׁמְמוֹת עוֹלָם.
And when seventy years are complete, I will punish the king of Babylon and that nation, declares Jehovah, for their iniquity, and the land of the Chaldeans, and I will make it ruins forever.note
Y cuando se cumplan los setenta años, castigaré al rey de Babilonia y a aquella nación, declara Jehová, por su iniquidad, y a la tierra de los caldeos, y la reduciré a ruinas perpetuas.
13
וְהֵבֵאתִי עַל-הָאָרֶץ הַהִיא, אֶת-כָּל-דְּבָרַי אֲשֶׁר-דִּבַּרְתִּי עָלֶיהָ--אֵת כָּל-הַכָּתוּב בַּסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה, אֲשֶׁר-נִבָּא יִרְמְיָהוּ עַל-כָּל-הַגּוֹיִם.
And I will bring on that land all my words that I have spoken against it — everything written in this book, which Jeremiah has prophesied against all the nations.note
Y traeré sobre aquella tierra todas mis palabras que he hablado contra ella — todo lo escrito en este libro, que Jeremías profetizó contra todas las naciones.
14
כִּי עָבְדוּ-בָם גַּם-הֵמָּה גּוֹיִם רַבִּים, וּמְלָכִים גְּדוֹלִים; וְשִׁלַּמְתִּי לָהֶם כְּפָעֳלָם, וּכְמַעֲשֵׂה יְדֵיהֶם.  {פ}
For they too will be enslaved by many nations and great kings; and I will repay them according to their deeds and according to the work of their own hands.note
Porque también ellos serán esclavizados por muchas naciones y grandes reyes; y les pagaré conforme a sus hechos y conforme a la obra de sus manos.
15
כִּי כֹה אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֵלַי, קַח אֶת-כּוֹס הַיַּיִן הַחֵמָה הַזֹּאת, מִיָּדִי; וְהִשְׁקִיתָה אֹתוֹ אֶת-כָּל-הַגּוֹיִם, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי שֹׁלֵחַ אוֹתְךָ אֲלֵיהֶם.
For thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel, to me: Take this cup of the wine of fury from my hand, and make all the nations to whom I send you drink it.note
Porque así me ha dicho Jehová, el Dios de Israel: Toma de mi mano esta copa del vino del furor, y da de beber de ella a todas las naciones a las cuales yo te envío.
16
וְשָׁתוּ, וְהִתְגֹּעֲשׁוּ וְהִתְהֹלָלוּ, מִפְּנֵי הַחֶרֶב, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי שֹׁלֵחַ בֵּינֹתָם.
And they will drink, and stagger, and go out of their minds, because of the sword that I am sending among them.note
Y beberán, y se tambalearán, y enloquecerán, a causa de la espada que enviaré entre ellos.
17
וָאֶקַּח אֶת-הַכּוֹס, מִיַּד יְהוָה; וָאַשְׁקֶה, אֶת-כָּל-הַגּוֹיִם, אֲשֶׁר-שְׁלָחַנִי יְהוָה, אֲלֵיהֶם.
So I took the cup from Jehovah's hand, and made all the nations to whom Jehovah sent me drink it:note
Y tomé la copa de la mano de Jehová, y di de beber a todas las naciones a las cuales Jehová me envió:
18
אֶת-יְרוּשָׁלִַם וְאֶת-עָרֵי יְהוּדָה, וְאֶת-מְלָכֶיהָ אֶת-שָׂרֶיהָ:  לָתֵת אֹתָם לְחָרְבָּה לְשַׁמָּה, לִשְׁרֵקָה וְלִקְלָלָה--כַּיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, and its kings and its princes, to make them a ruin, a horror, a hissing, and a curse, as they are this day;note
a Jerusalén y a las ciudades de Judá, y a sus reyes y a sus príncipes, para convertirlos en ruina, en horror, en burla y en maldición, como sucede hoy;
19
אֶת-פַּרְעֹה מֶלֶךְ-מִצְרַיִם וְאֶת-עֲבָדָיו וְאֶת-שָׂרָיו, וְאֶת-כָּל-עַמּוֹ.
Pharaoh king of Egypt, and his servants and his princes and all his people;note
a Faraón rey de Egipto, y a sus siervos y a sus príncipes y a todo su pueblo;
20
וְאֵת, כָּל-הָעֶרֶב, וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי אֶרֶץ הָעוּץ; וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי אֶרֶץ פְּלִשְׁתִּים, וְאֶת-אַשְׁקְלוֹן וְאֶת-עַזָּה וְאֶת-עֶקְרוֹן, וְאֵת שְׁאֵרִית אַשְׁדּוֹד.
and all the mixed peoples; and all the kings of the land of Uz; and all the kings of the land of the Philistines — Ashkelon, and Gaza, and Ekron, and the remnant of Ashdod;note
y a todo el pueblo mezclado; y a todos los reyes de la tierra de Uz; y a todos los reyes de la tierra de los filisteos — Ascalón, Gaza, Ecrón, y el resto de Asdod;
21
אֶת-אֱדוֹם וְאֶת-מוֹאָב, וְאֶת-בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן.
Edom, and Moab, and the sons of Ammon;note
a Edom, a Moab, y a los hijos de Amón;
22
וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי-צֹר, וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי צִידוֹן; וְאֵת מַלְכֵי הָאִי, אֲשֶׁר בְּעֵבֶר הַיָּם.
and all the kings of Tyre, and all the kings of Sidon, and the kings of the coastland across the sea;note
y a todos los reyes de Tiro, y a todos los reyes de Sidón, y a los reyes de la costa que está al otro lado del mar;
23
וְאֶת-דְּדָן וְאֶת-תֵּימָא וְאֶת-בּוּז, וְאֵת כָּל-קְצוּצֵי פֵאָה.
Dedan, and Tema, and Buz, and all who clip the corners of their hair;note
a Dedán, a Temá, a Buz, y a todos los que se rapan las sienes;
24
וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי עֲרָב; וְאֵת כָּל-מַלְכֵי הָעֶרֶב, הַשֹּׁכְנִים בַּמִּדְבָּר.
and all the kings of Arabia, and all the kings of the mixed peoples who dwell in the wilderness;note
y a todos los reyes de Arabia, y a todos los reyes del pueblo mezclado que habita en el desierto;
25
וְאֵת כָּל-מַלְכֵי זִמְרִי, וְאֵת כָּל-מַלְכֵי עֵילָם, וְאֵת, כָּל-מַלְכֵי מָדָי.
and all the kings of Zimri, and all the kings of Elam, and all the kings of Media;note
y a todos los reyes de Zimri, y a todos los reyes de Elam, y a todos los reyes de Media;
26
וְאֵת כָּל-מַלְכֵי הַצָּפוֹן, הַקְּרֹבִים וְהָרְחֹקִים אִישׁ אֶל-אָחִיו, וְאֵת כָּל-הַמַּמְלְכוֹת הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר עַל-פְּנֵי הָאֲדָמָה; וּמֶלֶךְ שֵׁשַׁךְ, יִשְׁתֶּה אַחֲרֵיהֶם.
and all the kings of the north, near and far, one after another — and all the kingdoms of the world that are on the face of the earth. And after them all, the king of Sheshach will drink.note
y a todos los reyes del norte, cercanos y lejanos, el uno con el otro — y a todos los reinos del mundo que están sobre la faz de la tierra. Y después de todos ellos, beberá el rey de Sesac.
cross-refs⤷ 23:6
27
וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵיהֶם כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, שְׁתוּ וְשִׁכְרוּ וּקְיוּ, וְנִפְלוּ, וְלֹא תָקוּמוּ--מִפְּנֵי הַחֶרֶב, אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי שֹׁלֵחַ בֵּינֵיכֶם.  {פ}
And you will say to them, ‘Thus says Jehovah of hosts, the God of Israel: Drink, get drunk, and vomit, and fall, and rise no more, because of the sword that I am sending among you.’note
Y les dirás: «Así ha dicho Jehová de los ejércitos, el Dios de Israel: Bebed, y embriagaos, y vomitad, y caed, y no os levantéis, a causa de la espada que enviaré entre vosotros.»
28
וְהָיָה, כִּי יְמָאֲנוּ לָקַחַת-הַכּוֹס מִיָּדְךָ--לִשְׁתּוֹת; וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵיהֶם, כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת--שָׁתוֹ תִשְׁתּוּ.
And if they refuse to take the cup from your hand to drink, then you will say to them, ‘Thus says Jehovah of hosts: You will surely drink!note

Translator’s Notes — verse by verse

Each note explains this translation's choice and compares the seven versions on the shelf. Quotations from copyrighted versions (NIV, TLB, NWT) are kept to short phrases for comparison; KJV, Geneva, Douay-Rheims and ASV are public domain.

Verses 1–7 · זֶה שָׁלֹשׁ וְעֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה zeh shalosh ve'esrim shanah — twenty-three years, and a refrain that never once changes

A date this precise pins the whole chapter to a single year: 605 BC. The fourth year of Jehoiakim was, in the same breath, the first year of Nebuchadrezzar — the year of Carchemish, when Egypt's army broke and Babylon became the region's undisputed power. Jeremiah dates almost nothing else in the book this exactly; this chapter wants the reader doing arithmetic from the first verse.

Jeremiah audits his own career. Verse 3 counts back to 1:2's own dating — “the thirteenth year of Josiah” — and does the subtraction for the reader: twenty-three years of “rising up early and speaking,” the same idiom used twice more of the whole line of prophets Jehovah sent (v4). Two decades of the identical message, met with the identical refrain, three times in seven verses: “you have not listened.”

The offer itself (vv5–6) restates the same three moves as every prophetic sermon before it — turn from evil ways, stay off other gods, and the land stays yours “from age to age” — not because Jeremiah has run out of things to say, but because the charge in this chapter is precisely that the message never needed to be new. Twenty-three years of a constant offer is what makes v9's sentence a judgment and not a surprise.

Verses 8–14 · שִׁבְעִים שָׁנָה shiv'im shanah — seventy years, the single most load-bearing number in the book

“My servant.” Verse 9 calls Nebuchadrezzar — a foreign, pagan king, actively about to destroy Jehovah's own city — avdi, “my servant,” the same possessive title given elsewhere to Moses and David. KJV/ASV/NWT all keep it plain; none soften it. The claim underneath the whole chapter is that Babylon's army is not outside Jehovah's control but is, however briefly and however against its own knowledge, an instrument inside it.

“I will devote them to destruction” (vahaḥaramtim) is the same root, the same concept, as the ban laid on Canaanite cities generations earlier — a category this book has until now reserved for conquest, now turned outward onto the nations themselves, Judah included. The chapter does not soften the word to make the reversal easier to read.

V10's catalogue of silenced sounds — joy, gladness, a bridegroom's voice, a bride's voice, the ordinary grinding of household millstones, a lamp still lit at night — names peacetime by what a besieged or emptied town no longer has. Every item is domestic, not military; the chapter measures the disaster in the smallest possible units.

⚠ The seventy years (vv11–12) is the single most-cited number in the rest of the book, and beyond it. Jeremiah 29:10, already on these pages, restates it word for word. The prophecy is treated as an accounting entry come due at Ezra 1:1, and the angel of Zechariah 1:12 asks “how long” with these same seventy years nearly spent. Whether the span is counted from 605 (this chapter's own date) to 539 (Babylon's fall) or from 586 (the temple's destruction) to 516 (the second temple's completion) is a real, open question this translation does not settle — both spans land close to seventy, and the text never specifies which terminus it means.

Verses 15–29 · כּוֹס הַיַּיִן הַחֵמָה kos ha-yayin ha-chemah — a cup passed to every nation Judah could name, and one it could not say out loud

The cup is handed to a real map, not an abstraction. Egypt, Uz (Job's own country), the five Philistine cities, Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre and Sidon, the Arabian tribes, Elam and Media — a roster that runs from Judah's nearest neighbors out to powers on the far edge of the known world. Judah drinks first (v18), not last — whatever this cup is, it starts at home.

“All the mixed peoples” (vv20, 24) is a plain, common phrase for a mixed or foreign population living among a settled one — the same idea, though not necessarily the identical occurrence, as the “great mixed multitude” that left Egypt with Israel at Exodus 12:38. Even the outsiders who attached themselves to other nations are handed the same cup.

⚠ “The king of Sheshach” (v26) is Babylon itself, in code. Run the Hebrew alphabet in reverse — aleph swaps with tav, bet with shin, and so on, a device called ATBASH — and Sheshach (sh-sh-k) unscrambles to Bavel (b-b-l), “Babylon.” The list has just spent nine verses handing every nation on earth a cup of judgment for Jehovah, and the very last name on it, hidden inside a cipher, is the nation about to serve as the sentence's own executioner. This is not the only place the book does this — it happens again, more famously, at 51:41, not yet on these pages.

Verses 30–38 · יְהוָה מִמָּרוֹם יִשְׁאָג YHVH mimarom yish'ag — Jehovah roars, and the woe against the shepherds comes home to roost

V30 opens with the exact words Amos used a century and a half earlier. “Jehovah roars from Zion, and gives his voice from Jerusalem” (Amos 1:2) becomes here “Jehovah roars from on high… raises his voice from his holy habitation” — the identical verb pair, aimed now at the whole earth instead of Israel's immediate neighbors. Whether this is a deliberate quotation or two prophets independently reaching for the same available image for a divine verdict, the text does not say; either way, the roar that once opened a shorter, more local book of judgment reopens here at a far larger scale.

The shepherds get their sentence at last. “Wail, you shepherds” (v34) answers 23:1–4's “woe to the shepherds who destroy and scatter the flock” almost verse for verse — the same officials-as-kings image, the same charge, now given its actual sentence rather than a warning.

⚠ Two real difficulties, reported rather than smoothed. V34's “the days of your slaughter and your scattering” renders a construction the shelf splits on — KJV/ASV keep “dispersions” as a second noun paired with “slaughter”; NIV abandons the word altogether and reads the following image as “you will fall like the best of the rams” instead of “a precious vessel.” And v38's “because of the fierceness of” a Hebrew word usually read as a form of ‘to oppress’ is a rare enough construction that KJV's “the oppressing sword” supplies a noun (“sword”) the verse itself does not contain, borrowed from how the same phrase reads elsewhere in the book. This translation keeps the more literal, less certain reading in both places rather than importing the smoother one.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact / distinct: “my servant” (v9) kept for Nebuchadrezzar without softening, the same title given Moses and David elsewhere; “devote to destruction” (v9) kept as the literal cherem-word rather than a generic “destroy”; and v34/v38's two genuinely difficult constructions given in their more literal, harder form rather than smoothed to match the more familiar shelf reading.

Echoes paid. The twenty-three years (v3) counted back to the prophet's own call-date at 1:2; the seventy years (vv11–12), already restated at 29:10 and already treated as an account come due at Ezra 1:1 and Zechariah 1:12; and the shepherds sentenced here (v34) after being warned at 23:1–4. V30's opening roar is the same verb pair Amos used to open his own book at 1:2, a century and a half before.

Echoes planted. Sheshach (v26), Babylon's own name run through a cipher, waiting for its more famous second use at 51:41. The cup of the wine of fury (v15), the image this book hands forward to Revelation's own cup of God's wrath (14:10; 16:19), neither yet on these pages. And the seventy years themselves, waiting for the chapter where a reader named Daniel finds this very prophecy written down and does the arithmetic (Daniel 9:2), not yet translated here.

This library already carries Jeremiah 29 and 31, both translated out of order; the sequential thread is still open here. Next in sequence: Jeremiah 26 — Jeremiah on trial for his life in the temple courtyard for a sermon that has not even been read yet, and the one time in this book someone else's earlier execution is told in full to explain why he is spared.
