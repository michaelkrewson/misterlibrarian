Belshazzar feasts a thousand lords from the temple’s vessels and praises the gods of gold and silver; fingers of a man’s hand write on the plaster by the lampstand; his knees knock, the wise men cannot read it, and the queen remembers a man in the kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods; Daniel refuses the gifts, tells the king the story of Nebuchadnezzar’s pride and fall, and reads the writing, mene, tekel, peres: numbered, weighed, divided; he is clothed in purple and made third; that night Belshazzar is killed, and Darius the Mede receives the kingdom at sixty-two.
Placed on the night Babylon falls (about 539 BC by the book’s own sequence, the same year Daniel 6 opens). ⚠ The chapter calls Nebuchadnezzar Belshazzar’s father and does not say who killed him or how the city fell; Babylonian records name Belshazzar as the son of Nabonidus.

א  בֵּלְשַׁאצַּר מַלְכָּא, עֲבַד לְחֶם רַב, לְרַבְרְבָנוֹהִי, אֲלַף; וְלָקֳבֵל אַלְפָּא, חַמְרָא שָׁתֵה.
Belshazzar the king made a great feast for a thousand of his lords, and before the thousand he was drinking wine.note
El rey Belsasar hizo un gran banquete para mil de sus señores, y delante de los mil bebía vino.
2
ב  בֵּלְשַׁאצַּר אֲמַר בִּטְעֵם חַמְרָא, לְהַיְתָיָה לְמָאנֵי דַּהֲבָא וְכַסְפָּא, דִּי הַנְפֵּק נְבוּכַדְנֶצַּר אֲבוּהִי, מִן-הֵיכְלָא דִּי בִירוּשְׁלֶם; וְיִשְׁתּוֹן בְּהוֹן, מַלְכָּא וְרַבְרְבָנוֹהִי, שֵׁגְלָתֵהּ, וּלְחֵנָתֵהּ.
Belshazzar, at the taste of the wine, said to bring the vessels of gold and silver that Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple that was in Jerusalem, that the king and his lords, his wives and his concubines might drink from them.note
Belsasar, al sabor del vino, dijo que trajeran los vasos de oro y de plata que Nabucodonosor su padre había sacado del templo que estaba en Jerusalén, para que bebieran de ellos el rey y sus señores, sus mujeres y sus concubinas.

Bebieron vino, y alabaron a los dioses de oro y de plata, de bronce, de hierro, de madera y de piedra.

ה  בַּהּ-שַׁעֲתָה, נפקו נְפַקָה אֶצְבְּעָן דִּי יַד-אֱנָשׁ, וְכָתְבָן לָקֳבֵל נֶבְרַשְׁתָּא, עַל-גִּירָא דִּי-כְתַל הֵיכְלָא דִּי מַלְכָּא; וּמַלְכָּא חָזֵה, פַּס יְדָא דִּי כָתְבָה.
In that very hour the fingers of a man’s hand came out and wrote, opposite the lampstand, on the plaster of the wall of the king’s palace; and the king saw the part of the hand that wrote.note
En aquella misma hora salieron los dedos de una mano de hombre y escribían, frente al candelero, sobre el enlucido de la pared del palacio del rey; y el rey veía la parte de la mano que escribía.

Then the king’s brightness was changed, and his thoughts alarmed him; the knots of his loins were loosed, and his knees knocked one against the other.note
Entonces el brillo del rey se cambió, y sus pensamientos lo alarmaron; los nudos de sus lomos se soltaron, y sus rodillas chocaban una contra otra.

ז  קָרֵא מַלְכָּא, בְּחַיִל, לְהֶעָלָה לְאָשְׁפַיָּא, כשדיא כַּשְׂדָּאֵי וְגָזְרַיָּא; עָנֵה מַלְכָּא וְאָמַר לְחַכִּימֵי בָבֶל, דִּי כָל-אֱנָשׁ דִּי-יִקְרֵה כְּתָבָה דְנָה וּפִשְׁרֵהּ יְחַוִּנַּנִי, אַרְגְּוָנָא יִלְבַּשׁ והמנוכא וְהַמְנִיכָא דִי-דַהֲבָא עַל-צַוְּארֵהּ, וְתַלְתִּי בְמַלְכוּתָא, יִשְׁלַט.  {ס}
The king called out with strength to bring in the exorcists, the Chaldeans and the diviners. The king answered and said to the wise men of Babylon: Whoever reads this writing and shows me its interpretation will be clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom.note
El rey clamó con fuerza que hicieran entrar a los exorcistas, a los caldeos y a los adivinos. Respondió el rey y dijo a los sabios de Babilonia: Cualquiera que lea esta escritura y me muestre su interpretación será vestido de púrpura, con un collar de oro al cuello, y gobernará como tercero en el reino.
8
ח  אֱדַיִן, עללין עָלִּין, כֹּל, חַכִּימֵי מַלְכָּא; וְלָא-כָהֲלִין כְּתָבָא לְמִקְרֵא, ופשרא וּפִשְׁרֵהּ לְהוֹדָעָה לְמַלְכָּא.
Then all the king’s wise men came in; but they could not read the writing, or make its interpretation known to the king.note
Entonces entraron todos los sabios del rey; pero no pudieron leer la escritura ni dar a conocer al rey su interpretación.

ט  אֱדַיִן מַלְכָּא בֵלְשַׁאצַּר, שַׂגִּיא מִתְבָּהַל, וְזִיוֹהִי, שָׁנַיִן עֲלוֹהִי; וְרַבְרְבָנוֹהִי, מִשְׁתַּבְּשִׁין.
Then King Belshazzar was greatly alarmed, and his brightness was changed upon him; and his lords were bewildered.note
Entonces el rey Belsasar se alarmó mucho, y su brillo se cambió sobre él; y sus señores quedaron perplejos.

La reina, por las palabras del rey y de sus señores, entró en la sala del banquete; respondió la reina y dijo: ¡Oh rey, vive para siempre! No te alarmen tus pensamientos, ni se cambie tu brillo.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · Daniel 5:1–4 A feast for a thousand — the vessels from Jerusalem — the gods of gold and silver

⚠ This chapter is in Aramaic. The language of Daniel changes at 2:4 and does not change back until 8:1, so the words printed above each verse are Aramaic, not Hebrew; the letters are the same square letters. Where this page says “the Hebrew Bible” it means the Masoretic text, which carries the Aramaic in the same book. The name Belshazzar is spelled in this chapter as Belshatsar, and the king’s name for Daniel at verse 12 is Belteshatsar: the two differ by one letter, teth.

⭐ The vessels. “the vessels of gold” stands in two verses, 2 and 3, and the story of those vessels began on these pages: Nebuchadnezzar carried some of them to the house of his god (Daniel 1:2, now on these pages). Verse 2 says bit’em chamra, “at the taste of the wine”, and the order is for the temple’s vessels to be used for the feast; the KJV has “whiles he tasted the wine”, the NIV “While Belshazzar was drinking his wine” and the NWT 1984 “under the influence of the wine”. The vessels are named the temple, the house of God at verse 3 and are brought by “his father”. The Douay adds a clause to verse 1 that is not in the Aramaic: “every one drank according to his age”.

⭐ Father, son. The chapter calls Nebuchadnezzar Belshazzar’s father at verses 2, 11, 13 and 18 and Belshazzar his son at verse 22. ⚠ Babylonian cuneiform records name Belshazzar as the son of Nabonidus, the last king; Aramaic av and bar can also mean an earlier king and one who came after him. That is a fact from outside the chapter and this page prints it as outside the chapter; the verses themselves say only “father” and “son”.

⭐ Six materials, twice. They praised the gods of six materials, and “of bronze, iron, wood and stone” stands in two verses, 4 and 23, the second time in Daniel’s mouth, with the added words that they “do not see or hear or know”. The KJV has “praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone”, the NIV “they praised the gods of gold and silver, of bronze, iron, wood and stone” and the NWT 1984 “they praised the gods of gold and of silver, copper, iron, wood and stone”. The vessels are the gold and silver of the God of Israel; the gods are the same metals, shaped.

Verses 5–9 · Daniel 5:5–9 Fingers of a man’s hand — the knots of his loins — the wise men cannot read — the king is alarmed

⭐ The hand. “fingers of a man’s hand” stands in this one verse, and “on the wall of the palace” stands in this one verse. What the king sees is pas yeda, “the part of the hand”, and at verse 24, when it is “sent from before him”, the same word returns as pasa di-yeda. The shelf prints four different things: KJV and ASV “the part of the hand that wrote”, NWT 1984 “the back of the hand that was writing”, Geneva “the palm of the hand that wrote” and Douay “the joints of the hand that wrote”. The NIV has “the fingers of a human hand appeared and wrote on the plaster of the wall, near the lampstand in the royal palace”. The nevrashta, the lampstand, is a light, and the writing is lit.

⭐ Knots. The king’s body comes undone: qitrei chartseh mishtarayin, “the knots of his loins are loosed”. The same two roots return when the queen describes Daniel at verse 12 (meshare qitrin, “loosing knots”) and when the king repeats it at verse 16 (qitrin lemishre). The KJV prints “the joints of his loins were loosed” at verse 6 and “dissolving of doubts” at verse 12, so the two uses do not meet; the NWT 1984 prints “his hip joints were loosening” at verse 6 and “the untying of knots” at verse 12. The one who has come undone sends for the one who unties.

⭐ Alarm, three times. The verb is bhl: his thoughts “alarmed him” (verse 6), he is “greatly alarmed” (verse 9), and the queen will say “do not let your thoughts alarm you” (verse 10). The KJV prints “his thoughts troubled him” at verse 6 and “greatly troubled” at verse 9. His ziv, his brightness, is changed at verses 6, 9 and 10; the KJV has “countenance” and the NWT 1984 “complexion”.

⭐ Third in the kingdom. The prize is purple, a chain of gold, and to rule as third (talti, verse 7; talta, verses 16 and 29). The KJV prints “scarlet” where the ASV prints “purple” for the same Aramaic word, argevana; the NIV has “the third highest ruler in the kingdom” and the NWT 1984 “as the third one in the kingdom he will rule”. ⚠ A common reading is that the king is himself second, under a father who is first; the chapter does not say so, and the number three is only what the verse prints. The experts named are ashfaya, kasdaye and gazrayya: the KJV has “the astrologers, the Chaldeans, and the soothsayers”, the ASV “the enchanters, the Chaldeans, and the soothsayers” and the NWT 1984 “the conjurers”.

Verses 10–16 · Daniel 5:10–16 The queen — there is a man in your kingdom — Are you that Daniel?

⭐ The queen. She is malketa, and she comes in uninvited, because of the words she heard; the ASV has “the queen by reason of the words of the king and his lords came into the banquet house”, and the TLB calls her “the queen mother” and has her rush “to the banquet hall”. The chapter does not say who she is; her speech about “the days of your father” is of someone who remembers them. “O king, live forever” stands in five verses: here, Daniel 2:4, 3:9, 6:6 and 6:21 (the Hebrew Bible numbers the last two 6:7 and 6:22). The KJV prints “O king, live for ever” and the NWT 1984 “O king, keep living even to times indefinite”.

⭐ The spirit of the holy gods. “the spirit of the holy gods” stands in four verses: here, and in Nebuchadnezzar’s own words about Daniel in the chapter on his tree (Daniel 4:8 and 4:9, with 4:18; the Hebrew Bible numbers these 4:5, 4:6 and 4:15). The word is elahin, a plural that can read “gods” or “God”; here, in the mouths of Babylonians, the shelf has KJV, ASV and Douay “the spirit of the holy gods”, and NWT 1984 “the spirit of holy gods”. “light and insight and wisdom” stands in two verses, 11 and 14: the KJV has “light and understanding and wisdom”, the NIV “insight and intelligence and wisdom” and the NWT 1984 “illumination and insight and wisdom”.

⭐ Found. Light, insight and wisdom were found in Daniel (hishtekhachat bei, verses 11 and 12; bakh, verse 14), and the verb will come back at verse 27, said of the king. The king also names the one who is brought: “that Daniel, of the sons of the exile of Judah, whom the king my father brought from Judah”. The KJV has “the children of the captivity of Judah, whom the king my father brought out of Jewry” and the NIV “one of the exiles my father the king brought from Judah”. His chief of the wise, rav chartummin, was already named by Nebuchadnezzar at Daniel 4:9, and the king’s name for him, Belteshazzar, was set at Daniel 1:7.

Verses 17–23 · Daniel 5:17–23 Keep your gifts — God Most High gave the kingdom — you have not brought your heart low

⭐ Keep your gifts. “your rewards” stands in this one verse; the KJV has “Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another”, the NIV “You may keep your gifts for yourself and give your rewards to someone else” and the NWT 1984 “Let your gifts prove to be to you yourself”. He will read it all the same (verse 17), and the king will clothe him in purple anyway at verse 29.

⭐ Daniel tells the king’s own story. Verses 18–21 are the chapter of the tree, Daniel 4, in six verses: the kingdom, greatness, glory and majesty given (verse 18), the nations’ fear (verse 19), the heart lifted up and the spirit hardened (verse 20), and the driving out to the wild donkeys, the grass like oxen and the dew of heaven (verse 21; the king’s own account is Daniel 4:33, the Hebrew Bible’s 4:30), until he knew that God Most High rules. The KJV has “his heart was lifted up, and his mind hardened in pride”, the NWT 1984 “his heart became haughty and his own spirit became hard” and the NIV “his heart became arrogant and hardened with pride”. And the self-lifting of the earlier king of Babylon, “I will ascend… I will be like the Most High”, is Isaiah 14:13 and 14:14 (now on these pages), with its fall to the pit.

⚠ Killed, struck, kept alive? Verse 19 has four pairs of what the king did to anyone he wished, and the second word of the first pair is macha. The shelf cannot agree on it: “kept alive” (KJV, ASV), “spared” (NIV), “destroyed” (Douay), “smote” (Geneva) and “striking” (NWT 1984). The sense “strike” is the common sense of the verb; this translation prints “struck”, and says here that “kept alive” is a reading, not a plain rendering.

⭐ Brought low. Verse 19 ends with mashpil, “whom he wished he brought low”, and verse 22 says lo hashpelt livavakh, “you have not brought your heart low”: the same root, the one the king does to others and has not done to himself. The NIV prints “humbled” in both places (“those he wanted to humble, he humbled”, “you, Belshazzar, his son, have not humbled yourself”), the KJV “put down” at verse 19 and “humbled” at verse 22. “the Lord of heaven” stands in this one verse, and “the God in whose hand is your breath, and to whom all your ways belong” is the last charge: the KJV has “the God in whose hand thy breath is, and whose are all thy ways, hast thou not glorified”.

Verses 24–28 · Daniel 5:24–28 Mene, mene, tekel, parsin — numbered, weighed, divided

⭐ The writing. “mene, mene, tekel” stands in this one verse, and “parsin” stands in this one verse. Four words are written and three are read: the interpretation takes mene once, tekel and peres. The KJV has “Mene, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin”, the NIV “mene, mene, tekel, parsin” and the Douay “MANE, THECEL, PHARES”. ⚠ The u of upharsin is “and”, and parsin is the plural of peres, which is why the English prints one word at verse 25 and another at verse 28. The writer of the text is not named: it is the hand “sent from before him” (verse 24), and him is the God of verse 23.

⭐ Three verbs. Mene is read as menah, “he has numbered”: the KJV has “God hath numbered thy kingdom, and finished it”, the NIV “God has numbered the days of your reign and brought it to an end” and the NWT 1984 brackets “[the days of]”, so the words “the days of” are in the translators and not in the Aramaic. Tekel is tekiltah, “you have been weighed”, and peres is perisat, “it has been divided”, with paras, Persia, a syllable away in the same verse. The KJV has “Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting” and the Geneva “found too light”. ⚠ Many readers have heard the three words as three weights, a mina, a shekel and half-minas; that is a reading laid over the verses, not something the chapter says.

⭐ Found. The verb of verse 27 is hishtekhachat, “you were found”: what was found in Daniel is light and insight and wisdom (verses 11, 12, 14), and what is found in the king is chasir, “lacking”. The KJV prints “found wanting” at verse 27, and the same “found” is earlier in the English, so both appear; the NWT 1984 “have been found deficient”.

Verses 29–31 · Daniel 5:29–31 Daniel is clothed — in that night — Darius the Mede

⭐ He keeps the promise. After the reading, Belshazzar commanded, and they clothed Daniel in purple, with a chain of gold, and proclaimed that he should rule as third; the KJV has “Then commanded Belshazzar, and they clothed Daniel with scarlet, and put a chain of gold about his neck” and the NIV “Daniel was clothed in purple, a gold chain was placed around his neck, and he was proclaimed the third highest ruler in the kingdom”. The reward is paid after the sentence, not before.

⭐ In that night. “in that night” stands in this one verse, and the verb is passive: qetil, “was killed”. The chapter does not say who killed him, or how the city fell. The KJV has “In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain”, the NIV “That very night Belshazzar, king of the Babylonians, was slain” and the NWT 1984 “In that very night Bel·shazʹzar the Chal·deʹan king was killed”.

⭐ Darius the Mede. ⚠ Numbering: English Bibles end the chapter with verse 31, and Darius the Mede received the kingdom; the Hebrew Bible numbers this line 6:1, so the Hebrew printed above verse 31 is the Hebrew’s 6:1 and English 6:1 is the Hebrew’s 6:2. “about sixty-two years old” stands in this one verse; the Aramaic idiom is ke-var shnin, “as a son of years”. The KJV has “Darius the Median took the kingdom, being about threescore and two years old”, the ASV “Darius the Mede received the kingdom”, the Geneva “Darius of the Medes” and the TLB “entered the city”, which the Aramaic does not say. The next chapter, Daniel 6, begins with Darius setting over the kingdom a hundred and twenty satraps.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “brightness” for ziv at verses 6, 9 and 10; “alarmed” for bhl at verses 6, 9 and 10; “knots” and “loosed” at verses 6, 12 and 16; “found” at verses 11, 12, 14 and 27; “brought low” for the root of mashpil and hashpelt at verses 19 and 22; “the part of the hand” at verses 5 and 24; “rule as third” at verses 7, 16 and 29. The Name is not written anywhere in the chapter; God is elaha, “God”, and mara shemaya, “the Lord of heaven”.

Echoes paid. Daniel 1:2 and 1:7; Daniel 4:8, 4:9, 4:18, 4:33 and 4:37; Isaiah 14:13 and 14:14; Daniel 6:1.

Echoes planted. Isaiah 21:5; Jeremiah 51:39 and 51:57; Exodus 31:18; Deuteronomy 4:28; Psalm 115:4–7. None of these is translated here yet except those named as paid above.

⚠ A note on order: Daniel stands on these pages at chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 11 and 12, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Daniel 7.
