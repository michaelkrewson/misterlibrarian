The anger of Jehovah burns against Israel again, and David is incited to number Israel and Judah. Joab objects and the king prevails; the officers go round the land in nine months and twenty days and report 800,000 and 500,000. David’s heart strikes him; Gad brings three things to choose from; David chooses the hand of Jehovah, and seventy thousand die from Dan to Beersheba until Jehovah relents. David buys the threshing floor of Araunah for fifty shekels of silver, builds an altar, and the plague is checked.
Late in David’s reign, after his last words: the book closes on a census, a plague and an altar bought at a price.

2 Samuel 24 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
וַיֹּסֶף, אַף-יְהוָה, לַחֲרוֹת, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיָּסֶת אֶת-דָּוִד בָּהֶם לֵאמֹר, לֵךְ מְנֵה אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאֶת-יְהוּדָה.
And again the anger of Jehovah burned against Israel, and he incited David against them, saying: ‘Go, number Israel and Judah.’note
Y otra vez se encendió la ira de Jehová contra Israel, e incitó a David contra ellos, diciendo: «Ve, cuenta a Israel y a Judá».
2
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-יוֹאָב שַׂר-הַחַיִל אֲשֶׁר-אִתּוֹ, שׁוּט-נָא בְּכָל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל מִדָּן וְעַד-בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, וּפִקְדוּ, אֶת-הָעָם; וְיָדַעְתִּי, אֵת מִסְפַּר הָעָם.  {ס}
And the king said to Joab the commander of the army who was with him: ‘Go to and fro, please, through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba, and muster the people, that I may know the number of the people.’note
Y el rey dijo a Joab, comandante del ejército que estaba con él: «Recorre, por favor, todas las tribus de Israel, desde Dan hasta Beerseba, y pasen revista al pueblo, para que yo sepa el número del pueblo».
3
וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, וְיוֹסֵף יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ אֶל-הָעָם כָּהֵם וְכָהֵם מֵאָה פְעָמִים, וְעֵינֵי אֲדֹנִי-הַמֶּלֶךְ, רֹאוֹת; וַאדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ, לָמָּה חָפֵץ בַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה.
And Joab said to the king: ‘May Jehovah your God add to the people, as many as they are and as many as they are, a hundred times, and may the eyes of my lord the king see it; but why does my lord the king delight in this thing?’note
Y Joab dijo al rey: «Que Jehová tu Dios añada al pueblo, tantos como son y tantos como son, cien veces, y que lo vean los ojos de mi señor el rey; pero ¿por qué se deleita mi señor el rey en esto?».
4
וַיֶּחֱזַק דְּבַר-הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-יוֹאָב, וְעַל שָׂרֵי הֶחָיִל; וַיֵּצֵא יוֹאָב וְשָׂרֵי הַחַיִל, לִפְנֵי הַמֶּלֶךְ, לִפְקֹד אֶת-הָעָם, אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
But the king’s word prevailed against Joab and against the commanders of the army; and Joab and the commanders of the army went out from before the king to muster the people, Israel.note
Pero la palabra del rey prevaleció contra Joab y contra los comandantes del ejército; y salieron Joab y los comandantes del ejército de delante del rey, para pasar revista al pueblo, a Israel.
5
וַיַּעַבְרוּ, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן; וַיַּחֲנוּ בַעֲרוֹעֵר, יְמִין הָעִיר אֲשֶׁר בְּתוֹךְ-הַנַּחַל הַגָּד--וְאֶל-יַעְזֵר.
And they crossed the Jordan and camped at Aroer, to the right of the city that is in the middle of the wadi of Gad, and toward Jazer.note
Y cruzaron el Jordán y acamparon en Aroer, a la derecha de la ciudad que está en medio del torrente de Gad, y hacia Jazer.
6
וַיָּבֹאוּ, הַגִּלְעָדָה, וְאֶל-אֶרֶץ תַּחְתִּים, חָדְשִׁי; וַיָּבֹאוּ דָּנָה יַּעַן, וְסָבִיב אֶל-צִידוֹן.
And they came to Gilead, and to the land of Tahtim-hodshi; and they came to Dan-jaan, and around toward Sidon.note
Y llegaron a Galaad y a la tierra de Tahtim-hodsi; y llegaron a Dan-jaán, y dieron la vuelta hacia Sidón.
7
וַיָּבֹאוּ, מִבְצַר-צֹר, וְכָל-עָרֵי הַחִוִּי, וְהַכְּנַעֲנִי; וַיֵּצְאוּ אֶל-נֶגֶב יְהוּדָה, בְּאֵר שָׁבַע.
And they came to the fortress of Tyre and to all the cities of the Hivites and the Canaanites; and they went out to the Negev of Judah, to Beersheba.note
Y llegaron a la fortaleza de Tiro y a todas las ciudades de los heveos y de los cananeos; y salieron al Néguev de Judá, a Beerseba.
8
וַיָּשֻׁטוּ, בְּכָל-הָאָרֶץ; וַיָּבֹאוּ מִקְצֵה תִשְׁעָה חֳדָשִׁים, וְעֶשְׂרִים יוֹם--יְרוּשָׁלִָם.
And they went to and fro through all the land, and came to Jerusalem at the end of nine months and twenty days.note
Y recorrieron toda la tierra, y llegaron a Jerusalén al cabo de nueve meses y veinte días.
9
וַיִּתֵּן יוֹאָב אֶת-מִסְפַּר מִפְקַד-הָעָם, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ; וַתְּהִי יִשְׂרָאֵל שְׁמֹנֶה מֵאוֹת אֶלֶף אִישׁ-חַיִל, שֹׁלֵף חֶרֶב, וְאִישׁ יְהוּדָה, חֲמֵשׁ-מֵאוֹת אֶלֶף אִישׁ.
And Joab gave the sum of the muster of the people to the king: Israel was eight hundred thousand men of valor who drew the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand men.note
Y Joab dio al rey el total del recuento del pueblo: Israel eran ochocientos mil hombres de valor que sacaban la espada, y los hombres de Judá, quinientos mil hombres.
10
וַיַּךְ לֵב-דָּוִד אֹתוֹ, אַחֲרֵי-כֵן סָפַר אֶת-הָעָם;  {פ} וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-יְהוָה, חָטָאתִי מְאֹד אֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתִי, וְעַתָּה יְהוָה הַעֲבֶר-נָא אֶת-עֲוֺן עַבְדְּךָ, כִּי נִסְכַּלְתִּי מְאֹד.
And David’s heart struck him after he had counted the people. And David said to Jehovah: ‘I have sinned greatly in what I have done. And now, Jehovah, please take away the iniquity of your servant, for I have acted very foolishly.’note
Y el corazón de David lo golpeó después de haber contado al pueblo. Y dijo David a Jehová: «He pecado gravemente en lo que he hecho. Y ahora, Jehová, quita, por favor, la iniquidad de tu siervo, porque he obrado muy neciamente».
11
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד, בַּבֹּקֶר;  {פ} וּדְבַר-יְהוָה, הָיָה אֶל-גָּד הַנָּבִיא, חֹזֵה דָוִד, לֵאמֹר.
And David rose in the morning; and the word of Jehovah had come to Gad the prophet, David’s seer, saying:note
Y se levantó David por la mañana; y la palabra de Jehová había venido al profeta Gad, vidente de David, diciendo:
12
הָלוֹךְ וְדִבַּרְתָּ אֶל-דָּוִד, כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה--שָׁלֹשׁ, אָנֹכִי נוֹטֵל עָלֶיךָ:  בְּחַר-לְךָ אַחַת-מֵהֶם, וְאֶעֱשֶׂה-לָּךְ.
‘Go, and speak to David: Thus says Jehovah: Three things I lay upon you; choose for yourself one of them, and I will do it to you.’note
«Ve, y habla a David: Así dice Jehová: Tres cosas pongo sobre ti; escoge para ti una de ellas, y yo te la haré».
13
וַיָּבֹא-גָד אֶל-דָּוִד, וַיַּגֶּד-לוֹ; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ הֲתָבוֹא לְךָ שֶׁבַע-שָׁנִים רָעָב בְּאַרְצֶךָ אִם-שְׁלֹשָׁה חֳדָשִׁים נֻסְךָ לִפְנֵי-צָרֶיךָ וְהוּא רֹדְפֶךָ, וְאִם-הֱיוֹת שְׁלֹשֶׁת יָמִים דֶּבֶר בְּאַרְצֶךָ--עַתָּה דַּע וּרְאֵה, מָה-אָשִׁיב שֹׁלְחִי דָּבָר.  {ס}
And Gad came to David and told him, and said to him: ‘Shall seven years of famine come to you in your land? Or three months of your fleeing before your adversaries while he pursues you? Or shall there be three days of pestilence in your land? Now know, and see what word I shall bring back to the one who sent me.’note
Y vino Gad a David, y se lo hizo saber, y le dijo: «¿Vendrán a ti siete años de hambre en tu tierra? ¿O tres meses de huir tú delante de tus adversarios mientras él te persigue? ¿O habrá tres días de peste en tu tierra? Ahora sabe y mira qué palabra he de llevar de vuelta al que me envió».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-גָּד, צַר-לִי מְאֹד; נִפְּלָה-נָּא בְיַד-יְהוָה כִּי-רַבִּים רַחֲמָו, וּבְיַד-אָדָם אַל-אֶפֹּלָה.
And David said to Gad: ‘I am in great distress. Let us fall, please, into the hand of Jehovah, for his mercies are many; but into the hand of man let me not fall.’note
Y David dijo a Gad: «Estoy en gran angustia. Caigamos, por favor, en la mano de Jehová, porque sus misericordias son muchas; pero en la mano del hombre no caiga yo».
15
וַיִּתֵּן יְהוָה דֶּבֶר בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, מֵהַבֹּקֶר וְעַד-עֵת מוֹעֵד; וַיָּמָת מִן-הָעָם, מִדָּן וְעַד-בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע, שִׁבְעִים אֶלֶף, אִישׁ.
And Jehovah gave a pestilence in Israel from the morning until the appointed time; and of the people, from Dan to Beersheba, seventy thousand men died.note
Y Jehová dio una peste en Israel desde la mañana hasta el tiempo señalado; y murieron del pueblo, desde Dan hasta Beerseba, setenta mil hombres.
16
וַיִּשְׁלַח יָדוֹ הַמַּלְאָךְ יְרוּשָׁלִַם, לְשַׁחֲתָהּ, וַיִּנָּחֶם יְהוָה אֶל-הָרָעָה, וַיֹּאמֶר לַמַּלְאָךְ הַמַּשְׁחִית בָּעָם רַב עַתָּה הֶרֶף יָדֶךָ; וּמַלְאַךְ יְהוָה הָיָה, עִם-גֹּרֶן האורנה הָאֲרַוְנָה הַיְבֻסִי.  {ס}
And the angel put out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, and Jehovah relented of the evil; and he said to the angel who was destroying among the people: ‘Enough! Now let your hand drop.’ And the angel of Jehovah was by the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.note
Y el ángel extendió su mano hacia Jerusalén para destruirla, y Jehová se arrepintió del mal; y dijo al ángel que destruía entre el pueblo: «¡Basta! Ahora deja caer tu mano». Y el ángel de Jehová estaba junto a la era de Arauna el jebuseo.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-יְהוָה בִּרְאֹתוֹ אֶת-הַמַּלְאָךְ הַמַּכֶּה בָעָם, וַיֹּאמֶר הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי חָטָאתִי וְאָנֹכִי הֶעֱוֵיתִי, וְאֵלֶּה הַצֹּאן, מֶה עָשׂוּ:  תְּהִי נָא יָדְךָ בִּי, וּבְבֵית אָבִי.  {פ}
And David said to Jehovah, when he saw the angel who was striking the people, and he said: ‘Look — I have sinned, and I have done iniquity; but these sheep, what have they done? Let your hand be, please, against me and against the house of my father.’note
Y David dijo a Jehová, cuando vio al ángel que hería al pueblo, y dijo: «Mira, yo he pecado, y yo he hecho iniquidad; pero estas ovejas, ¿qué han hecho? Que tu mano sea, por favor, contra mí y contra la casa de mi padre».
18
וַיָּבֹא-גָד אֶל-דָּוִד, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא; וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, עֲלֵה הָקֵם לַיהוָה מִזְבֵּחַ, בְּגֹרֶן, ארניה אֲרַוְנָה הַיְבֻסִי.
And Gad came to David on that day and said to him: ‘Go up, raise an altar to Jehovah on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite.’note
Y vino Gad a David aquel día y le dijo: «Sube, levanta un altar a Jehová en la era de Arauna el jebuseo».
19
וַיַּעַל דָּוִד כִּדְבַר-גָּד, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוָּה יְהוָה.
And David went up according to the word of Gad, as Jehovah had commanded.note
Y subió David conforme a la palabra de Gad, como Jehová había mandado.
20
וַיַּשְׁקֵף אֲרַוְנָה, וַיַּרְא אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ וְאֶת-עֲבָדָיו, עֹבְרִים, עָלָיו; וַיֵּצֵא אֲרַוְנָה, וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ לַמֶּלֶךְ אַפָּיו אָרְצָה.
And Araunah looked down, and saw the king and his servants coming over toward him; and Araunah went out and bowed down to the king, his face to the ground.note
Y Arauna miró hacia abajo, y vio al rey y a sus siervos que venían hacia él; y salió Arauna y se postró ante el rey, rostro a tierra.
21
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲרַוְנָה, מַדּוּעַ בָּא אֲדֹנִי-הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-עַבְדּוֹ; וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד לִקְנוֹת מֵעִמְּךָ אֶת-הַגֹּרֶן, לִבְנוֹת מִזְבֵּחַ לַיהוָה, וְתֵעָצַר הַמַּגֵּפָה, מֵעַל הָעָם.
And Araunah said: ‘Why has my lord the king come to his servant?’ And David said: ‘To buy the threshing floor from you, to build an altar to Jehovah, that the plague may be checked from upon the people.’note
Y Arauna dijo: «¿Por qué ha venido mi señor el rey a su siervo?». Y David dijo: «Para comprarte la era, para edificar un altar a Jehová, a fin de que se detenga la plaga de sobre el pueblo».
22
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲרַוְנָה אֶל-דָּוִד, יִקַּח וְיַעַל אֲדֹנִי הַמֶּלֶךְ הַטּוֹב בְּעֵינָו; רְאֵה הַבָּקָר לָעֹלָה, וְהַמֹּרִגִּים וּכְלֵי הַבָּקָר לָעֵצִים.
And Araunah said to David: ‘Let my lord the king take it and offer up what is good in his eyes. Look — the oxen for the burnt offering, and the threshing sledges and the equipment of the oxen for the wood.’note
Y Arauna dijo a David: «Tome y ofrezca mi señor el rey lo que es bueno a sus ojos. Mira, los bueyes para el holocausto, y los trillos y el equipo de los bueyes para la leña».
23
הַכֹּל, נָתַן אֲרַוְנָה הַמֶּלֶךְ--לַמֶּלֶךְ;  {ס}  וַיֹּאמֶר אֲרַוְנָה אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ יִרְצֶךָ.
All of it Araunah the king gave to the king. And Araunah said to the king: ‘May Jehovah your God accept you.’note
Todo esto Arauna el rey lo dio al rey. Y Arauna dijo al rey: «Jehová tu Dios te acepte».
24
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-אֲרַוְנָה, לֹא כִּי-קָנוֹ אֶקְנֶה מֵאוֹתְךָ בִּמְחִיר, וְלֹא אַעֲלֶה לַיהוָה אֱלֹהַי, עֹלוֹת חִנָּם; וַיִּקֶן דָּוִד אֶת-הַגֹּרֶן וְאֶת-הַבָּקָר, בְּכֶסֶף שְׁקָלִים חֲמִשִּׁים.
And the king said to Araunah: ‘No, but I will surely buy it from you for a price; and I will not offer up to Jehovah my God burnt offerings for nothing.’ And David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver.note
Y el rey dijo a Arauna: «No, sino que ciertamente te lo compraré por un precio; y no ofreceré a Jehová mi Dios holocaustos gratis». Y David compró la era y los bueyes por cincuenta siclos de plata.
25
וַיִּבֶן שָׁם דָּוִד מִזְבֵּחַ לַיהוָה, וַיַּעַל עֹלוֹת וּשְׁלָמִים; וַיֵּעָתֵר יְהוָה לָאָרֶץ, וַתֵּעָצַר הַמַּגֵּפָה מֵעַל יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {ש}
And David built there an altar to Jehovah, and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings. And Jehovah was entreated for the land, and the plague was checked from upon Israel.note
Y edificó allí David un altar a Jehová, y ofreció holocaustos y ofrendas de paz. Y Jehová fue rogado por la tierra, y se detuvo la plaga de sobre Israel.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 2 Samuel 24 The census — “he incited David”, three words for counting, and Joab’s “why does my lord the king delight in this thing?”

Again, and who incited. “And again the anger of Jehovah burned against Israel, and he incited David against them”: the Hebrew says that the anger “added to burn”, vayyosef af-Yehovah lacharot, and gives no reason for it. The ASV has “And again the anger of Jehovah was kindled against Israel, and he moved David against them”. The NIV has “Again the anger of the Lord burned against Israel, and he incited David against them”. The NWT 1984 has “when one incited David against them”. The Douay has “and stirred up David among them”. “And he incited David” stands only here; no new subject is named, and the verb is the one David used to Saul, “If it is Jehovah who has incited you against me” (1 Samuel 26:19, now on these pages; see sut). Chronicles gives the inciting to Satan (1 Chronicles 21:1). The page keeps the Hebrew’s silence about who.

Number, muster, count. There are three verbs. “Go, number Israel and Judah” (mene, verse 1); “muster the people” (paqad, verses 2 and 4; see paqad); and “after he had counted the people” (safar, verse 10). The word “census” is not in the Hebrew. The ASV has “number” for all of the first two. The NIV has “take a census” in verse 1 and “enroll the fighting men” in verse 2. The NWT 1984 has “take a count” in verse 1 and “register the people” in verse 2. The TLB has “taking a national census”. Exodus 30:12 ties the verb paqad to a plague: “when you muster them … that there be no plague among them” (Exodus 30:12, now on these pages); this chapter does not cite it.

To and fro, from Dan to Beersheba. The king’s order is “Go to and fro, please, through all the tribes of Israel, from Dan to Beersheba”: “from Dan to Beersheba” stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible. Four of them are in this book: 3:10, 17:11, and verses 2 and 15 of this chapter. The verb for “go to and fro” is shut, and it comes back in verse 8, “and they went to and fro through all the land”. The NWT 1984 keeps one verb for both, “Move about, please, through all the tribes of Israel” and “Thus they went moving about through all the land”. The ASV has “Go now to and fro through all the tribes of Israel” and “had gone to and from through all the land”. The KJV has “Go now through all the tribes” and “had gone through all the land”.

Joab’s question. “May Jehovah your God add to the people, as many as they are and as many as they are, a hundred times”: the doubled “as they and as they”, kahem vekahem, stands only here. The ASV has “how many soever they may be, a hundredfold”. The NIV has “multiply the troops a hundred times over”. The NWT 1984 has “a hundred times as many as they are”. The question is “why does my lord the king delight in this thing?”, with the verb chafets of 20:11 and 22:20 (see chafets). The NWT 1984 has “why has he found delight in this thing?”, the ASV “delight in this thing”, the Geneva “desire this thing”. Verse 4 says the king’s word “prevailed against” Joab, the Hebrew vayyechezak, “was strong”. The NIV has “overruled”.

Verses 5–9 · 2 Samuel 24 The round of the land — Aroer to Dan-jaan, the fortress of Tyre, nine months and twenty days, and the sums

Across the Jordan. “And they crossed the Jordan and camped at Aroer, to the right of the city that is in the middle of the wadi of Gad, and toward Jazer”: the city in the wadi is not named, and the Hebrew has “the Gad” with the article, haGad. The ASV has “in the middle of the valley of Gad”. The NWT 1984 has “in the middle of the torrent valley, toward the Gadʹites”. The KJV has “in the midst of the river of Gad”. The NIV has “south of the town in the gorge, and then went through Gad”. The Douay has “in the vale of Gad”. The order of the round is east first: Aroer (see Aroer), Jazer and Gilead, then north to Sidon and Tyre, then south to Beersheba.

Two names found only here. “The land of Tahtim-hodshi” and “Dan-jaan”: “Tahtim-hodshi” stands in one verse, and “Dan-jaan” in one verse; the places are not otherwise known. The ASV has “the land of Tahtim-hodshi” and “Dan-jaan”. The NIV has “the region of Tahtim Hodshi” and “Dan Jaan”. The Douay has “the lower land of Hodsi” and “the woodlands of Dan”, from the Latin. The page keeps both names as they are, and does not say where they were.

The fortress of Tyre. “And they came to the fortress of Tyre and to all the cities of the Hivites and the Canaanites”: the word is mivtsar, not metsudah (see metsudah), and “the fortress of Tyre” stands in two verses: here, and the border of Asher at Joshua 19:29 (now on these pages). The ASV has “the stronghold of Tyre”. The NIV has “the fortress of Tyre”. The NWT 1984 has “the fortress of Tyre”. The Douay has “the walls of Tyre”. They end “to the Negev of Judah, to Beersheba”, and the round takes “nine months and twenty days”, a count all seven versions on the shelf give alike.

The sums. “Israel was eight hundred thousand men of valor who drew the sword, and the men of Judah were five hundred thousand men”: the two sums are 800,000 and 500,000, and together 1,300,000. The ASV has “eight hundred thousand valiant men that drew the sword”. The NIV has “eight hundred thousand able-bodied men who could handle a sword”. The NWT 1984 has “eight hundred thousand valiant men drawing sword”. The TLB has “800,000 men of conscription age”. Chronicles has other numbers, 1,100,000 for Israel and 470,000 for Judah, and says Levi and Benjamin were not counted (1 Chronicles 21:5–6). This page does not reconcile the two.

Verses 10–14 · 2 Samuel 24 David’s heart struck him — “I have acted very foolishly”, Gad the seer, three things to choose, and “let us fall into the hand of Jehovah”

His heart struck him. “And David’s heart struck him after he had counted the people”: “and David’s heart struck him”, vayyak lev David, stands in two verses, this one and the day he cut off the corner of Saul’s robe (1 Samuel 24:5, now on these pages). The ASV has “And David’s heart smote him after that he had numbered the people”. The NIV has “David was conscience-stricken after he had counted the fighting men”. The NWT 1984 has “David’s heart began to beat him”. The TLB has “David’s conscience began to bother him”. Nothing in the verse says what moved him.

Take away; acted foolishly. “I have sinned greatly in what I have done. And now, Jehovah, please take away the iniquity of your servant”: the verb is he’evir, the one in Nathan’s “Jehovah also has taken away your sin” (2 Samuel 12:13, now on these pages; see avon). “For I have acted very foolishly” is niskalti me’od, and the form niskalti stands in two verses, this one and 1 Chronicles 21:8; the root is Samuel’s “you have acted foolishly” to Saul (1 Samuel 13:13) and Saul’s “I have acted foolishly” (26:21, both now on these pages; see sakal). The ASV has “I have done very foolishly”. The NIV has “I have done a very foolish thing”. The NWT 1984 has “I have acted very foolishly”. The TLB has “this foolish wickedness of mine”.

Gad. “And David rose in the morning; and the word of Jehovah had come to Gad the prophet, David’s seer”: “Gad the prophet” stands in two verses, this one and the one where he tells the hunted David “Do not stay in the stronghold” (1 Samuel 22:5, now on these pages; see Gad). The ASV has “when David rose up in the morning, the word of Jehovah came unto the prophet Gad”. The NIV has “Before David got up the next morning, the word of the Lord had come to Gad the prophet”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah’s word itself came to Gad the prophet, David’s visionary”. The TLB has “The next morning the word of the Lord came to the prophet Gad”. The Hebrew says David rose and the word “had come”; it does not say that it came before he rose.

Three things. “Three things I lay upon you; choose for yourself one of them, and I will do it to you”, then: “Shall seven years of famine come to you in your land? Or three months of your fleeing before your adversaries while he pursues you? Or shall there be three days of pestilence in your land?” The first is “seven years” in the Hebrew, and “three years” in the Greek of Rahlfs, as in 1 Chronicles 21:12; the NIV has “three years of famine”, and the ASV, the NWT 1984 and the KJV have “seven years”. This page keeps the Hebrew. David says “I am in great distress”, and “I am in great distress”, tsar li me’od, stands in three verses: Saul’s at En-dor (1 Samuel 28:15, now on these pages), this one, and 1 Chronicles 21:13. “Let us fall, please, into the hand of Jehovah, for his mercies are many” has rachamav (see rachamim); the Douay adds “it is better that I should fall”.

Verses 15–17 · 2 Samuel 24 The pestilence — seventy thousand from Dan to Beersheba, “Jehovah relented of the evil”, and “these sheep, what have they done?”

Jehovah gave a pestilence. “And Jehovah gave a pestilence in Israel from the morning until the appointed time”: the verb is nathan, “gave”, and the word is dever (see dever). The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah gave a pestilence in Israel from the morning until the time appointed”. The ASV has “sent a pestilence”, and the NIV “sent a plague on Israel from that morning until the end of the time designated”. The TLB has “it lasted for three days”, which the Hebrew does not say. Rahlfs’ Greek has “until the hour of the midday meal”, and says it was the days of wheat harvest. The count is “from Dan to Beersheba, seventy thousand men”; the pair of places is the same as in the king’s order in verse 2.

Relented of the evil. “And the angel put out his hand toward Jerusalem to destroy it, and Jehovah relented of the evil”: “and Jehovah relented of the evil”, with the word el, stands in three verses, this one and Jeremiah 26:13, 19 (now on these pages); Exodus 32:14 has the same verb with al (see nacham). The ASV has “Jehovah repented him of the evil”. The NIV has “the Lord relented concerning the disaster”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah began to feel regret over the calamity”. The TLB has “the Lord was sorry for what was happening”. The Douay has “the Lord had pity on the affliction”. What he says to the angel is “Enough! Now let your hand drop”; the NWT 1984 has “It is enough! Now let your hand drop”, and the ASV “It is enough; now stay thy hand”.

Araunah, written and read. “And the angel of Jehovah was by the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite”: the name is written haornah in this verse and read haaravnah, and in verse 18 it is written aranyah and read aravnah; the page prints the reading. The ASV and KJV have “Araunah”, the Douay “Areuna”. Chronicles has Ornan (1 Chronicles 21:15), and Rahlfs’ Greek has Orna. He is a Jebusite, one of the people of the city David took in 2 Samuel 5:6–9 (now on these pages; see the Jebusites and Araunah).

The sheep. “And David said to Jehovah, when he saw the angel who was striking the people, and he said: ‘Look — I have sinned, and I have done iniquity; but these sheep, what have they done?’”: “I have done iniquity”, he’evêti, stands in two verses, this one and Job 33:27. The ASV has “I have done perversely”, the KJV “I have done wickedly”, and the NWT 1984 “it is I that have done wrong”. The NIV has “I, the shepherd, have done wrong”; “the shepherd” is not in the Hebrew, and Rahlfs’ Greek has ego eimi ho poimen ekakopoiesa, “I am the shepherd who did evil”. David had been “with the sheep” (1 Samuel 16:11) and taken “from following the sheep” (2 Samuel 7:8, both now on these pages). His asking is “let your hand be, please, against me and against the house of my father”.

Verses 18–23 · 2 Samuel 24 Araunah’s threshing floor — “raise an altar”, a servant who bows, the plague checked, and “Araunah the king”

Go up. “And Gad came to David on that day and said to him: ‘Go up, raise an altar to Jehovah on the threshing floor of Araunah the Jebusite’”: the verb is haqem, “raise”, and the place is a goren, a threshing floor, like the one at which Uzzah took hold of the ark (2 Samuel 6:6, now on these pages). The ASV has “rear an altar”. The NIV has “build an altar”. The NWT 1984 has “set up for Jehovah an altar”. Verse 19 says David went up “according to the word of Gad, as Jehovah had commanded”; the NIV has “as the Lord had commanded through Gad”.

Araunah looked down. “And Araunah looked down, and saw the king and his servants coming over toward him”: “and he looked down”, vayyashqef, stands in four verses: Abraham toward Sodom (Genesis 19:28), Abimelech at the window (26:8), Jehovah toward the Egyptians (Exodus 14:24), and this one. The same root is Michal at the window, who “saw the king David” (2 Samuel 6:16, now on these pages). The ASV has “looked forth”. The NWT 1984 has “looked down and saw the king and his servants passing along toward him”. The NIV has “looked and saw the king and his officials coming toward him”. He “bowed down to the king, his face to the ground”.

Pestilence and plague. “That the plague may be checked from upon the people”: the Hebrew has two words for the one event, dever in verses 13 and 15 and maggefah in verses 21 and 25 (see maggefah). The ASV keeps two, “pestilence” and “plague”; the NWT 1984 has “pestilence” and “scourge”; the NIV has “plague” for both. The page keeps two. “And the plague was checked” stands in six verses: Aaron with the incense (Numbers 17:13), Phinehas (25:8), verses 21 and 25 of this chapter, and Psalm 106:30 and 1 Chronicles 21:22.

The oxen, the sledges, “Araunah the king”. “Look — the oxen for the burnt offering, and the threshing sledges and the equipment of the oxen for the wood”: “the threshing sledges”, hamorigim, stands only here; the singular is the morag of Isaiah 41:15 (now on these pages). Then: “All of it Araunah the king gave to the king.” The Hebrew has “the king” after Araunah, with no word “as” or “O”. The KJV has “All these things did Araunah, as a king, give unto the king”, the ASV “all this, O king, doth Araunah give unto the king”, and the NIV “Your Majesty, Araunah gives all this to the king”. Rahlfs’ Greek has no “king” after Orna. The blessing is “May Jehovah your God accept you”: the ASV has “accept thee”, the NWT 1984 “show pleasure in you”, the Douay “receive thy vow”.

Verses 24–25 · 2 Samuel 24 “Burnt offerings for nothing” — fifty shekels of silver, an altar, and Jehovah entreated for the land

For nothing. “No, but I will surely buy it from you for a price; and I will not offer up to Jehovah my God burnt offerings for nothing”: the word is chinnam (see chinnam), Laban’s “shall you serve me for nothing?” (Genesis 29:15) and the fish “we ate in Egypt for nothing” (Numbers 11:5, both now on these pages). The ASV has “which cost me nothing”. The NIV has “that cost me nothing”. The NWT 1984 has “burnt sacrifices without cost”. The TLB has “No, I will not have it as a gift”. The Douay has “holocausts free cost”.

Fifty shekels of silver. “And David bought the threshing floor and the oxen for fifty shekels of silver”: Chronicles has “six hundred shekels of gold by weight” for the place (1 Chronicles 21:25), and this page does not reconcile the two. A gift offered and a price insisted on is also the exchange at a burial: Ephron the Hittite says “the field I give you” and Abraham says “I will give the price of the field; take it from me” (Genesis 23:11, 13, now on these pages), and weighs out “four hundred shekels of silver” (23:16).

An altar, and no house. “And David built there an altar to Jehovah, and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings” (see olah and shelamim). The chapter says nothing of a house. Chronicles gives what follows: fire from heaven on the altar (1 Chronicles 21:26), “This is the house of Jehovah God” (22:1), and Solomon building “in mount Moriah … in the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite” (2 Chronicles 3:1), and the reference works connect that mountain with the land of Moriah of Genesis 22:2 (reported here, not decided). Rahlfs’ Greek adds, after the offerings, that Solomon added to the altar at the end, “for it was small at first”.

Entreated for the land. “And Jehovah was entreated for the land, and the plague was checked from upon Israel”: “and Jehovah was entreated for the land” stands only here; the earlier one has God as the subject, “after that God was entreated for the land” (2 Samuel 21:14, now on these pages; see atar). The ASV has “Jehovah was entreated for the land”. The KJV has “the LORD was intreated for the land”. The NIV has “the Lord answered his prayer in behalf of the land”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah began letting himself be entreated for the land”. The Douay has “the Lord became merciful to the land”. With it the book ends.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “he incited”, as in 1 Samuel 26:19; “number” for mene, “muster” for paqad and “count” for safar; “go to and fro” for shut in verses 2 and 8 alike; “from Dan to Beersheba”; “delight” for chafets, as in 22:20; “prevailed against”; “the fortress of Tyre”, distinct from “stronghold”; “men of valor who drew the sword”; “struck him”, as in 1 Samuel 24:5; “take away” for he’evir, as in 12:13; “acted foolishly”, as in 1 Samuel 26:21; “I am in great distress”, as in 1 Samuel 28:15; “pestilence” for dever and “plague” for maggefah; “relented of the evil”, as in Exodus 32:14; “threshing floor”; “checked” for ta’atsar, as in Numbers 17:13 and 25:8; “these sheep”; “for nothing”; and “entreated for the land”, as in 21:14; with the formulas that differ in the notes.

Echoes paid. David’s “If it is Jehovah who has incited you” to Saul (1 Samuel 26:19); the muster and the plague of Exodus 30:12; “from Dan to Beersheba” (2 Samuel 3:10; 17:11); Joab’s defiance, and the king’s delight (20:11; 22:20); the fortress of Tyre on Asher’s border (Joshua 19:29); “David’s heart struck him” at Saul’s robe (1 Samuel 24:5); Nathan’s “taken away your sin” (12:13); Samuel’s and Saul’s “acted foolishly” (1 Samuel 13:13; 26:21); Gad the prophet in the stronghold (1 Samuel 22:5); Saul’s “great distress” at En-dor (1 Samuel 28:15); Exodus’ “relented of the evil” (32:14) and Jeremiah’s (26:13, 19); the sheep and the shepherd David (1 Samuel 16:11; 2 Samuel 7:8); the Jebusites of Jerusalem (5:6–9); the threshing floor of Nacon and Uzzah (6:6); Abraham looking down toward Sodom, and Abimelech at the window (Genesis 19:28; 26:8) and Michal’s looking out (2 Samuel 6:16); the plague checked by Aaron and by Phinehas (Numbers 17:13; 25:8); Isaiah’s threshing sledge (41:15); Laban’s and Israel’s “for nothing” (Genesis 29:15; Numbers 11:5); Ephron’s gift and Abraham’s price (Genesis 23); and God entreated for the land after the burial of Saul and Jonathan (2 Samuel 21:14).

Echoes planted. The parallel account in Chronicles, Satan standing up, the other sums, three years of famine, Ornan, six hundred shekels of gold, the fire from heaven, and “the house of Jehovah God” (1 Chronicles 21:1–22:1); Solomon’s house on Mount Moriah (2 Chronicles 3:1); Job 33:27; Psalm 106:30; and Gad the seer among the writers of David’s acts (1 Chronicles 29:29). None of these is translated here yet.

2 Samuel ends here. The story continues in 1 Kings 1 (now on these pages), where King David is old, advanced in years, and covered with clothes, but cannot get warm.
