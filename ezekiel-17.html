Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · Ezekiel 17:1–2 a riddle and a parable

⭐ Riddle. Chidah is Samson’s word (Judges 14:12). The Hebrew doubles each noun with its own verb, roughly ‘riddle a riddle’ and ‘parable a parable’. Judges prints ‘riddle you a riddle’; here it is ‘put a riddle’.

⭐ Parable. Mashal is the word printed ‘proverb’ at 14:8 and 16:44. Here it is a story that stands for something else, and verses 11–15 say what.

Verses 3–6 · Ezekiel 17:3–6 the first eagle — the cedar — a low vine

⭐ The great eagle. The first nesher has ‘the’; the second, in verse 7, is ‘one great eagle’. The word covers vultures as well as eagles. Deuteronomy 28:49 pictures a coming nation by the swoop of one.

⭐ Embroidered colors. The noun (riqmah) is that of the ‘embroidered work’ of 16:10. The KJV has “divers colours”, the NWT 1984 “color variety”.

⭐ Treetop. The spelling tsammeret stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in Ezekiel: here, verse 22, and chapter 31. The Douay has “the marrow of the cedar”.

⭐ Canaan, city of traders. ‘A land of Canaan’ stands beside ‘a city of traders’, and verse 12 names Babylon. The KJV has “a land of traffick”, the NIV “a land of merchants”. Compare 16:29.

⚠ As a willow. Tsaftsafah is usually taken as a willow; it stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. ‘By many waters he took it’ is awkward in Hebrew and is left so. The Douay reads “that it might take a firm root”.

⚠ Under whom? Verse 6 says the roots were ‘under him’. That may be the eagle or the plant itself: the KJV has “under him”, the NIV “remained under it”.

⭐ Five words for growth. Branches (daliyot), limbs (baddim), sprigs (po’rot), boughs (‘anaf, verses 8 and 23) and young shoots (verses 4 and 22) are kept apart. The vine is ‘low in height’, a word that returns in verses 14 and 24.

Verses 7–10 · Ezekiel 17:7–10 the other eagle — “will it succeed?” — the east wind

⚠ One great eagle. Verse 7 says ‘one great eagle’ where English expects ‘another’. The KJV has “also another great eagle”.

⚠ Bent its roots. The verb kafnah is rare; this form stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. It is often linked with hunger: the NWT 1984 has “stretched its roots hungrily toward him”, the KJV “did bend her roots toward him”.

⭐ Already well planted. Verse 8 says the vine had a good field and many waters before it turned. It was to ‘make boughs’ and ‘carry fruit’; verse 23 says the cedar will ‘carry boughs’ and ‘make fruit’, the two verbs changed over.

⭐ Will it succeed? The question was raised of charred wood at 15:4, with the verb of tsalach. It comes three times here, at verses 9, 10 and 15. Verse 9 has no question particle; verse 10 has one.

⚠ Strip off. The form yeqoses stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The KJV has “cut off the fruit”, the NIV “stripped of its fruit”, the NWT 1984 “make its very fruit scaly”.

⭐ East wind. The wind that scorches Pharaoh’s ears of grain (Genesis 41:6). The Douay has “burning wind”.

⭐ Beds. The full spelling of ‘beds’ stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: verse 7 and Song of Solomon 6:2; verse 10 writes it shorter. ‘Planted’ in verse 7 is also another root from that of verses 8, 10, 22 and 23.

Verses 11–15 · Ezekiel 17:11–15 what these things are

⭐ The explanation. The first eagle is the king of Babylon, the treetop Jerusalem’s king and princes, the seed one of the royal line. The second eagle is not named, but verse 15 sends messengers to Egypt.

⚠ No names. The chapter names no king. Jeremiah 24:1 tells of Jeconiah carried to Babylon, and 2 Kings 24:15–17 of another set on the throne in his place. The TLB writes the names Nebuchadnezzar and Zedekiah into the text.

⭐ Leading men. The phrase ‘eile ha’arets, as read aloud, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here and 2 Kings 24:15. The NWT 1984 has “the foremost men of the land”.

⚠ That it might stand. The end of verse 14 does not say whether ‘it’ that stands is the kingdom or the covenant.

⭐ The rebellious house. As at 12:2. The ‘you’ of verse 12 is plural.

Verses 16–21 · Ezekiel 17:16–21 the oath — “my net”

⭐ His oath, my oath. Verse 16 calls it the king of Babylon’s oath and covenant; verse 19 has Jehovah call it ‘my oath’ and ‘my covenant’. The text sets the two side by side without comment. ‘Despised the oath by breaking the covenant’ (verse 18) is the charge of 16:59.

⭐ Given his hand. A pledge by the hand; the verse does not describe it further.

⚠ Pharaoh. ‘Will not act for him’ is short in Hebrew. The KJV has “make for him”, the NIV “of no help to him”, the NWT 1984 “make him effective in the war”. Who heaps the ramp is not said. ‘A large assembly’ is the phrase printed ‘great congregation’ at Psalm 40:10. Ramp and siege wall are the pair of 4:2.

⭐ Net and snare. Verse 20 repeats the words of 12:13.

⚠ Fugitives. The word is mivrachav, from the verb ‘flee’. Some read ‘chosen men’: the NIV has “choice troops”; the KJV has “fugitives”.

⭐ Spread. ‘Spread to every wind’ uses the verb of ‘I will spread my net’ in verse 20, not the ‘scatter’ of 12:14. The NWT 1984 has “spread abroad”, the KJV “scattered”.

Verses 22–24 · Ezekiel 17:22–24 a tender shoot — the cedar — the trees of the field

⭐ I myself. ‘I’ is added to the verbs for emphasis. What the eagle did in verses 3–4, taking from the treetop, Jehovah now says he will do.

⚠ The tender one. Many readers, Jewish and Christian, have understood a coming king of David’s line. The chapter does not name him.

⭐ Every bird. The words ‘every bird, everything with wings’ stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Genesis 7:14, at the ark, and here. Birds in a great tree return at Daniel 4:9 (4:12 in English Bibles), and in the mustard-seed saying (Matthew 13:32; Mark 4:32).

⭐ High and low, green and dry. The last verse turns each pair over. ‘Dried up’ and ‘dry’ are the root of verses 9 and 10. ‘Green’ is lach, printed ‘fresh’ at Numbers 6:3; the NWT 1984 has “still-moist”.

⭐ Spoken and done. ‘I have spoken and done it’ stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, Ezekiel 22:14, 36:36 and 37:14.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “succeed” at 9, 10 and 15; “low” at 6, 14 and 24; “dry” at 9, 10 and 24; “seed” at 5 and 13; “majestic” at 8 and 23; and a separate English word for each kind of branch.

Echoes paid. ‘Will it succeed?’ from 15:4; the despised oath of 16:59; and the net and snare of 12:13.

Echoes planted. A vine and the east wind again at Ezekiel 19:12. A cedar again at Ezekiel 31:3.

Ezekiel 18, now on these pages: the proverb of the sour grapes, and whose sin each one dies for.
