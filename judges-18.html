The tribe of Dan, still without an inheritance it holds, sends five men of valour from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out the land. They lodge at Micah's house, recognize the voice of the young Levite, and ask him to inquire of God; he sends them off: 'Go in peace.' At Laish, far from Sidon, they find a people quiet and secure, and report that the land is very good. Six hundred armed men march north, camping at Mahaneh-dan behind Kiriath-jearim, and at Micah's house they take the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim and the cast image, and the priest, who goes gladly to serve a tribe instead of one man. Micah and his neighbours pursue and turn back. The Danites strike Laish with the sword, burn it, rebuild it as Dan, and set up the image, with Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Ma(n)asseh, and his sons as priests until the exile of the land, all the days the house of God was at Shiloh.
Undated within the period of the judges; the Danite migration is told again, briefly, at Joshua 19:47. Judges 18:30 says the priesthood at Dan lasted 'until the day of the exile of the land', which the verse does not date (Rashi: Sennacherib's time).

1
בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם, אֵין מֶלֶךְ בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וּבַיָּמִים הָהֵם, שֵׁבֶט הַדָּנִי מְבַקֶּשׁ-לוֹ נַחֲלָה לָשֶׁבֶת--כִּי לֹא-נָפְלָה לּוֹ עַד-הַיּוֹם הַהוּא בְּתוֹךְ-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, בְּנַחֲלָה.  {פ}
In those days there was no king in Israel; and in those days the tribe of the Danites was seeking an inheritance for itself to dwell in, for until that day no inheritance had fallen to it among the tribes of Israel.note
En aquellos días no había rey en Israel; y en aquellos días la tribu de los danitas buscaba para sí una heredad donde habitar, porque hasta aquel día no le había tocado heredad entre las tribus de Israel.
2
וַיִּשְׁלְחוּ בְנֵי-דָן מִמִּשְׁפַּחְתָּם חֲמִשָּׁה אֲנָשִׁים מִקְצוֹתָם אֲנָשִׁים בְּנֵי-חַיִל מִצָּרְעָה וּמֵאֶשְׁתָּאֹל, לְרַגֵּל אֶת-הָאָרֶץ וּלְחָקְרָהּ, וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֲלֵהֶם, לְכוּ חִקְרוּ אֶת-הָאָרֶץ; וַיָּבֹאוּ הַר-אֶפְרַיִם עַד-בֵּית מִיכָה, וַיָּלִינוּ שָׁם.
The sons of Dan sent five men of their clan, from their whole number, men of valour, from Zorah and from Eshtaol, to spy out the land and to explore it. They said to them: ‘Go, explore the land.’ They came to the hill country of Ephraim, to the house of Micah, and lodged there.note
Los hijos de Dan enviaron cinco hombres de su clan, de entre todos ellos, hombres valientes, de Zora y de Estaol, para espiar la tierra y explorarla. Les dijeron: «Vayan, exploren la tierra». Llegaron a la serranía de Efraín, a la casa de Miqueas, y pasaron allí la noche.
3
הֵמָּה, עִם-בֵּית מִיכָה, וְהֵמָּה הִכִּירוּ, אֶת-קוֹל הַנַּעַר הַלֵּוִי; וַיָּסוּרוּ שָׁם, וַיֹּאמְרוּ לוֹ מִי-הֱבִיאֲךָ הֲלֹם, וּמָה-אַתָּה עֹשֶׂה בָּזֶה, וּמַה-לְּךָ פֹה.
While they were by the house of Micah, they recognized the voice of the young man, the Levite; and they turned aside there and said to him: ‘Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place? What have you got here?’note
Estando junto a la casa de Miqueas, reconocieron la voz del joven, el levita; y se desviaron hacia allí y le dijeron: «¿Quién te trajo acá? ¿Qué haces en este lugar? ¿Qué tienes tú aquí?».
4
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם--כָּזֹה וְכָזֶה, עָשָׂה לִי מִיכָה; וַיִּשְׂכְּרֵנִי, וָאֱהִי-לוֹ לְכֹהֵן.
He said to them: ‘Thus and so Micah has done for me: he hired me, and I have become his priest.’note
Él les dijo: «Así y así ha hecho conmigo Miqueas: me contrató, y he llegado a ser su sacerdote».
5
וַיֹּאמְרוּ לוֹ, שְׁאַל-נָא בֵאלֹהִים; וְנֵדְעָה--הֲתַצְלִחַ דַּרְכֵּנוּ, אֲשֶׁר אֲנַחְנוּ הֹלְכִים עָלֶיהָ.
They said to him: ‘Ask of God, please, so that we may know whether the way we are going will succeed.’note
Le dijeron: «Consulta a Dios, por favor, para que sepamos si prosperará el camino por el que vamos».
6
וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם הַכֹּהֵן, לְכוּ לְשָׁלוֹם; נֹכַח יְהוָה, דַּרְכְּכֶם אֲשֶׁר תֵּלְכוּ-בָהּ.  {פ}
The priest said to them: ‘Go in peace. The way you are going is before Jehovah.’note
El sacerdote les dijo: «Vayan en paz. El camino por el que van está delante de Jehová».
7
וַיֵּלְכוּ חֲמֵשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים, וַיָּבֹאוּ לָיְשָׁה; וַיִּרְאוּ אֶת-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר-בְּקִרְבָּהּ יוֹשֶׁבֶת-לָבֶטַח כְּמִשְׁפַּט צִדֹנִים שֹׁקֵט וּבֹטֵחַ, וְאֵין-מַכְלִים דָּבָר בָּאָרֶץ יוֹרֵשׁ עֶצֶר, וּרְחוֹקִים הֵמָּה מִצִּידֹנִים, וְדָבָר אֵין-לָהֶם עִם-אָדָם.
The five men went on and came to Laish, and they saw the people who were in it living in security, after the manner of the Sidonians, quiet and secure, with no one in the land putting them to shame in anything, no one holding power; they were far from the Sidonians, and had no dealings with anyone.note
Los cinco hombres se fueron y llegaron a Lais, y vieron que el pueblo que había en ella vivía confiado, a la manera de los sidonios, tranquilo y confiado, sin que nadie en la tierra los avergonzara en nada, sin nadie que tuviera el poder; estaban lejos de los sidonios, y no tenían trato con nadie.
8
וַיָּבֹאוּ, אֶל-אֲחֵיהֶם, צָרְעָה, וְאֶשְׁתָּאֹל; וַיֹּאמְרוּ לָהֶם אֲחֵיהֶם, מָה אַתֶּם.
They came to their brothers at Zorah and Eshtaol, and their brothers said to them: ‘How did you fare?’note
Llegaron a sus hermanos, a Zora y Estaol, y sus hermanos les dijeron: «¿Cómo les fue?».
9
וַיֹּאמְרוּ, קוּמָה וְנַעֲלֶה עֲלֵיהֶם, כִּי רָאִינוּ אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, וְהִנֵּה טוֹבָה מְאֹד; וְאַתֶּם מַחְשִׁים--אַל-תֵּעָצְלוּ, לָלֶכֶת לָבֹא לָרֶשֶׁת אֶת-הָאָרֶץ.
They said: ‘Arise, let us go up against them! For we have seen the land, and look — it is very good. And you sit silent? Do not be slow to go, to come in and take possession of the land.note
Ellos dijeron: «¡Levántense, subamos contra ellos! Porque hemos visto la tierra, y miren —es muy buena. ¿Y ustedes se quedan callados? No sean perezosos para ir, para entrar y tomar posesión de la tierra.
10
כְּבֹאֲכֶם תָּבֹאוּ אֶל-עַם בֹּטֵחַ, וְהָאָרֶץ רַחֲבַת יָדַיִם--כִּי-נְתָנָהּ אֱלֹהִים, בְּיֶדְכֶם:  מָקוֹם אֲשֶׁר אֵין-שָׁם מַחְסוֹר, כָּל-דָּבָר אֲשֶׁר בָּאָרֶץ.
When you go, you will come to a people secure, and the land is wide on every side; for God has given it into your hand — a place where there is no lack of anything that is on the earth.’note
Cuando vayan, llegarán a un pueblo confiado, y la tierra es ancha por todos lados; porque Dios la ha entregado en su mano —un lugar donde no falta nada de lo que hay en la tierra».
11
וַיִּסְעוּ מִשָּׁם מִמִּשְׁפַּחַת הַדָּנִי, מִצָּרְעָה וּמֵאֶשְׁתָּאֹל, שֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ, חָגוּר כְּלֵי מִלְחָמָה.
From there, from the clan of the Danites, from Zorah and from Eshtaol, six hundred men set out, girded with weapons of war.note
Partieron de allí, del clan de los danitas, de Zora y de Estaol, seiscientos hombres ceñidos con armas de guerra.
12
וַיַּעֲלוּ, וַיַּחֲנוּ בְּקִרְיַת יְעָרִים--בִּיהוּדָה; עַל-כֵּן קָרְאוּ לַמָּקוֹם הַהוּא מַחֲנֵה-דָן, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה--הִנֵּה, אַחֲרֵי קִרְיַת יְעָרִים.
They went up and camped at Kiriath-jearim in Judah; that is why that place is called Mahaneh-dan to this day — look, it is behind Kiriath-jearim.note
Subieron y acamparon en Quiriat-jearim, en Judá; por eso aquel lugar se llama Mahané-dan hasta hoy —miren, está detrás de Quiriat-jearim.
13
וַיַּעַבְרוּ מִשָּׁם, הַר-אֶפְרָיִם; וַיָּבֹאוּ, עַד-בֵּית מִיכָה.
From there they passed on to the hill country of Ephraim and came to the house of Micah.note
De allí pasaron a la serranía de Efraín y llegaron a la casa de Miqueas.
14
וַיַּעֲנוּ חֲמֵשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים, הַהֹלְכִים לְרַגֵּל אֶת-הָאָרֶץ לַיִשׁ, וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֶל-אֲחֵיהֶם, הַיְדַעְתֶּם כִּי יֵשׁ בַּבָּתִּים הָאֵלֶּה אֵפוֹד וּתְרָפִים וּפֶסֶל וּמַסֵּכָה; וְעַתָּה, דְּעוּ מַה-תַּעֲשׂוּ.
Then the five men who had gone to spy out the land of Laish spoke up and said to their brothers: ‘Do you know that in these houses there are an ephod and teraphim, and a carved and cast image? Now consider what you will do.’note
Entonces los cinco hombres que habían ido a espiar la tierra de Lais tomaron la palabra y dijeron a sus hermanos: «¿Saben que en estas casas hay un efod y terafim, y una imagen tallada y fundida? Ahora piensen lo que van a hacer».
15
וַיָּסוּרוּ שָׁמָּה, וַיָּבֹאוּ אֶל-בֵּית-הַנַּעַר הַלֵּוִי בֵּית מִיכָה; וַיִּשְׁאֲלוּ-לוֹ, לְשָׁלוֹם.
They turned aside there and came to the house of the young man, the Levite, the house of Micah, and asked after his welfare.note
Se desviaron hacia allí y llegaron a la casa del joven, el levita, la casa de Miqueas, y le preguntaron por su bienestar.
16
וְשֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ, חֲגוּרִים כְּלֵי מִלְחַמְתָּם, נִצָּבִים, פֶּתַח הַשָּׁעַר--אֲשֶׁר, מִבְּנֵי-דָן.
The six hundred men girded with their weapons of war, who were of the sons of Dan, stood at the entrance of the gate.note
Los seiscientos hombres ceñidos con sus armas de guerra, que eran de los hijos de Dan, estaban a la entrada de la puerta.
17
וַיַּעֲלוּ חֲמֵשֶׁת הָאֲנָשִׁים, הַהֹלְכִים לְרַגֵּל אֶת-הָאָרֶץ--בָּאוּ שָׁמָּה, לָקְחוּ אֶת-הַפֶּסֶל וְאֶת-הָאֵפוֹד וְאֶת-הַתְּרָפִים וְאֶת-הַמַּסֵּכָה; וְהַכֹּהֵן, נִצָּב פֶּתַח הַשַּׁעַר, וְשֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת הָאִישׁ, הֶחָגוּר כְּלֵי הַמִּלְחָמָה.
The five men who had gone to spy out the land went up; they came in there and took the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim and the cast image, while the priest stood at the entrance of the gate with the six hundred men girded with weapons of war.note
Los cinco hombres que habían ido a espiar la tierra subieron; entraron allí y tomaron la imagen tallada, el efod, los terafim y la imagen fundida, mientras el sacerdote estaba a la entrada de la puerta con los seiscientos hombres ceñidos con armas de guerra.
18
וְאֵלֶּה, בָּאוּ בֵּית מִיכָה, וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת-פֶּסֶל הָאֵפוֹד, וְאֶת-הַתְּרָפִים וְאֶת-הַמַּסֵּכָה; וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵיהֶם הַכֹּהֵן, מָה אַתֶּם עֹשִׂים.
When these went into Micah’s house and took the carved image of the ephod, and the teraphim, and the cast image, the priest said to them: ‘What are you doing?’note
Cuando estos entraron en la casa de Miqueas y tomaron la imagen tallada del efod, y los terafim, y la imagen fundida, el sacerdote les dijo: «¿Qué están haciendo?».
19
וַיֹּאמְרוּ לוֹ הַחֲרֵשׁ שִׂים-יָדְךָ עַל-פִּיךָ, וְלֵךְ עִמָּנוּ, וֶהְיֵה-לָנוּ, לְאָב וּלְכֹהֵן:  הֲטוֹב הֱיוֹתְךָ כֹהֵן, לְבֵית אִישׁ אֶחָד, אוֹ הֱיוֹתְךָ כֹהֵן, לְשֵׁבֶט וּלְמִשְׁפָּחָה בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל.
They said to him: ‘Be quiet! Put your hand over your mouth and come with us, and be a father and a priest to us. Is it better for you to be priest to the house of one man, or to be priest to a tribe and a clan in Israel?’note
Ellos le dijeron: «¡Calla! Pon la mano sobre la boca y ven con nosotros, y sé para nosotros padre y sacerdote. ¿Es mejor para ti ser sacerdote de la casa de un solo hombre, o ser sacerdote de una tribu y de un clan en Israel?».
20
וַיִּיטַב, לֵב הַכֹּהֵן, וַיִּקַּח אֶת-הָאֵפוֹד, וְאֶת-הַתְּרָפִים וְאֶת-הַפָּסֶל; וַיָּבֹא, בְּקֶרֶב הָעָם.
The priest’s heart was glad; he took the ephod, the teraphim and the carved image, and went in among the people.note
Se alegró el corazón del sacerdote; tomó el efod, los terafim y la imagen tallada, y se fue en medio del pueblo.
21
וַיִּפְנוּ, וַיֵּלֵכוּ; וַיָּשִׂימוּ אֶת-הַטַּף וְאֶת-הַמִּקְנֶה, וְאֶת-הַכְּבוּדָּה--לִפְנֵיהֶם.
They turned and went, and put the little ones, the livestock and the baggage in front of them.note
Se volvieron y se fueron, y pusieron delante de ellos a los niños, el ganado y el bagaje.
22
הֵמָּה הִרְחִיקוּ, מִבֵּית מִיכָה; וְהָאֲנָשִׁים, אֲשֶׁר בַּבָּתִּים אֲשֶׁר עִם-בֵּית מִיכָה, נִזְעֲקוּ, וַיַּדְבִּיקוּ אֶת-בְּנֵי-דָן.
When they were some distance from the house of Micah, the men who were in the houses near Micah’s house were called out, and they caught up with the sons of Dan.note
Cuando ya estaban lejos de la casa de Miqueas, los hombres que estaban en las casas cercanas a la casa de Miqueas fueron convocados, y alcanzaron a los hijos de Dan.
23
וַיִּקְרְאוּ, אֶל-בְּנֵי-דָן, וַיַּסֵּבּוּ, פְּנֵיהֶם; וַיֹּאמְרוּ לְמִיכָה, מַה-לְּךָ כִּי נִזְעָקְתָּ.
They shouted to the sons of Dan, who turned their faces and said to Micah: ‘What is the matter with you, that you have been called out?’note
Gritaron a los hijos de Dan, y estos volvieron la cara y dijeron a Miqueas: «¿Qué te pasa, que has sido convocado?».
24
וַיֹּאמֶר אֶת-אֱלֹהַי אֲשֶׁר-עָשִׂיתִי לְקַחְתֶּם וְאֶת-הַכֹּהֵן, וַתֵּלְכוּ--וּמַה-לִּי עוֹד; וּמַה-זֶּה תֹּאמְרוּ אֵלַי, מַה-לָּךְ.
He said: ‘My gods that I made you have taken, and the priest, and gone away; what more do I have? And how can you say to me, “What is the matter with you?”’note
Él dijo: «Mis dioses que yo hice me los han quitado, y al sacerdote, y se van; ¿qué más me queda? ¿Y cómo me dicen: “¿Qué te pasa?”».
25
וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֵלָיו בְּנֵי-דָן, אַל-תַּשְׁמַע קוֹלְךָ עִמָּנוּ--פֶּן-יִפְגְּעוּ בָכֶם, אֲנָשִׁים מָרֵי נֶפֶשׁ, וְאָסַפְתָּה נַפְשְׁךָ, וְנֶפֶשׁ בֵּיתֶךָ.
The sons of Dan said to him: ‘Do not let your voice be heard among us, or men bitter of soul will fall upon you, and you will lose your life and the lives of your household.’note
Los hijos de Dan le dijeron: «No se oiga tu voz entre nosotros, no sea que hombres amargados de alma caigan sobre ustedes, y pierdas tu vida y la vida de tu casa».
26
וַיֵּלְכוּ בְנֵי-דָן, לְדַרְכָּם; וַיַּרְא מִיכָה, כִּי-חֲזָקִים הֵמָּה מִמֶּנּוּ, וַיִּפֶן, וַיָּשָׁב אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ.
The sons of Dan went on their way; and Micah saw that they were stronger than he, and he turned and went back to his house.note
Los hijos de Dan siguieron su camino; y Miqueas vio que eran más fuertes que él, y se volvió y regresó a su casa.
27
וְהֵמָּה לָקְחוּ אֵת אֲשֶׁר-עָשָׂה מִיכָה, וְאֶת-הַכֹּהֵן אֲשֶׁר הָיָה-לוֹ, וַיָּבֹאוּ עַל-לַיִשׁ עַל-עַם שֹׁקֵט וּבֹטֵחַ, וַיַּכּוּ אוֹתָם לְפִי-חָרֶב; וְאֶת-הָעִיר, שָׂרְפוּ בָאֵשׁ.
And they took what Micah had made, and the priest who had been his, and came upon Laish, upon a people quiet and secure, and struck them with the edge of the sword, and burned the city with fire.note
Y ellos tomaron lo que Miqueas había hecho, y al sacerdote que había sido suyo, y vinieron sobre Lais, sobre un pueblo tranquilo y confiado, y los hirieron a filo de espada, y quemaron la ciudad con fuego.
28
וְאֵין מַצִּיל כִּי רְחוֹקָה-הִיא מִצִּידוֹן, וְדָבָר אֵין-לָהֶם עִם-אָדָם, וְהִיא, בָּעֵמֶק אֲשֶׁר לְבֵית-רְחוֹב; וַיִּבְנוּ אֶת-הָעִיר, וַיֵּשְׁבוּ בָהּ.
There was no one to deliver them, for it was far from Sidon, and they had no dealings with anyone; it was in the valley that belongs to Beth-rehob. They built the city and lived in it.note
No hubo quien los librara, porque estaba lejos de Sidón, y no tenían trato con nadie; estaba en el valle que pertenece a Bet-rehob. Edificaron la ciudad y habitaron en ella.
29
וַיִּקְרְאוּ שֵׁם-הָעִיר, דָּן, בְּשֵׁם דָּן אֲבִיהֶם, אֲשֶׁר יוּלַּד לְיִשְׂרָאֵל; וְאוּלָם לַיִשׁ שֵׁם-הָעִיר, לָרִאשֹׁנָה.
They named the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father, who was born to Israel; but Laish was the name of the city before.note
Llamaron a la ciudad Dan, del nombre de Dan su padre, que le nació a Israel; pero Lais era antes el nombre de la ciudad.
30
וַיָּקִימוּ לָהֶם בְּנֵי-דָן, אֶת-הַפָּסֶל; וִיהוֹנָתָן בֶּן-גֵּרְשֹׁם בֶּן-מְנַשֶּׁה הוּא וּבָנָיו, הָיוּ כֹהֲנִים לְשֵׁבֶט הַדָּנִי, עַד-יוֹם, גְּלוֹת הָאָרֶץ.
The sons of Dan set up for themselves the carved image; and Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to the tribe of the Danites until the day of the exile of the land.note
Los hijos de Dan levantaron para sí la imagen tallada; y Jonatán hijo de Gersón, hijo de Manasés, él y sus hijos fueron sacerdotes de la tribu de los danitas hasta el día del exilio de la tierra.
31
וַיָּשִׂימוּ לָהֶם, אֶת-פֶּסֶל מִיכָה אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה, כָּל-יְמֵי הֱיוֹת בֵּית-הָאֱלֹהִים, בְּשִׁלֹה.  {פ}
They kept Micah’s carved image that he had made set up for themselves all the days that the house of God was in Shiloh.note
Mantuvieron levantada para sí la imagen tallada que Miqueas había hecho, todos los días que la casa de Dios estuvo en Siló.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · The Danites look for land — “no inheritance had fallen to it”, five spies, a voice they knew, and “Go in peace”

No inheritance. The chapter opens on the refrain of Judges 17:6 (now on these pages), “there was no king in Israel”, and on a tribe without land: “until that day no inheritance had fallen to it”. Joshua had drawn Dan a lot, the seventh (Joshua 19:40), but “the border of the sons of Dan went out from them” (19:47), and “the Amorite pressed the sons of Dan back into the hill country” (Judges 1:34), all now on these pages. The NWT 1984 has “an inheritance had not fallen to them in the midst of the tribes of Israel”; the KJV softens it, “all their inheritance had not fallen unto them”, and the TLB explains it, “for they had not yet driven out the people living in the land assigned to them”.

Five spies. The tribe sends five “men of valour” from Zorah and Eshtaol, Samson’s country (Judges 13:25, now on these pages), “to spy out the land”. The words “to spy out the land” stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: Caleb’s “Moses … sent me from Kadesh-barnea to spy out the land” (Joshua 14:7, now on these pages), and three times in this chapter (vv. 2, 14, 17). Joshua’s two spies found a house in Jericho (Joshua 2:1, now on these pages); these five find one too, Micah’s, and lodge there.

A voice they knew. “They recognized the voice of the young man, the Levite.” The chapter does not say how: they may have known him, or known his speech, as a single word had told a man’s tribe at the Jordan (Judges 12:6, now on these pages). Their three questions, “Who brought you here? What are you doing in this place? What have you got here?”, Rashi hears as God’s words to Moses at the bush, “Do not come near here” (Exodus 3:5) and “What is that in your hand?” (Exodus 4:2), both now on these pages, as though they knew him for a grandson of Moses (v. 30). His answer is a wage: “he hired me”, sakhar, the verb of Abimelech, who “hired empty and reckless men” with silver from the house of Baal-berith (Judges 9:4, now on these pages).

“Go in peace.” “Ask of God,” they say, “so that we may know whether the way we are going will succeed”: hatatsliach, from tsalach, the verb of the spirit “rushing” on Samson. The priest of the image answers in Jehovah’s name: “The way you are going is before Jehovah.” The words nokhach YHWH, “before Jehovah”, stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and they can mean that Jehovah approves the road, or only that it lies under his eye. The ASV keeps the ambiguity, “before Jehovah is your way wherein ye go”, and so does the NWT 1984, “It is before Jehovah that YOUR way is in which YOU go”; the NIV turns it into approval.

Verses 7–10 · Laish — a people “quiet and secure”, a clause of uncertain sense, and a land “very good”

Quiet and secure. At Laish the spies see a people “living in security, after the manner of the Sidonians, quiet and secure”. The pair shoqet u-voteach stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, at verse 27 when the sword falls on them, and in Isaiah 32:17, where quietness and trust for ever are the fruit of righteousness. Layish is also a poetic word for a lion (Job 4:11 and Proverbs 30:30), and Moses had said, “Dan is a lion’s cub, that leaps forth from Bashan” (Deuteronomy 33:22, now on these pages).

A hard clause. The words that follow, ve-ein makhlim davar ba-arets yoresh etser, run word for word “and none shaming a thing in the land, possessing restraint”, and their sense is uncertain. This translation reads them as a land where nothing put the people to shame and no ruler held power over them. The KJV has “there was no magistrate in the land, that might put them to shame in any thing”; the NWT 1984 “there was no oppressive conqueror that was molesting a thing in the land”; the NIV reads a different sense altogether, “since their land lacked nothing, they were prosperous”, and the Douay “being very rich”. “They had no dealings with anyone” closes the verse; there the A Greek text has “with Syria”, reading Aram for adam, two words whose Hebrew letters differ by one easily confused stroke.

“Very good.” The spies’ report is the report of Joshua and Caleb, who said “the land is very, very good” (Numbers 14:7, now on these pages), with a scolding added: “And you sit silent? Do not be slow.” The NWT 1984 has “Do not be sluggish”. “God has given it into your hand” are the words of conquest, said of a city on no list of the land promised to Israel; and “a place where there is no lack of anything” is Moses’ promised land, “in which you will lack nothing” (Deuteronomy 8:9, now on these pages). The ASV has “a place where there is no want of anything that is in the earth”.

Verses 11–13 · Six hundred men and a second Mahaneh-dan — “behind Kiriath-jearim”

Mahaneh-dan. Six hundred men set out “girded with weapons of war”, and their camp gives a place its name “to this day”: Mahaneh-dan, “the camp of Dan”. Samson’s Mahaneh-dan lay “between Zorah and Eshtaol” (Judges 13:25, now on these pages); this one is “behind Kiriath-jearim” in Judah. Whether they are one place or two the chapters do not say. “Behind” is west, since Hebrew faces east: the NIV and the NWT 1984 have “west of Kiriath-jearim”, the KJV and the ASV “behind”. Kiriath-jearim is one of the Gibeonite towns Israel swore to spare (Joshua 9:17, now on these pages).

Verses 14–21 · Taking Micah’s gods — ephod, teraphim, the carved and cast image, and a priest promoted

The list that will not stay still. The spies name the treasure: “an ephod and teraphim, and a carved and cast image”, pesel u-massekhah together as at Judges 17:3–4 (now on these pages). When the five go in, the pair comes apart: “the carved image, the ephod, the teraphim and the cast image” (v. 17), then “the carved image of the ephod, and the teraphim, and the cast image” (v. 18), and the priest carries off “the ephod, the teraphim and the carved image”, with no cast image at all (v. 20). Both Greek texts read “the carved image and the ephod” in verse 18 and add the cast image in verse 20. The KJV and the ASV have “now therefore consider what ye have to do”.

Peace at the gate. They “asked after his welfare”, sha’al le-shalom, the shalom of his own “Go in peace” (v. 6), while six hundred armed men stand at the entrance of the gate.

“A father and a priest.” “Be quiet! Put your hand over your mouth and come with us, and be a father and a priest to us.” Micah had offered him the same title (Judges 17:10, now on these pages); the Danites offer a tribe and a clan instead of “the house of one man”. The NWT 1984 has “Put your hand over your mouth”; the Douay “put thy finger on thy mouth”.

A glad heart. “The priest’s heart was glad”: vayitav lev. The phrase stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, twice in the next chapter, when another Levite’s father-in-law urges him to let his heart be glad (Judges 19:6 and 19:9, now on these pages), and of Boaz after his supper (Ruth 3:7, now on these pages). It is the root of Micah’s “Now I know that Jehovah will do me good” (Judges 17:13, now on these pages): the good comes to the priest. The KJV and the ASV have “the priest’s heart was glad”; the NWT 1984 “the heart of the priest was pleased”. The column puts “the little ones and the livestock and the baggage” in front, where a pursuit from behind cannot reach them; the KJV calls the baggage “the carriage”, in the older sense of what is carried.

Verses 22–26 · Micah’s last words — “My gods that I made”, and men “bitter of soul”

Called out. Micah’s neighbours “were called out”, niz’aqu, the verb of the muster, as when “the men of Ephraim were called out” against Jephthah (Judges 12:1, now on these pages). The Danites turn and ask, “What is the matter with you, that you have been called out?”

“My gods that I made.” Micah answers: “My gods that I made you have taken, and the priest, and gone away; what more do I have?” The man named “Who is like Jehovah?” (Judges 17:1, now on these pages) mourns gods he made with his own silver. The KJV and the ASV have “ye have taken away my gods which I made”; the NWT 1984 “My gods that I made YOU have taken”.

Bitter of soul. The Danites warn him off: “men bitter of soul will fall upon you”. The words marei nefesh stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible; one of them is Hushai’s warning that David’s men are “bitter of soul, like a bear robbed of her cubs” (2 Samuel 17:8), and the same words in the singular describe Hannah, “bitter in soul” at Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:10, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 keeps “men bitter of soul”; the KJV and the ASV have “angry fellows”. Micah “saw that they were stronger than he”, and went home; after this he appears only as the maker of the image (vv. 27 and 31).

Verses 27–29 · Laish burned and renamed Dan — “but Laish was the name of the city before”

The sword and the fire. The Danites come “upon a people quiet and secure, and struck them with the edge of the sword, and burned the city with fire”: the words of Jericho, struck “with the edge of the sword” and burned “with fire” (Joshua 6:21 and 6:24, now on these pages). Far from Sidon, Laish had no deliverer; the ASV has “And there was no deliverer”, and the NWT 1984 says they “had nothing at all to do with mankind”.

Beth-rehob. The city lay “in the valley that belongs to Beth-rehob”. Moses’ spies had gone “as far as Rehob, at Lebo-hamath” (Numbers 13:21, now on these pages). Josephus places it “not far from Mount Libanus and the springs of the lesser Jordan”, a day’s journey from Sidon, and names the new city Dana (Antiquities 5.178, numbered 5.3.1 in Whiston).

A new name. “They named the city Dan, after the name of Dan their father … but Laish was the name of the city before.” The words “the name of the city before” stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, and at Bethel, “but Luz was the name of the city before” (Genesis 28:19, now on these pages). In both places the Greek misread the Hebrew ve-ulam, “but”, as part of the old name: Genesis gets a city “Oulamlouz”, and the B Greek text here “Oulamais”. Joshua 19:47 tells the same capture under the name Leshem, and Abram had already pursued his enemies “as far as Dan” under the later name (Genesis 14:14, now on these pages).

Verses 30–31 · Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Manasseh — a hanging letter, Moses’ grandson, and Shiloh

A hanging letter. In the Masoretic text the nun of “Manasseh” is written above the line: Mnsh. Without it the letters spell Mosheh, Moses, and Gershom was Moses’ son, named “for he said, ‘I have been a sojourner in a foreign land’” (Exodus 18:3, now on these pages). The Talmud says he is called “son of Manasseh” because he acted as Manasseh, the idol-worshipping king of Judah, acted (Bava Batra 109b); Rashi, citing it, says the nun was put in out of respect for Moses and hung to show that he was really Moses’ grandson. This translation prints the name as the Hebrew does, with the letter raised. The chapter never names the young Levite of Judges 17:7 (now on these pages); the Talmud reads him and this Jonathan as one man.

Moses or Manasseh. The shelf divides. The KJV and the Geneva print “the son of Manasseh”; the ASV and the NIV print “the son of Moses”, and so do the NWT 1984, “Moses’ son”, and the Douay, “Jonathan the son of Gersam the son of Moses”; the TLB has “son of Gershom and grandson of Moses!”. The A Greek text has “son of Moses”, the B text “son of Manasseh”.

Until the exile. Jonathan’s sons were priests to the Danites “until the day of the exile of the land”; the verse does not say which exile, and Rashi puts it in the days of Sennacherib. The NWT 1984 has “until the day of the land’s being taken into exile”. Micah’s image stood “all the days that the house of God was in Shiloh”, where the congregation had set up the tent of meeting (Joshua 18:1, now on these pages): Micah’s own “house of God” (17:5) beside the one at Shiloh. The Douay closes the verse with the refrain, “In those days there was no king in Israel”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “to spy out the land” for leraggel et ha-arets, as with Caleb; “hired” for sakhar, as with Abimelech; “before Jehovah” for nokhach YHWH; “quiet and secure” for shoqet u-voteach; “behind” for achar at Kiriath-jearim; “a carved and cast image” for pesel u-massekhah when the pair is named together, and “the carved image” and “the cast image” when it is split; “a father and a priest”, as Micah said it; “the priest’s heart was glad” for vayitav lev; “called out” for niz’aq, as at Judges 12:1; “bitter of soul” for marei nefesh; “but Laish was the name of the city before”, as at Bethel; and “Manasseh” with the letter raised, as the Hebrew writes it.

Echoes paid. The refrain of Judges 17:6 opens the chapter. Micah’s “a father and a priest” (17:10) is the Danites’ offer, his “Jehovah will do me good” (17:13) becomes the priest’s glad heart, and his carved and cast image of 17:3–4 goes north. The Gershom of Exodus 2:22 and 18:3 is named, and the capture of Leshem at Joshua 19:47 is told in full. Bethel’s “the name of the city before” at Genesis 28:19 is said of Dan, and Jericho’s sword and fire at Joshua 6:21–24 fall on Laish.

Echoes planted. The glad heart returns at Judges 19:6 and 19:9, when another Levite is urged to stay and feast, now on these pages. “Bitter of soul” returns for David’s men at 2 Samuel 17:8; Jeroboam sets a golden calf at Dan at 1 Kings 12:29; and Shiloh loses the ark at 1 Samuel 4, now on these pages.

Next installment in this book: Judges 19 — another Levite of the hill country of Ephraim: his concubine leaves him for her father’s house in Bethlehem, the father-in-law keeps him feasting day after day, and on the road home they stop for the night at Gibeah of Benjamin, where no one takes them in but an old man from Ephraim — and the night that follows.
