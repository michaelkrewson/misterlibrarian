From Elim to the wilderness of Sin, where the people grumble for Egypt's food. Jehovah answers with quail and manna, a daily portion that cannot be hoarded, and — on the sixth day, before Sinai — the first commanded sabbath, a jar of manna set aside before the Testimony to be kept.
c. 1446 BC on the early-date chronology this project foregrounds — the fifteenth day of the second month after leaving Egypt (16:1), roughly a month out.

Exodus 16 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיִּסְעוּ, מֵאֵילִם, וַיָּבֹאוּ כָּל-עֲדַת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל-מִדְבַּר-סִין, אֲשֶׁר בֵּין-אֵילִם וּבֵין סִינָי--בַּחֲמִשָּׁה עָשָׂר יוֹם לַחֹדֶשׁ הַשֵּׁנִי, לְצֵאתָם מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם.
And they set out from Elim, and all the congregation of the children of Israel came to the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after their departure from the land of Egypt.note
Y partieron de Elim, y llegó toda la congregación de los hijos de Israel al desierto de Sin, que está entre Elim y Sinaí, a los quince días del segundo mes después que salieron de la tierra de Egipto.
2
וַיִּלּוֹנוּ כָּל-עֲדַת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, עַל-מֹשֶׁה וְעַל-אַהֲרֹן--בַּמִּדְבָּר.
And the whole congregation of the children of Israel grumbled against Moses and against Aaron in the wilderness;note
Y toda la congregación de los hijos de Israel murmuró contra Moisés y Aarón en el desierto;
cross-refs⤷ 17:3⤷ John 6:41
3
וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֲלֵהֶם בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִי-יִתֵּן מוּתֵנוּ בְיַד-יְהוָה בְּאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם, בְּשִׁבְתֵּנוּ עַל-סִיר הַבָּשָׂר, בְּאָכְלֵנוּ לֶחֶם לָשֹׂבַע: כִּי-הוֹצֵאתֶם אֹתָנוּ אֶל-הַמִּדְבָּר הַזֶּה, לְהָמִית אֶת-כָּל-הַקָּהָל הַזֶּה בָּרָעָב. {ס}
and the children of Israel said to them, ‘Would that we had died by the hand of Jehovah in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the pots of meat, when we ate bread to the full; for you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill this whole assembly with hunger.’note
y les decían los hijos de Israel: Ojalá hubiéramos muerto por mano de Jehová en la tierra de Egipto, cuando nos sentábamos a las ollas de carne, cuando comíamos pan hasta saciarnos; pues nos habéis sacado a este desierto para matar de hambre a toda esta multitud.
4
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, הִנְנִי מַמְטִיר לָכֶם לֶחֶם מִן-הַשָּׁמָיִם; וְיָצָא הָעָם וְלָקְטוּ דְּבַר-יוֹם בְּיוֹמוֹ, לְמַעַן אֲנַסֶּנּוּ הֲיֵלֵךְ בְּתוֹרָתִי אִם-לֹא.
Then Jehovah said to Moses, ‘Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go out and gather a day’s portion every day, that I may test them, whether they will walk in my law or not.note
Y Jehová dijo a Moisés: He aquí, yo os haré llover pan del cielo, y el pueblo saldrá y recogerá para cada día lo necesario, para que yo lo pruebe si anda en mi ley, o no.
5
וְהָיָה בַּיּוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי, וְהֵכִינוּ אֵת אֲשֶׁר-יָבִיאוּ; וְהָיָה מִשְׁנֶה, עַל אֲשֶׁר-יִלְקְטוּ יוֹם יוֹם.
And on the sixth day, when they prepare what they bring in, it shall be twice as much as they gather daily.’note
Mas al sexto día prepararán lo que han de recoger, que será el doble de lo que suelen recoger cada día.
6
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה וְאַהֲרֹן, אֶל-כָּל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל: עֶרֶב--וִידַעְתֶּם, כִּי יְהוָה הוֹצִיא אֶתְכֶם מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם.
And Moses and Aaron said to all the children of Israel, ‘At evening you shall know that Jehovah has brought you out from the land of Egypt,note
Entonces dijeron Moisés y Aarón a todos los hijos de Israel: Al atardecer sabréis que Jehová os ha sacado de la tierra de Egipto,
7
וּבֹקֶר, וּרְאִיתֶם אֶת-כְּבוֹד יְהוָה, בְּשָׁמְעוֹ אֶת-תְּלֻנֹּתֵיכֶם, עַל-יְהוָה; וְנַחְנוּ מָה, כִּי תַלִּינוּ עָלֵינוּ.
and in the morning you shall see the glory of Jehovah, because he has heard your grumblings against Jehovah — for what are we, that you grumble against us?’note
y a la mañana veréis la gloria de Jehová, porque él ha oído vuestras murmuraciones contra Jehová; porque ¿quiénes somos nosotros, para que murmuréis contra nosotros?
8
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, בְּתֵת יְהוָה לָכֶם בָּעֶרֶב בָּשָׂר לֶאֱכֹל וְלֶחֶם בַּבֹּקֶר לִשְׂבֹּעַ, בִּשְׁמֹעַ יְהוָה אֶת-תְּלֻנֹּתֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר-אַתֶּם מַלִּינִם עָלָיו; וְנַחְנוּ מָה, לֹא-עָלֵינוּ תְלֻנֹּתֵיכֶם כִּי עַל-יְהוָה.
And Moses said, ‘When Jehovah gives you in the evening flesh to eat and in the morning bread to the full, because Jehovah has heard your grumblings that you grumble against him — for what are we? Your grumblings are not against us but against Jehovah.’note
Dijo también Moisés: Jehová os dará al atardecer carne para comer, y al mañana pan hasta saciaros; porque Jehová ha oído vuestras murmuraciones con que murmuráis contra él; porque ¿quiénes somos nosotros? Vuestras murmuraciones no son contra nosotros, sino contra Jehová.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, אֶל-אַהֲרֹן, אֱמֹר אֶל-כָּל-עֲדַת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, קִרְבוּ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה: כִּי שָׁמַע, אֵת תְּלֻנֹּתֵיכֶם.
And Moses said to Aaron, ‘Say to all the congregation of the children of Israel, “Come near before Jehovah, for he has heard your grumblings.”’note
Y dijo Moisés a Aarón: Di a toda la congregación de los hijos de Israel: Acercaos a la presencia de Jehová, porque él ha oído vuestras murmuraciones.
10
וַיְהִי, כְּדַבֵּר אַהֲרֹן אֶל-כָּל-עֲדַת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיִּפְנוּ, אֶל-הַמִּדְבָּר; וְהִנֵּה כְּבוֹד יְהוָה, נִרְאָה בֶּעָנָן. {פ}
And as Aaron spoke to the whole congregation of the children of Israel, they turned toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of Jehovah appeared in the cloud.note
Y hablando Aarón a toda la congregación de los hijos de Israel, miraron hacia el desierto, y he aquí la gloria de Jehová apareció en la nube.
11
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה, אֶל-מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר.
And Jehovah spoke to Moses, saying,note
Y Jehová habló a Moisés, diciendo:
12
שָׁמַעְתִּי, אֶת-תְּלוּנֹּת בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--דַּבֵּר אֲלֵהֶם לֵאמֹר בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם תֹּאכְלוּ בָשָׂר, וּבַבֹּקֶר תִּשְׂבְּעוּ-לָחֶם; וִידַעְתֶּם, כִּי אֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם.
‘I have heard the grumblings of the children of Israel. Speak to them, saying, “Between the two evenings you shall eat flesh, and in the morning you shall be filled with bread, and you shall know that I am Jehovah your God.”’note
Yo he oído las murmuraciones de los hijos de Israel; háblales, diciendo: Al caer la tarde comeréis carne, y por la mañana os saciaréis de pan, y sabréis que yo soy Jehová vuestro Dios.
13
וַיְהִי בָעֶרֶב--וַתַּעַל הַשְּׂלָו, וַתְּכַס אֶת-הַמַּחֲנֶה; וּבַבֹּקֶר, הָיְתָה שִׁכְבַת הַטַּל, סָבִיב, לַמַּחֲנֶה.
And in the evening quail came up and covered the camp, and in the morning there was a layer of dew around the camp.note
Y aconteció que al atardecer subieron codornices que cubrieron el campamento; y a la mañana descendió rocío en derredor del campamento.
14
וַתַּעַל, שִׁכְבַת הַטָּל; וְהִנֵּה עַל-פְּנֵי הַמִּדְבָּר, דַּק מְחֻסְפָּס--דַּק כַּכְּפֹר, עַל-הָאָרֶץ.
And when the layer of dew lifted, behold, on the surface of the wilderness, a fine, flake-like thing, fine as frost on the ground.note
Y cuando aquel rocío cesó de descender, he aquí sobre la faz del desierto una cosa menuda, redonda, tan menuda como la escarcha sobre la tierra.
15
וַיִּרְאוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיֹּאמְרוּ אִישׁ אֶל-אָחִיו מָן הוּא--כִּי לֹא יָדְעוּ, מַה-הוּא; וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, אֲלֵהֶם, הוּא הַלֶּחֶם, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן יְהוָה לָכֶם לְאָכְלָה.
And when the children of Israel saw it, they said to one another, ‘What is it?’ — for they did not know what it was. And Moses said to them, ‘It is the bread that Jehovah has given you to eat.note
Y viendo aquello los hijos de Israel, se dijeron unos a otros: ¿Qué es esto?, porque no sabían qué era. Entonces Moisés les dijo: Es el pan que Jehová os da para comer.
cross-refs⤷ John 6:31
16
זֶה הַדָּבָר, אֲשֶׁר צִוָּה יְהוָה, לִקְטוּ מִמֶּנּוּ, אִישׁ לְפִי אָכְלוֹ: עֹמֶר לַגֻּלְגֹּלֶת, מִסְפַּר נַפְשֹׁתֵיכֶם--אִישׁ לַאֲשֶׁר בְּאָהֳלוֹ, תִּקָּחוּ.
This is the thing that Jehovah has commanded: gather of it, each according to his eating; an omer a head, according to the number of your persons, each of you shall take for those in his tent.’note
Esto es lo que Jehová ha mandado: Recoged de él cada uno según lo que pudiere comer; un gomer por cabeza, conforme al número de vuestras personas, tomaréis cada uno para los que están en su tienda.
17
וַיַּעֲשׂוּ-כֵן, בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיִּלְקְטוּ, הַמַּרְבֶּה וְהַמַּמְעִיט.
And the children of Israel did so, and gathered, some more, some less.note
Y los hijos de Israel lo hicieron así; y recogieron unos más, otros menos.
18
וַיָּמֹדּוּ בָעֹמֶר--וְלֹא הֶעְדִּיף הַמַּרְבֶּה, וְהַמַּמְעִיט לֹא הֶחְסִיר: אִישׁ לְפִי-אָכְלוֹ, לָקָטוּ.
And when they measured it with the omer, he who gathered much had nothing over, and he who gathered little had no lack; each gathered according to his eating.note
Y lo medían por gomer, y no sobraba al que había recogido mucho, ni faltaba al que había recogido poco; cada uno recogió conforme a lo que había de comer.
19
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, אֲלֵהֶם: אִישׁ, אַל-יוֹתֵר מִמֶּנּוּ עַד-בֹּקֶר.
And Moses said to them, ‘Let no man leave any of it until morning.’note
Y les dijo Moisés: Ninguno deje nada de ello para mañana.
20
וְלֹא-שָׁמְעוּ אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, וַיּוֹתִרוּ אֲנָשִׁים מִמֶּנּוּ עַד-בֹּקֶר, וַיָּרֻם תּוֹלָעִים, וַיִּבְאַשׁ; וַיִּקְצֹף עֲלֵהֶם, מֹשֶׁה.
But they did not listen to Moses; some left part of it until morning, and it bred worms and stank; and Moses was angry with them.note
Mas ellos no obedecieron a Moisés, sino que algunos dejaron de ello para otro día, y crió gusanos, y se pudrió; y se enojó contra ellos Moisés.
21
וַיִּלְקְטוּ אֹתוֹ בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר, אִישׁ כְּפִי אָכְלוֹ; וְחַם הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ, וְנָמָס.
And they gathered it morning by morning, each according to his eating; and when the sun grew hot, it melted.note
Y lo recogían cada mañana, cada uno según lo que había de comer; y luego que el sol calentaba, se derretía.
22
וַיְהִי בַּיּוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי, לָקְטוּ לֶחֶם מִשְׁנֶה--שְׁנֵי הָעֹמֶר, לָאֶחָד; וַיָּבֹאוּ כָּל-נְשִׂיאֵי הָעֵדָה, וַיַּגִּידוּ לְמֹשֶׁה.
And on the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for each; and all the leaders of the congregation came and told Moses.note
En el sexto día recogieron doble cantidad de pan, dos gomer para cada uno; y todos los principales de la congregación vinieron y dieron cuenta a Moisés.
23
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם, הוּא אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה--שַׁבָּתוֹן שַׁבַּת-קֹדֶשׁ לַיהוָה, מָחָר: אֵת אֲשֶׁר-תֹּאפוּ אֵפוּ, וְאֵת אֲשֶׁר-תְּבַשְּׁלוּ בַּשֵּׁלוּ, וְאֵת כָּל-הָעֹדֵף, הַנִּיחוּ לָכֶם לְמִשְׁמֶרֶת עַד-הַבֹּקֶר.
And he said to them, ‘This is what Jehovah has spoken: Tomorrow is a solemn rest, a holy sabbath to Jehovah. Bake what you will bake, and boil what you will boil, and all that is left over, put aside for yourselves to be kept until morning.’note
Y él les dijo: Esto es lo que ha dicho Jehová: Mañana es reposo, sábado consagrado a Jehová; lo que habéis de cocer, cocedlo hoy, y lo que habéis de cocinar, cocinadlo; y todo lo que os sobrare, guardadlo para mañana.
cross-refs⤷ Gen 2:3
24
וַיַּנִּיחוּ אֹתוֹ עַד-הַבֹּקֶר, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוָּה מֹשֶׁה; וְלֹא הִבְאִישׁ, וְרִמָּה לֹא-הָיְתָה בּוֹ.
And they put it aside until morning, as Moses commanded, and it did not stink, and there were no worms in it.note
Y ellos lo guardaron hasta la mañana, según que Moisés había mandado, y no se pudrió, ni hubo en él gusano.
25
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה אִכְלֻהוּ הַיּוֹם, כִּי-שַׁבָּת הַיּוֹם לַיהוָה: הַיּוֹם, לֹא תִמְצָאֻהוּ בַּשָּׂדֶה.
And Moses said, ‘Eat it today, for today is a sabbath to Jehovah; today you will not find it in the field.note
Y dijo Moisés: Comedlo hoy, porque hoy es sábado para Jehová; hoy no lo hallaréis en el campo.
26
שֵׁשֶׁת יָמִים, תִּלְקְטֻהוּ; וּבַיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי שַׁבָּת, לֹא יִהְיֶה-בּוֹ.
Six days you shall gather it, but on the seventh day is the sabbath; there will be none in it.’note
Seis días lo recogeréis; mas el séptimo día es sábado, en el cual no se hallará.
cross-refs⤷ 20:9
27
וַיְהִי בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי, יָצְאוּ מִן-הָעָם לִלְקֹט; וְלֹא, מָצָאוּ. {ס}
And on the seventh day, some of the people went out to gather, and they found none.note
Y aconteció que algunos del pueblo salieron en el séptimo día a recoger, y no hallaron.
28
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, אֶל-מֹשֶׁה: עַד-אָנָה, מֵאַנְתֶּם, לִשְׁמֹר מִצְוֺתַי, וְתוֹרֹתָי.
And Jehovah said to Moses, ‘How long do you refuse to keep my commandments and my laws?note
Y Jehová dijo a Moisés: ¿Hasta cuándo no querréis guardar mis mandamientos y mis leyes?
29
רְאוּ, כִּי-יְהוָה נָתַן לָכֶם הַשַּׁבָּת--עַל-כֵּן הוּא נֹתֵן לָכֶם בַּיּוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי, לֶחֶם יוֹמָיִם; שְׁבוּ אִישׁ תַּחְתָּיו, אַל-יֵצֵא אִישׁ מִמְּקֹמוֹ--בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי.
See — because Jehovah has given you the sabbath, therefore he gives you on the sixth day bread for two days. Remain each of you in his place; let no man go out of his place on the seventh day.’note
Mirad que Jehová os dio el sábado, y por eso en el sexto día os da pan para dos días; estése, pues, cada uno en su lugar, y nadie salga de su lugar en el séptimo día.
30
וַיִּשְׁבְּתוּ הָעָם, בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִעִי.
So the people rested on the seventh day.note
Así el pueblo reposó el séptimo día.
31
וַיִּקְרְאוּ בֵית-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-שְׁמוֹ, מָן; וְהוּא, כְּזֶרַע גַּד לָבָן, וְטַעְמוֹ, כְּצַפִּיחִת בִּדְבָשׁ.
And the house of Israel called its name Manna; it was like coriander seed, white, and its taste was like wafers with honey.note
Y la casa de Israel lo llamó Maná; y era como semilla de culantro, blanco, y su sabor como de hojuelas con miel.

וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, זֶה הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר צִוָּה יְהוָה--מְלֹא הָעֹמֶר מִמֶּנּוּ, לְמִשְׁמֶרֶת לְדֹרֹתֵיכֶם: לְמַעַן יִרְאוּ אֶת-הַלֶּחֶם, אֲשֶׁר הֶאֱכַלְתִּי אֶתְכֶם בַּמִּדְבָּר, בְּהוֹצִיאִי אֶתְכֶם, מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרָיִם.
And Moses said, ‘This is the thing that Jehovah has commanded: let an omerful of it be kept throughout your generations, so that they may see the bread with which I fed you in the wilderness when I brought you out of the land of Egypt.’note
Y dijo Moisés: Esto es lo que Jehová ha mandado: Llenarás un gomer de él para que se guarde para vuestros descendientes, a fin de que vean el pan que yo os di a comer en el desierto, cuando os saqué de la tierra de Egipto.

Translator’s Notes

A wilderness named Sin, and pots the people romanticize

⚠ The wilderness of “Sin” has nothing to do with the English word. The Hebrew Sin (סִין) is simply a place name — its origin unknown, possibly connected to the Sinai peninsula’s own name — and shares no root, sound, or meaning with the English word for wrongdoing. The coincidence is purely a translator’s accident of English spelling; readers meeting a whole chapter set in a desert of that name should not read anything into it.

The “pots of meat” (v3) is where English gets the idiom “flesh-pots” — still used today for a place of indulgent, easy living, a meaning it kept even as most speakers lost track of where it came from. The irony the text itself is making: it took six weeks of hunger for slavery to start sounding like a buffet.

A test disguised as a solution, and a root repeated nine times

“That I may TEST them” (v4) states the purpose before the provision arrives: the daily bread is not simply relief, it is an experiment in whether Israel will trust a supply that cannot be stockpiled. Six centuries later, standing on this exact chapter, a crowd who has just eaten multiplied bread quotes it back — “he gave them bread from heaven to eat” — and receives an answer built entirely around this chapter’s own vocabulary.

⚠ One root, nine times in eleven verses. Lun, TO GRUMBLE, falls at vv2, 7 (twice, once as the verb and once as the noun “grumblings”), 8 (three times), 9, and 12 — the densest concentration of one word in this book so far. Two of its four occurrences in the Hebrew text are actually spelled defectively (ketiv) and read by tradition in the fuller form (qere) used here; the scribes who preserved this text apparently found the grumbling worth flagging even at the level of spelling. Moses’s own reply (v8) makes the theological point explicit: the complaint is aimed at him and Aaron, but its real target, he insists, is Jehovah.

The shelf splits cleanly down the middle on how to render it at v2: KJV and ASV both keep the older “murmured,” while NIV and ESV both modernize to “grumbled” — this translation follows the newer half, for the same reason: nine repetitions of an archaism read as decoration, nine repetitions of a plain word read as a drumbeat.

The name that is a question

“What is it?” (v15) is not only the people’s honest confusion — it is the chapter quietly building its own etymology. Man hu, “what is it,” becomes, by v31, the thing’s actual name: MANNA. The question the people ask when they cannot identify the substance is the very word later generations will use to identify it. ⚠ KJV is the one version checked here that breaks the joke rather than keeps it — rendering v15 “It is manna: for they wist not what it was,” supplying the name three verses early instead of the question. ASV, NIV, ESV, RV60 and NVI all keep the question, letting the name arrive on schedule at v31.

An omer, and a miracle that only shows up when you measure it

The omer was a small dry-measure, roughly two liters — not a distinctive amount by itself, which is exactly the point of v18: “he who gathered much had nothing over, and he who gathered little had no lack.” Nobody sees the miracle while gathering; it only becomes visible back at the tent, at the scale, when the wildly different amounts each household scooped up all measure out even. Centuries later, arguing for a voluntary collection among churches, Paul quotes this exact verse almost word for word (2 Corinthians 8:15) — turning a story about foraged bread into an argument about economic fairness among believers who never met each other.

The instruction to leave none until morning, and the rot that follows disobedience (vv19–20), is not really about food safety. It is the first test repeated in miniature: manna gathered today cannot be banked against tomorrow’s fear, only received again.

The sabbath commanded before it is legislated

⚠ This is the sabbath’s first appearance as a COMMAND in this book’s narrative — a full four chapters before the Ten Commandments name it (20:9-11). The pattern was already there: Genesis 2 has God resting on the seventh day and making it holy (2:2-3) without ever using the word “sabbath” itself. Here, in a chapter about bread, the noun shabbat is spoken for the first time in this book, attached not to a legal code but to a practical fact: on this one day, the food will not be there to gather, so resting is not virtue, it is simply what the day allows. By the time the same rest becomes law at Sinai, the people have already lived it once, gathered twice as much and gone hungry-proof, six weeks before anyone carved it into stone.

Even the verb Moses uses to introduce it (v23) splits the shelf: KJV and ASV render it “this is that which Jehovah hath said/spoken,” while NIV and ESV tighten it to “this is what Jehovah has commanded” — a small shift from speech to instruction that matches how quickly the day stops being an announcement and starts being a rule.

A jar of bread kept where it could never be eaten

The omerful set aside “before the Testimony” (v34) means inside the ark, alongside the tablets not yet given — a detail the text states in advance of the tabernacle it presumes. Later tradition (Hebrews 9:4) lists this jar among three objects kept in the ark itself, with Aaron’s staff and the covenant tablets: a sample of daily bread, permanently preserved specifically so it could never be eaten, its whole purpose from that point on being to be looked at rather than used.

The closing note on the omer (v36, “a tenth of an ephah”) is a units conversion, nothing more — but it is worth noticing that the narrator interrupts a story to supply one, trusting a reader forty years or four centuries later to still need the measurement spelled out.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Lun rendered grumbled / grumblings throughout rather than the more archaic “murmured,” to keep its ninefold repetition (vv2, 7, 8, 9, 12) legible as one word doing one thing across eleven verses. V15’s “What is it?” kept as a genuine question rather than KJV’s pre-emptive “it is manna,” since the whole chapter is building toward v31 supplying the name the question becomes.

⚠ A note on order: translated in sequence — the road from the manna camp continues at Exodus 17.
