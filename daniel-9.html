In the first year of Darius the Mede, Daniel reads in the books the seventy years of Jeremiah and sets his face to pray: a confession in the plural, we have sinned, to the God who keeps the covenant and the steadfast love; the curse written in the law of Moses poured out; and now, hear, forgive, listen and act, for your name is called on your city. While he is still speaking Gabriel comes with understanding, and a word of seventy sevens for his people and holy city: six things to be done, a word to restore and rebuild, an anointed one cut off, the city and sanctuary destroyed, a covenant made strong for the many, and sacrifice made to cease.
The first year of Darius son of Ahasuerus, of the seed of the Medes, who was made king over the kingdom of the Chaldeans. The prayer is dated by the evening offering (verse 21). ⚠ The sevens are not dated in the chapter, and this page does not date them.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Daniel 9:1–3 the first year of Darius — the books — I set my face

⭐ The books. “I understood from the books” stands in this one verse: Daniel reads, and what he reads is a number. “For the fulfilling of the desolations of Jerusalem” stands in this one verse, and the word that came to Jeremiah is the seventy years of Jeremiah 25 and 29 (both now on these pages). The KJV has “I Daniel understood by books the number of the years”, the NIV “I, Daniel, understood from the Scriptures”, the NWT 1984 “I myself, Daniel, discerned by the books the number of the years” and the Douay “I Daniel understood by books the number of the years”. The Name stands in this verse and in six others (verses 4, 8, 10, 13, 14 and 20), eight times in all, and not once in the speech of Gabriel from verse 21 on.

⭐ I set my face. “And I set my face” stands in this one verse; what follows is the whole outfit of petition, “with fasting and sackcloth and ashes” stands in this one verse (tsom, saq, tachanun). The KJV has “I set my face unto the Lord God”, the NIV “So I turned to the Lord God and pleaded with him in prayer and petition, in fasting, and in sackcloth and ashes”, the NWT 1984 “I proceeded to set my face to Jehovah the [true] God” and the Douay “I set my face to the Lord my God”. He has read a promise of an end, and his answer is to confess, not to wait.

Verses 4–6 · Daniel 9:4–6 the great and fearful God — we have sinned, done wrong, acted wickedly, rebelled

⭐ Keeping the covenant. “Please, Lord, the great and fearful God” stands in this one verse, and “keeping the covenant and the steadfast love” stands in four verses, Deuteronomy 7:9, 1 Kings 8:23, 2 Chronicles 6:14 and this one (chesed): Daniel opens with the words Solomon used at the temple’s dedication, and the verb he is going to take back is the other half of that promise. The KJV has “keeping the covenant and mercy to them that love him”, the NIV “who keeps his covenant of love with those who love him and keep his commandments”, the NWT 1984 “keeping the covenant and the loving-kindness to those loving him” and the Douay “who keepest the covenant, and mercy to them that love thee”.

⭐ Four verbs and a fifth. “We have sinned and done wrong and acted wickedly” stands in this one verse, and then the fourth, and rebelled (marad), and the fifth, and turned aside. The KJV has “We have sinned, and have committed iniquity, and have done wickedly, and have rebelled”, the NIV “we have sinned and done wrong. We have been wicked and have rebelled”, the NWT 1984 “we have sinned and done wrong and acted wickedly and rebelled” and the Douay “We have sinned, we have committed iniquity, we have done wickedly, and have revolted”. Daniel speaks in the plural, we, though nothing in the book charges him with any of it; at verse 20 he says “confessing my sin and the sin of my people” stands in this one verse, his own sin first. The prophets they did not listen to (“we have not listened to your servants the prophets” stands in this one verse) are the ones he has just been reading: the KJV has “Neither have we hearkened unto thy servants the prophets”, the NIV “We have not listened to your servants the prophets”, the NWT 1984 “we have not listened to your servants the prophets” and the Douay “We have not hearkened to thy servants the prophets”.

Verses 7–10 · Daniel 9:7–10 to you the righteousness, to us the shame of face — the compassions and the forgivenesses

⭐ To you, to us. “To you, Lord, belongs the righteousness” stands in this one verse, and “the shame of face” stands in two verses, this one and the next, which says it again of the kings, princes and fathers. The sentence is built as two possessions: righteousness is yours, shame is ours, and then it says again, in verse 8, to us. The KJV has “O Lord, righteousness belongeth unto thee, but unto us confusion of faces, as at this day”, the NIV “Lord, you are righteous, but this day we are covered with shame”, the NWT 1984 “To you, O Jehovah, there belongs the righteousness, but to us the shame of face as at this day” and the Douay “To thee, O Lord, justice: but to us confusion of face, as at this day”. ⚠ Verses 7 and 9 have Adonai, “the Lord”, and verses 8 and 10 have the Name.

⭐ The compassions and the forgivenesses. “The compassions and the forgivenesses” stands in this one verse (rachamim, selichah), both plurals, and they are the answer to the five verbs of verse 5: the answer to rebellion is not a smaller rebellion but a larger compassion. The KJV has “To the Lord our God belong mercies and forgivenesses, though we have rebelled against him”, the NIV “The Lord our God is merciful and forgiving, even though we have rebelled against him”, the NWT 1984 “To Jehovah our God belong the mercies and the acts of forgiveness, for we have rebelled against him” and the Douay “But to thee, the Lord our God, mercy and forgiveness, for we have departed from thee”. Moses asked for the same pardon at Exodus 34:9 (now on these pages).

Verses 11–14 · Daniel 9:11–14 the curse and the oath that is written — he has established his words — Jehovah watched over the evil

⭐ It is written. “The curse and the oath … has been poured out on us” stands in this one verse, and “in the law of Moses, the servant of God” stands in this one verse; the passages Daniel has in mind are the curses of Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28 (both now on these pages). The KJV has “the curse is poured upon us, and the oath that is written in the law of Moses the servant of God”, the NIV “Therefore the curses and sworn judgments written in the Law of Moses, the servant of God, have been poured out on us”, the NWT 1984 “you poured out upon us the curse and the sworn oath that is written in the law of Moses” and the Douay “the malediction, and the curse, which is written in the book of Moses the servant of God, is fallen upon us”. ⚠ The verb in the Hebrew is feminine, agreeing with the curse; the New World Translation has “you poured out”, with God as the one who poured.

⭐ He has established his words. “And he has established his words” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “And he hath confirmed his words”, the NIV “You have fulfilled the words spoken against us”, the NWT 1984 “he proceeded to carry out his words” and the Douay “And he hath confirmed his words”. In verse 13 comes the plain confession, “we have not softened the face of Jehovah our God” stands in this one verse (chalah), and the KJV has “yet made we not our prayer before the Lord our God”, the NIV “yet we have not sought the favor of the Lord our God”, the NWT 1984 “we have not softened the face of Jehovah our God” and the Douay “we entreated not thy face, O Lord our God”.

⭐ Jehovah watched. “And Jehovah watched over the evil” stands in this one verse (shaqad, to keep awake, to watch), the verb of the almond rod and the watching of Jeremiah 1:12 (now on these pages): what he had spoken he watched over to carry out. The KJV has “Therefore hath the Lord watched upon the evil”, the NIV “The Lord did not hesitate to bring the disaster on us”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah kept alert to the calamity” and the Douay “And the Lord hath watched upon the evil”. The verdict on God is in the same verse: Jehovah our God is righteous in all his works, and they did not listen to his voice.

Verses 15–19 · Daniel 9:15–19 and now — make your face shine — hear, forgive, listen and act

⭐ And now. The prayer turns on ve’attah, “and now”, which stands at verses 15 and 17, and “and made for yourself a name, as at this day” stands in this one verse: the name God made at the Exodus is the ground of the plea. The KJV has “hast gotten thee renown, as at this day”, the NIV “who made for yourself a name that endures to this day”, the NWT 1984 “proceeded to make a name for yourself as at this day” and the Douay “and hast made thee a name as at this day”. At verse 16 comes the asking itself: the KJV has “let thine anger and thy fury be turned away from thy city Jerusalem, thy holy mountain”, the NIV “turn away your anger and your wrath from Jerusalem, your city, your holy hill” and the NWT 1984 “may your anger and your rage turn back from your city Jerusalem, your holy mountain”.

⭐ Make your face shine. “And make your face shine” stands in three verses, Psalm 80:4 and 80:8 and here; it is the request of the priestly blessing at Numbers 6:25 (now on these pages) turned into a plea, and “on your desolate sanctuary” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “cause thy face to shine upon thy sanctuary that is desolate”, the NIV “look with favor on your desolate sanctuary” and the NWT 1984 “cause your face to shine upon your sanctuary that is desolated”.

⭐ Not for our righteousness. “Open your eyes, and see” stands in this one verse, and “and the city on which your name is called” stands in this one verse. The reason given is the reverse of a claim: the KJV has “for we do not present our supplications before thee for our righteousnesses, but for thy great mercies”, the NIV “We do not make requests of you because we are righteous, but because of your great mercy”, the NWT 1984 “not according to our righteous acts are we letting our entreaties fall before you, but according to your many mercies” and the Douay “it is not for our justifications that we present our prayers before thy face, but for the multitude of thy tender mercies”. Then the three short commands, “Lord, hear! Lord, forgive! Lord, listen and act!” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “O Lord, hear; O Lord, forgive; O Lord, hearken and do”, the NIV “Lord, listen! Lord, forgive! Lord, hear and act!”, the NWT 1984 “O Jehovah, do hear. O Jehovah, do forgive. O Jehovah, do pay attention and act” and the Douay “O Lord, hear: O Lord, be appeased: hearken and do”.

Verses 20–23 · Daniel 9:20–23 while I was still speaking — Gabriel — a word went out — you are greatly desired

⭐ While I was still speaking. “While I was still speaking” stands in two verses, verses 20 and 21, the same words twice, with the prayer cut short at the very moment of his saying it. “About the time of the evening offering” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “about the time of the evening oblation”, the NIV “about the time of the evening sacrifice”, the NWT 1984 “at the time of the evening gift offering” and the Douay “at the time of the evening sacrifice”, an hour when there was no sacrifice to offer, the temple lying in ruins.

⚠ Gabriel in flight. “In swift flight” stands in this one verse, a pair of rare words that the versions read in two ways: the KJV has “being caused to fly swiftly”, the NIV “in swift flight”, the Douay “flying swiftly” and the NWT 1984 “having been made weary with tiredness”, which takes the word from ya’ef, to be weary. The man is the one of the earlier vision (“whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning”, which points to Daniel 8).

⭐ A word went out. “At the beginning of your pleadings a word went out”: the KJV has “At the beginning of thy supplications the commandment came forth”, the NIV “As soon as you began to pray, a word went out”, the NWT 1984 “At the start of your entreaties a word went forth” and the Douay “From the beginning of thy prayers the word came forth”. “For you are greatly desired” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “thou art greatly beloved”, the NIV “you are highly esteemed”, the NWT 1984 “you are someone very desirable” and the Douay “thou art a man of desires”. ⚠ The noun here is davar, “word”, and it is the same noun in verse 25, motsa davar, “the going out of a word”; whether it is the same word is read both ways, and this translation says only that the noun is the same.

Verse 24 · Daniel 9:24 sevens, seventy — six things to be done

⭐ Sevens, seventy. The Hebrew is “sevens, seventy, are cut off” stands in this one verse, the order reversed from our “seventy sevens”: shavu’im shiv’im, the same consonants twice, pointed once as “sevens” and once as “seventy”. The word for a “seven” (shavua) is the one most often translated “week”, and the verb nechtak (chatak) is “cut off” or “decreed”, a rare word. The KJV has “Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city”, the NIV “Seventy ‘sevens’ are decreed for your people and your holy city”, the NWT 1984 “There are seventy weeks that have been determined upon your people and upon your holy city” and the Douay “Seventy weeks are shortened upon thy people, and upon thy holy city”. It is not said in this verse that a seven is a week of days or of years, and this translation says “sevens”.

⭐ Six purposes. The list has six infinitives: “to finish the transgression” stands in this one verse; to make an end of sin; “and to cover iniquity” stands in this one verse (kaphar, the verb of the atonement); “righteousness of the ages” stands in this one verse; to seal vision and prophet; “and to anoint the holy of holies” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness”, the NIV “to atone for wickedness, to bring in everlasting righteousness”, the NWT 1984 “to terminate the transgression, and to finish off sin, and to make atonement for error, and to bring in righteousness for times indefinite” and the Douay “that transgression may be finished”; and at the end the KJV has “and to anoint the most Holy”, the NWT 1984 “and to anoint the Holy of Holies” and the Douay “and the saint of saints may be anointed”. ⚠ The Hebrew has a written-and-read variant in the third clause: the written text has to seal (ulachtom) and the margin reads to finish (ulehatem), and sins is written in one form and read in another; the KJV follows the reading, “to make an end of sins”. ⚠ The phrase qodesh qodashim, “holy of holies”, can name a thing (as of the altar, the tent, the inner room) or, less often, a person; it does not say “place” or “person” here.

Verses 25–27 · Daniel 9:25–27 a word to restore and rebuild — an anointed one, a leader — cut off — one seven, half a seven

⭐ The going out of a word. “Know and understand” stands in this one verse (bin, sakal): from motsa davar, “the going out of a word”, to restore and to rebuild Jerusalem, “an anointed one, a leader” stands in this one verse (mashiach, nagid). The KJV has “from the going forth of the commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks”, the NIV “From the time the word goes out to restore and rebuild Jerusalem until the Anointed One, the ruler, comes”, the NWT 1984 “to restore and to rebuild Jerusalem until” the Messiah the Leader, and the Douay “from the going forth of the word, to build up Jerusalem again, unto Christ the prince, there shall be seven weeks, and sixty-two weeks”. ⚠ In the Hebrew mashiach has no article in verse 25 or in verse 26: “an anointed one”, not “the Messiah”. ⚠ The accent after nagid separates “seven sevens” from “sixty-two sevens”, and the verb tashuv, “it shall be restored”, goes with the sixty-two, “square and moat” stands in this one verse.

⭐ Cut off, and nothing for him. “An anointed one shall be cut off, and have nothing” stands in this one verse (yikkaret, the verb of cutting a covenant, and ein lo, “not to him”): the KJV has “shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself”, the NIV “the Anointed One will be put to death and will have nothing”, the NWT 1984 “will be cut off, with nothing for himself” and the Douay “Christ shall be slain: and the people that shall deny him shall not be his”. “Desolations are decreed” stands in this one verse, and the KJV goes on “and the end thereof shall be with a flood”, with war until the end.

⭐ One seven, and the half. “And he shall make a covenant strong with the many” stands in this one verse, “he shall make sacrifice and offering cease” stands in this one verse, and “on the wing of abominations one who makes desolate” stands in this one verse (shiqquts), the line of which Daniel 11:31, 12:11 and Matthew 24:15 (all now on these pages) say “the abomination that desolates”. The KJV has “he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease”, the NIV “he will put an end to sacrifice and offering”, the NWT 1984 “he must keep [the] covenant in force for the many for one week” and “at the half of the week he will cause sacrifice and gift offering to cease”, and the Douay “he shall confirm the covenant with many, in one week: and in the half of the week the victim and the sacrifice shall fall”.

⚠ What this translation does not decide. The text does not date the sevens, name the anointed one of verse 25 and the anointed one of verse 26 (it does not say they are the same), say what the “word” of verse 25 is, or name the “he” of verse 27 as the anointed one or the leader of verse 26; readers have taken each of these several ways, and that is a reading and not something these verses say. What they do say is in the Hebrew: a number of sevens cut off for Daniel’s people and city, a word to rebuild, a cutting off, a destruction of city and sanctuary, a covenant made strong for the many for one seven, and sacrifice made to cease at its half.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “sin” for chata, “done wrong” for avah, “acted wickedly” for rasha and “rebelled” for marad in verse 5, each in its own word; “the shame of face” for boshet hapanim in verses 7 and 8; “compassions” for rachamim at verses 9 and 18; “the covenant and the steadfast love” at verse 4; “sevens” for shavu’im in verses 24–27; “an anointed one” for mashiach in verses 25 and 26; “Jehovah” for the Name, written eight times in verses 2, 4, 8, 10, 13, 14 (twice) and 20.

Echoes paid. Jeremiah 25 and 29; Deuteronomy 7:9 and 1 Kings 8:23; Exodus 34:9; Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28; Jeremiah 1:12; Numbers 6:25; Daniel 11:31 and 12:11; Matthew 24:15.

Echoes planted. 2 Chronicles 6:14; Psalm 80:4 and 80:8; Daniel 8:16.

⚠ A note on order: Daniel stands on these pages at chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 11 and 12, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Daniel 5.
