A captive girl from Israel tells the wife of Naaman, the commander of Aram’s army, who has the scaly disease, that a prophet in Samaria could gather him in. The king of Aram sends him with a letter and gifts to the king of Israel, who tears his clothes; Elisha sends word that Naaman should come to him. Elisha does not come out, and tells him through a messenger to wash in the Jordan seven times. Naaman is angry, his servants talk him round, he dips seven times and is clean, and he offers a blessing that Elisha refuses. Gehazi runs after him, takes two talents of silver and two changes of garments by a lie, and is told that Naaman’s disease will cling to him and his seed.
The chapter names no king and gives no date. Aram and Israel are at war in raiding bands (5:2), and the king of Israel is not named; the verse does not say which reign this is.

1
וְנַעֲמָן שַׂר-צְבָא מֶלֶךְ-אֲרָם הָיָה אִישׁ גָּדוֹל לִפְנֵי אֲדֹנָיו, וּנְשֻׂא פָנִים--כִּי-בוֹ נָתַן-יְהוָה תְּשׁוּעָה, לַאֲרָם; וְהָאִישׁ, הָיָה גִּבּוֹר חַיִל--מְצֹרָע.
And Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram, was a great man before his lord, and honored, for by him Jehovah had given salvation to Aram; and the man was a mighty man of valor — one with the scaly disease.note
Y Naamán, jefe del ejército del rey de Aram, era un varón grande delante de su señor, y honrado, porque por medio de él Jehová había dado salvación a Aram; y el hombre era un hombre valiente y poderoso —uno con la enfermedad escamosa.
2
וַאֲרָם יָצְאוּ גְדוּדִים, וַיִּשְׁבּוּ מֵאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל נַעֲרָה קְטַנָּה; וַתְּהִי, לִפְנֵי אֵשֶׁת נַעֲמָן.
And Aram had gone out in raiding bands, and had taken captive from the land of Israel a little girl; and she was in attendance on Naaman’s wife.note
Y Aram había salido en bandas de merodeadores, y había tomado cautiva de la tierra de Israel a una niña pequeña; y ella estaba al servicio de la mujer de Naamán.
3
וַתֹּאמֶר, אֶל-גְּבִרְתָּהּ, אַחֲלֵי אֲדֹנִי, לִפְנֵי הַנָּבִיא אֲשֶׁר בְּשֹׁמְרוֹן; אָז יֶאֱסֹף אֹתוֹ, מִצָּרַעְתּוֹ.
And she said to her mistress: “Would that my lord were before the prophet who is in Samaria! Then he would gather him in from his scaly disease.”note
Y ella dijo a su señora: «¡Ojalá mi señor estuviera delante del profeta que está en Samaria! Entonces él lo recogería de su enfermedad escamosa».
4
וַיָּבֹא, וַיַּגֵּד לַאדֹנָיו לֵאמֹר:  כָּזֹאת וְכָזֹאת דִּבְּרָה הַנַּעֲרָה, אֲשֶׁר מֵאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And he went in and told his lord, saying: “Thus and thus the girl who is from the land of Israel spoke.”note
Y él entró y se lo contó a su señor, diciendo: «Así y así habló la niña que es de la tierra de Israel».
5
וַיֹּאמֶר מֶלֶךְ-אֲרָם לֶךְ-בֹּא, וְאֶשְׁלְחָה סֵפֶר אֶל-מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיֵּלֶךְ וַיִּקַּח בְּיָדוֹ עֶשֶׂר כִּכְּרֵי-כֶסֶף, וְשֵׁשֶׁת אֲלָפִים זָהָב, וְעֶשֶׂר, חֲלִיפוֹת בְּגָדִים.
And the king of Aram said: “Go on, then, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel.” And he went, and took in his hand ten talents of silver, and six thousand of gold, and ten changes of garments.note
Y el rey de Aram dijo: «Anda, pues, y yo enviaré una carta al rey de Israel». Y fue, y tomó en su mano diez talentos de plata, y seis mil de oro, y diez mudas de ropa.
6
וַיָּבֵא הַסֵּפֶר, אֶל-מֶלֶךְ יִשְׂרָאֵל לֵאמֹר:  וְעַתָּה, כְּבוֹא הַסֵּפֶר הַזֶּה אֵלֶיךָ, הִנֵּה שָׁלַחְתִּי אֵלֶיךָ אֶת-נַעֲמָן עַבְדִּי, וַאֲסַפְתּוֹ מִצָּרַעְתּוֹ.
And he brought the letter to the king of Israel, saying: “And now, when this letter comes to you — look, I have sent Naaman my servant to you, and you shall gather him in from his scaly disease.”note
Y llevó la carta al rey de Israel, diciendo: «Y ahora, cuando te llegue esta carta —mira, te he enviado a Naamán mi siervo, y tú lo recogerás de su enfermedad escamosa».
7
וַיְהִי כִּקְרֹא מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-הַסֵּפֶר וַיִּקְרַע בְּגָדָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר הַאֱלֹהִים אָנִי לְהָמִית וּלְהַחֲיוֹת, כִּי-זֶה שֹׁלֵחַ אֵלַי, לֶאֱסֹף אִישׁ מִצָּרַעְתּוֹ:  כִּי אַךְ-דְּעוּ-נָא וּרְאוּ, כִּי-מִתְאַנֶּה הוּא לִי.
And it happened, when the king of Israel read the letter, that he tore his clothes and said: “Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man sends to me to gather a man in from his scaly disease? But know, please, and see, that he is seeking an occasion against me.”note
Y sucedió que, cuando el rey de Israel leyó la carta, rasgó sus vestidos y dijo: «¿Soy yo Dios, para matar y para dar vida, que este me envía a un hombre para que lo recoja de su enfermedad escamosa? Pero sepan, por favor, y vean, que busca un pretexto contra mí».
8
וַיְהִי כִּשְׁמֹעַ אֱלִישָׁע אִישׁ-הָאֱלֹהִים, כִּי-קָרַע מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-בְּגָדָיו, וַיִּשְׁלַח אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ לֵאמֹר, לָמָּה קָרַעְתָּ בְּגָדֶיךָ; יָבֹא-נָא אֵלַי--וְיֵדַע, כִּי יֵשׁ נָבִיא בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל.
And it happened, when Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes, that he sent to the king, saying: “Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come now to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.”note
Y sucedió que, cuando Eliseo el varón de Dios oyó que el rey de Israel había rasgado sus vestidos, envió a decir al rey: «¿Por qué has rasgado tus vestidos? Venga ahora a mí, y sabrá que hay un profeta en Israel».
9
וַיָּבֹא נַעֲמָן, בְּסוּסָו וּבְרִכְבּוֹ; וַיַּעֲמֹד פֶּתַח-הַבַּיִת, לֶאֱלִישָׁע.
And Naaman came with his horses and with his chariots, and stood at the door of the house of Elisha.note
Y vino Naamán con sus caballos y con sus carros, y se detuvo a la puerta de la casa de Eliseo.
10
וַיִּשְׁלַח אֵלָיו אֱלִישָׁע, מַלְאָךְ לֵאמֹר:  הָלוֹךְ, וְרָחַצְתָּ שֶׁבַע-פְּעָמִים בַּיַּרְדֵּן, וְיָשֹׁב בְּשָׂרְךָ לְךָ, וּטְהָר.
And Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying: “Go, and wash seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh shall come back to you, and be clean.”note
Y Eliseo le envió un mensajero, diciendo: «Ve, y lávate siete veces en el Jordán, y tu carne volverá a ti, y sé limpio».
11
וַיִּקְצֹף נַעֲמָן, וַיֵּלַךְ; וַיֹּאמֶר הִנֵּה אָמַרְתִּי אֵלַי יֵצֵא יָצוֹא, וְעָמַד וְקָרָא בְּשֵׁם-יְהוָה אֱלֹהָיו, וְהֵנִיף יָדוֹ אֶל-הַמָּקוֹם, וְאָסַף הַמְּצֹרָע.
And Naaman was angry and went away, and said: “Look, I said to myself: He will surely come out to me, and stand, and call on the name of Jehovah his God, and wave his hand over the place, and gather in the one with the scaly disease.note
Y Naamán se enojó y se fue, y dijo: «Mira, yo me decía: Saldrá sin falta a mí, y se pondrá de pie, e invocará el nombre de Jehová su Dios, y agitará su mano sobre el lugar, y recogerá al que tiene la enfermedad escamosa.
12
הֲלֹא טוֹב אבנה אֲמָנָה וּפַרְפַּר נַהֲרוֹת דַּמֶּשֶׂק, מִכֹּל מֵימֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--הֲלֹא-אֶרְחַץ בָּהֶם, וְטָהָרְתִּי; וַיִּפֶן, וַיֵּלֶךְ בְּחֵמָה.
Are not Amanah and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? May I not wash in them and be clean?” And he turned and went away in a rage.note
¿No son mejores Amaná y Farfar, los ríos de Damasco, que todas las aguas de Israel? ¿No puedo lavarme en ellos y ser limpio?». Y se volvió y se fue con furia.
13
וַיִּגְּשׁוּ עֲבָדָיו, וַיְדַבְּרוּ אֵלָיו, וַיֹּאמְרוּ אָבִי דָּבָר גָּדוֹל הַנָּבִיא דִּבֶּר אֵלֶיךָ, הֲלוֹא תַעֲשֶׂה; וְאַף כִּי-אָמַר אֵלֶיךָ, רְחַץ וּטְהָר.
And his servants came near and spoke to him and said: “My father, if the prophet had spoken a great thing to you, would you not do it? How much more, when he has said to you: Wash, and be clean?”note
Y sus siervos se acercaron y le hablaron y dijeron: «Padre mío, si el profeta te hubiera dicho una cosa grande, ¿no la harías? ¿Cuánto más cuando te ha dicho: Lávate, y sé limpio?».
14
וַיֵּרֶד, וַיִּטְבֹּל בַּיַּרְדֵּן שֶׁבַע פְּעָמִים, כִּדְבַר, אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים; וַיָּשָׁב בְּשָׂרוֹ, כִּבְשַׂר נַעַר קָטֹן--וַיִּטְהָר.
And he went down and dipped himself in the Jordan seven times, according to the word of the man of God; and his flesh came back like the flesh of a little boy — and he was clean.note
Y bajó y se sumergió siete veces en el Jordán, conforme a la palabra del varón de Dios; y su carne volvió como la carne de un niño pequeño, y quedó limpio.
15
וַיָּשָׁב אֶל-אִישׁ הָאֱלֹהִים הוּא וְכָל-מַחֲנֵהוּ, וַיָּבֹא וַיַּעֲמֹד לְפָנָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר הִנֵּה-נָא יָדַעְתִּי כִּי אֵין אֱלֹהִים בְּכָל-הָאָרֶץ, כִּי אִם-בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וְעַתָּה קַח-נָא בְרָכָה, מֵאֵת עַבְדֶּךָ.
And he returned to the man of God, he and all his camp, and came and stood before him, and said: “Look now, I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. And now, please, take a blessing from your servant.”note
Y volvió al varón de Dios, él y todo su campamento, y llegó y se puso delante de él, y dijo: «Mira ahora, sé que no hay Dios en toda la tierra sino en Israel. Y ahora, por favor, toma una bendición de tu siervo».
16
וַיֹּאמֶר, חַי-יְהוָה אֲשֶׁר-עָמַדְתִּי לְפָנָיו אִם-אֶקָּח; וַיִּפְצַר-בּוֹ לָקַחַת, וַיְמָאֵן.
And he said: “As Jehovah lives, before whom I stand, I will not take.” And he urged him to take, and he refused.note
Y él dijo: «Vive Jehová, delante de quien estoy, que no tomaré». Y le insistió que tomara, y él rehusó.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר, נַעֲמָן, וָלֹא, יֻתַּן-נָא לְעַבְדְּךָ מַשָּׂא צֶמֶד-פְּרָדִים אֲדָמָה:  כִּי לוֹא-יַעֲשֶׂה עוֹד עַבְדְּךָ עֹלָה וָזֶבַח, לֵאלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים--כִּי, אִם-לַיהוָה.
And Naaman said: “If not, please let there be given to your servant a pair of mules’ burden of earth; for your servant will no longer make a burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but to Jehovah.note
Y Naamán dijo: «Si no, por favor, que se dé a tu siervo la carga de un par de mulas de tierra; porque tu siervo ya no hará holocausto ni sacrificio a otros dioses, sino a Jehová.
18
לַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, יִסְלַח יְהוָה לְעַבְדֶּךָ--בְּבוֹא אֲדֹנִי בֵית-רִמּוֹן לְהִשְׁתַּחֲוֺת שָׁמָּה וְהוּא נִשְׁעָן עַל-יָדִי, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוֵיתִי בֵּית רִמֹּן, בְּהִשְׁתַּחֲוָיָתִי בֵּית רִמֹּן, יִסְלַח-נא    יְהוָה לְעַבְדְּךָ בַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה.
For this thing may Jehovah pardon your servant: when my lord goes into the house of Rimmon to bow down there, and he leans on my hand, and I bow down in the house of Rimmon — when I bow down in the house of Rimmon, may Jehovah pardon, please, your servant in this thing.”note
Por esta cosa perdone Jehová a tu siervo: cuando mi señor entre en la casa de Rimón para inclinarse allí, y se apoye en mi mano, y yo me incline en la casa de Rimón —cuando me incline en la casa de Rimón, perdone Jehová, por favor, a tu siervo en esta cosa».
19
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ, לֵךְ לְשָׁלוֹם; וַיֵּלֶךְ מֵאִתּוֹ, כִּבְרַת-אָרֶץ.  {ס}
And he said to him: “Go in peace.” And he went from him a stretch of land.note
Y él le dijo: «Ve en paz». Y se fue de junto a él un trecho de tierra.
20
וַיֹּאמֶר גֵּיחֲזִי, נַעַר אֱלִישָׁע אִישׁ-הָאֱלֹהִים, הִנֵּה חָשַׂךְ אֲדֹנִי אֶת-נַעֲמָן הָאֲרַמִּי הַזֶּה, מִקַּחַת מִיָּדוֹ אֵת אֲשֶׁר-הֵבִיא; חַי-יְהוָה כִּי-אִם-רַצְתִּי אַחֲרָיו, וְלָקַחְתִּי מֵאִתּוֹ מְאוּמָה.
And Gehazi, the young man of Elisha the man of God, said: “Look, my master has spared this Naaman the Aramean, not taking from his hand what he brought. As Jehovah lives, I will run after him and take something from him.”note
Y Giezi, el criado de Eliseo el varón de Dios, dijo: «Mira, mi señor ha eximido a este Naamán el arameo de tomar de su mano lo que trajo. Vive Jehová, que correré tras él y tomaré de él algo».
21
וַיִּרְדֹּף גֵּיחֲזִי, אַחֲרֵי נַעֲמָן; וַיִּרְאֶה נַעֲמָן, רָץ אַחֲרָיו, וַיִּפֹּל מֵעַל הַמֶּרְכָּבָה לִקְרָאתוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר הֲשָׁלוֹם.
And Gehazi pursued Naaman; and Naaman saw one running after him, and he got down from the chariot to meet him and said: “Is it well?”note
Y Giezi persiguió a Naamán; y Naamán vio a uno que corría tras él, y bajó del carro a su encuentro, y dijo: «¿Va bien?».
22
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁלוֹם, אֲדֹנִי שְׁלָחַנִי לֵאמֹר, הִנֵּה עַתָּה זֶה בָּאוּ אֵלַי שְׁנֵי-נְעָרִים מֵהַר אֶפְרַיִם, מִבְּנֵי הַנְּבִיאִים; תְּנָה-נָּא לָהֶם כִּכַּר-כֶּסֶף, וּשְׁתֵּי חֲלִפוֹת בְּגָדִים.
And he said: “Well. My master has sent me, saying: Look, just now there have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim two young men of the sons of the prophets; give them, please, a talent of silver and two changes of garments.”note
Y él dijo: «Bien. Mi señor me ha enviado, diciendo: Mira, ahora mismo han venido a mí desde la región montañosa de Efraín dos jóvenes de los hijos de los profetas; dales, por favor, un talento de plata y dos mudas de ropa».
23
וַיֹּאמֶר נַעֲמָן, הוֹאֵל קַח כִּכָּרָיִם; וַיִּפְרָץ-בּוֹ, וַיָּצַר כִּכְּרַיִם כֶּסֶף בִּשְׁנֵי חֲרִטִים וּשְׁתֵּי חֲלִפוֹת בְּגָדִים, וַיִּתֵּן אֶל-שְׁנֵי נְעָרָיו, וַיִּשְׂאוּ לְפָנָיו.
And Naaman said: “Be willing, take two talents.” And he pressed him, and bound two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of garments, and gave them to two of his young men, and they carried them before him.note
Y Naamán dijo: «Consiente, toma dos talentos». Y le insistió, y ató dos talentos de plata en dos bolsas, con dos mudas de ropa, y los dio a dos de sus criados, y los llevaron delante de él.
24
וַיָּבֹא, אֶל-הָעֹפֶל, וַיִּקַּח מִיָּדָם, וַיִּפְקֹד בַּבָּיִת; וַיְשַׁלַּח אֶת-הָאֲנָשִׁים, וַיֵּלֵכוּ.
And he came to the Ophel, and took them from their hand, and deposited them in the house; and he sent the men away, and they went.note
Y llegó al Ofel, y los tomó de su mano, y los depositó en la casa; y despidió a los hombres, y se fueron.

וְהוּא-בָא, וַיַּעֲמֹד אֶל-אֲדֹנָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו אֱלִישָׁע, מאן מֵאַיִן גֵּחֲזִי; וַיֹּאמֶר, לֹא-הָלַךְ עַבְדְּךָ אָנֶה וָאָנָה.

Y entró y se puso delante de su señor; y Eliseo le dijo: «¿De dónde, Giezi?». Y él dijo: «Tu siervo no fue a ninguna parte».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 2 Kings 5:1–4 Naaman, the girl who has no name, and the word that means “gather in”

⭐ “By him Jehovah had given salvation to Aram”. The verse says it plainly, of a man the same verse calls “one with the scaly disease”, and does not say how. Radak gives two older answers: the Targum, “by his hand Jehovah wrought victory for the men of Aram”, and a midrash that this is the man who drew the bow at random and killed Ahab. The Hebrew of 1 Kings 22:34 (now on these pages) says “a man drew the bow in his innocence” and gives no name. The ASV and the NIV have “victory” here, the KJV and the Douay “deliverance”, the Geneva “delivered”, and the NWT 1984 “salvation”. “Honored” stands for a Hebrew idiom, nesu panim, “lifted of face”. Rashi takes the chapter as the next in a list of the miracles done through Elisha. Josephus goes from the widow’s oil (Antiquities 9.47–50) to the Aramean ambush (9.51), and a search of his Greek for Naaman’s name and Gehazi’s finds neither.

⚠ “One with the scaly disease”. The Hebrew word is metsora, from a root that stands in verses 1, 3, 6, 7, 11 and 27 of this chapter, and mitsar’ato, “from his scaly disease”, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: verses 3, 6 and 7, all here. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has lelepromenos in verse 1 and lepra in verse 3. The ASV, the KJV, the TLB, the NWT 1984 and the Douay have “leper”, and the NIV has “leprosy”. This page keeps the scaly disease it uses in Leviticus. Leviticus 13:46 (now on these pages) says of the one who has it, “He shall live alone; his dwelling shall be outside the camp.” Nothing in this chapter keeps Naaman from anyone: his own king sends him, his wife has a servant, the king of Israel reads his letter, and a messenger comes out to him at the door. Leviticus is Israel’s law, and the verse does not say what Aram does.

The girl. She has no name. Raiding bands from Aram “had gone out” and taken her “from the land of Israel”; the Hebrew calls her a little girl, na’arah ketannah, and says she was “before” Naaman’s wife. “Before”, lifnei or lefanav, stands in seven of the chapter’s twenty-seven verses (1, 2, 3, 15, 16, 23 and 27), and her wish uses it: “Would that my lord were before the prophet who is in Samaria!” The KJV and the ASV have “with the prophet”, the NIV “see the prophet”, and the Douay “had been with the prophet”. Her word for “would that”, achalai, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, this one and Psalm 119:5 (now on these pages); the other two verses that spell the same letters, 1 Chronicles 2:31 and 11:41, have the name Ahlai. Rashi hears a prayer in it and sets beside it “and Moses entreated” (Exodus 32:11). She does not name Elisha, and she does not say what he can do.

⭐ “Gather him in from his scaly disease”. The verb is asaf, “gather”, and it stands in verses 3, 6, 7 and 11, every time for the cure. It is the verb of Numbers 12:14–15 (now on these pages), where Miriam is shut up outside the camp seven days and then “gathered back in”. Whether the Hebrew here keeps the camp in view or means only “take away” is open; this page keeps the one word, so that four verses read as one. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has the same picture in a compound, aposynaxei, “gather away”. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 have “recover him”, the NIV “cure”, the TLB and the Douay “heal”, and the Geneva “deliver”. In verse 4 the one who goes in and tells “his lord” is not named: the Hebrew says only “he”, and the Greek has “she”. The ASV and the KJV have “one went in”, and the NIV, the TLB and the Douay “Naaman”.

Verses 5–7 · 2 Kings 5:5–7 A letter that asks for nothing, and a king who reads it as a quarrel

The gifts and the letter. The king of Aram sends ten talents of silver, six thousand “of gold” and ten changes of garments, and a letter. The Hebrew has no noun after “six thousand of gold”: the ASV, the KJV, the Douay and the Geneva have “pieces”, the NIV “shekels”, and the TLB turns the gift into dollars, “$20,000 in silver, $60,000 in gold”. The letter does not ask for a cure. It says “I have sent Naaman my servant to you, and you shall gather him in from his scaly disease”: an outcome stated as if it were an order, in the verb of verse 3. Neither king is named in this chapter; 2 Kings 3:1 (now on these pages) has Jehoram son of Ahab reigning over Israel in Samaria.

⭐ “Am I God, to kill and to make alive”. The king hears the letter as a demand for what belongs to God, and says so in words Israel already has from God’s own mouth: “I put to death, and I bring to life” (Deuteronomy 32:39) and, in Hannah’s song, “Jehovah puts to death and brings to life” (1 Samuel 2:6), both now on these pages. Rashi notes only that the first letter of “Am I God” carries the vowel that marks a question. The ASV and the KJV have “Am I God, to kill and to make alive”, the NIV “Am I God? Can I kill and bring back to life?”, the TLB “Am I God, that I can kill and give life?”, and the NWT 1984 “Am I God, to put to death and to preserve alive?”

“Seeking an occasion against me”. The king ends by telling those around him to “know, please, and see”; both verbs are plural. The Hebrew verb mit’anneh, “is seeking an occasion”, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible; the only other verse that spells the same letters, Isaiah 34:4, has them as “from the fig tree”. The ASV, the KJV and the Geneva have “a quarrel”, the NIV “pick a quarrel”, the NWT 1984 “a quarrel”, and the TLB “an excuse”. The Douay has “occasions”, which is nearest. He tears his clothes, as Elisha tore his own when Elijah was taken (2 Kings 2:12, now on these pages); the next verse has Elisha hear of it.

Verses 8–14 · 2 Kings 5:8–14 Wash, and be clean — the door that does not open, the rivers of Damascus, and seven dips

⭐ “There is a prophet in Israel”. Elisha hears that the king has torn his clothes (the verse does not say who told him) and sends: “Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come now to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel.” Naaman comes “with his horses and with his chariots” and stands at the door, and Elisha does not come out; he sends “a messenger”, who is not named: “Go, and wash seven times in the Jordan, and your flesh shall come back to you, and be clean.” The last word is a command, utehar, as it is in the servants’ “Wash, and be clean” at verse 13. The ASV and the KJV have “thou shalt be clean”, the Geneva “thou shalt be cleansed”, the NIV “you will be cleansed”, and the TLB “he would be healed of every trace of his leprosy”. Mark 1:41 (now on these pages) has “be clean” from Jesus, in Greek.

Five verbs, and none of them “wash”. Naaman had a picture: Elisha “will surely come out to me, and stand, and call on the name of Jehovah his God, and wave his hand over the place, and gather in the one with the scaly disease.” That is five verbs, and Elisha is the subject of every one; what he is sent is a command with Naaman as its subject. The ASV has “wave his hand over the place”, the KJV “strike his hand over the place”, the NIV “wave his hand over the spot”, the Geneva “put his hand on the place”, the Douay “touched with his hand the place of the leprosy”, and the NWT 1984 “move his hand to and fro over the place”. Naaman already has the name, and he gives it to Elisha’s God: “Jehovah his God”.

⚠ “Amanah and Pharpar”. Ketiv and qere meet again in the Hebrew line, which prints avanah and, beside it, amanah. Radak says the name is written with a bet and read with a mem, and that the river was called both. This page prints the reading, Amanah. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has Abana and Pharphar, and all seven English versions on the shelf print the written form: the ASV, the Geneva and the NWT 1984 have “Abanah”, and the KJV, the NIV, the Douay and the TLB “Abana”. Naaman’s comparison is with “all the waters of Israel”; he does not name the Jordan, and Elisha did. The TLB has him say, “I’ll wash at home”. See Amanah and Pharpar and Damascus.

“Wash” and “dipped himself”. Elisha, Naaman and the servants all say rachats, “wash” (verses 10, 12 and 13); the narrator says tabal, “dipped”, once, at verse 14. The ASV, the KJV, the NIV and the TLB have “dipped”, the Geneva and the Douay “washed”, and the NWT 1984 “plunge”, where it has “bathe” at verse 10. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has ebaptisato, “baptized himself”, in verse 14 and lousai, “wash”, in verse 10. “Seven times” is Elisha’s word at verse 10 and the narrator’s at verse 14, in the same Hebrew words, which stand in eighteen verses of the Hebrew Bible, so the number is not what is strange. Leviticus 14:7 (now on these pages) has the priest sprinkle “the one to be cleansed from the scaly disease seven times”.

“My father”, and “the word”. The servants say: “My father, if the prophet had spoken a great thing to you, would you not do it?” They call him “my father” as Elisha cried “my father, my father” after Elijah (2 Kings 2:12, now on these pages). The Hebrew noun for “thing” is davar, and it is the noun of verse 14, “according to the word of the man of God”: the great davar he would have done is the davar he does. The ASV and the KJV have “saying” there, and the Douay and the NWT 1984 “word”. His flesh “came back like the flesh of a little boy”; na’ar qaton stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible: 1 Samuel 20:35, 1 Kings 3:7 (where Solomon calls himself, as this page prints it, “a little youth”), 1 Kings 11:17, Isaiah 11:6 and this one. The verb for “came back” is shuv, the verb of Moses’ hand: “restored like the rest of his flesh” (Exodus 4:7, now on these pages). The NIV has “restored and became clean like that of a young boy”, the TLB “as healthy as a little child’s”, and the NWT 1984 “like the flesh of a little boy”.

Verses 15–19 · 2 Kings 5:15–19 No God but in Israel — the blessing Elisha will not take, two mules’ burden of earth, and the house of Rimmon

⭐ “He returned to the man of God”. In verse 14 his flesh “came back”; in verse 15 he “returned”, in the same Hebrew verb, vayashav, first the flesh and then the man. He comes with “all his camp”, and “stood before him”: the girl’s wish at verse 3, that her lord be “before the prophet”, is now done. His word is “I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel.” The KJV and the ASV have “no God in all the earth, but in Israel”, the NIV “no God in all the world except in Israel”, and the Douay “no other God in all the earth, but only in Israel”. Then he calls himself “your servant”, as he does five times in verses 15 to 18.

“Take a blessing from your servant”. The Hebrew word is berakhah, “blessing”, and it is also what Jacob calls his gift to Esau: “Take, I pray, my blessing” (Genesis 33:11, now on these pages). The KJV and the Douay have “a blessing”, the ASV “a present”, the NIV “a gift”, the Geneva “a reward”, the NWT 1984 “a blessing gift”, and the TLB “my gifts”. Genesis 33:11 goes on, “And he pressed him, and he took it”; the Hebrew phrase for “pressed”, vayiftsar-bo, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Genesis 33:11, Judges 19:7 and this chapter’s verse 16, where Naaman urges and Elisha refuses. The ASV, the KJV, the NIV and the TLB have “urged”, the Douay “pressed”, and the Geneva “constrained”.

“As Jehovah lives, before whom I stand”. Elisha’s oath is Elijah’s: asher amadeti lefanav stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, 1 Kings 17:1 and 18:15, then 2 Kings 3:14 and this one (all now on these pages). Elijah swears it twice and Elisha twice, here with “I will not take” (verse 16). The KJV, the ASV and the Douay have “I will receive none”, the NIV “I will not accept a thing”, and the TLB “I swear by Jehovah my God that I will not accept them”.

⚠ “A pair of mules’ burden of earth”. The Hebrew has “a burden, a pair of mules, earth”. The ASV has “two mules’ burden of earth”, the NIV “as much earth as a pair of mules can carry”, and the TLB “two muleloads of earth”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition adds a clause, “and you shall give me of the red earth”, which this page notes and does not adopt. The verse gives its own reason: from now on Naaman will offer “neither burnt offering nor sacrifice to other gods, but to Jehovah”. A reader may think of the altar of earth in Exodus 20:20 (20:24 in English Bibles, now on these pages), but the verse does not say that this is why he wants it.

⚠ “The house of Rimmon”. Naaman asks pardon for what he has not yet done: when his lord goes into the house of Rimmon and leans on his hand, he will bow down there too. “The house of Rimmon” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one, and three times over. Rashi says only “the name of an idol”, and the king of Aram in 1 Kings 15:18 (now on these pages) is Ben-hadad son of Tabrimmon, a name that ends in the same word. “Leans on my hand” is said again, with “his hand”, of a king and his officer in 2 Kings 7:2 and 7:17. The one verb for “bow down” stands three times in the Hebrew; the KJV and the ASV have “worship” for the first and “bow” for the others, where the NIV and the NWT 1984 have “bow down” for the first. The ASV, the KJV, the Geneva and the NWT 1984 have “house of Rimmon”, and the NIV, the TLB and the Douay “temple”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition makes the plea a statement, “the Lord will be merciful”. The Hebrew line also prints a bare na, “please”, with no vowels and no reading beside it, in the last clause of verse 18: the NWT 1984 has “please” there, and the ASV and the KJV do not.

“Go in peace”. Elisha neither grants it nor refuses it. Lekh leshalom is what Jethro says to Moses (Exodus 4:18) and Jonathan to David (1 Samuel 20:42), both now on these pages, and the verse does not say what Elisha thinks. Then Naaman goes “a stretch of land”, kivrat eretz: kivrat stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, and “kivrat eretz” in two, Genesis 48:7 and this one. The KJV and the ASV have “a little way”, the NIV “some distance”, the NWT 1984 “a good stretch of the land”, the Geneva “about half a day’s journey of ground”, and the Douay “in the springtime of the earth”.

Verses 20–24 · 2 Kings 5:20–24 Gehazi runs — an oath that mirrors Elisha’s, a lie that says “Well”, and two talents in two bags

⭐ “As Jehovah lives, I will run after him and take something from him”. Gehazi is named here for the first time in this chapter, and the Hebrew gives him the whole title: “the young man of Elisha the man of God”. His oath has the shape of the one at verse 16: Elisha swears “As Jehovah lives… I will not take”, and Gehazi swears “As Jehovah lives… I will take”. The verb is laqach, “take”, and it stands in verses 5, 15, 16, 20, 23, 24 and 26 of this chapter, from the gift Naaman brings to the verdict of verse 26. “Something”, me’umah, is a word of Samuel’s farewell, where the people say “you have not taken anything from any man’s hand” (1 Samuel 12:4, now on these pages). Gehazi’s plan is to take me’umah “from him”. The ASV, the KJV, the Geneva and the Douay have “spared”, and the NIV has “too easy on”. He also calls him “this Naaman the Aramean”, and the TLB has Gehazi say “this fellow”.

He got down from the chariot. Naaman “saw one running after him”, and the Hebrew says he fell from the chariot to meet him: vayippol, the verb of Rebekah when she saw Isaac and “got down from the camel” (Genesis 24:64, now on these pages). The commander of an army gets down for a prophet’s servant. He asks hashalom, “Is it well?”, and Gehazi answers shalom, “Well”. Shalom stands in three verses of this chapter and no others: verse 19, Elisha’s “Go in peace”, verse 21, and verse 22, which is a lie.

What he says, and what Naaman gives. “My master has sent me, saying: Look, just now there have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim two young men of the sons of the prophets; give them, please, a talent of silver and two changes of garments.” The request is one talent and two changes; Naaman answers, “Be willing, take two talents”. The KJV has “Be content”, the ASV “Be pleased”, the Douay “It is better that thou take two talents”, and the TLB has “Take $4,000”. “Two” is in verse 23 five times in Hebrew: two talents, two talents of silver, two bags, two changes of garments, two of his young men. Naaman’s servants carry it “before him”, lefanav, the word of verse 3. The phrase for his insistence, vayifrats-bo, “and he pressed him”, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: 2 Samuel 13:25, 2 Samuel 13:27 and this one. It is a second verb, with the same three letters in a different order, from the vayiftsar-bo of verse 16. The ASV, the KJV and the NIV have “urged” in both verses, the Douay “pressed” at verse 16 and “forced” at verse 23, and the Geneva “constrained” at verse 16 and “compelled” at verse 23.

⚠ “The Ophel”. Gehazi “came to the Ophel, and took them from their hand, and deposited them in the house; and he sent the men away”. Ha-ofel is “the mound”, with the article. The ASV and the NIV have “the hill”, the KJV and the Geneva “the tower”, the NWT 1984 “Ophel”, and the Douay “the evening”. The Targum, in Rashi’s words, has “a hidden place”, and the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has to skoteinon, “the dark place”. The verse does not say where the Ophel was; it says only that he sent away the men who carried the load, and put it in the house, and went in to his master.

Verses 25–27 · 2 Kings 5:25–27 “Did my heart not go?” — the question, the eight things, and a disease that clings

⚠ “From where, Gehazi?” The Hebrew line prints two forms for the question, me’an as written and me’ayin as read, the last ketiv and qere of the chapter; the reading is “from where”. Gehazi answers, “Your servant went nowhere”, in the words anah va’anah, “to-where and to-where”. It is the sixth “your servant” of the chapter, and the first in the mouth of someone who is lying. The KJV, the ASV, the Geneva and the Douay have “Thy servant went no whither”, the NIV “Your servant didn’t go anywhere”, and the TLB “I haven’t been anywhere”.

⭐ “Did my heart not go?” At 2 Kings 4:27 (now on these pages) Elisha said of the Shunammite, “Jehovah has hidden it from me and has not told me.” Here the verse gives him no informant, and says only “my heart went”. The Hebrew has no “with you”. The ASV has “Went not my heart with thee”, the KJV and the Geneva “Went not mine heart with thee”, the NIV “Was not my spirit with you”, the Douay “Was not my heart present”, and the TLB “I was there in thought”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “with you”, meta sou. Then he lists eight things: silver, garments, olive groves, vineyards, sheep, cattle, male servants and female servants. Gehazi took two of them. Rashi reads the list as what the money would buy.

⭐ “Shall cling to you and to your seed forever”. The verb is davaq, and the word Gehazi took, me’umah, is in Deuteronomy 13:18 (now on these pages) with the same verb: “nothing of what is devoted shall cling to your hand”. The verdict is not a new disease: it is “the scaly disease of Naaman”, the one the chapter opened with, and it is to cling to the servant who took his silver and to his seed. The KJV and the ASV have “cleave”, the NIV “cling”, the NWT 1984 and the Douay “stick”, and the TLB “shall be upon you”. The last words of the chapter are “one with the scaly disease, white as snow”. The Hebrew words for the scaly disease “as snow” stand in three verses: Moses’ hand (Exodus 4:6), Miriam (Numbers 12:10) and Gehazi, all now on these pages. In the first two it is undone: Moses’ hand is “restored”, and Miriam is “gathered back in”. Here the chapter ends with him going out, and does not say what became of him. Luke 4:27 (now on these pages) has Jesus say that of the many lepers in Israel in Elisha’s time “none of them was cleansed — only Naaman the Syrian”; Luke 17:18 has him call the one of ten who turned back “this foreigner”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “one with the scaly disease” and “from his scaly disease”, as at Leviticus 13 and Numbers 12; “gather him in”, one verb at verses 3, 6, 7 and 11, as at Numbers 12:14–15; “before”, for the girl’s wish and Naaman’s standing; “wash” for what Elisha says and “dipped himself” for what Naaman does; “and be clean”, a command; “the word of the man of God”; “a little boy”; “take a blessing”, as at Genesis 33:11; “As Jehovah lives, before whom I stand”, as at 1 Kings 17:1; “urged” at verse 16 and “pressed” at verse 23, two different verbs; “Well” for shalom at verses 21 and 22; “got down”, as at Genesis 24:64; “cling”, as at Deuteronomy 13:18; and “white as snow”. The written and read Hebrew are recorded at verses 12 and 25, and the bare na at verse 18.

Echoes paid. Ahab’s death by an arrow shot “in his innocence” (1 Kings 22:34); the camp and the gathering in (Numbers 12:14–15); the leper’s law outside the camp (Leviticus 13:46) and the seven sprinklings (Leviticus 14:7); “I put to death, and I bring to life” (Deuteronomy 32:39; 1 Samuel 2:6); “my father, my father” (2 Kings 2:12); the hand restored (Exodus 4:6–7); the oath of Elijah and Elisha (1 Kings 17:1; 1 Kings 18:15; 2 Kings 3:14); Jacob’s blessing (Genesis 33:11); the altar of earth (Exodus 20:20); Tabrimmon (1 Kings 15:18); “Go in peace” (Exodus 4:18; 1 Samuel 20:42); Rebekah getting down from the camel (Genesis 24:64); Samuel’s “anything from any man’s hand” (1 Samuel 12:4); “nothing devoted shall cling to your hand” (Deuteronomy 13:18); Elisha’s “Jehovah has hidden it from me” (2 Kings 4:27); “none of them was cleansed — only Naaman” (Luke 4:27); and the one foreigner who turned back (Luke 17:15–18).

Echoes planted. The king who leans on an officer’s hand (2 Kings 7:2, 17); Gehazi still “the young man of the man of God”, telling a king “all the great things that Elisha has done” (2 Kings 8:4–5); and Elisha in Damascus, with Ben-hadad king of Aram sick (2 Kings 8:7). None of these is translated here yet.
