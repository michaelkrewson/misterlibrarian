The heads of the Levites come to Eleazar, Joshua and the tribal chiefs at Shiloh and ask for the cities and pasture-land Moses had promised them, and the lots are cast: thirteen cities for Aaron's sons, ten for the rest of Kohath, thirteen for Gershon, twelve for Merari, forty-eight in all. The Masoretic text drops the four cities of Reuben (verses 36-37), which the Chronicles parallel supplies. The chapter ends with the formula that the land was given and that not one word failed.
Like chapters 13-20, a legal/administrative chapter with no date or duration of its own; it follows the division of the land at Shiloh (18:1, 19:51) and the book does not say how long afterwards. Same contested early/late-date question as the rest of the conquest (c. 1406 BC on the early date this project foregrounds, c. 1230-1210 BC on the late).

Verses 1–3 · The Levites ask at Shiloh, Joshua 21:1–3: the request is Numbers 35 read back, and the answer is given in the same words

★★★ The Levites come to the priest and the leader together, and they come as heads of fathers’ houses. The first verse stacks three groups in one sentence: the heads of the Levites’ families, Eleazar the priest, and Joshua son of Nun, plus the heads of the other tribes. Nothing else in the book puts the tribal chiefs and the Levitical chiefs in front of the same two men at the same time. The Levites are the only tribe that the book has said receives no land at all (Joshua 13:33, already on these pages), so they arrive not with a claim to territory but with a request about the cities they are owed.

★★ The request is Numbers 35, quoted back to the people who were supposed to keep it. V2 has the Levites say that Jehovah commanded by the hand of Moses that cities be given them to dwell in, with their pasture-land for their livestock. Numbers 35:2 (already on these pages) gives that command in nearly the same words. The chapter does not add a single new requirement; it confirms the old one is still in force after the conquest.

★★ The place of the request is Shiloh, the same place where the tent stood. The Levites speak to the chiefs at Shiloh, which the book has already made the seat of the tent of meeting (Joshua 18:1 and 19:51, both already on these pages). The chapter that gives the Levites their cities therefore opens where the Levites’ own sanctuary stands.

⚠ V3 gives the cities “at the mouth of Jehovah.” The Hebrew is el-pi YHWH, which here means ‘by the command of Jehovah’, not a place. The NWT 1984 reads ‘at the order of Jehovah’ and the ASV ‘according to the commandment of Jehovah’. This translation keeps the Hebrew’s own words, so the English carries the idiom; the note gives its sense.

Verses 4–8 · The lot for the Levites, Joshua 21:4–8: thirteen, ten, thirteen and twelve, which add up to forty-eight

★★★ The Levites receive their cities by lot, which is the same instrument that divided the land. The noun goral, ‘lot’, stands in v4, v5, v6 and v8, and the Hebrew of v10 gives the first lot to Aaron’s sons. The Levites have no territory of their own, so they receive a share of the other tribes’ land by the same method that gave the tribes their own shares.

★★ Four families, four sums, and the sums are exact. Aaron’s priests get thirteen cities (v4), the rest of Kohath ten (v5), Gershon thirteen (v6), and Merari twelve (v7). Thirteen plus ten is twenty-three, plus thirteen is thirty-six, plus twelve is forty-eight. V41 later says ‘forty-eight cities’, which is why the sum cannot be checked against anything but the list itself.

★★ Each family draws from a fixed set of tribes. V4 gives Aaron’s thirteen from Judah, Simeon and Benjamin. V5 gives the rest of Kohath ten from Ephraim, Dan and the half-tribe of Manasseh. Gershon in v6 takes from four tribes, including the half-tribe in Bashan, and Merari in v7 from three.

⚠ The Merari sum includes Reuben, whose cities the Masoretic text does not print. V7 gives Merari twelve cities “out of the tribe of Reuben, and out of the tribe of Gad, and out of the tribe of Zebulun.” The surviving list (vv34–39) gives Zebulun four and Gad four: eight. The missing four are Reuben’s, and only they make the sum come out to twelve. The gap is therefore not a guess; the count the chapter has already committed itself to requires it.

Verses 9–12 · Judah’s and Simeon’s cities, and Hebron, which Caleb keeps while the priests get the city: Joshua 21:9–12

★★ Hebron is given to the priests, and the field around it is given to Caleb. V11 gives Aaron’s sons ‘Kiriath-arba, the father of Anak, that is Hebron’, with its pasture-land. V12 gives the city’s fields and villages to Caleb. Joshua 14:13–14 (already on these pages) has already given Caleb Hebron as his inheritance. The chapter divides what that earlier verse had given whole: the town to the priests and the land around it to the man.

★★ The clause ‘Kiriath-arba, that is Hebron’ stands in two verses. The clause Qiryat Arba hi Chevron is found in Joshua 20:7 and here at v11, both already on these pages. This verse is the one that gives the city to the priests.

★ V9 says the cities ‘are named by name’, and then names them. The phrase asher yiqra etHen beshem, ‘which are called by name’, is the book’s own way of promising a list. The list follows at v13, in the same order of tribes it gave in v4.

Verses 13–19 · Aaron’s thirteen cities, Joshua 21:13–19: nine from two tribes, four from a third, and Hebron the only refuge in the group

★★ The priests’ list has the one refuge city of the group, and it is Hebron. V13 calls Hebron ‘the city of refuge for the manslayer’. The phrase ir miqlat ha-rotzeach stands in five verses of the surviving text of Joshua 21 (vv13, 21, 27, 32 and, in English numbering, 38) and in Numbers 35 and Joshua 20 besides. Each of the five names one of the six cities of refuge (Hebron, Shechem, Golan, Kedesh and Ramoth in Gilead), and the sixth, Bezer, is one of the cities of the gap in vv36–37.

★★ Nine cities out of two tribes is the sum the verse reports, and the list supports it. V16 says ‘nine cities out of those two tribes’. The list gives Hebron, Libnah, Jattir, Eshtemoa, Holon, Debir, Ain, Juttah and Beth-shemesh: nine, all from Judah and Simeon. The four from Benjamin in v17–18 (Gibeon, Geba, Anathoth and Almon) make the thirteen of v19.

★ The list is read in three blocks, and each block closes with its own count. Judah and Simeon close at v16 with ‘nine’, Benjamin at v18 with ‘four cities’, and v19 sums them to thirteen. The Hebrew uses the same closing formula for each block, so the count is part of the list, not an afterthought.

Verses 20–26 · The rest of Kohath, Joshua 21:20–26: Shechem for a refuge, two Gath-rimmons, and ten cities in all

★★ Shechem is the first refuge city of the Kohathites, and it is also the city where Simeon and Levi killed on purpose. V21 names Shechem as a refuge in the hill country of Ephraim. The city is the place of the massacre in Genesis 34:25–26 (already on these pages); Joshua 20:7 (already on these pages) had already made it a refuge for the man who killed by error. The chapter therefore gives the same town two roles, for two kinds of killing.

★★ ‘Gath-rimmon’ appears twice in five verses, and the text does not say whether it is one town or two. V24 gives Dan ‘Aijalon with its pasture-land, Gath-rimmon with its pasture-land’, and v25 gives Manasseh ‘Taanach with its pasture-land, and Gath-rimmon with its pasture-land’. The Hebrew has the same consonants both times (Gat Rimmon), and the name also stands in the list of Dan’s inheritance (Joshua 19:45, already on these pages), so the book knows a single place with that name in Dan’s territory. Whether v25 names a second town of the same name in Manasseh, or the same town claimed by two lots, is not stated, and this translation does not decide it.

★★ Ten, from three tribes, in three blocks. Ephraim gives four (Shechem, Gezer, Kibzaim and Beth-horon, vv21–22), Dan four (Eltekeh, Gibbethon, Aijalon and Gath-rimmon, vv23–24), and Manasseh two (Taanach and Gath-rimmon, v25): ten, as v26 says.

Verses 27–33 · The sons of Gershon and the Naphtali refuge, Joshua 21:27–33: Beeshterah where Chronicles has Ashtaroth, and Kedesh a second time

★★★ Beeshterah appears here and nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, and the Chronicles parallel gives a different name in its place. V27 gives Gershon ‘Golan in Bashan … and Beeshterah’, the second of the Manasseh-Bashan cities. The consonants stand only here (checked across the whole corpus). The parallel in 1 Chronicles 6:56 gives Ashtaroth, the plural of the same root, in that slot. This translation prints what Joshua says and records the variant here.

★★ Golan and Kedesh are both refuges, and Kedesh is a name the book has already tied to Naphtali. V27 names Golan of Manasseh’s Bashan-half as a refuge; v32 names Kedesh in Galilee, in Naphtali, as the next. Joshua 20:7 (already on these pages) named Kedesh first among the six, and Joshua 19:37 had already set it in Naphtali’s lot.

★ Thirteen again, and the arithmetic holds. V27 gives two (Golan and Beeshterah), v28–29 Issachar four (Kishion, Daberath, Jarmuth and En-gannim), v30–31 Asher four (Mishal, Abdon, Helkath and Rehob), and v32 Naphtali three (Kedesh, Hammoth-dor and Kartan). That is thirteen, as v33 says.

Verses 34–35 and the gap at 36–37 · Merari begins in Zebulun, Joshua 21:34–35; then the English gap, where Reuben’s four cities should stand

★★★ The Masoretic text is two verses short, and the shortfall begins exactly where Reuben’s cities should be. The Hebrew of v34–35 gives Zebulun’s four cities, Jokneam, Kartah, Dimnah and Nahalal. The next verse in the Hebrew is about Gad. Between them the Masoretic text prints nothing for Reuben, and the English numbering that the KJV and the NWT both follow has two verses there, numbered 36 and 37, which this translation prints as a gap rather than renumbering the rest.

★★ Two Masoretic witnesses confirm the gap; neither one supplies the missing words. The archived Mechon text (the house’s own source) has no Reuben verses, and a fresh copy of the same Mechon page taken the same day does not either. A second Masoretic edition, the JPS text on Sefaria, prints the chapter in forty-five numbered slots and leaves the two slots for Reuben blank, so the gap is in the Masoretic tradition as printed today, not a fault in our archive. The Reuben names are preserved in the Chronicles parallel, which is Masoretic too, and this translation takes them from there.

★★ Reuben’s four cities are Bezer, Jahaz (Jahazah), Kedemoth and Mephaath, and the Chronicles list gives them in that order. Bezer is the town that Joshua 20:8 already named as a refuge ‘in the wilderness, on the tableland’. So the sixth refuge city of the six is named only in the verses the Masoretic text lacks. The six shelf versions all print the four names with their pasture-land, and they differ only in their word for that land (‘suburbs’ in the KJV and ASV, ‘pasture ground’ in the NWT 1984, ‘pastures’ in the NWT 2013, ‘dehesa’ and ‘campos de pasto’ in the TNM).

⚠ The gap is not filled here in the translation. The gap panel shows the source and says which. Had we restored the names into the English as though the Hebrew said them, we would have printed a reading that the Masoretic text does not contain. The panel above gives the names as the Chronicles parallel gives them; this note gives the reason.

Verses 38–39 · Gad’s four cities, Joshua 21:38–39: Ramoth in Gilead, the fifth refuge in the chapter’s own list

★★ Ramoth in Gilead is the fifth refuge city the surviving Hebrew names, and the gap has been counted in English numbers. V38 gives Gad ‘Ramoth in Gilead, the city of refuge for the manslayer’. Joshua 20:8 (already on these pages) had named it among the three east of the Jordan. Here the Hebrew gives the refuge formula and the Gad list in the same verse.

★ Four in Gad, and four in all. V39 lists Heshbon and Jazer and then says ‘four cities in all’, which the sum of the Gad group confirms (Ramoth and Mahanaim in v38, Heshbon and Jazer here). Mahanaim is the place where Jacob met the angels and where David fled from Absalom.

Verses 40–42 · The Merari total, Joshua 21:40, and the forty-eight, Joshua 21:41–42: pasture-land round every city

★★★ The total of twelve for Merari, and forty-eight for the Levites, depends on the four missing cities of Reuben. V40 says Merari’s lot was ‘twelve cities’, and v41 says the Levites’ cities total ‘forty-eight cities’. The surviving Merari list has eight. The sum is only right if the four from Reuben are added back, and the note on v7 showed that the chapter had already committed to the same twelve. Both totals are on the page, and the page shows that the Masoretic list does not reach them.

★★ Every city comes with its pasture-land round about it. V42 says each city had its pasture-land ‘round about it’, and the list repeats the phrase at every entry. The word migrash names the open ground around a town, which is the Levites’ grazing land. Numbers 35:4–5 (already on these pages) sets that ground by measure: a thousand cubits from the city wall, and two thousand for the outer measure.

Verses 43–45 · “Not one word failed”, Joshua 21:43–45: the book’s own closing formula, said here before it is repeated in Joshua 23

★★★ The sentence ‘not one word failed’ stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible. The clause lo-nafal davar is checked consonantally across the whole Hebrew Bible: it stands in Joshua 21:45 (here), Joshua 23:14 (now on these pages), and 1 Kings 8:56. Joshua 23:14 repeats it in Joshua’s own last speech; 1 Kings 8:56 uses it for Solomon’s dedication.

★★ The land is given, the enemies are given, and the rest is given, and the chapter names each in turn. V43 says Jehovah gave all the land he swore to give. V44 says he gave rest on every side, and that not one man of their enemies stood before them. V45 says that not one word failed. The chapter’s last three verses turn the conquest from a list of cities into a finished promise.

⚠ V43 has two verbs for the land, and the translation keeps both. The first is natan, ‘to give’, for what Jehovah did; the second is yarash, ‘to take possession’, for what Israel did. The KJV reads ‘they possessed it, and dwelt therein’, which keeps the second verb and drops the distinction between giving and taking.

Echoes paid. Joshua 20 had promised that the Levites’ cities would be named, and that the six refuges would be the Levites’ towns (20:4–5). The Levitical lot now names five of them: Hebron (v13), Shechem (v21), Golan (v27), Kedesh (v32) and Ramoth in Gilead (v38). Bezer, the sixth, is one of the gap verses. Joshua 19:51 had set the tent at Shiloh, and Joshua 21:2 has the Levites meet there.

Echoes planted. Joshua 22:9 (now on these pages) sends Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh home from Shiloh, the place where the Levites’ cities were given. 1 Chronicles 6 is the parallel that supplies the gap’s names and two of its variants (Ashtaroth for Beeshterah, and the other Manasseh towns). Joshua 23:14 (now on these pages) repeats the phrase that closes this chapter.

Decisions. The English numbering is kept, with 36 and 37 left as a gap rather than renumbering the rest of the chapter. Verses 38 to 45 of this translation therefore carry the Hebrew of Masoretic verses 36 to 43. This follows the house rule for missing verses (Acts 8:37). The Reuben names are given in the gap panel from the Chronicles parallel and are not presented as Hebrew of Joshua. Migrash is ‘pasture-land’ throughout, the same rendering used in Numbers 35.

Library. No new dictionary or encyclopedia entries are needed in this chapter. The existing entries miqlat, migrash, goral, levi and ratzach carry the chapter’s terms. The encyclopedia entries linked here are the ones already on the site.
