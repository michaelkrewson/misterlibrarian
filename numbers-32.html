🕰
Reuben and Gad, rich in cattle, ask to settle east of the Jordan instead of crossing into Canaan. Moses retells Numbers 13-14 to them almost verbatim, then strikes a covenant with no divine speech in it: every armed man crosses first, or the land is forfeit and 'your sin will find you out'. The deal is formalized before Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads, and Manasseh's half tribe is added to the grant once Machir, Jair, and Nobah are found to have already been conquering Gilead on their own.
⚠ Undated within the chapter, continuous with the war-tax and purification of Numbers 31 just given, still on the plains of Moab at the end of the fortieth year after the exodus — c. 1407 BC on the early-date chronology this project foregrounds. Hebrew and English versification agree: 42 verses on both sides. ⭐ Four Masoretic paragraph breaks (after vv4, 15, 19, and the chapter's own close), with everything from v20 onward running as one unbroken scribal unit.

Numbers 32 — The Mister Translation

1
וּמִקְנֶה רַב, הָיָה לִבְנֵי רְאוּבֵן וְלִבְנֵי-גָד--עָצוּם מְאֹד; וַיִּרְאוּ אֶת-אֶרֶץ יַעְזֵר, וְאֶת-אֶרֶץ גִּלְעָד, וְהִנֵּה הַמָּקוֹם, מְקוֹם מִקְנֶה.
Now the children of Reuben and the children of Gad had livestock in great number — very great — and they saw the land of Jazer and the land of Gilead, and look, the place was a place for livestock.note
Y los hijos de Rubén y los hijos de Gad tenían ganado en gran cantidad —muy numeroso—, y vieron la tierra de Jazer y la tierra de Galaad, y he aquí que el lugar era lugar de ganado.
2
וַיָּבֹאוּ בְנֵי-גָד, וּבְנֵי רְאוּבֵן; וַיֹּאמְרוּ אֶל-מֹשֶׁה וְאֶל-אֶלְעָזָר הַכֹּהֵן, וְאֶל-נְשִׂיאֵי הָעֵדָה לֵאמֹר.
the children of Gad and the children of Reuben came and said to Moses, and to Eleazar the priest, and to the chieftains of the congregation, saying:note
los hijos de Gad y los hijos de Rubén vinieron y hablaron a Moisés, y a Eleazar el sacerdote, y a los jefes de la congregación, diciendo:
3
עֲטָרוֹת וְדִיבֹן וְיַעְזֵר וְנִמְרָה, וְחֶשְׁבּוֹן וְאֶלְעָלֵה, וּשְׂבָם וּנְבוֹ, וּבְעֹן.
Ataroth, and Dibon, and Jazer, and Nimrah, and Heshbon, and Elealeh, and Sebam, and Nebo, and Beon —note
Atarot, y Dibón, y Jazer, y Nimra, y Hesbón, y Elealé, y Sebán, y Nebo, y Beón —
4
הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר הִכָּה יְהוָה לִפְנֵי עֲדַת יִשְׂרָאֵל--אֶרֶץ מִקְנֶה, הִוא; וְלַעֲבָדֶיךָ, מִקְנֶה. {ס}
the land that Jehovah struck down before the congregation of Israel — it is a land for livestock, and your servants have livestock. {S}note
la tierra que Jehová hirió delante de la congregación de Israel —tierra de ganado es—, y tus siervos tienen ganado. {S}
5
וַיֹּאמְרוּ, אִם-מָצָאנוּ חֵן בְּעֵינֶיךָ--יֻתַּן אֶת-הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת לַעֲבָדֶיךָ, לַאֲחֻזָּה: אַל-תַּעֲבִרֵנוּ, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן.
And they said: If we have found favor in your eyes, let this land be given to your servants for a holding; do not make us cross the Jordan.note
Y dijeron: Si hemos hallado gracia ante tus ojos, sea dada esta tierra a tus siervos como posesión; no nos hagas pasar el Jordán.
6
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה, לִבְנֵי-גָד וְלִבְנֵי רְאוּבֵן: הַאַחֵיכֶם, יָבֹאוּ לַמִּלְחָמָה, וְאַתֶּם, תֵּשְׁבוּ פֹה.
And Moses said to the children of Gad and to the children of Reuben: Shall your brothers go to the war, and you sit here?note
Y Moisés dijo a los hijos de Gad y a los hijos de Rubén: ¿Irán vuestros hermanos a la guerra, y vosotros os quedaréis aquí?
7
וְלָמָּה תנואון תְנִיאוּן, אֶת-לֵב בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--מֵעֲבֹר, אֶל-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר-נָתַן לָהֶם, יְהוָה.
And why do you override the heart of the children of Israel, so that they will not cross over into the land that Jehovah has given them?note
¿Y por qué contrariáis el corazón de los hijos de Israel, para que no pasen a la tierra que Jehová les ha dado?
8
כֹּה עָשׂוּ, אֲבֹתֵיכֶם, בְּשָׁלְחִי אֹתָם מִקָּדֵשׁ בַּרְנֵעַ, לִרְאוֹת אֶת-הָאָרֶץ.
This is what your fathers did, when I sent them from Kadesh-barnea to see the land.note
Así hicieron vuestros padres, cuando los envíe desde Cades-barnea a ver la tierra.
9
וַיַּעֲלוּ עַד-נַחַל אֶשְׁכּוֹל, וַיִּרְאוּ אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, וַיָּנִיאוּ, אֶת-לֵב בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--לְבִלְתִּי-בֹא, אֶל-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר-נָתַן לָהֶם, יְהוָה.
For they went up to the valley of Eshcol and saw the land, and they overrode the heart of the children of Israel, so that they would not go into the land that Jehovah had given them.note
Pues subieron hasta el valle de Escol, y vieron la tierra, y contrariaron el corazón de los hijos de Israel, para que no entrasen en la tierra que Jehová les había dado.
10
וַיִּחַר-אַף יְהוָה, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא; וַיִּשָּׁבַע, לֵאמֹר.
And Jehovah’s anger burned on that day, and he swore, saying:note
Y se encendió el furor de Jehová en aquel día, y juró, diciendo:
11
אִם-יִרְאוּ הָאֲנָשִׁים הָעֹלִים מִמִּצְרַיִם, מִבֶּן עֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה וָמַעְלָה, אֵת הָאֲדָמָה, אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי לְאַבְרָהָם לְיִצְחָק וּלְיַעֲקֹב: כִּי לֹא-מִלְאוּ, אַחֲרָי.
Not one of the men who came up out of Egypt, from twenty years old and upward, will see the ground that I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob — because they did not fill up after me —note
Ninguno de los hombres que subieron de Egipto, de veinte años arriba, verá la tierra que juré a Abraham, a Isaac y a Jacob —porque no llenaron en pos de mí—
12
בִּלְתִּי כָּלֵב בֶּן-יְפֻנֶּה, הַקְּנִזִּי, וִיהוֹשֻׁעַ, בִּן-נוּן: כִּי מִלְאוּ, אַחֲרֵי יְהוָה.
except Caleb son of Jephunneh the Kenizzite, and Joshua son of Nun — because they filled up after Jehovah.note
excepto Caleb hijo de Jefone el cenezeo, y Josué hijo de Nun —porque ellos llenaron en pos de Jehová.
13
וַיִּחַר-אַף יְהוָה, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיְנִעֵם בַּמִּדְבָּר, אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה--עַד-תֹּם, כָּל-הַדּוֹר, הָעֹשֶׂה הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
And Jehovah’s anger burned against Israel, and he made them wander in the wilderness forty years, until the whole generation that did evil in the eyes of Jehovah was finished.note
Y se encendió el furor de Jehová contra Israel, y los hizo andar errantes por el desierto cuarenta años, hasta que se acabó toda aquella generación que había hecho el mal ante los ojos de Jehová.
14
וְהִנֵּה קַמְתֶּם, תַּחַת אֲבֹתֵיכֶם--תַּרְבּוּת, אֲנָשִׁים חַטָּאִים: לִסְפּוֹת עוֹד, עַל חֲרוֹן אַף-יְהוָה--אֶל-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And look — you have risen up in your fathers’ place, a brood of sinful men, to add still more to the burning of Jehovah’s anger against Israel.note
Y he aquí os habéis levantado en lugar de vuestros padres, una camada de hombres pecadores, para añadir aún más al ardor de la ira de Jehová contra Israel.
15
כִּי תְשׁוּבֻן, מֵאַחֲרָיו, וְיָסַף עוֹד, לְהַנִּיחוֹ בַּמִּדְבָּר; וְשִׁחַתֶּם, לְכָל-הָעָם הַזֶּה. {ס}
For if you turn away from following him, he will yet again leave them in the wilderness, and you will destroy all this people. {S}note
Porque si os apartáis de en pos de él, él volverá otra vez a dejarlos en el desierto, y destruiréis a todo este pueblo. {S}
16
וַיִּגְּשׁוּ אֵלָיו וַיֹּאמְרוּ, גִּדְרֹת צֹאן נִבְנֶה לְמִקְנֵנוּ פֹּה, וְעָרִים, לְטַפֵּנוּ.
And they drew near to him and said: We will build sheepfolds here for our livestock, and cities for our little ones;note
Y se acercaron a él y dijeron: Aquí edificaremos apriscos para nuestro ganado, y ciudades para nuestros pequeñuelos;
17
וַאֲנַחְנוּ נֵחָלֵץ חֻשִׁים, לִפְנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, עַד אֲשֶׁר אִם-הֲבִיאֹנֻם, אֶל-מְקוֹמָם; וְיָשַׁב טַפֵּנוּ בְּעָרֵי הַמִּבְצָר, מִפְּנֵי יֹשְׁבֵי הָאָרֶץ.
but we ourselves will arm ourselves in haste before the children of Israel, until we have brought them to their place; and our little ones will dwell in the fortified cities, because of the inhabitants of the land.note
pero nosotros mismos nos armaremos apresuradamente delante de los hijos de Israel, hasta haberlos introducido en su lugar; y nuestros pequeñuelos habitarán en las ciudades fortificadas, a causa de los habitantes de la tierra.
18
לֹא נָשׁוּב, אֶל-בָּתֵּינוּ--עַד, הִתְנַחֵל בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אִישׁ, נַחֲלָתוֹ.
We will not return to our houses until the children of Israel have each taken possession of his inheritance.note
No volveremos a nuestras casas hasta que los hijos de Israel hayan tomado posesión cada uno de su heredad.
19
כִּי לֹא נִנְחַל אִתָּם, מֵעֵבֶר לַיַּרְדֵּן וָהָלְאָה: כִּי בָאָה נַחֲלָתֵנוּ אֵלֵינוּ, מֵעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן מִזְרָחָה. {פ}
For we will not inherit with them across the Jordan and beyond, because our inheritance has come to us on the other side of the Jordan, eastward. {P}note
Porque no heredaremos con ellos al otro lado del Jordán ni más allá, pues nuestra heredad nos ha tocado a este lado del Jordán, al oriente. {P}
20
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵיהֶם מֹשֶׁה, אִם-תַּעֲשׂוּן אֶת-הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה: אִם-תֵּחָלְצוּ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, לַמִּלְחָמָה.
And Moses said to them: If you will do this thing — if you will arm yourselves before Jehovah for the war,note
Y Moisés les dijo: Si hiciéreis esto —si os armáreis delante de Jehová para la guerra,
21
וְעָבַר לָכֶם כָּל-חָלוּץ אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, עַד הוֹרִישׁוֹ אֶת-אֹיְבָיו, מִפָּנָיו.
and every armed man among you crosses the Jordan before Jehovah, until he has driven out his enemies from before him,note
y todo hombre armado de entre vosotros pasare el Jordán delante de Jehová, hasta que haya echado a sus enemigos de delante de sí,
22
וְנִכְבְּשָׁה הָאָרֶץ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, וְאַחַר תָּשֻׁבוּ--וִהְיִיתֶם נְקִיִּם מֵיְהוָה, וּמִיִּשְׂרָאֵל; וְהָיְתָה הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת לָכֶם, לַאֲחֻזָּה--לִפְנֵי יְהוָה.
and the land is subdued before Jehovah, and afterward you return — then you will be free before Jehovah and before Israel, and this land will be yours for a holding before Jehovah.note
y el país fuere sojuzgado delante de Jehová, y después volviereis —entonces seréis libres delante de Jehová y delante de Israel, y esta tierra será vuestra en posesión delante de Jehová.
23
וְאִם-לֹא תַעֲשׂוּן כֵּן, הִנֵּה חֲטָאתֶם לַיהוָה; וּדְעוּ, חַטַּאתְכֶם, אֲשֶׁר תִּמְצָא, אֶתְכֶם.
But if you will not do so — look, you will have sinned against Jehovah; and know that your sin will find you out.note
Pero si no lo hiciéreis así, he aquí habréis pecado contra Jehová; y sabed que os alcanzará vuestro pecado.
24
בְּנוּ-לָכֶם עָרִים לְטַפְּכֶם, וּגְדֵרֹת לְצֹנַאֲכֶם; וְהַיֹּצֵא מִפִּיכֶם, תַּעֲשׂוּ.
Build cities for your little ones, and folds for your flocks; and do what has gone out of your mouth.note
Edificaos ciudades para vuestros pequeñuelos, y apriscos para vuestros rebaños; y haced lo que ha salido de vuestra boca.
25
וַיֹּאמֶר בְּנֵי-גָד וּבְנֵי רְאוּבֵן, אֶל-מֹשֶׁה לֵאמֹר: עֲבָדֶיךָ יַעֲשׂוּ, כַּאֲשֶׁר אֲדֹנִי מְצַוֶּה.
And the children of Gad and the children of Reuben said to Moses: Your servants will do as my lord commands.note
Y los hijos de Gad y los hijos de Rubén hablaron a Moisés, diciendo: Tus siervos harán como mi señor ha mandado.
26
טַפֵּנוּ נָשֵׁינוּ, מִקְנֵנוּ וְכָל-בְּהֶמְתֵּנוּ--יִהְיוּ-שָׁם, בְּעָרֵי הַגִּלְעָד.
Our little ones, our wives, our livestock, and all our cattle will remain there, in the cities of Gilead;note
Nuestros pequeñuelos, nuestras mujeres, nuestro ganado y todos nuestros animales estarán allí, en las ciudades de Galaad;
27
וַעֲבָדֶיךָ יַעַבְרוּ כָּל-חֲלוּץ צָבָא, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה--לַמִּלְחָמָה: כַּאֲשֶׁר אֲדֹנִי, דֹּבֵר.
but your servants — every man armed for war — will cross over before Jehovah, to the war, as my lord has spoken.note
pero tus siervos —todo hombre armado para la guerra— pasarán delante de Jehová, a la guerra, como mi señor ha dicho.
28
וַיְצַו לָהֶם, מֹשֶׁה, אֵת אֶלְעָזָר הַכֹּהֵן, וְאֵת יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בִּן-נוּן; וְאֶת-רָאשֵׁי אֲבוֹת הַמַּטּוֹת, לִבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
So Moses gave charge concerning them to Eleazar the priest, and to Joshua son of Nun, and to the heads of the fathers’ houses of the tribes of the children of Israel.note
Entonces Moisés dio orden acerca de ellos a Eleazar el sacerdote, y a Josué hijo de Nun, y a los jefes de las casas paternas de las tribus de los hijos de Israel.
29
וַיֹּאמֶר מֹשֶׁה אֲלֵהֶם, אִם-יַעַבְרוּ בְנֵי-גָד וּבְנֵי-רְאוּבֵן אִתְּכֶם אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן כָּל-חָלוּץ לַמִּלְחָמָה לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, וְנִכְבְּשָׁה הָאָרֶץ, לִפְנֵיכֶם--וּנְתַתֶּם לָהֶם אֶת-אֶרֶץ הַגִּלְעָד, לַאֲחֻזָּה.
And Moses said to them: If the children of Gad and the children of Reuben cross the Jordan with you, every man armed for the war, before Jehovah, and the land is subdued before you, then you shall give them the land of Gilead for a holding;note
Y Moisés les dijo: Si los hijos de Gad y los hijos de Rubén pasaren con vosotros el Jordán, todo hombre armado para la guerra, delante de Jehová, y el país fuere sojuzgado delante de vosotros, entonces les daréis la tierra de Galaad en posesión;
30
וְאִם-לֹא יַעַבְרוּ חֲלוּצִים, אִתְּכֶם--וְנֹאחֲזוּ בְתֹכְכֶם, בְּאֶרֶץ כְּנָעַן.
but if they will not cross over armed with you, they shall hold their possession among you in the land of Canaan.note
pero si no pasaren armados con vosotros, tendrán posesión entre vosotros en la tierra de Canaán.
31
וַיַּעֲנוּ בְנֵי-גָד וּבְנֵי רְאוּבֵן, לֵאמֹר: אֵת אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה אֶל-עֲבָדֶיךָ, כֵּן נַעֲשֶׂה.
And the children of Gad and the children of Reuben answered, saying: What Jehovah has spoken to your servants, so we will do.note
Y respondieron los hijos de Gad y los hijos de Rubén, diciendo: Lo que Jehová ha dicho a tus siervos, eso haremos.
32

Numbers 32:1-5 — Cattle-rich, and asking before a single battle is fought

⭐ Reuben and Gad make their case from PROPERTY, not prophecy: they are ‘very great’ in livestock, and the land east of the Jordan is, twice in four verses, called simply ‘a land for livestock’ (vv1, 4). Nine towns are named in v3 — Ataroth, Dibon, Jazer, Nimrah, Heshbon, Elealeh, Sebam, Nebo, Beon — a working inventory of the very land Israel has just taken from Sihon, and every name on the list except Sebam and Beon reappears later in the chapter as a place these two tribes actually build (vv34-38). ⚠ Nothing here is illegitimate: v4 has them state plainly that this is ‘the land that Jehovah struck down before the congregation of Israel’ — they are not asking for someone else’s territory, only for the one already won. The difficulty the rest of the chapter exists to solve is not whose land this is, but what happens to the OTHER nine tribes and a half if two and a half stop here.

⚠ ‘Do not make us cross the Jordan’ (v5) is the whole request in one line, and it is a request to be EXCUSED from something no one has yet asked them to do. The verb is the same root, avar, ‘to cross over,’ that will recur roughly ten times before the chapter is finished — the entire negotiation that follows is an argument conducted almost entirely in this one verb.

Numbers 32:6-15 — Moses answers a land request with a history lesson he lived through himself

⭐⭐ Moses does not argue policy; he retells Numbers 13-14, almost verbatim, to men who were children when it happened. ‘Kadesh-barnea,’ ‘the valley of Eshcol,’ the ten scouts who ‘override the heart of the children of Israel’ (the same verb twice, vv7 and 9), the oath sworn ‘in that day,’ the forty years, and the closing exemption — ‘except Caleb… and Joshua… because they filled up after Jehovah’ — all of it is Numbers 14:22-24, 30 (already on these pages), restated by the one man in the camp who was there for both scenes. ⚠ The verb mille acharai, ‘filled up after me,’ is not a paraphrase; it is the SAME two Hebrew words Numbers 14:24 used of Caleb alone, now applied to Caleb and Joshua together at v12, and Moses is using it to accuse the sons of the very generation it once excused.

⭐ ‘The valley of Eshcol’ (v9) is a place this project has already visited. Numbers 13:23-24 (already on these pages) named the Wadi of the Cluster for the single grape-cluster the scouts carried on a pole between two men; here, one generation later, it is shorthand for the whole catastrophe that cluster of grapes touched off. And Kadesh-barnea (v8) is the same oasis this project has already profiled as ‘the wilderness base-camp of the Exodus generation’ — now named as the actual departure point of the mission that ended it.

⚠ V12 is the FIRST of only three places in the whole Hebrew Bible that call Caleb ‘the Kenizzite’ — searched consonantally, the other two are both in Joshua 14 (vv6, 14) — and this project’s own Caleb entry has been carrying the promise of it since it was written. The shelf’s own history of this one clause is worth reading side by side: KJV ‘wholly followed,’ ASV ‘wholly followed’ — identical to each other, and identical to their own rendering of v11’s negative two verses earlier; Geneva does NOT repeat itself that way — v11 ‘have not wholly followed me,’ but v12 ‘have constantly followed the Lord’ — the one classic witness on this shelf that varies its own wording between the two verses rather than reusing it; NWT 1984 ‘followed Jehovah wholly,’ but its own 2013 revision changes it to ‘followed Jehovah wholeheartedly’ — and the Spanish TNM makes the identical move a generation later, 1987 ‘seguido a Jehová íntegramente’, 2019 ‘seguido a Jehová con todo el corazón’. Douay-Rheims, following the Vulgate, drops the idiom for ‘these have fulfilled my will’ and even respells the names ‘Jephone’ and ‘the Cenezite.’ Five separate witnesses, two of them the same book revising itself decades later, and not one of them lands on the same English or Spanish word twice — not even Geneva, across its own two verses.

⭐⭐ ‘Override’ is not this translation’s free choice here — it is a promise being kept. The verb, heni’a, is the identical root Numbers 30:6 (already on these pages) used of a father overriding his daughter’s vow, where that chapter’s own note named this exact verse in advance and chose ‘overrides’ over the whole shelf’s ‘disallow’/‘forbid’/‘gainsay’ specifically so the two uses would match. One father restraining one daughter’s resolve; ten scouts restraining a whole nation’s. This translation keeps the promise.

⚠ V7’s verb is a ketiv/qere: the consonants as written spell one form of this same root, and the vowel tradition supplies a slightly different form of it. Nothing in the sense turns on it — this translation follows the read form, as it does throughout — but it is worth flagging precisely because the identical root recurs two verses later (v9) with no textual difficulty at all, so the scribes were not troubled by the verb, only by one particular spelling of it.

Numbers 32:16-19 — The counter-offer, and the tribe blessed as a raiding band becomes a vanguard

⭐⭐ Gad and Reuben do not retreat from the request; they refine it into a schedule. Sheepfolds and cities first (v16, the order itself is telling — livestock housed before people), then ‘we ourselves will arm ourselves in haste before the children of Israel’ (v17), then a firm end-date: not until every other tribe has ‘taken possession of his inheritance’ (v18) will they go home. ⚠ This is Jacob’s own deathbed oracle over Gad landing on the actual page: ‘Gad — a raiding band shall raid him, but he shall raid at their heel’ (Genesis 49:19, already on these pages, its own note built on the same root, gedud, repeated three times in six Hebrew words). Four books and roughly forty years later, the tribe named for raiding volunteers to be the one that goes first and returns last — the Torah never states the connection outright, and this translation notes it without claiming the text intends it. ⚠ This is not only a forward plant: Joshua 1:12-15 (already on these pages) is where Joshua himself cashes this exact promise on his first day in command, sending these same three tribes across armed before the rest of the nation, word for word the deal struck here.

⭐ V19 is where achuzzah, ‘holding,’ and nachalah, ‘inheritance,’ are used side by side and mean two different things. The tribes are asking for an achuzzah — a holding, seized and kept — precisely because their real nachalah, the inheritance that passes down the generations, ‘has come to us’ already, on this side of the Jordan. The distinction matters for what comes next: Moses is about to make the holding CONDITIONAL on a promise, in a way an inheritance, by this project’s own established sense of the word, never is.

Numbers 32:20-24 — A pact with no divine speech in it, and the most famous line in the chapter

⭐⭐ Nowhere in this whole negotiation — not once, in forty-two verses — does the text say ‘Jehovah spoke to Moses.’ Searched consonantally across the chapter, every instance of the narrator’s own ‘and NN said’ formula (vayomer/vatomer) has a human subject: Gad and Reuben together (vv2, 5, 16, 25), or Moses alone (vv6, 20, 29) — seven ‘saids’ in all. The one place the verb changes, v31’s ‘answered’ (vaya’anu, a different root), the subject is Gad and Reuben again. Compare Numbers 27:5-7 (already on these pages) — a strikingly similar scene, five petitioners bringing Moses a claim about land — where the very next step is ‘Moses brought their case before Jehovah,’ and Jehovah answers directly. Here Moses never refers the matter upward at all; the entire covenant, terms and penalty both, is negotiated and sealed by a human being, in Jehovah’s name but not in his voice.

⭐⭐ The condition is symmetrical, and it turns on a word this translation has used three times before without naming it. ‘You will be free before Jehovah, and before Israel’ (v22) is the identical root behind the sotah ordeal’s own acquittal — ‘be free from this water’ and ‘she shall be free,’ Numbers 5:19, 28 (already on these pages), and the husband’s own matching exemption at v31, ‘the man shall be free from iniquity.’ There, one woman’s status is pronounced in advance, contingent on a fact no one can yet check. Here it is a whole tribal confederation’s, doubled for a divine and a national audience at once.

⭐⭐ V23’s ‘your sin will find you out’ personifies sin a second time in this project, an entire book after its first appearance — and the shelf splits on exactly which image survives the trip. Genesis 4:7 (already on these pages) has sin ‘crouching at the door,’ its desire turned toward Cain; here the same word is the subject of a verb of FINDING, timtza. KJV, ASV, and Geneva all keep the detective image, ‘your sin will find you out’ — word for word identical across all three. NWT breaks from them: ‘your sin will catch up with you’ — and on this one point the ENTIRE Spanish shelf agrees with the NWT’s English rather than with the KJV’s: RV antigua ‘os alcanzará vuestro pecado,’ RV60 ‘vuestro pecado os alcanzará,’ and both editions of the TNM — ‘alcanzará’ is pursuit, not discovery. Both images trace to the identical Hebrew verb, matza, ‘to find, to reach’ — a sin that hunts you down and a sin that is merely uncovered are not quite the same threat, and the shelf has never agreed on which one Moses meant.

Numbers 32:25-32 — The deal is formalized, twice, and answered the same way twice

⭐ Moses does not simply accept the tribes’ word; he restates the whole condition to a THIRD party — Eleazar, Joshua, and the tribal heads (v28) — before he will let it stand, and the two tribes answer him a second time, verbatim in substance, at v31. The repetition is the safeguard: an oral agreement between Moses and two tribes alone would leave no one else positioned to enforce it once Moses himself is gone (already under his own death sentence since Numbers 27:12-14, already on these pages) — so the terms are formally handed to the two men who will actually lead the conquest.

⚠ V32’s closing line puts the chapter’s two key nouns back together: ‘the achuzzah of our nachalah,’ the holding OF our inheritance. The same phrase this translation’s own achuzzah entry has already flagged at Genesis 17:8 and 23:4 — an achuzzah is what Abraham asked for a grave in and what God promised for the whole land — now claimed, for the first time in this project, as something already possessed rather than merely promised.

Numbers 32:33-38 — The land is given, the towns are rebuilt, and two names are quietly changed

⭐ The half tribe of Manasseh appears here for the first time as a landholder in its own right, alongside Reuben and Gad, though it never asked — the request in vv1-5 came only from Gad and Reuben. Moses adds Manasseh’s half tribe to the grant at v33 with no explanation given; the reason surfaces only in the next section, once Machir and Jair’s own independent conquests (vv39-41) make plain that part of Manasseh had already been fighting for this same ground on its own account.

⚠ V38’s parenthetical — ‘Nebo, and Baal-meon — their names changed’ — names the fact without saying what the new names were, and the reason is not hard to guess. Nebo is the Babylonian god Nabu’s own name; Baal-meon means ‘lord of the dwelling,’ Baal being Canaan’s storm-god. Two of the nine towns Reuben and Gad set out to occupy carried a foreign god’s name on the sign, and the text records — without ceremony, in the middle of a building list — that Israel would not leave it there. Douay-Rheims, working from the Vulgate, keeps the same fact but respells both names to ‘Nabo’ and ‘Sabama’ for Nebo and Sibmah, its usual practice of transliterating through Latin rather than Hebrew.

⭐ The building lists in vv34-38 only partly match the wish-list in v3, and the mismatch is worth noticing rather than smoothing over. Dibon, Ataroth, Jazer, Heshbon, Elealeh and Nebo are exact repeats; Beth-nimrah, Sibmah and Baal-meon are plausible fuller or variant forms of v3’s Nimrah, Sebam and Beon; but Aroer, Atroth-shophan, Jogbehah, Kiriathaim and Beth-haran name places nobody asked for in v3 at all — the two tribes end up building more than they originally requested, not merely what they listed. Jazer is the one name on both lists this project can actually check against an old promise: this project’s own atlas entry, written at Numbers 21, already stated, in advance of any chapter proving it, that Jazer ‘is later allotted to Gad’; v35 is where that claim is finally checked against the text it was written to anticipate, and it holds.

Numbers 32:39-42 — A family conquest with no army, no war-tax, and no waiting for Moses

⭐⭐ Machir, Jair, and Nobah act entirely on their own, outside every term Moses just negotiated — no crossing-condition, no report to Eleazar, not even a stated command to go. ‘The children of Machir son of Manasseh went to Gilead and captured it’ (v39) reads as a private clan action, and Moses’s own response — simply handing Gilead to Machir after the fact (v40) — looks less like a grant than a ratification of ground already taken. This is very likely WHY the half tribe of Manasseh was added to the formal grant at v33 with no explanation there: its own men had already been fighting for this exact territory before the ink on the Reuben-and-Gad agreement was dry.

⚠ ‘Machir’ dwelling in Gilead at v40, generations after the man himself was born on Joseph’s own knees in Egypt (Genesis 50:23, already on these pages), is this book’s own idiom — the clan standing in for its founder, the way ‘Israel’ itself names both one man and a whole nation throughout this book. This chapter has already used the identical shorthand of Machir’s own descendants two chapters earlier: Numbers 27:1 traces Zelophehad — whose five daughters changed the inheritance law — four generations down this same line, son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh. The daughters who won a place in the inheritance and the clan that wins Gilead by conquest are the same family.

⚠ Havvoth-jair — literally ‘the tent-villages of Jair’ — outlives the man who named it by centuries, and this translation flags a genealogical difficulty it does not resolve. This Jair is called ‘son of Manasseh’ (v41), a contemporary of the conquest generation; but 1 Chronicles 2:21-23 (not yet on these pages) traces a different Jair — son of Segub, son of Hezron of Judah, by a daughter of Machir — who is ALSO credited with Havvoth-jair, in a genealogy that closes by calling the whole line ‘the sons of Machir the father of Gilead.’ Whether one man is remembered through two lines of descent, or two men several generations apart both held ground by this name, the text does not say, and this translation reports the difficulty rather than choosing a side.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. The Hebrew has four Masoretic paragraph breaks — after v4, v15, v19, and the chapter’s own close at v42 — and everything from v20 to the end runs as ONE unbroken scribal unit: the covenant terms, the formal charge to Eleazar and Joshua, the land grant, the town-building, and the independent conquests of Machir, Jair, and Nobah are never once paused between. Achuzzah is kept as ‘holding’ throughout (vv5, 22, 29, 32), matching the fixed rendering already established at Numbers 27:4 and in the dictionary entry itself; nachalah stays ‘inheritance’ (vv18, 19, 32), kept deliberately distinct from it. V7 carries a ketiv/qere on the verb ‘override’; this translation follows the read form, as always. ‘Little ones’ (taph) and ‘livestock’ (miqneh) both continue this project’s established renderings.

Library. Two new dictionary entries in both languages: chalutz, ‘armed,’ the word the whole covenant turns on; and naqah, ‘free, clear of guilt,’ extending a root this translation had already used three times at Numbers 5 without naming it. Two existing entries extended: chatta’t, with this chapter’s own personification of sin as something that FINDS a person, set beside its first appearance at Genesis 4:7; and tenu’ah’s own root, which lands here exactly as Numbers 30 promised it would. Three new encyclopedia entries in both languages: Gad, whose father’s deathbed blessing this chapter puts into action; Machir, Manasseh’s son and the ancestor of both Zelophehad’s daughters and the clan that conquers Gilead; and Jair, whose place-name survives him by centuries under a genealogy this translation does not fully untangle. Existing entries extended: Reuben, Gilead, Manasseh, Bashan, Sihon, Og, Jazer, the Amorites, Caleb, Joshua, and Eleazar all gained this chapter’s references; Gilead and Manasseh also gained their first NUMBERS-book reference of any kind, which is what lets this chapter’s own mentions of both actually resolve into links rather than sitting as plain text the way they have in every earlier Numbers chapter that named them.

Echoes paid — four. Numbers 30’s own note named this exact verse two chapters in advance and chose ‘overrides’ specifically so the two uses of heni’a would match — a promise this chapter keeps at vv7 and 9. Jacob’s deathbed oracle over Genesis 49:19 (already on these pages) — ‘Gad, a raiding band shall raid him’ — lands on the actual page as Gad volunteers to be Israel’s armed vanguard. This project’s own Jazer atlas entry, written at Numbers 21, carried the forward claim that the town would be ‘later allotted to Gad’; v35 is where it resolves. And Moses’s own retelling of Numbers 13-14 at vv8-12 reuses ‘filled up after me,’ the identical two Hebrew words that first excused Caleb alone, now stretched to cover Caleb and Joshua together.

Patterns worth carrying forward. First: this is the first extended legal negotiation of the whole book conducted with no direct divine speech at all — contrast Numbers 27’s near-identical scene, where Moses brings a land dispute before Jehovah and gets a direct answer; here he settles it himself, in Jehovah’s name but not his voice. Second: a covenant’s condition and its penalty can be built from the same root the text has already used for someone else’s acquittal — naqah moves from one woman’s ordeal to a whole confederation’s standing without changing what the word itself means. Third: a genealogical difficulty (Jair, vv39-42) is sometimes best served by being named rather than smoothed — two real traditions claim the same place-name, and forcing them into one story would cost more than it explains.

Echoes planted. Chalutz is flagged for its return at Deuteronomy 25:9-10 (not yet on these pages), the sandal-removal ceremony built from the identical root. Machir’s own line is flagged again at 1 Chronicles 2:21-23 (not yet on these pages) for whoever eventually untangles the two Jairs. And Jazer’s vineyards, already noted in this project’s own atlas entry, are still waiting on Isaiah 16 and Jeremiah 48 (neither yet on these pages) to pay off from the other direction.
