Verses 1–4 · the collection for the saints, the first day, the letters, and the journey to Jerusalem

“The collection for the saints.” tēs logeias tēs eis tous hagious, the collection for the holy ones. The noun logeia is the gathering of money for a purpose, and the saints are the believers of Jerusalem. The instruction is the same one Paul gave the congregations of Galatia. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now concerning the collection for the holy ones, you may follow the directions I gave to the congregations of Ga·laʹti·a.” The KJV “Now concerning the collection for the saints, as I have given order to the churches of Galatia, even so do ye.”

“On the first day of the week.” kata mian sabbatou, literally “on one of the sabbaths,” the Greek idiom for the first day of the week (the day after the sabbath). The phrase says each sets something aside and stores it up at home, so that collections are not made when Paul arrives. The note records only what the phrase says; the sentence is about storing money, not about a gathering. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “On the first day of every week, each of you should set something aside according to his own means.” The KJV “Upon the first day of the week let every one of you lay by him in store, as God hath prospered him.”

Verses 5–9 · the route through Macedonia, the stay in Ephesus until Pentecost, and the open door

“I will come after Macedonia.” the verb dierchomai, to go through. The route is given as a plan with a condition: Paul will come when he has gone through Macedonia, and he says he is going through it now. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But I will come to you when I have gone through Mac·e·doʹni·a, for I will be going through Mac·e·doʹni·a;” (the transliteration is the NWT’s). The KJV “Now I will come unto you, when I shall pass through Macedonia: for I do pass through Macedonia.”

“Until Pentecost.” heōs tēs pentēkostēs, until Pentecost. The festival is the Jewish feast, and the statement is the plan for the stay in Ephesus. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But I am remaining in Ephʹe·sus until the Festival of Pentecost.” The TNM 2019 “me quedaré en Éfeso hasta la Fiesta de Pentecostés”.

“A great and effective door.” thyra gar moi aneōgen megalē kai energēs, a great and effective door has opened to me. The door is the image of an opportunity for work, and the sentence says there are many who oppose. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “because a large door that leads to activity has been opened to me, but there are many opposers.” The KJV “For a great door and effectual is opened unto me, and there are many adversaries.”

Verses 10–12 · Timothy without fear, not to be despised, and Apollos who would not come now

“Without fear.” aphobōs, without fear. The adverb says Timothy is to be with them without fright. The sentence grounds the request in his work: he does the work of the Lord, as Paul does. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “make sure that he has nothing to fear while among you, for he is performing the work of Jehovah, just as I am.” The NWT prints the name Jehovah for kyrios here; the translation keeps “the Lord” because the sentence is about the work that Paul and Timothy do for the Lord; the note records the NWT reading. The KJV “for he worketh the work of the Lord, as I also do.”

“…despise him.” exoutheneō, to despise, to treat as nothing. The word is the one of 1:28, where God chooses what is despised. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “Ayúdenlo a seguir su camino en paz”

“Apollos.” polla parekalesa auton, I urged him strongly. The sentence says Paul urged Apollos to come and that Apollos did not intend to come now. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “I strongly urged him to come to you with the brothers. It was not his intention to come now, but he will come when he has the opportunity.”

Verses 13–14 · stay awake, stand firm, be manly and strong, and let all be done in love

“Stay awake, stand firm.” grēgoreite, stēkete en tē pistei. Four imperatives follow: be awake, stand in the faith, be men of courage, be strong. The verbs are the verbs of a soldier on watch and a line that holds. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Stay awake, stand firm in the faith, carry on in a manly way, grow mighty.” The KJV “Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong.”

“Let all be done in love.” en agapē, in love. The word agapē is the word of chapter 13, and the sentence closes the instructions with it. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Let everything you do be done with love.” The KJV “Let all your things be done with charity.”

Verses 15–18 · the household of Stephanas, the firstfruits of Achaia, and the three who made up for the absence

“The firstfruits of Achaia.” aparchē tēs Achaias, the firstfruits of Achaia. The household of Stephanas is named as the first of the believers in the province. The phrase tassō heautous, devoted themselves, is the verb for setting oneself in order for a service. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “the household of Stephʹa·nas is the firstfruits of A·chaʹia and that they devoted themselves to ministering to the holy ones.” The KJV “the house of Stephanas, that it is the firstfruits of Achaia, and that they have addicted themselves to the ministry of the saints.”

“Submit yourselves.” hypotassēsthe, submit. The verb is the one of 14:34, where the women are to be in subjection, and the sentence here uses it for submission to those who labor. The translation keeps the same verb in both places. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “Ustedes también sométanse a personas como esas y a todos los que cooperan y trabajan duro.”

“They refreshed my spirit.” anepausan gar to emon pneuma, they refreshed my spirit. The verb is the verb of rest and comfort. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For they have refreshed my spirit and yours.” The KJV “For they have refreshed my spirit and yours: therefore acknowledge ye them that are such.”

Verses 19–20 · the congregations of Asia, Aquila and Prisca with the congregation in their house, and the holy kiss

“The congregation in their house.” kat’ oikon autōn ekklēsia, the congregation in their house. The word ekklēsia is rendered “congregation” in every chapter of this translation; here it names a household gathering. The phrase is the NT’s own, and the translation prints it as it stands. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Aqʹui·la and Prisʹca together with the congregation that is in their house greet you heartily in the Lord.” The KJV “Aquila and Priscilla salute you much in the Lord, with the church that is in their house.”

“A holy kiss.” en philēmati hagiō, with a holy kiss. The sentence gives the kiss as the way the brothers greet one another. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Greet one another with a holy kiss.” The KJV “Greet ye one another with an holy kiss.”

Verses 21–24 · Paul’s own hand, the curse and Maranatha, the grace, and love in Christ Jesus

“In my own hand.” ho aspasmos tē emē cheiri Paulou, the greeting in my hand, Paul’s. The sentence marks the end of the letter as written by Paul himself, and the sentence marks the greeting as Paul’s own. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Here is my greeting, Paul’s, in my own hand.” The KJV “The salutation of me Paul with mine own hand.”

“Let him be accursed.” ētō anathema, let him be accursed (anathema). The word is the one of 12:3, where the speaker by God’s spirit does not say Jesus is accursed. The sentence pronounces the curse on the one who does not love the Lord. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “If anyone has no affection for the Lord, let him be accursed.” The KJV “If any man love not the Lord Jesus Christ, let him be Anathema Maranatha.”

“Marana tha.” The phrase is Aramaic, given in Greek letters, and it means “our Lord, come.” The translation keeps the Aramaic and gives its sense. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “O our Lord, come!” The TNM 2019 “¡Oh, Señor nuestro, ven!”

“The grace of the Lord Jesus.” hē charis tou kyriou Iēsou, the grace of the Lord Jesus. The letter closes with grace, and Paul’s love is with all of them in Christ Jesus. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “May the undeserved kindness of the Lord Jesus be with you.” The KJV “The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “congregation” for ekklēsia at 16:19, the fixed rendering of every NT chapter; “collection” for logeia at 16:1–2; “firstfruits” for aparchē at 16:15, the word of 15:20 and 15:23; “love” for agapē at 16:14 and 16:24, the word of chapter 13; “submit” for hypotassō at 16:16, the verb of 14:34.

Echoes paid. The saints of 1:2 return as the holy ones who receive the collection (16:1); the door and the many opposers (16:9) answer the hardship of the apostles in 4:9–13 and the danger of 15:30–32; the love of chapter 13 closes the letter (16:14, 16:24); the anathema of 12:3 returns at 16:22; the firstfruits of 15:20 and 15:23 return in the household of Stephanas (16:15); and the watch that Paul told the Corinthians to keep (16:13) closes the letter after the warnings of 10:12.

Echoes planted. Maranatha (16:22) is the Aramaic prayer that the letter ends on, and it is the last word of the letter.

This is the last chapter of 1 Corinthians. The next letter is 2 Corinthians 1, already on these pages.
