An alphabet psalm headed ‘A praise, of David’: the speaker will exalt and bless the name of his God the King forever; Jehovah is great and greatly to be praised, one generation lauding his works to another, gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and great in loyal love, good to all; his works give thanks and his loyal ones bless; they speak of the glory of his kingdom, a kingdom of all ages; he upholds all who fall and raises the bowed down; the eyes of all look to him and he opens his hand; he is righteous, near to all who call on him in truth, does the goodwill of those who fear him, guards all who love him and destroys all the wicked; and let all flesh bless his holy name forever and ever. The line for the letter nun is missing.
Headed ‘of David’ and undated by the psalm itself. ⚠ The chapter carries no event and no place; the heading is the only mark of its author.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Psalm 145:1–2 A praise, of David — I will exalt you, my God the King — every day I will bless you — forever and ever

⭐ An alphabet psalm. The lines begin with the letters of the Hebrew alphabet in order, alef at verse 1 (the heading stands before it), bet at 2, gimel at 3, dalet at 4, he at 5, vav at 6, zayin at 7, chet at 8, tet at 9, yod at 10, kaf at 11, lamed at 12, mem at 13, samekh at 14, ayin at 15, pe at 16, tsade at 17, qof at 18, resh at 19, shin at 20 and tav at 21. ⚠ One letter is missing: there is no nun between mem and samekh (see verse 13). The other alphabet psalms on these pages are Psalm 25 and Psalm 34, which run less cleanly than this one.

⭐ A praise. “A praise, of David”, tehillah ledavid, stands in this one verse: no other psalm is headed so, though the book is named for the plural of the same word, Tehillim (tehillah). The heading is part of verse 1 and is not numbered apart. The first line has the speaker raise a God he calls my God the King: the KJV has “I will extol thee, my God, O king”, the Douay “I will extol thee, O God my king”, the NIV “I will exalt you, my God the King” and the NWT 1984 “I will exalt you, O my God the King”; the Hebrew has the article, ha-Melekh, “the King”.

⭐ Bless, praise. The two verbs are different: avarkha, “I will bless you”, in verse 1, and ahallelah, “I will praise”, in verse 2, from the root of halal. The KJV has “Every day will I bless thee; and I will praise thy name for ever and ever”; the NIV prints “I will praise your name for ever and ever” in verse 1, so that its “praise” stands for bless, and “Every day I will praise you and extol your name” in verse 2, where its “extol” stands for halal. Le’olam va’ed, “forever and ever”, is in verse 1 and verse 2, and it comes back in the last verse, so the psalm is held between the same words at both ends.

Verses 3–7 · Psalm 145:3–7 Great is Jehovah — generation to generation — the splendor of your majesty — they speak, and I recount

⭐ Great is Jehovah. “Great is Jehovah, and greatly to be praised” stands in four verses: here, Psalm 48:1 and Psalm 96:4 (the Hebrew Bible numbers them 48:2 and 96:4) and 1 Chronicles 16:25, none of the others translated here yet. The KJV has “Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised”, the ASV “Great is Jehovah, and greatly to be praised”, the NIV “Great is the Lord and most worthy of praise”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is great and very much to be praised” and the TLB “Great is Jehovah! Greatly praise him!”. “to his greatness there is no searching out” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “his greatness is unsearchable”, the Geneva “his greatness is incomprehensible” and the Douay “of his greatness there is no end”. ⚠ The root gdl runs through the psalm: gadol and ligdulato here, gedulatekha at verse 6, and gedal-chesed, “great in loyal love”, at verse 8.

⭐ Generation to generation. “One generation will laud your works to another” stands in this one verse: the KJV has “One generation shall praise thy works to another, and shall declare thy mighty acts”, the NIV “One generation commends your works to another; they tell of your mighty acts”, the NWT 1984 “Generation after generation will commend your works”, the Douay “Generation and generation shall praise thy works” and the TLB “Let each generation tell its children what glorious things he does”.

⚠ Who speaks? The I and the they alternate: “they will laud” (4), “I will ponder” (5), “they will speak” (6), “I will recount” (6), “they will pour out” (7). “the splendor of the glory of your majesty” stands in this one verse, and the verb that governs it, asichah, is in the first person: the KJV has “I will speak of the glorious honour of thy majesty”, the ASV “Of the glorious majesty of thine honor, And of thy wondrous works, will I meditate”, the NWT 1984 “I will make my concern” and the Geneva “I will meditate of the beauty of thy glorious majesty”; the NIV has “They speak of the glorious splendor of your majesty” and the Douay “They shall speak of the magnificence of the glory of thy holiness”, which turn the I into a they.

⚠ Your greatness, or your great deeds. Verse 6 ends with a written and a read form: gedulotekha, “your great deeds”, as written, and gedulatekha, “your greatness”, as read. The KJV has “I will declare thy greatness” and the TLB “I will proclaim your greatness”, with the read form; the NIV has “I will proclaim your great deeds”, with the written. In verse 7 what is poured out is zekher rav tuvkha: the KJV has “They shall abundantly utter the memory of thy great goodness, and shall sing of thy righteousness”, the NWT 1984 “they will bubble over” and the Douay “They shall publish the memory of the abundance of thy sweetness”. The word for singing, yerannenu, is a cry of joy (rinnah).

Verses 8–10 · Psalm 145:8–10 Gracious and compassionate — slow to anger, great in loyal love — good to all — your works give thanks, your loyal ones bless

⭐ The old formula. “slow to anger and great in loyal love” stands in this one verse, and it is the formula the God of Exodus gave of himself (Exodus 34:6, now on these pages) with gedal in place of rav, “great” for “abundant”. “great in loyal love” stands in three verses: Genesis 19:19, Numbers 14:19 and here. Moses quotes the formula back to God at Numbers 14:18 (now on these pages). The KJV has “The Lord is gracious, and full of compassion; slow to anger, and of great mercy”, the ASV “Jehovah is gracious, and merciful; Slow to anger, and of great lovingkindness”, the NIV “The Lord is gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and rich in love”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is gracious and merciful, Slow to anger and great in loving-kindness”, the Douay “The Lord is gracious and merciful: patient and plenteous in mercy” and the TLB “Jehovah is kind and merciful, slow to get angry, full of love”.

⭐ Good to all. “Good is Jehovah to all” stands in this one verse, and “his compassions are over all his works” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “The Lord is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works”, the NIV “The Lord is good to all; he has compassion on all he has made” and the Douay “The Lord is sweet to all”. “All”, kol, is the psalm’s word: it is in nearly every verse from here to the last.

⚠ Loyal love, three ways. The root of chesed (chesed) is at verse 8, gedal-chesed; at verse 10, chasidekha, “your loyal ones” (chasid); and at verse 17, chasid, said of God himself. The shelf prints them differently every time. Verse 10: the KJV has “All thy works shall praise thee, O Lord; and thy saints shall bless thee”, the ASV “All thy works shall give thanks unto thee, O Jehovah; And thy saints shall bless thee”, the NIV “All your works praise you, Lord; your faithful people extol you”, the NWT 1984 “All your works will laud you, O Jehovah, And your loyal ones will bless you” and the TLB “All living things shall thank you, Lord, and your people will bless you”. The first verb is yodukha, “will give thanks to you”, which the ASV keeps as “give thanks unto thee”.

Verses 11–13 · Psalm 145:11–13 The glory of your kingdom — to make known to the sons of men — a kingdom of all ages — and the missing nun

⭐ His kingdom, your kingdom. Verses 11–13 are about the kingdom, malkhut, four times, and the person changes in the middle: “your kingdom” (11), “his mighty acts” and “his kingdom” (12), “your kingdom” (13). The KJV has “To make known to the sons of men his mighty acts, and the glorious majesty of his kingdom” and the NWT 1984 “To make known to the sons of men his mighty acts And the glory of the splendor of his kingship”; the NIV smooths it with “so that all people may know of your mighty acts and the glorious splendor of your kingdom”.

⭐ A kingdom of all ages. Malkhutkha malkhut kol olamim: the KJV has “Thy kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and thy dominion endureth throughout all generations”, the NWT 1984 “Your kingship is a kingship for all times indefinite”, the Douay “Thy kingdom is a kingdom of all ages” and the TLB “For your kingdom never ends. You rule generation after generation”. The same two lines are said in Aramaic by Nebuchadnezzar and by the angel: “His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion is from generation to generation” (Daniel 4:3, the Hebrew Bible’s 3:33; and Daniel 4:34), and “his kingdom is an everlasting kingdom” (Daniel 7:27), all now on these pages.

⚠ The missing nun. Verse 13 begins with mem and verse 14 with samekh; the line for nun is not in the Hebrew text printed above. A line that begins with nun is found in the Greek, in the Syriac and in a Dead Sea scroll of the Psalms, ne’eman Yehovah bidvarav vechasid bekhol ma’asav, “faithful is Jehovah in his words, and loyal in all his works”; the second half of it is also the second half of verse 17. Two versions on the shelf print it inside verse 13: the NIV “Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and your dominion endures through all generations. The Lord is trustworthy in all he promises and faithful in all he does”, and the Douay “The Lord is faithful in all his words: and holy in all his works”. The KJV, the ASV and the NWT 1984 do not. This page prints the Hebrew as it stands, with the gap, and does not supply the line.

Verses 14–16 · Psalm 145:14–16 Jehovah upholds all who fall — the eyes of all look to you — you open your hand

⭐ The fallen, the bowed down. “raises up all who are bowed down” stands in this one verse, and “the bowed down”, with the article, stands in this one verse. The KJV has “The Lord upholdeth all that fall, and raiseth up all those that be bowed down”, the NIV “The Lord upholds all who fall and lifts up all who are bowed down”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is giving support to all who are falling, And is raising up all who are bowed down”, the Douay “The Lord lifteth up all that fall: and setteth up all that are cast down” and the TLB “The Lord lifts the fallen and those bent beneath their loads”. The raising is zoqef: a person bent double stood straight, in Luke 13:13 (now on these pages).

⭐ The eyes of all. “the eyes of all look to you with hope” stands in this one verse; the verb is sabar, to wait with hope. The KJV has “The eyes of all wait upon thee; and thou givest them their meat in due season” (“meat” is food), the NIV “The eyes of all look to you, and you give them their food at the proper time”, the NWT 1984 “To you the eyes of all look hopefully, And you are giving them their food in its season”, the Douay “The eyes of all hope in thee, O Lord” and the TLB “The eyes of all mankind look up to you for help; you give them their food as they need it”. It is the line Matthew 6:26 answers when it says the heavenly Father feeds the birds (now on these pages); Psalm 104:27–28 says nearly the same words.

⚠ An open hand, and what it fills. “You open your hand, and satisfy every living thing” stands in this one verse, and the last word is ratson (ratson), which the site translates “goodwill”; it is also the word of verse 19. The shelf splits: the KJV has “satisfiest the desire of every living thing”, the NIV “satisfy the desires of every living thing”, the NWT 1984 “satisfying the desire of every living thing”, the Geneva “fillest all things living of thy good pleasure”, the Douay “fillest with blessing every living creature” and the TLB “You constantly satisfy the hunger and thirst of every living thing”. Whether it is what the living creature wants, or what the open hand is pleased to give, the Hebrew says only ratson.

Verses 17–21 · Psalm 145:17–21 Righteous in all his ways — near to all who call — he will hear their cry — he guards, he destroys — all flesh will bless

⚠ Righteous, and what? “Righteous is Jehovah in all his ways, and loyal in all his works” stands in this one verse, and the second word, chasid, is printed as “holy”, “gracious”, “faithful”, “loyal” and “kindness”, according to the version: the KJV has “The Lord is righteous in all his ways, and holy in all his works”, the ASV “Jehovah is righteous in all his ways, And gracious in all his works”, the NIV “The Lord is righteous in all his ways and faithful in all he does”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is righteous in all his ways And loyal in all his works”, the Douay “The Lord is just in all his ways: and holy in all his works” and the TLB “The Lord is fair in everything he does and full of kindness”. It is the word of verse 10 said of him: the loyal ones bless the One who is himself chasid.

⭐ Near to all who call. “Near is Jehovah to all who call on him” stands in this one verse, and “to all who call on him in truth” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “The Lord is nigh unto all them that call upon him, to all that call upon him in truth”, the NIV “The Lord is near to all who call on him, to all who call on him in truth”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is near to all those calling upon him, To all those who call upon him in trueness” and the TLB “He is close to all who call on him sincerely”. The nearness is the one Moses asked, “For what great nation has a god so near to it…” (Deuteronomy 4:7), and the one of Psalm 34:18, near to the brokenhearted, both now on these pages.

⭐ He will do, he will hear. The goodwill (ratson) of those who fear him he will do: the KJV has “He will fulfil the desire of them that fear him: he also will hear their cry, and will save them”, the NIV “He fulfills the desires of those who fear him; he hears their cry and saves them”, the NWT 1984 “The desire of those fearing him he will perform, And their cry for help he will hear, and he will save them” and the Douay “He will do the will of them that fear him: and he will hear their prayer, and save them”. The verbs are ya’aseh, yishma, yoshi’em: do, hear, save.

⭐ Guards, destroys. “Jehovah guards all who love him” stands in this one verse, and the two verbs are almost the same word: shomer, “guards”, and yashmid, “will destroy”, differ by one letter, resh for dalet. The KJV has “The Lord preserveth all them that love him: but all the wicked will he destroy”, the NIV “The Lord watches over all who love him, but all the wicked he will destroy”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah is guarding all those loving him, But all the wicked ones he will annihilate” and the TLB “He protects all those who love him, but destroys the wicked”. The psalm that said “all” a dozen times has one group it does not.

⭐ The circle closes. The heading was tehillah, and the last verse is tehillat Yehovah, “the praise of Jehovah”, spoken by my mouth; and what the speaker said he would do in verse 1, “I will bless your name forever and ever”, he now asks all flesh to do: “let all flesh bless his holy name” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “My mouth shall speak the praise of the Lord: and let all flesh bless his holy name for ever and ever”, the NIV “My mouth will speak in praise of the Lord. Let every creature praise his holy name for ever and ever” (“praise” for bless again), the NWT 1984 “let all flesh bless his holy name to time indefinite, even forever” and the TLB “I will praise the Lord and call on all men everywhere to bless his holy name forever and forever”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “bless” for barakh (verses 1, 2 in part, 10, 21) and “praise” for halal and tehillah; “great” for the root gdl at verses 3, 6 and 8; “loyal love” for chesed and “loyal” for chasid at verses 8, 10 and 17; “kingdom” for malkhut and “dominion” for memshalah at verses 11–13; “goodwill” for ratson at verses 16 and 19; shomer and yashmid as “guards” and “destroys” at verse 20. Jehovah is written nine times, at verses 3, 8, 9, 10, 14, 17, 18, 20 and 21; Elohai, “my God”, once, at verse 1.

Echoes paid. Exodus 34:6; Numbers 14:18; Daniel 4:3, 4:34 and 7:27; Luke 13:13; Matthew 6:26; Deuteronomy 4:7; Psalm 34:18.

Echoes planted. Psalm 48:1, 96:4 and 104:27–28; 1 Chronicles 16:25; Genesis 19:19. None of these is translated here yet except those named as paid above.
