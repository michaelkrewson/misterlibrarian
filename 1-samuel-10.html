Samuel anoints Saul with a flask of oil as leader over Jehovah's inheritance and gives him three signs: men at Rachel's tomb with news of the donkeys, three men at the oak of Tabor who give him bread, and a band of prophets at Gibeah of God. God gives Saul another heart, the spirit rushes on him, and he prophesies. At Mizpah Samuel tells the people they have rejected God; Benjamin, the Matrites and Saul are taken, Saul is found hiding among the baggage, and the people shout 'Long live the king!' Samuel writes the due of the kingship in a book.
The day after Saul's anointing, and the assembly at Mizpah that follows (1 Samuel 10:9, 17).

And Samuel took the flask of oil and poured it on his head, and kissed him, and said: ‘Is it not that Jehovah has anointed you as leader over his inheritance?note
Y Samuel tomó el frasco de aceite y lo derramó sobre su cabeza, y lo besó, y dijo: «¿No es que Jehová te ha ungido como caudillo sobre su heredad?
2
בְּלֶכְתְּךָ הַיּוֹם, מֵעִמָּדִי, וּמָצָאתָ שְׁנֵי אֲנָשִׁים עִם-קְבֻרַת רָחֵל בִּגְבוּל בִּנְיָמִן, בְּצֶלְצַח; וְאָמְרוּ אֵלֶיךָ, נִמְצְאוּ הָאֲתֹנוֹת אֲשֶׁר הָלַכְתָּ לְבַקֵּשׁ, וְהִנֵּה נָטַשׁ אָבִיךָ אֶת-דִּבְרֵי הָאֲתֹנוֹת, וְדָאַג לָכֶם לֵאמֹר מָה אֶעֱשֶׂה לִבְנִי.
When you go from me today, you will find two men by Rachel’s tomb, in the territory of Benjamin, at Zelzah; and they will say to you, The she-donkeys that you went to look for have been found; and look — your father has given up the matter of the she-donkeys and is anxious about you, saying, What shall I do for my son?note
Cuando te vayas hoy de mi lado, hallarás dos hombres junto al sepulcro de Raquel, en el territorio de Benjamín, en Selsá; y te dirán: Se han hallado las asnas que fuiste a buscar; y mira, tu padre ha dejado el asunto de las asnas y se inquieta por ustedes, diciendo: ¿Qué haré por mi hijo?
3
וְחָלַפְתָּ מִשָּׁם וָהָלְאָה, וּבָאתָ עַד-אֵלוֹן תָּבוֹר, וּמְצָאוּךָ שָּׁם שְׁלֹשָׁה אֲנָשִׁים, עֹלִים אֶל-הָאֱלֹהִים בֵּית-אֵל; אֶחָד נֹשֵׂא שְׁלֹשָׁה גְדָיִים, וְאֶחָד נֹשֵׂא שְׁלֹשֶׁת כִּכְּרוֹת לֶחֶם, וְאֶחָד, נֹשֵׂא נֵבֶל-יָיִן.
And you shall go on from there and further, and come to the oak of Tabor; and three men going up to God at Bethel will meet you there, one carrying three young goats, one carrying three loaves of bread, and one carrying a skin of wine.note
Y pasarás de allí más adelante y llegarás a la encina de Tabor; y allí te encontrarán tres hombres que suben a Dios a Betel, uno llevando tres cabritos, otro llevando tres panes y otro llevando un odre de vino.
4
וְשָׁאֲלוּ לְךָ, לְשָׁלוֹם; וְנָתְנוּ לְךָ שְׁתֵּי-לֶחֶם, וְלָקַחְתָּ מִיָּדָם.
And they will ask after your welfare and give you two loaves of bread, and you shall take them from their hand.note
Y te preguntarán por tu bienestar y te darán dos panes, y los tomarás de su mano.
5
אַחַר כֵּן, תָּבוֹא גִּבְעַת הָאֱלֹהִים, אֲשֶׁר-שָׁם, נְצִבֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים; וִיהִי כְבֹאֲךָ שָׁם הָעִיר, וּפָגַעְתָּ חֶבֶל נְבִאִים יֹרְדִים מֵהַבָּמָה, וְלִפְנֵיהֶם נֵבֶל וְתֹף וְחָלִיל וְכִנּוֹר, וְהֵמָּה מִתְנַבְּאִים.
After that you will come to Gibeah of God, where the Philistines’ garrisons are; and when you come there, to the city, you will meet a band of prophets coming down from the high place, with harp and tambourine and flute and lyre before them, and they will be prophesying.note
Después de eso llegarás a Guibeá de Dios, donde están las guarniciones de los filisteos; y cuando entres allí, en la ciudad, encontrarás una banda de profetas que bajan del lugar alto, y delante de ellos arpa, pandero, flauta y lira, y ellos profetizando.
6
וְצָלְחָה עָלֶיךָ רוּחַ יְהוָה, וְהִתְנַבִּיתָ עִמָּם; וְנֶהְפַּכְתָּ, לְאִישׁ אַחֵר.
And the spirit of Jehovah will rush on you, and you will prophesy with them, and be turned into another man.note
Y el espíritu de Jehová irrumpirá sobre ti, y profetizarás con ellos, y serás cambiado en otro hombre.
7
וְהָיָה, כִּי תבאינה תָבֹאנָה הָאֹתוֹת הָאֵלֶּה--לָךְ:  עֲשֵׂה לְךָ אֲשֶׁר תִּמְצָא יָדֶךָ, כִּי הָאֱלֹהִים עִמָּךְ.
And when these signs come to you, do for yourself what your hand finds, for God is with you.note
Y cuando te vengan estas señales, haz lo que tu mano halle, porque Dios está contigo.
8
וְיָרַדְתָּ לְפָנַי, הַגִּלְגָּל, וְהִנֵּה אָנֹכִי יֹרֵד אֵלֶיךָ, לְהַעֲלוֹת עֹלוֹת לִזְבֹּחַ זִבְחֵי שְׁלָמִים; שִׁבְעַת יָמִים תּוֹחֵל, עַד-בּוֹאִי אֵלֶיךָ, וְהוֹדַעְתִּי לְךָ, אֵת אֲשֶׁר תַּעֲשֶׂה.
And you shall go down before me to Gilgal; and look — I am coming down to you to offer up burnt offerings, to sacrifice sacrifices of peace offerings. Seven days you shall wait, until I come to you and let you know what you shall do.’note
Y bajarás delante de mí a Gilgal; y mira, yo bajo a ti para ofrecer holocaustos y sacrificar sacrificios de paz. Siete días esperarás, hasta que yo venga a ti y te haga saber lo que has de hacer».
9
וְהָיָה, כְּהַפְנֹתוֹ שִׁכְמוֹ לָלֶכֶת מֵעִם שְׁמוּאֵל, וַיַּהֲפָךְ-לוֹ אֱלֹהִים, לֵב אַחֵר; וַיָּבֹאוּ כָּל-הָאֹתוֹת הָאֵלֶּה, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא.  {ס}
And as he turned his shoulder to go from Samuel, God turned his heart into another heart; and all these signs came about that day.note
Y al volver él el hombro para irse de Samuel, Dios le cambió el corazón en otro corazón; y todas estas señales sucedieron aquel día.
10
וַיָּבֹאוּ שָׁם הַגִּבְעָתָה, וְהִנֵּה חֶבֶל-נְבִאִים לִקְרָאתוֹ; וַתִּצְלַח עָלָיו רוּחַ אֱלֹהִים, וַיִּתְנַבֵּא בְּתוֹכָם.
And they came there, to Gibeah, and look — a band of prophets met him; and the spirit of God rushed on him, and he prophesied among them.note
Y llegaron allá, a Guibeá, y mira, una banda de profetas le salió al encuentro; y el espíritu de Dios irrumpió sobre él, y profetizó en medio de ellos.
11
וַיְהִי, כָּל-יוֹדְעוֹ מֵאִתְּמוֹל שִׁלְשֹׁם, וַיִּרְאוּ, וְהִנֵּה עִם-נְבִאִים נִבָּא:  {ס}  וַיֹּאמֶר הָעָם אִישׁ אֶל-רֵעֵהוּ, מַה-זֶּה הָיָה לְבֶן-קִישׁ--הֲגַם שָׁאוּל, בַּנְּבִיאִים.
And all who had known him before saw, and look — he was prophesying with prophets; and the people said each to his neighbour: ‘What is this that has happened to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?’note
Y todos los que lo conocían de antes vieron, y mira, profetizaba con profetas; y el pueblo decía cada uno a su prójimo: «¿Qué le ha pasado al hijo de Cis? ¿También Saúl entre los profetas?».
12
וַיַּעַן אִישׁ מִשָּׁם וַיֹּאמֶר, וּמִי אֲבִיהֶם; עַל-כֵּן הָיְתָה לְמָשָׁל, הֲגַם שָׁאוּל בַּנְּבִאִים.
And a man from there answered and said: ‘And who is their father?’ Therefore it became a proverb: ‘Is Saul also among the prophets?’note
Y un hombre de allí respondió y dijo: «¿Y quién es el padre de ellos?». Por eso se hizo proverbio: «¿También Saúl entre los profetas?».
13
וַיְכַל, מֵהִתְנַבּוֹת, וַיָּבֹא, הַבָּמָה.
And he finished prophesying, and came to the high place.note
Y acabó de profetizar, y llegó al lugar alto.
14
וַיֹּאמֶר דּוֹד שָׁאוּל אֵלָיו וְאֶל-נַעֲרוֹ, אָן הֲלַכְתֶּם; וַיֹּאמֶר, לְבַקֵּשׁ אֶת-הָאֲתֹנוֹת, וַנִּרְאֶה כִי-אַיִן, וַנָּבוֹא אֶל-שְׁמוּאֵל.
And Saul’s uncle said to him and to his servant: ‘Where did you go?’ And he said: ‘To look for the she-donkeys; and when we saw that they were nowhere, we came to Samuel.’note
Y el tío de Saúl le dijo a él y a su criado: «¿Adónde fueron?». Y él dijo: «A buscar las asnas; y como vimos que no estaban por ninguna parte, fuimos a Samuel».
15
וַיֹּאמֶר, דּוֹד שָׁאוּל:  הַגִּידָה-נָּא לִי, מָה-אָמַר לָכֶם שְׁמוּאֵל.
And Saul’s uncle said: ‘Tell me, please, what Samuel said to you.’note
Y el tío de Saúl dijo: «Dime, por favor, qué les dijo Samuel».
16
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל, אֶל-דּוֹדוֹ, הַגֵּד הִגִּיד לָנוּ, כִּי נִמְצְאוּ הָאֲתֹנוֹת; וְאֶת-דְּבַר הַמְּלוּכָה לֹא-הִגִּיד לוֹ, אֲשֶׁר אָמַר שְׁמוּאֵל.  {פ}
And Saul said to his uncle: ‘He told us plainly that the she-donkeys had been found.’ But the matter of the kingship, of which Samuel had spoken, he did not tell him.note
Y Saúl dijo a su tío: «Nos declaró claramente que las asnas se habían hallado». Pero del asunto del reino, del que Samuel había hablado, no le dijo nada.
17
וַיַּצְעֵק שְׁמוּאֵל אֶת-הָעָם, אֶל-יְהוָה הַמִּצְפָּה.
And Samuel called the people together to Jehovah at Mizpah;note
Y Samuel convocó al pueblo ante Jehová en Mizpá;
18
וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל,  {פ} כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אָנֹכִי הֶעֱלֵיתִי אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, מִמִּצְרָיִם; וָאַצִּיל אֶתְכֶם, מִיַּד מִצְרַיִם, וּמִיַּד כָּל-הַמַּמְלָכוֹת, הַלֹּחֲצִים אֶתְכֶם.
and he said to the sons of Israel: ‘Thus says Jehovah the God of Israel: It was I who brought Israel up out of Egypt, and delivered you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of all the kingdoms that oppressed you.note
y dijo a los hijos de Israel: «Así dice Jehová, el Dios de Israel: Yo fui quien hizo subir a Israel de Egipto, y los libré a ustedes de la mano de Egipto y de la mano de todos los reinos que los oprimían.
19
וְאַתֶּם הַיּוֹם מְאַסְתֶּם אֶת-אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר-הוּא מוֹשִׁיעַ לָכֶם מִכָּל-רָעוֹתֵיכֶם וְצָרֹתֵיכֶם, וַתֹּאמְרוּ לוֹ, כִּי-מֶלֶךְ תָּשִׂים עָלֵינוּ; וְעַתָּה, הִתְיַצְּבוּ לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, לְשִׁבְטֵיכֶם, וּלְאַלְפֵיכֶם.
But you today have rejected your God, who saves you out of all your evils and your distresses; and you have said to him, A king you shall set over us. Now present yourselves before Jehovah by your tribes and by your thousands.’note
Pero ustedes hoy han rechazado a su Dios, que los salva de todos sus males y sus angustias; y le han dicho: Un rey pondrás sobre nosotros. Ahora, pues, preséntense delante de Jehová por sus tribus y por sus millares».
20
וַיַּקְרֵב שְׁמוּאֵל, אֵת כָּל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיִּלָּכֵד, שֵׁבֶט בִּנְיָמִן.
And Samuel brought all the tribes of Israel near, and the tribe of Benjamin was taken.note
Y Samuel hizo acercarse a todas las tribus de Israel, y fue tomada la tribu de Benjamín.
21
וַיַּקְרֵב אֶת-שֵׁבֶט בִּנְיָמִן, לְמִשְׁפְּחֹתָו, וַתִּלָּכֵד, מִשְׁפַּחַת הַמַּטְרִי; וַיִּלָּכֵד שָׁאוּל בֶּן-קִישׁ, וַיְבַקְשֻׁהוּ וְלֹא נִמְצָא.
And he brought the tribe of Benjamin near by its clans, and the clan of the Matrites was taken; and Saul son of Kish was taken. And they looked for him, but he was not found.note
E hizo acercarse a la tribu de Benjamín por sus clanes, y fue tomado el clan de los matritas; y fue tomado Saúl hijo de Cis. Y lo buscaron, pero no fue hallado.
22
וַיִּשְׁאֲלוּ-עוֹד, בַּיהוָה, הֲבָא עוֹד, הֲלֹם אִישׁ;  {ס}  וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, הִנֵּה-הוּא נֶחְבָּא אֶל-הַכֵּלִים.
And they asked again of Jehovah: ‘Is there yet a man to come here?’ And Jehovah said: ‘Look — he has hidden himself among the baggage.’note
Y preguntaron otra vez a Jehová: «¿Ha de venir aún un hombre aquí?». Y Jehová dijo: «Mira, se ha escondido entre el equipaje».
23
וַיָּרֻצוּ וַיִּקָּחֻהוּ מִשָּׁם, וַיִּתְיַצֵּב בְּתוֹךְ הָעָם; וַיִּגְבַּהּ, מִכָּל-הָעָם, מִשִּׁכְמוֹ, וָמָעְלָה.
And they ran and took him from there; and he stood among the people, and he was taller than all the people from his shoulders upward.note
Y corrieron y lo tomaron de allí; y se puso en medio del pueblo, y era más alto que todo el pueblo de los hombros arriba.
24
וַיֹּאמֶר שְׁמוּאֵל אֶל-כָּל-הָעָם, הַרְּאִיתֶם אֲשֶׁר בָּחַר-בּוֹ יְהוָה, כִּי אֵין כָּמֹהוּ, בְּכָל-הָעָם; וַיָּרִעוּ כָל-הָעָם וַיֹּאמְרוּ, יְחִי הַמֶּלֶךְ.  {ס}
And Samuel said to all the people: ‘Do you see him whom Jehovah has chosen, that there is none like him among all the people?’ And all the people shouted and said: ‘Long live the king!’note
Y Samuel dijo a todo el pueblo: «¿Ven al que ha escogido Jehová, que no hay nadie como él en todo el pueblo?». Y todo el pueblo gritó y dijo: «¡Viva el rey!».
25
וַיְדַבֵּר שְׁמוּאֵל אֶל-הָעָם, אֵת מִשְׁפַּט הַמְּלֻכָה, וַיִּכְתֹּב בַּסֵּפֶר, וַיַּנַּח לִפְנֵי יְהוָה; וַיְשַׁלַּח שְׁמוּאֵל אֶת-כָּל-הָעָם, אִישׁ לְבֵיתוֹ.
And Samuel spoke to the people the due of the kingship, and wrote it in a book, and laid it up before Jehovah. And Samuel sent all the people away, each man to his house.note
Y Samuel dijo al pueblo el derecho del reino, y lo escribió en un libro, y lo depositó delante de Jehová. Y Samuel despidió a todo el pueblo, cada uno a su casa.
26
וְגַם-שָׁאוּל--הָלַךְ לְבֵיתוֹ, גִּבְעָתָה; וַיֵּלְכוּ עִמּוֹ--הַחַיִל, אֲשֶׁר-נָגַע אֱלֹהִים בְּלִבָּם.
And Saul too went to his house, to Gibeah; and with him went the valiant men whose hearts God had touched.note
Y también Saúl se fue a su casa, a Guibeá; y fueron con él los valientes cuyo corazón Dios había tocado.
27
וּבְנֵי בְלִיַּעַל אָמְרוּ, מַה-יֹּשִׁעֵנוּ זֶה, וַיִּבְזֻהוּ, וְלֹא-הֵבִיאוּ לוֹ מִנְחָה; וַיְהִי, כְּמַחֲרִישׁ.  {פ}
But sons of worthlessness said: ‘How will this one save us?’ And they despised him, and brought him no gift. But he was like one keeping silent.note
Pero unos hijos de la nada dijeron: «¿Cómo nos va a salvar este?». Y lo despreciaron, y no le trajeron presente. Pero él estaba como quien calla.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–8 · Saul anointed — the flask of oil, a kiss, “leader over his inheritance”, and three signs: Rachel’s tomb, the oak of Tabor, and a band of prophets at Gibeah of God

The flask and the kiss. “Samuel took the flask of oil and poured it on his head, and kissed him”: “the flask of oil” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and twice at the anointing of Jehu (2 Kings 9:1, 3). The title is the one Jehovah gave the day before, nagid, “leader” (1 Samuel 9:16, now on these pages), now “over his inheritance”. The Greek is longer, adding that Saul will rule the Lord’s people and save them from their enemies round about, “and this shall be the sign to you”. The Douay has the longer text too, “and thou shalt deliver his people out of the hands of their enemies”; the ASV has “Is it not that Jehovah hath anointed thee to be prince over his inheritance?”; the NWT 1984 “Is it not because Jehovah has anointed you as a leader over his inheritance?”. Josephus calls it “the holy oil” and has Samuel say, “Know that you are king, chosen by God” (Antiquities 6.54, numbered 6.4.2 in Whiston).

Rachel’s tomb. The first sign is “two men by Rachel’s tomb, in the territory of Benjamin, at Zelzah”. Genesis buries Rachel “on the way to Ephrath — that is, Bethlehem” (Genesis 35:19, now on these pages), south of Jerusalem, while this verse puts her tomb on Benjamin’s border to the north; the two are not reconciled (Ramah). Zelzah is named nowhere else, and the Greek reads the word as a verb, two men “leaping greatly”. The father has “given up the matter of the she-donkeys and is anxious about you”, as Saul feared in 9:5. The ASV has “by Rachel’s sepulchre, in the border of Benjamin at Zelzah”; the NWT 1984 “close by the tomb of Rachel in the territory of Benjamin”. Josephus puts the three men first and Rachel’s tomb second (Antiquities 6.55–56).

The oak of Tabor. At “the oak of Tabor” (an oak or great tree, not the mountain of that name in the north) three men “going up to God at Bethel”, with goats, bread and wine for the sanctuary, give Saul two of their loaves. A skin of wine is nevel yayin; two verses later nevel is a harp. The ASV has “thou shalt come to the oak of Tabor”; the KJV “thou shalt come to the plain of Tabor”; the NIV “the great tree of Tabor”.

Gibeah of God. The third sign is at “Gibeah of God, where the Philistines’ garrisons are”, the garrisons that seemed ruled out by “they did not come again into the territory of Israel” (1 Samuel 7:13, now on these pages); the Greek takes the word as a man’s name, “Nasib the Philistine”. There Saul will meet “a band of prophets”, a chevel, a cord or a company, coming down from the high place with harp, tambourine, flute and lyre, “prophesying”, the ecstatic speech of the seventy elders who “prophesied — and did not continue” (Numbers 11:25, now on these pages). “The spirit of Jehovah will rush on you”: tsalach, the verb of the spirit on Samson (Judges 14:6; 15:14, both now on these pages). Then “do for yourself what your hand finds”, Zebul’s words to Abimelech for seizing a chance (Judges 9:33, now on these pages), and “seven days you shall wait” at Gilgal, an instruction the book will return to (1 Samuel 13:8, now on these pages). The ASV has “a band of prophets coming down from the high place with a psaltery, and a timbrel, and a pipe, and a harp”; the NWT 1984 “while they are speaking as prophets”.

Verses 9–16 · “Is Saul also among the prophets?” — another heart, the spirit rushes on Saul, “who is their father?”, and an uncle who is not told

Another heart. “As he turned his shoulder to go”: shikhmo, the shoulder from which he stood taller than everyone (9:2). “God turned his heart into another heart”: hafakh, the verb of verse 6, “be turned into another man”; “another heart” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “God gave him another heart”; the NWT 1984 “God began changing the heart of his into another”.

“Is Saul also among the prophets?” At Gibeah “the spirit of God rushed on him, and he prophesied among them”, and those who knew him ask, “What is this that has happened to the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets?” The question stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, each spelling “prophets” differently: verses 11 and 12 here, and a second story of Saul among the prophets at Ramah (1 Samuel 19:24). It “became a proverb”, a mashal. The NIV has “Is Saul also among the prophets?”; the TLB “Is Saul a prophet too?”

“And who is their father?” A man of the place answers with a question of his own. It can be heard as a shrug, that the prophets have no famous father either, or as saying that a prophet’s “father” is his master and not his family; the text does not explain it. The Greek reads “his father”. The ASV has “And who is their father?”; the TLB “With a father like his?”

The uncle. Saul’s uncle, dod, asks where they went; Saul tells him about the she-donkeys, “but the matter of the kingship, of which Samuel had spoken, he did not tell him”. Josephus names the relative Abner (Antiquities 6.58, numbered 6.4.3 in Whiston); the book later calls Abner the son of Ner, Saul’s uncle (1 Samuel 14:50). Josephus says Saul kept quiet because he expected envy and disbelief (6.59). The ASV has “But concerning the matter of the kingdom, whereof Samuel spake, he told him not”; the NWT 1984 “And the matter of the kingship about which Samuel had talked, he did not tell him”.

Verses 17–24 · Saul chosen at Mizpah — “you have rejected your God”, tribe, clan and man “taken”, Saul hidden among the baggage, and “Long live the king!”

“You have rejected your God.” Samuel “called the people together”, vayyats‘eq, the causative of the verb behind the people’s “outcry” in 9:16. His speech repeats Jehovah’s word of 8:7: “you today have rejected your God”, ma’as. “And you have said to him, A king you shall set over us”: the Hebrew reads lo, “to him”; spelled with another letter the same sound is “No”, and the Greek reads “No”, as the people said in 8:19. The ASV has both, “and ye have said unto him, Nay, but set a king over us”; the NWT 1984 “No, but a king is what you should put over us”. They present themselves “by your thousands”, elef, the word for a clan.

“Taken.” “The tribe of Benjamin was taken … the clan of the Matrites was taken; and Saul son of Kish was taken”: lakad, the verb and the procedure of Achan’s trial, “the tribe that Jehovah takes shall come forward by clans” (Joshua 7:14, now on these pages). No lot is named, but the procedure that once found a thief now finds a king, and the Hebrew goes straight from the clan to Saul; the Greek adds the step between, that they brought the clan of Matri near man by man. The ASV has “and the tribe of Benjamin was taken”; the NWT 1984 “and the tribe of Benjamin came to be picked”.

Among the baggage. “They asked again of Jehovah”, sha’al, the verb of Saul’s name, and the answer is “Look — he has hidden himself among the baggage”, the kelim, gear and vessels. Josephus says he hid so as not to seem to take the rule willingly (Antiquities 6.63, numbered 6.4.5 in Whiston). The ASV has “Behold, he hath hid himself among the baggage”; the KJV “he hath hid himself among the stuff”.

“Long live the king!” Brought out, he is “taller than all the people from his shoulders upward”, as in 9:2. “Do you see him whom Jehovah has chosen”: in 8:18 the king was the one “you have chosen for yourselves”. The people shout yechi ha-melekh, “Long live the king!”, and the cry stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, from this one to Solomon’s anointing, “Long live King Solomon!” (1 Kings 1:39, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “Let the king live!”; the KJV “God save the king”.

Verses 25–27 · “The due of the kingship” written in a book — Saul home to Gibeah, valiant men “whose hearts God had touched”, and the sons of worthlessness

The due of the kingship. “Samuel spoke to the people the due of the kingship, and wrote it in a book, and laid it up before Jehovah”: mishpat ha-melukhah, and the phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. In chapter 8 “the due of the king” was a list of what a king would take (8:11; mishpat); now the rules of the office are written down, as the king law told the king to “write out for himself a copy of this instruction in a scroll” (Deuteronomy 17:18, now on these pages). Josephus says the book was laid up “in the tabernacle of God” as a witness to later generations (Antiquities 6.66, numbered 6.4.6 in Whiston). The ASV has “the manner of the kingdom”; the NWT 1984 “the rightful due of the kingship”; the Douay “the law of the kingdom”.

Valiant men. Saul goes home to Gibeah, the town of the outrage in Judges (Judges 19:22, now on these pages), and “the valiant men”, ha-chayil, go with him, men “whose hearts God had touched”. The NIV has “valiant men whose hearts God had touched”; the ASV “there went with him the host, whose hearts God had touched”.

Sons of worthlessness. Others are “sons of worthlessness”, the phrase used of Eli’s sons (1 Samuel 2:12, now on these pages) and of the men of Gibeah. “How will this one save us?” turns Jehovah’s promise “he will save my people” (9:16) into a sneer. They “despised him”, bazah, and brought no gift, minchah. The ASV has “But certain worthless fellows said, How shall this man save us?”; the NWT 1984 “As for the good-for-nothing men”.

“Like one keeping silent.” The chapter ends “But he was like one keeping silent”, ke-macharish, from charash, to be silent; the word stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The Greek reads nearly the same letters as “after about a month” and opens the next chapter with them, and Josephus also puts the Ammonite war “a month later” (Antiquities 6.68, numbered 6.5.1 in Whiston). A Hebrew manuscript from Qumran, 4QSama, is reported to carry a further paragraph here, about Nahash the Ammonite putting out the right eyes of the Israelites beyond the Jordan; Josephus tells of it too (6.69–70). This translation follows the Masoretic text. The NWT 1984 has “But he continued like one grown speechless”; the ASV “But he held his peace”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the flask of oil”; “leader” for nagid, as in 9:16; “rush on” for tsalach, as in Judges 14:6; “turned into another man” and “another heart” for hafakh; “what your hand finds”, as in Judges 9:33; “a band of prophets”; “Is Saul also among the prophets?”; “taken” for lakad, as in Joshua 7; “baggage” for kelim; “Long live the king!” for yechi ha-melekh; “the due of the kingship” beside “the due of the king” of 8:9, 11; and “sons of worthlessness”.

Echoes paid. The anointing promised at 9:16 and the “word of God” of 9:27 arrive; the father’s worry Saul feared at 9:5 is reported; the tall man of 9:2 stands above the crowd; the people’s rejection of 8:7 is named to their faces; and the “Philistine garrisons” the book let stand beside 7:13 appear.

Echoes planted. The seven days at Gilgal (1 Samuel 13:8, now on these pages), Saul among the prophets a second time (19:24), and Abner son of Ner (14:50).

Next in this book: 1 Samuel 11 — Nahash the Ammonite besieges Jabesh-gilead and demands every man’s right eye; the spirit of God rushes on Saul, who cuts a yoke of oxen in pieces, musters Israel and rescues the town, and the kingdom is renewed at Gilgal.
