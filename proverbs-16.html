Proverbs of Solomon on the plans of the heart and the answer that belongs to Jehovah, works rolled onto him, kindness and faithfulness that atone, the king whose lips carry a verdict and whose fury is messengers of death, honest scales and the stones of the bag, pride before a crash, the whisperer who separates close friends, gray hair as a crown of beauty, and the lot hurled into the lap.
Part of the collection headed “The proverbs of Solomon” (10:1–22:16), attributed to Solomon, son of David (reigned c. 970–931 BC); the book keeps no calendar of its own.

Proverbs 16 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
לְאָדָם מַעַרְכֵי-לֵב;    וּמֵיְהוָה, מַעֲנֵה לָשׁוֹן.
To a human belong the arrangements of the heart,
but from Jehovah is the answer of the tongue.note
Del ser humano son los planes del corazón,
pero de Jehová es la respuesta de la lengua.
2
כָּל-דַּרְכֵי-אִישׁ, זַךְ בְּעֵינָיו;    וְתֹכֵן רוּחוֹת יְהוָה.
All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes,
but Jehovah weighs the spirits.note
Todos los caminos del hombre son puros a sus propios ojos,
pero Jehová pesa los espíritus.
3
גֹּל אֶל-יְהוָה מַעֲשֶׂיךָ;    וְיִכֹּנוּ, מַחְשְׁבֹתֶיךָ.
Roll your works onto Jehovah,
and your plans will be established.note
Haz rodar tus obras sobre Jehová,
y tus planes serán afirmados.
4
כֹּל פָּעַל יְהוָה, לַמַּעֲנֵהוּ;    וְגַם-רָשָׁע, לְיוֹם רָעָה.
Jehovah has made everything for its purpose,
even the wicked for the day of evil.note
Todo lo ha hecho Jehová para su propósito,
aun al malvado para el día del mal.
5
תּוֹעֲבַת יְהוָה, כָּל-גְּבַהּ-לֵב;    יָד לְיָד, לֹא יִנָּקֶה.
Everyone who is proud of heart is an abomination to Jehovah;
hand to hand, he will not go unpunished.note
Abominación es para Jehová todo el que es altivo de corazón;
mano con mano, no quedará impune.
6
בְּחֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת, יְכֻפַּר עָוֺן;    וּבְיִרְאַת יְהוָה, סוּר מֵרָע.
By kindness and faithfulness iniquity is atoned for,
and by the fear of Jehovah one turns from evil.note
Con bondad y verdad se expía la iniquidad,
y con el temor de Jehová uno se aparta del mal.
7
בִּרְצוֹת יְהוָה, דַּרְכֵי-אִישׁ;    גַּם-אוֹיְבָיו, יַשְׁלִם אִתּוֹ.
When Jehovah is pleased with a man’s ways,
he makes even his enemies to be at peace with him.note
Cuando a Jehová le agradan los caminos de un hombre,
aun a sus enemigos hace estar en paz con él.
8
טוֹב-מְעַט, בִּצְדָקָה--    מֵרֹב תְּבוּאוֹת, בְּלֹא מִשְׁפָּט.
Better a little with righteousness
than great produce without justice.note
Mejor es poco con justicia
que muchos productos sin derecho.
9
לֵב אָדָם, יְחַשֵּׁב דַּרְכּוֹ;    וַיהוָה, יָכִין צַעֲדוֹ.
A man’s heart plans his way,
but Jehovah establishes his step.note
El corazón del hombre planea su camino,
pero Jehová afirma su paso.
10
קֶסֶם עַל-שִׂפְתֵי-מֶלֶךְ;    בְּמִשְׁפָּט, לֹא יִמְעַל-פִּיו.
Divination is on the lips of a king;
in judgment his mouth does not act faithlessly.note
Adivinación hay en los labios del rey;
en el juicio su boca no es infiel.
11
פֶּלֶס, וּמֹאזְנֵי מִשְׁפָּט--לַיהוָה;    מַעֲשֵׂהוּ, כָּל-אַבְנֵי-כִיס.
Scales and a balance of justice are Jehovah’s;
all the stones of the bag are his work.note
La balanza y las pesas de justicia son de Jehová;
obra suya son todas las piedras de la bolsa.
12
תּוֹעֲבַת מְלָכִים, עֲשׂוֹת רֶשַׁע:    כִּי בִצְדָקָה, יִכּוֹן כִּסֵּא.
Doing wickedness is an abomination to kings,
for by righteousness a throne is established.note
Abominación es para los reyes hacer maldad,
porque con justicia se afirma el trono.
13
רְצוֹן מְלָכִים, שִׂפְתֵי-צֶדֶק;    וְדֹבֵר יְשָׁרִים יֶאֱהָב.
The goodwill of kings is for righteous lips,
and one who speaks upright things they love.note
La benevolencia de los reyes es para los labios justos,
y aman al que habla lo recto.
14
חֲמַת-מֶלֶךְ מַלְאֲכֵי-מָוֶת;    וְאִישׁ חָכָם יְכַפְּרֶנָּה.
The fury of a king is messengers of death,
but a wise man will appease it.note
La furia del rey son mensajeros de muerte,
pero el hombre sabio la aplacará.
15
בְּאוֹר-פְּנֵי-מֶלֶךְ חַיִּים;    וּרְצוֹנוֹ, כְּעָב מַלְקוֹשׁ.
In the light of the king’s face is life,
and his goodwill is like a cloud of the late rain.note
En la luz del rostro del rey está la vida,
y su benevolencia es como nube de lluvia tardía.
16
קְנֹה-חָכְמָה--מַה-טּוֹב מֵחָרוּץ;    וּקְנוֹת בִּינָה, נִבְחָר מִכָּסֶף.
To acquire wisdom —
how much better than fine gold! and to acquire understanding is to be chosen above silver.note
Adquirir sabiduría, ¡cuánto mejor que el oro fino!
y adquirir entendimiento ha de escogerse antes que la plata.
17
מְסִלַּת יְשָׁרִים, סוּר מֵרָע;    שֹׁמֵר נַפְשׁוֹ, נֹצֵר דַּרְכּוֹ.
The highway of the upright is to turn from evil;
whoever keeps his soul guards his way.note
La calzada de los rectos es apartarse del mal;
el que guarda su alma custodia su camino.
18
לִפְנֵי-שֶׁבֶר גָּאוֹן;    וְלִפְנֵי כִשָּׁלוֹן, גֹּבַהּ רוּחַ.
Before a crash, pride;
and before stumbling, a lofty spirit.note
Antes del quebranto, la soberbia;
y antes del tropiezo, la altivez de espíritu.
19
טוֹב שְׁפַל-רוּחַ, אֶת-עניים עֲנָוִים;    מֵחַלֵּק שָׁלָל, אֶת-גֵּאִים.
Better to be lowly of spirit with the humble
than to divide spoil with the proud.note
Mejor es ser humilde de espíritu con los humildes
que repartir botín con los soberbios.
20
מַשְׂכִּיל עַל-דָּבָר, יִמְצָא-טוֹב;    וּבוֹטֵחַ בַּיהוָה אַשְׁרָיו.
Whoever has insight into a matter will find good, and whoever trusts in Jehovah —
happy is he.note
El que tiene perspicacia en un asunto hallará el bien,
y el que confía en Jehová, ¡feliz es!
21
לַחֲכַם-לֵב, יִקָּרֵא נָבוֹן;    וּמֶתֶק שְׂפָתַיִם, יֹסִיף לֶקַח.
The wise of heart is called a man of understanding,
and sweetness of lips adds learning.note
El sabio de corazón es llamado entendido,
y la dulzura de los labios añade saber.
22
מְקוֹר חַיִּים, שֵׂכֶל בְּעָלָיו;    וּמוּסַר אֱוִלִים אִוֶּלֶת.
Insight is a fountain of life to its owners,
but the discipline of fools is folly.note
Fuente de vida es la perspicacia para sus dueños,
pero la disciplina de los necios es necedad.
23
לֵב חָכָם, יַשְׂכִּיל פִּיהוּ;    וְעַל-שְׂפָתָיו, יֹסִיף לֶקַח.
The heart of the wise gives insight to his mouth,
and on his lips it adds learning.note
El corazón del sabio da perspicacia a su boca,
y sobre sus labios añade saber.
24
צוּף-דְּבַשׁ, אִמְרֵי-נֹעַם;    מָתוֹק לַנֶּפֶשׁ, וּמַרְפֵּא לָעָצֶם.
A honeycomb are pleasant sayings,
sweet to the soul and healing to the bones.note
Panal de miel son los dichos agradables,
dulces para el alma y curación para los huesos.
25
יֵשׁ דֶּרֶךְ יָשָׁר, לִפְנֵי-אִישׁ;    וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ, דַּרְכֵי-מָוֶת.
There is a way that is straight in a man’s sight,
but its end is the ways of death.note
Hay camino que es recto a los ojos del hombre,
pero su final son caminos de muerte.
26
נֶפֶשׁ עָמֵל, עָמְלָה לּוֹ:    כִּי-אָכַף עָלָיו פִּיהוּ.
The appetite of a laborer labors for him,
for his mouth urges him on.note
El apetito del que trabaja trabaja para él,
porque su boca lo apremia.
27
אִישׁ בְּלִיַּעַל, כֹּרֶה רָעָה;    וְעַל-שפתיו שְׂפָתוֹ, כְּאֵשׁ צָרָבֶת.
A man of worthlessness digs up evil,
and on his lips it is like a scorching fire.note
El hombre de la nada cava el mal,
y sobre sus labios es como fuego abrasador.
28
אִישׁ תַּהְפֻּכוֹת, יְשַׁלַּח מָדוֹן;    וְנִרְגָּן, מַפְרִיד אַלּוּף.
A man of perversities sends out contention,
and a whisperer separates close friends.note
El hombre de perversidades envía contienda,
y el murmurador separa a los amigos íntimos.
29
אִישׁ חָמָס, יְפַתֶּה רֵעֵהוּ;    וְהוֹלִיכוֹ, בְּדֶרֶךְ לֹא-טוֹב.
A man of violence entices his neighbor,
and leads him in a way that is not good.note
El hombre de violencia seduce a su prójimo,
y lo lleva por un camino que no es bueno.
30
עֹצֶה עֵינָיו, לַחְשֹׁב תַּהְפֻּכוֹת;    קֹרֵץ שְׂפָתָיו, כִּלָּה רָעָה.
One who shuts his eyes to devise perversities, one who purses his lips —
he has brought evil to completion.note
El que cierra sus ojos para tramar perversidades,
el que aprieta sus labios, ya ha consumado el mal.
31
עֲטֶרֶת תִּפְאֶרֶת שֵׂיבָה;    בְּדֶרֶךְ צְדָקָה, תִּמָּצֵא.
Gray hair is a crown of beauty;
it is found in the way of righteousness.note
Corona de hermosura son las canas;
en el camino de la justicia se hallan.
32
טוֹב אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם, מִגִּבּוֹר;    וּמֹשֵׁל בְּרוּחוֹ, מִלֹּכֵד עִיר.
Better one slow to anger than a mighty man,
and one who rules his spirit than one who captures a city.note
Mejor es el tardo para la ira que el poderoso,
y el que domina su espíritu que el que toma una ciudad.
33
בַּחֵיק, יוּטַל אֶת-הַגּוֹרָל;    וּמֵיְהוָה, כָּל-מִשְׁפָּטוֹ.
The lot is hurled into the lap,
but its every decision is from Jehovah.note
La suerte se arroja en el regazo,
pero de Jehová es toda su decisión.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · “The answer of the tongue is from Jehovah” — plans and answers, weighed spirits, and works rolled onto Jehovah

Plans and the answer. “To a human belong the arrangements of the heart, but from Jehovah is the answer of the tongue”: ma’arkhei-lev, “the arrangements of the heart”, is a word for setting things in order, as a battle line or a table is set; the person arranges, and the word that actually comes out belongs to Jehovah. The ASV has “The plans of the heart belong to man; But the answer of the tongue is from Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “To earthling man belong the arrangings of the heart”; the NIV “but from the Lord comes the proper answer of the tongue”.

Weighed spirits. “All the ways of a man are pure in his own eyes, but Jehovah weighs the spirits”: tokhen, “weighs”, is the verb of Hannah’s song, “by him deeds are weighed” (1 Samuel 2:3, now on these pages). The ASV has “But Jehovah weigheth the spirits”; the NWT 1984 “but Jehovah is making an estimate of spirits”; the NIV “but motives are weighed by the Lord”.

Roll your works. “Roll your works onto Jehovah, and your plans will be established”: gol is from galal, to roll, the verb of “Roll your way onto Jehovah” (Psalm 37:5). The load is rolled off one’s own back. The NWT 1984 keeps the verb, “Roll your works upon Jehovah himself”; the ASV has “Commit thy works unto Jehovah”.

For its purpose. “Jehovah has made everything for its purpose, even the wicked for the day of evil”: la-ma’anehu, “for its purpose”, sounds like the ma’aneh, “answer”, of v1, and some hear it so, everything made for its answer, its corresponding end. The word stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “Jehovah hath made everything for its own end”; the KJV reads it as God’s own end, “hath made all things for himself”; the NWT 1984 “Everything Jehovah has made for his purpose”. The Greek of this chapter has no verse 1, 3 or 4; its order runs differently from here to verse 9.

Verses 5–7 · “By mercy and truth iniquity is purged” — an abomination, kindness that atones, and enemies at peace

Hand to hand. “Everyone who is proud of heart is an abomination to Jehovah; hand to hand, he will not go unpunished”: to’evah, “abomination”, the strongest word Proverbs has for what Jehovah cannot stand. Yad le-yad, “hand to hand”, is a pledge, a handshake on it: you can be sure. The two words stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in a saying about the wicked (Proverbs 11:21). The ASV reads it as a conspiracy, “Though hand join in hand, he shall not be unpunished”; the NIV as a pledge, “Be sure of this: They will not go unpunished”.

Kindness and faithfulness. “By kindness and faithfulness iniquity is atoned for, and by the fear of Jehovah one turns from evil”: chesed ve-emet, “kindness and faithfulness”, are the pair of “Let not kindness and faithfulness forsake you” (Proverbs 3:3), and yekhuppar, “is atoned for”, is from kaphar, the verb of the altar. Here no altar is mentioned: kindness and faithfulness do what a sacrifice does. The ASV has “By mercy and truth iniquity is atoned for”; the NWT 1984 “By loving-kindness and trueness error is atoned for”; the KJV “By mercy and truth iniquity is purged”. The Greek has this line in its chapter 15, with “alms” for kindness: “by almsgiving and faithfulness sins are cleansed”.

Enemies at peace. “When Jehovah is pleased with a man’s ways, he makes even his enemies to be at peace with him”: birtsot, “when he is pleased”, is from the root of ratson, goodwill, and yashlim, “makes to be at peace”, from shalom. The NWT 1984 has “When Jehovah takes pleasure in the ways of a man”; the ASV “When a man’s ways please Jehovah”.

Verses 8–9 · “A man’s heart deviseth his way: but Jehovah directeth his steps” — a little with righteousness, and the step Jehovah sets

A little with righteousness. “Better a little with righteousness than great produce without justice”: tevu’ot, “produce”, is the harvest word of “from the first of all your produce” (Proverbs 3:9). The ASV has “Better is a little, with righteousness, Than great revenues with injustice”; the NWT 1984 “than an abundance of products without justice”.

Jehovah sets the step. “A man’s heart plans his way, but Jehovah establishes his step”: yekhin, “establishes”, is the verb of v3, “your plans will be established”, and of v12, “a throne is established”. The heart plans the road; the step is set by someone else. The ASV has “A man’s heart deviseth his way; But Jehovah directeth his steps”; the NWT 1984 “but Jehovah himself does the directing of his steps”.

Verses 10–15 · “The wrath of a king is as messengers of death” — the king: divination on his lips, honest scales, goodwill, and a cloud of the late rain

Divination on his lips. “Divination is on the lips of a king; in judgment his mouth does not act faithlessly”: qesem is the forbidden art of the law, “one who divines divinations” (Deuteronomy 18:10, now on these pages), and Samuel calls rebellion “the sin of divination” (1 Samuel 15:23). Here the word is turned to praise: a king’s verdict should carry the certainty people went to diviners for. The ASV has “A divine sentence is in the lips of the king”; the NWT 1984 “Inspired decision should be upon the lips of a king”; the Douay keeps the word, “Divination is in the lips of the king”.

The stones of the bag. “Scales and a balance of justice are Jehovah’s; all the stones of the bag are his work”: peles and moznayim are the pair of “weighed the mountains in scales, and the hills in a balance” (Isaiah 40:12), and peles is from the root of palles, “level”, in “Level the track of your foot” (Proverbs 4:26). Weights were stones carried in a bag, the bag of “a stone and a stone, a large one and a small one” (Deuteronomy 25:13) and of “a bag of deceitful stones” (Micah 6:11); see even. “The stones of the bag” stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The NWT 1984 has “all the stone weights of the bag are his work”; the ASV “All the weights of the bag are his work”.

The goodwill of kings. “Doing wickedness is an abomination to kings, for by righteousness a throne is established. The goodwill of kings is for righteous lips”: retson melakhim uses ratson, the goodwill drawn out from Jehovah in Proverbs 18:22. The ASV has “Righteous lips are the delight of kings”; the NWT 1984 “The lips of righteousness are a pleasure to a grand king”.

Messengers of death. “The fury of a king is messengers of death, but a wise man will appease it”: yekhapperennah, “will appease it”, is the atoning verb of v6, kaphar again; the wise man covers the king’s fury as an offering covers guilt. The ASV has “The wrath of a king is as messengers of death; But a wise man will pacify it”; the NWT 1984 “but the wise man is one that averts it”.

The late rain. “In the light of the king’s face is life, and his goodwill is like a cloud of the late rain”: malqosh is the late rain of spring that fills the grain before harvest, “the early rain and the late rain” (Deuteronomy 11:14). The ASV has “And his favor is as a cloud of the latter rain”; the NWT 1984 “like the cloud of spring rain”.

Verses 16–19 · “Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall” — wisdom above gold, a highway, a crash, and spoil

Better than fine gold. “To acquire wisdom — how much better than fine gold! and to acquire understanding is to be chosen above silver”: the verb is qanah, the buying of “Acquire wisdom, acquire understanding” (Proverbs 4:5), and charuts is the “fine gold” of Proverbs 3:14. Nivchar, “to be chosen”, is the word of “A name is to be chosen rather than great riches” (Proverbs 22:1). The Greek seems to have read the first word as a form of qen, “nest”: “nests of wisdom are more to be chosen than gold”. The ASV has “How much better is it to get wisdom than gold!”; the NWT 1984 “And the getting of understanding is to be chosen more than silver”.

The highway. “The highway of the upright is to turn from evil; whoever keeps his soul guards his way”: mesillah is a built-up road, a highway, not a footpath. The Hebrew sets “keeps his soul” first; several versions reverse it. The ASV has “He that keepeth his way preserveth his soul”; the NWT 1984 “One who is safeguarding his way is keeping his soul”.

Before a crash, pride. “Before a crash, pride; and before stumbling, a lofty spirit”: shever, “a crash”, opens the line, as it opens “Before a crash, a man’s heart is lofty” (Proverbs 18:12). “Before a crash, pride” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The famous English is the KJV, “Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall”, whose “goeth” is not in the Hebrew; the NWT 1984 has “Pride is before a crash, and a haughty spirit before stumbling”.

The humble and the spoil. “Better to be lowly of spirit with the humble than to divide spoil with the proud”: the written text has aniyyim, “the afflicted”, and the text as read anavim, “the humble” (see anav). Shalal is spoil, plunder, what the husband of the woman of valour never lacks (Proverbs 31:11). The ASV has “Better it is to be of a lowly spirit with the poor”; the NWT 1984 “with the meek ones”.

Verses 20–24 · “Pleasant words are as an honeycomb” — insight, a fountain of life, and sayings sweet to the soul

Insight into a matter. “Whoever has insight into a matter will find good, and whoever trusts in Jehovah — happy is he”: maskil is from the root of haskel, insight that succeeds. Ashrav, “happy is he”, is the word of “Happy is the human who finds wisdom” (Proverbs 3:13). The NWT 1984 has “He that is showing insight in a matter will find good”; the KJV “He that handleth a matter wisely shall find good”; the ASV reads davar as the word of God, “He that giveth heed unto the word shall find good”.

Learning added. “The wise of heart is called a man of understanding, and sweetness of lips adds learning”: leqach, “learning”, is the father’s gift in “For good learning I give you” (Proverbs 4:2). The point is how it is said: a sweet way of speaking makes the teaching go further. The ASV has “And the sweetness of the lips increaseth learning”; the NWT 1984 “and he that is sweet in [his] lips adds persuasiveness”.

A fountain of life. “Insight is a fountain of life to its owners, but the discipline of fools is folly”: meqor chayyim, the fountain of life, is a phrase Proverbs gives to several things, the mouth of the righteous, the teaching of the wise, the fear of Jehovah and here insight; Proverbs 18 had “a fountain of wisdom” (Proverbs 18:4). Be’alav, “its owners”, is the plural of ba’al. The second line can mean that fools’ discipline is folly, or that folly is the discipline fools get. The NWT 1984 has “To its owners insight is a well of life”; the ASV “But the correction of fools is their folly”; the NIV “but folly brings punishment to fools”.

Honeycomb. “The heart of the wise gives insight to his mouth, and on his lips it adds learning. A honeycomb are pleasant sayings, sweet to the soul and healing to the bones”: tsuf devash, the dripping comb. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Marpe, “healing”, is the word of “healing to all their flesh” (Proverbs 4:22). The NWT 1984 has “Pleasant sayings are a honeycomb, sweet to the soul and a healing to the bones”; the ASV “Pleasant words are as a honeycomb”.

Verses 25–26 · “There is a way that seemeth right unto a man” — a straight-looking road, and an appetite that works

A way that looks straight. “There is a way that is straight in a man’s sight, but its end is the ways of death”: yashar lifnei-ish, “straight before a man”, straight as he sees it. The whole first line stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and word for word earlier in the book (Proverbs 14:12). The ASV has “There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, But the end thereof are the ways of death”; the NWT 1984 “There exists a way that is upright before a man”.

The working appetite. “The appetite of a laborer labors for him, for his mouth urges him on”: nefesh, usually “soul”, is first of all the throat, and so the appetite; a hungry worker is driven by his own mouth. Amel, “laborer”, and amelah, “labors”, are the same root twice. The ASV has “The appetite of the laboring man laboreth for him”; the NWT 1984 “The soul of the hard worker has worked hard for him”; the KJV “He that laboureth laboureth for himself”.

Verses 27–30 · “A whisperer separateth chief friends” — a man of worthlessness, scorching lips, a whisperer, and a wink

A man of worthlessness. “A man of worthlessness digs up evil, and on his lips it is like a scorching fire”: ish beliyya’al, the beliyya’al of the “sons of worthlessness” in Deuteronomy 13:14 and of Eli’s sons (1 Samuel 2:12). He digs for evil as one digs a pit. The written text has “his lips”, plural, the text as read “his lip”. The ASV has “A worthless man deviseth mischief; And in his lips there is as a scorching fire”; the NWT 1984 “A good-for-nothing man is digging up what is bad”.

A whisperer separates close friends. “A man of perversities sends out contention, and a whisperer separates close friends”: tahpukhot, “perversities”, things turned upside down; madon, “contention”, as in Proverbs 22:10; and nirgan, the whisperer whose words go down “into the inner rooms of the belly” (Proverbs 18:8). Alluf, “close friend”, is the intimate, the one a person is used to. The ASV has “And a whisperer separateth chief friends”; the NWT 1984 “and a slanderer is separating those familiar with one another”; the NIV “and a gossip separates close friends”.

Enticed. “A man of violence entices his neighbor, and leads him in a way that is not good”: yefatteh, “entices”, is from patah, to persuade, to lead on. The ASV has “A man of violence enticeth his neighbor”; the NWT 1984 “A man of violence will seduce his fellow”.

A wink and pursed lips. “One who shuts his eyes to devise perversities, one who purses his lips — he has brought evil to completion”: otseh, “shuts”, and qorets, “purses”, are the small signals of a plotter; by the time the lips are pressed shut, the evil is already done. The ASV has “He that shutteth his eyes, it is to devise perverse things”; the NWT 1984 “He is blinking with his eyes to scheme up intrigues”; the NIV “Whoever winks with their eye is plotting perversity”.

Verses 31–33 · “The lot is cast into the lap” — gray hair, the man who rules his spirit, and the lot

A crown of beauty. “Gray hair is a crown of beauty; it is found in the way of righteousness”: ateret tif’eret, “a crown of beauty”, is what Wisdom gives in Proverbs 4:9; see tif’eret. The NWT 1984 has “Gray-headedness is a crown of beauty”; the ASV “The hoary head is a crown of glory”.

Better than a mighty man. “Better one slow to anger than a mighty man, and one who rules his spirit than one who captures a city”: erekh appayim, “slow to anger”, long of nostrils, is said of Jehovah himself, “slow to anger and abundant in steadfast love” (Psalm 103:8). The ASV has “He that is slow to anger is better than the mighty; And he that ruleth his spirit, than he that taketh a city”; the NIV “Better a patient person than a warrior”.

The lot in the lap. “The lot is hurled into the lap, but its every decision is from Jehovah”: goral is the lot, and yutal, “is hurled”, the verb for throwing it into a fold of the robe; mishpato, “its decision”, is the word for a judge’s ruling. The chance is in the hand, the verdict is not. The ASV has “The lot is cast into the lap; But the whole disposing thereof is of Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “Into the lap the lot is cast down, but every decision by it is from Jehovah”. The Greek has no lot here: “into the laps of the unjust come all things, but from the Lord come all just things”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “roll” for galal, as in Psalm 37:5; “weighs” for tokhen, as in 1 Samuel 2:3; “kindness and faithfulness” for chesed ve-emet, as in 3:3; “atoned for” for kipper; “established” for every kun in vv3, 9 and 12; “divination” for qesem, as in Deuteronomy 18:10; “scales” and “a balance” for peles and moznayim, as in Isaiah 40:12; “the stones of the bag”; “goodwill” for ratson, as in 18:22; “the late rain” for malqosh; “acquire” for qanah, as in chapter 4; “a crash” for shever, as in 18:12; “learning” for leqach; “a man of worthlessness” for ish beliyya’al; “a whisperer” for nirgan; “a crown of beauty”, as in 4:9; and “hurled” for the lot’s yutal.

Echoes paid. The deeds weighed in Hannah’s song (1 Samuel 2:3); the way rolled onto Jehovah (Psalm 37:5); kindness and faithfulness (3:3); the produce of 3:9; divination in Deuteronomy 18:10 and 1 Samuel 15:23; the scales and balance of Isaiah 40:12 and the level track of 4:26; the stones of the bag of Deuteronomy 25:13 and Micah 6:11; the goodwill of 18:22; the late rain of Deuteronomy 11:14; acquire wisdom (4:5), fine gold (3:14) and the name to be chosen (22:1); the crash of 18:12, planted there; the spoil of 31:11; the happy one of 3:13; the learning of 4:2; the fountain of 18:4 and the healing of 4:22; the sons of worthlessness of Deuteronomy 13:14 and 1 Samuel 2:12; the contention of 22:10 and the whisperer of 18:8, planted there; the crown of 4:9; the slow anger of Psalm 103:8; and the lot of the dictionary’s goral.

Echoes planted. Hand to hand again (Proverbs 11:21); the way that looks straight, said first (Proverbs 14:12). Neither is translated here yet.
