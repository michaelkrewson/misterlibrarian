🕰
The guilt-offering's own priestly economics, the peace-offering's three occasions and three deadlines (thanksgiving eaten same-day, vow or freewill given an extra day, anything later tainted meat), the flat ban on eating fat or blood, and the two portions — breast waved, thigh contributed — permanently taxed for the priesthood. Closes with a formal colophon naming all six offerings and ending the whole seven-chapter section.
Same setting as ch. 1-6 — spoken at Sinai in the same roughly one-month window before Israel breaks camp (Numbers 10:11), and its own closing colophon (v37-38) says so explicitly: commanded on Mount Sinai, in the wilderness of Sinai. Hebrew and English both run 1-38 here — no versification split, unlike Leviticus 5-6.

Leviticus 7 — The Mister Translation

1
וְזֹאת תּוֹרַת, הָאָשָׁם: קֹדֶשׁ קָדָשִׁים, הוּא.
And this is the law of the guilt-offering: it is most holy.note
Y esta es la ley de la ofrenda por la culpa: es santísima.
2
בִּמְקוֹם, אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁחֲטוּ אֶת-הָעֹלָה, יִשְׁחֲטוּ, אֶת-הָאָשָׁם; וְאֶת-דָּמוֹ יִזְרֹק עַל-הַמִּזְבֵּחַ, סָבִיב.
In the place where they slaughter the burnt-offering, they shall slaughter the guilt-offering; and its blood he shall dash against the altar all around.note
En el lugar donde degüellan el holocausto, degollarán la ofrenda por la culpa; y su sangre la rociará sobre el altar todo alrededor.
3
וְאֵת כָּל-חֶלְבּוֹ, יַקְרִיב מִמֶּנּוּ--אֵת, הָאַלְיָה, וְאֶת-הַחֵלֶב, הַמְכַסֶּה אֶת-הַקֶּרֶב.
And he shall offer from it all its fat: the fat tail, and the fat that covers the entrails,note
Y ofrecerá de ella toda su grasa: la cola entera, y la grasa que cubre los intestinos,
4
וְאֵת, שְׁתֵּי הַכְּלָיֹת, וְאֶת-הַחֵלֶב אֲשֶׁר עֲלֵיהֶן, אֲשֶׁר עַל-הַכְּסָלִים; וְאֶת-הַיֹּתֶרֶת, עַל-הַכָּבֵד, עַל-הַכְּלָיֹת, יְסִירֶנָּה.
and the two kidneys, and the fat that is on them, which is by the loins, and the lobe on the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys.note
y los dos riñones, y la grasa que hay sobre ellos, la que está junto a los lomos, y el lóbulo sobre el hígado, que quitará junto con los riñones.
5
וְהִקְטִיר אֹתָם הַכֹּהֵן הַמִּזְבֵּחָה, אִשֶּׁה לַיהוָה; אָשָׁם, הוּא.
And the priest shall turn them into smoke on the altar, an offering made by fire to Jehovah; it is a guilt-offering.note
Y el sacerdote los hará arder en el altar, ofrenda encendida a Jehová; es ofrenda por la culpa.
6
כָּל-זָכָר בַּכֹּהֲנִים, יֹאכְלֶנּוּ; בְּמָקוֹם קָדוֹשׁ יֵאָכֵל, קֹדֶשׁ קָדָשִׁים הוּא.
Every male among the priests may eat it; it shall be eaten in a holy place; it is most holy.note
Todo varón entre los sacerdotes podrá comerla; se comerá en un lugar santo; es santísima.
7
כַּחַטָּאת, כָּאָשָׁם--תּוֹרָה אַחַת, לָהֶם; הַכֹּהֵן אֲשֶׁר יְכַפֶּר-בּוֹ, לוֹ יִהְיֶה.
As is the sin-offering, so is the guilt-offering; there is one law for them; the priest who makes atonement with it shall have it.note
Como es la ofrenda por el pecado, así es la ofrenda por la culpa; una sola ley hay para ambas; el sacerdote que haga expiación con ella, la tendrá para sí.
8
וְהַכֹּהֵן--הַמַּקְרִיב, אֶת-עֹלַת אִישׁ: עוֹר הָעֹלָה אֲשֶׁר הִקְרִיב, לַכֹּהֵן לוֹ יִהְיֶה.
And the priest who offers anyone’s burnt-offering, the priest shall have for himself the skin of the burnt-offering that he has offered.note
Y el sacerdote que ofrezca el holocausto de cualquiera, el sacerdote tendrá para sí la piel del holocausto que haya ofrecido.
9
וְכָל-מִנְחָה, אֲשֶׁר תֵּאָפֶה בַּתַּנּוּר, וְכָל-נַעֲשָׂה בַמַּרְחֶשֶׁת, וְעַל-מַחֲבַת--לַכֹּהֵן הַמַּקְרִיב אֹתָהּ, לוֹ תִהְיֶה.
And every grain-offering that is baked in the oven, and all that is prepared in a pan or on a griddle, shall belong to the priest who offers it.note
Y toda ofrenda de grano que se cueza en el horno, y toda la que se prepare en la sartén o en la plancha, será del sacerdote que la ofrezca.
10
וְכָל-מִנְחָה בְלוּלָה-בַשֶּׁמֶן, וַחֲרֵבָה--לְכָל-בְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן תִּהְיֶה, אִישׁ כְּאָחִיו. {פ}
And every grain-offering, mixed with oil or dry, shall belong to all the sons of Aaron alike. {פ}note
Y toda ofrenda de grano, mezclada con aceite o seca, será de todos los hijos de Aarón por igual. {פ}
11
וְזֹאת תּוֹרַת, זֶבַח הַשְּׁלָמִים, אֲשֶׁר יַקְרִיב, לַיהוָה.
And this is the law of the sacrifice of peace-offerings, which one may offer to Jehovah.note
Y esta es la ley del sacrificio de ofrendas de paz, que se ofrece a Jehová.
12
אִם עַל-תּוֹדָה, יַקְרִיבֶנּוּ--וְהִקְרִיב עַל-זֶבַח הַתּוֹדָה חַלּוֹת מַצּוֹת בְּלוּלֹת בַּשֶּׁמֶן, וּרְקִיקֵי מַצּוֹת מְשֻׁחִים בַּשָּׁמֶן; וְסֹלֶת מֻרְבֶּכֶת, חַלֹּת בְּלוּלֹת בַּשָּׁמֶן.
If he offers it for thanksgiving, then he shall offer with the sacrifice of thanksgiving unleavened cakes mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers spread with oil, and cakes of fine flour well-soaked, mixed with oil.note
Si la ofrece en acción de gracias, entonces ofrecerá con el sacrificio de acción de gracias tortas sin levadura mezcladas con aceite, obleas sin levadura untadas con aceite, y tortas de flor de harina bien empapada, mezcladas con aceite.
13
עַל-חַלֹּת לֶחֶם חָמֵץ, יַקְרִיב קָרְבָּנוֹ, עַל-זֶבַח, תּוֹדַת שְׁלָמָיו.
With cakes of leavened bread he shall present his offering, together with the sacrifice of his peace-offerings for thanksgiving.note
Con tortas de pan leudado presentará su ofrenda, junto con el sacrificio de sus ofrendas de paz por acción de gracias.
14
וְהִקְרִיב מִמֶּנּוּ אֶחָד מִכָּל-קָרְבָּן, תְּרוּמָה לַיהוָה; לַכֹּהֵן, הַזֹּרֵק אֶת-דַּם הַשְּׁלָמִים--לוֹ יִהְיֶה.
And from it he shall present one out of each offering as a contribution to Jehovah; it shall belong to the priest who dashes the blood of the peace-offerings against the altar.note
Y de ella presentará uno de cada ofrenda como contribución a Jehová; será del sacerdote que rocía la sangre de las ofrendas de paz.
15
וּבְשַׂר, זֶבַח תּוֹדַת שְׁלָמָיו--בְּיוֹם קָרְבָּנוֹ, יֵאָכֵל: לֹא-יַנִּיחַ מִמֶּנּוּ, עַד-בֹּקֶר.
And the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace-offerings for thanksgiving shall be eaten on the day of his offering; he shall leave none of it until morning.note
Y la carne del sacrificio de sus ofrendas de paz por acción de gracias se comerá el día de su ofrenda; no dejará nada de ella hasta la mañana.
16
וְאִם-נֶדֶר אוֹ נְדָבָה, זֶבַח קָרְבָּנוֹ--בְּיוֹם הַקְרִיבוֹ אֶת-זִבְחוֹ, יֵאָכֵל; וּמִמָּחֳרָת, וְהַנּוֹתָר מִמֶּנּוּ יֵאָכֵל.
But if the sacrifice of his offering is a vow or a freewill-offering, it shall be eaten on the day that he offers his sacrifice; and what remains of it may be eaten on the next day.note
Pero si el sacrificio de su ofrenda es voto u ofrenda voluntaria, se comerá el día que ofrezca su sacrificio; y lo que quede de ella podrá comerse al día siguiente.
17

Pero lo que quede de la carne del sacrificio hasta el tercer día, se quemará con fuego.
18

And if any of the flesh of the sacrifice of his peace-offerings is eaten at all on the third day, it shall not be accepted, it shall not be credited to the one who offered it; it shall be tainted meat, and the one who eats of it shall bear his guilt.note
Y si se come algo de la carne del sacrificio de sus ofrendas de paz al tercer día, no será aceptada, ni se le acreditará al que la ofreció; será carne corrompida, y la persona que coma de ella cargará su culpa.

The skin, the stew-pot, and the small print of who gets what

The guilt-offering law repeats, almost verbatim, the sin-offering’s own blood rite from Leviticus 4 — same place at the altar, same fat portions burned, same ‘most holy’ standing — and then says so outright: ‘there is ONE law for them’ (v7). What this chapter actually adds is not ritual but ECONOMICS: the officiating priest personally keeps the meat (v6), the hide of every burnt-offering he handles (v8), and the whole of any grain-offering he prepares (v9–10) — a running account of exactly which priest is paid from which sacrifice, spelled out nowhere else this precisely.

⚠ Verse 8’s hide-rule is easy to read past, but it is a real, separate piece of priestly income: the skin of EVERY burnt-offering, however large the animal, belongs entirely to the priest who offered it — on top of, not instead of, his other portions. Leather from a bull’s worth of burnt-offerings was not a small perquisite in an economy without much else to tan.

Three reasons to bring the same sacrifice, and three different clocks

The peace-offering splits into three occasions that share one ritual but not one deadline: THANKSGIVING (v12–15), for something already received, must be eaten the very day it is offered, nothing left until morning; a VOW or FREEWILL-offering (v16–17) gets an extra day, eaten that day or the next. The thanksgiving offering alone comes with leavened bread as well as unleavened (v13) — the one place in the sacrificial system where ordinary, everyday bread is deliberately allowed onto the altar precinct, because this offering is closest to an ordinary meal shared in gratitude, not a formal presentation.

⚠ Verse 18 names what happens to meat eaten too late with a single technical word, piggul: not simply spoiled food, but an offering retroactively made WORTHLESS — not accepted, not credited to the one who brought it, as if it had never been offered at all. The chapter treats lateness as more than bad hygiene: a peace-offering has a narrow window in which it is actually functioning as an offering, and outside that window the meat reverts to being just meat, and unclean meat carries real consequence for the eater (v19–21). This translation's ‘tainted meat’ sits closer to ESV’s ‘tainted’ than to KJV’s more traditional ‘an abomination’ — the older word has drifted too far toward moral horror for what is, here, a purely procedural failure.

The one rule aimed at everyone, not just the altar

After three straight sections addressed to the priests, this one turns outward: ‘speak to the CHILDREN OF ISRAEL’ (v23) — no fat, no blood, ever, full stop, ‘in any of your dwellings’ (v26), not just at the sanctuary. The fat prohibited here is specifically the fat OFFERED to Jehovah (the same portions detailed in Leviticus 3 and this chapter’s own opening) — fat from an animal that died naturally or was killed by a predator is explicitly permitted for other uses (v24), so this is not a blanket dietary law against all animal fat, but a claim on the specific fat that belongs on the altar.

⚠ Both prohibitions carry the same penalty, repeated four times across six verses: cut off from his people (v20, v21, v25, v27). That repetition is itself the point — unlike the graduated, offering-based atonement system of Leviticus 4–6, eating fat or blood has no offering that fixes it. The text simply names the consequence and moves on.

The two portions no offerer ever sees again

Every peace-offering permanently taxes two specific cuts for the priesthood: the breast, waved before Jehovah and then eaten by any priest’s family (v31, compare Leviticus 10:14–15, already on these pages); and the right thigh, a contribution claimed specifically by the priest who performed the blood rite (v32–33). Two different gestures for two different portions — one waved toward the altar and back, one simply set aside — and the chapter is explicit that this is not a one-time gift but a permanent tax on every future peace-offering: ‘a perpetual due from the children of Israel’ (v34).

⚠ Verse 30’s instruction that ‘his own hands shall bring’ the offering is oddly specific — the OFFERER, not the priest, personally carries the fat and breast forward. Every other transfer of an offering in this book happens at the priest’s hands; here, for one moment, the person who brought the animal is required to be the one who physically delivers its most valuable cuts, before the priest ever touches them.

Where the whole offering-law section closes — and the one item on its own list not yet explained

Verses 37–38 are a formal colophon, closing not just this chapter but the entire seven-chapter block that opened at Leviticus 1: ‘this is the law’ of six named offerings, commanded at Sinai, on the day Israel was first told to bring them. Burnt-offering, grain-offering, sin-offering, and guilt-offering have each had a full chapter; the peace-offering has had two (Leviticus 3 and this one).

⚠ But the list names a SIXTH offering this section never actually legislates: the millu'im, the ‘consecration-offering’ or ordination-offering (v37) — the same word behind the idiom this project has already met at Exodus 29, ‘filling the hand’ (see the Dictionary). No law for it appears anywhere in Leviticus 1–7; it is only ever NARRATED, in the ordination ceremony of Leviticus 8 (already on these pages), which finally carries out in full what Exodus 29 already commanded. The colophon reaches one chapter ahead of itself — naming an offering whose only law is the story of the one time it was actually performed.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English Leviticus 7 both run 1–38 — no versification split this time, unlike Leviticus 5–6. ‘Contribution’ is kept for terumah (v14, v32, v34) over the older shelf’s more literal but stranger-sounding ‘heave offering’ (KJV, ASV) — see the Dictionary for the word’s own range, from tabernacle-building donations to this chapter’s priestly due. ‘Tainted meat’ is kept for piggul (v18), matching this translation’s own choice already established at Leviticus 19:7 — see the Dictionary. Every {פ} in this chapter is a petuchah, an OPEN paragraph space — all three (v10, v21, v38) close a fully self-contained unit: the guilt-offering law, the peace-offering law, and the whole seven-chapter section itself.
