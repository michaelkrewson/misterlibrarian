David musters the people who are with him and puts commanders of thousands and of hundreds over them, sending them out in three thirds under Joab, Abishai and Ittai the Gittite; the people will not let him go out with them, and he stands by the gate and charges the three commanders, in the hearing of all, to deal gently for his sake with the young man, Absalom. In the forest of Ephraim Israel is beaten, twenty thousand fall, and the forest devours more than the sword. Absalom, riding his mule, is caught by his head in a great terebinth; a man who saw him will not strike him, but Joab thrusts three rods into his heart and ten of his young men finish him; Joab blows the horn, and Absalom is thrown into a pit under a great heap of stones, and the pillar he set up for himself in the king’s valley is called Absalom’s hand. Ahimaaz asks to run with the news and Joab sends the Cushite instead, but Ahimaaz runs by the way of the plain and reaches David first, who sits between the two gates; he says Peace and does not say what he knows, and the Cushite, asked whether it is peace for the young man, answers that the king’s enemies be as that young man.
The day of the battle in the forest of Ephraim, in the land of Gilead east of the Jordan, with David waiting at the city gate; the news arrives the same day.

2 Samuel 18 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיִּפְקֹד דָּוִד, אֶת-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ; וַיָּשֶׂם עֲלֵיהֶם, שָׂרֵי אֲלָפִים וְשָׂרֵי מֵאוֹת.
And David mustered the people who were with him, and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.note
Y David pasó revista al pueblo que estaba con él, y puso sobre ellos jefes de millares y jefes de centenas.
2
וַיְשַׁלַּח דָּוִד אֶת-הָעָם, הַשְּׁלִשִׁית בְּיַד-יוֹאָב וְהַשְּׁלִשִׁית בְּיַד אֲבִישַׁי בֶּן-צְרוּיָה אֲחִי יוֹאָב, וְהַשְּׁלִשִׁת, בְּיַד אִתַּי הַגִּתִּי;  {ס}  וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-הָעָם, יָצֹא אֵצֵא גַּם-אֲנִי עִמָּכֶם.
And David sent out the people, a third under the hand of Joab, and a third under the hand of Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, and a third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite. And the king said to the people: ‘I will surely go out with you myself also.’note
Y David envió al pueblo, una tercera parte bajo la mano de Joab, y una tercera parte bajo la mano de Abisai hijo de Sarvia, hermano de Joab, y una tercera parte bajo la mano de Itai el geteo. Y el rey dijo al pueblo: «Saldré sin falta con ustedes, yo también».
3
וַיֹּאמֶר הָעָם לֹא תֵצֵא, כִּי אִם-נֹס נָנוּס לֹא-יָשִׂימוּ אֵלֵינוּ לֵב וְאִם-יָמֻתוּ חֶצְיֵנוּ לֹא-יָשִׂימוּ אֵלֵינוּ לֵב--כִּי-עַתָּה כָמֹנוּ, עֲשָׂרָה אֲלָפִים; וְעַתָּה טוֹב, כִּי-תִהְיֶה-לָּנוּ מֵעִיר לעזיר לַעְזוֹר.  {ס}
And the people said: ‘You shall not go out; for if we flee away, they will not set their heart upon us, and if half of us die, they will not set their heart upon us; for now — like us, ten thousand — and now it is good that you be to us a help from the city.’note
Y el pueblo dijo: «No saldrás; porque si huimos, no pondrán su corazón en nosotros, y si muere la mitad de nosotros, no pondrán su corazón en nosotros; porque ahora — como nosotros, diez mil — y ahora es bueno que seas para nosotros una ayuda desde la ciudad».
4
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵיהֶם הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֲשֶׁר-יִיטַב בְּעֵינֵיכֶם אֶעֱשֶׂה; וַיַּעֲמֹד הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֶל-יַד הַשַּׁעַר, וְכָל-הָעָם יָצְאוּ, לְמֵאוֹת וְלַאֲלָפִים.
And he said to them: ‘What is good in your eyes I will do.’ And the king stood by the side of the gate, and all the people went out by hundreds and by thousands.note
Y él les dijo: «Lo que sea bueno a sus ojos haré». Y el rey se puso de pie al lado de la puerta, y todo el pueblo salió de cien en cien y de mil en mil.
5
וַיְצַו הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶת-יוֹאָב וְאֶת-אֲבִישַׁי וְאֶת-אִתַּי, לֵאמֹר, לְאַט-לִי, לַנַּעַר לְאַבְשָׁלוֹם; וְכָל-הָעָם שָׁמְעוּ, בְּצַוֺּת הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶת-כָּל-הַשָּׂרִים--עַל-דְּבַר אַבְשָׁלוֹם.
And the king commanded Joab and Abishai and Ittai, saying: ‘Gently for me, for the young man, for Absalom.’ And all the people heard when the king commanded all the commanders concerning Absalom.note
Y el rey ordenó a Joab y a Abisai y a Itai, diciendo: «Con suavidad por mí, con el joven, con Absalón». Y todo el pueblo oyó cuando el rey ordenó a todos los jefes acerca de Absalón.
6
וַיֵּצֵא הָעָם הַשָּׂדֶה, לִקְרַאת יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַתְּהִי הַמִּלְחָמָה, בְּיַעַר אֶפְרָיִם.
And the people went out into the field to meet Israel; and the battle was in the forest of Ephraim.note
Y el pueblo salió al campo al encuentro de Israel; y la batalla fue en el bosque de Efraín.
7
וַיִּנָּגְפוּ שָׁם עַם יִשְׂרָאֵל, לִפְנֵי עַבְדֵי דָוִד; וַתְּהִי-שָׁם הַמַּגֵּפָה גְדוֹלָה, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא--עֶשְׂרִים אָלֶף.
And the people of Israel were struck down there before the servants of David; and the slaughter there that day was great — twenty thousand.note
Y el pueblo de Israel fue derrotado allí delante de los siervos de David; y la matanza allí aquel día fue grande — veinte mil.
8
וַתְּהִי-שָׁם הַמִּלְחָמָה נפצות נָפוֹצֶת, עַל-פְּנֵי כָל-הָאָרֶץ; וַיֶּרֶב הַיַּעַר לֶאֱכֹל בָּעָם, מֵאֲשֶׁר אָכְלָה הַחֶרֶב בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא.
And the battle there was scattered over the face of all the land; and the forest devoured more of the people than the sword devoured that day.note
Y la batalla se extendió allí sobre la faz de toda la tierra; y el bosque devoró más del pueblo que lo que devoró la espada aquel día.
9
וַיִּקָּרֵא, אַבְשָׁלוֹם, לִפְנֵי, עַבְדֵי דָוִד; וְאַבְשָׁלוֹם רֹכֵב עַל-הַפֶּרֶד, וַיָּבֹא הַפֶּרֶד תַּחַת שׂוֹבֶךְ הָאֵלָה הַגְּדוֹלָה וַיֶּחֱזַק רֹאשׁוֹ בָאֵלָה וַיֻּתַּן בֵּין הַשָּׁמַיִם וּבֵין הָאָרֶץ, וְהַפֶּרֶד אֲשֶׁר-תַּחְתָּיו, עָבָר.
And Absalom chanced to meet the servants of David. And Absalom was riding on the mule, and the mule came under the thick branches of the great terebinth, and his head was caught fast in the terebinth, and he was given between the heavens and the earth; and the mule that was under him passed on.note
Y Absalón se encontró con los siervos de David. Y Absalón iba montado en la mula, y la mula entró bajo las ramas espesas del gran terebinto, y su cabeza quedó atrapada en el terebinto, y fue puesto entre los cielos y la tierra; y la mula que estaba debajo de él pasó adelante.
10
וַיַּרְא אִישׁ אֶחָד, וַיַּגֵּד לְיוֹאָב; וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה רָאִיתִי אֶת-אַבְשָׁלֹם, תָּלוּי, בָּאֵלָה.
And a certain man saw it, and told Joab; and he said: ‘Look, I saw Absalom hanging in a terebinth.’note
Y un hombre lo vio, y avisó a Joab; y dijo: «Mira, vi a Absalón colgado de un terebinto».
11
וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב, לָאִישׁ הַמַּגִּיד לוֹ, וְהִנֵּה רָאִיתָ, וּמַדּוּעַ לֹא-הִכִּיתוֹ שָׁם אָרְצָה; וְעָלַי, לָתֶת לְךָ עֲשָׂרָה כֶסֶף, וַחֲגֹרָה, אֶחָת.
And Joab said to the man who told him: ‘And look, you saw it! And why did you not strike him there to the ground? And it would have been on me to give you ten of silver and one belt.’note
Y Joab dijo al hombre que se lo avisó: «¡Y mira, lo viste! ¿Y por qué no lo heriste allí hasta el suelo? Y me tocaba a mí darte diez de plata y un cinto».
12
וַיֹּאמֶר הָאִישׁ, אֶל-יוֹאָב, ולא וְלוּא אָנֹכִי שֹׁקֵל עַל-כַּפַּי אֶלֶף כֶּסֶף, לֹא-אֶשְׁלַח יָדִי אֶל-בֶּן-הַמֶּלֶךְ:  כִּי בְאָזְנֵינוּ צִוָּה הַמֶּלֶךְ, אֹתְךָ וְאֶת-אֲבִישַׁי וְאֶת-אִתַּי לֵאמֹר, שִׁמְרוּ-מִי, בַּנַּעַר בְּאַבְשָׁלוֹם.
And the man said to Joab: ‘Even if I were weighing in my palms a thousand of silver, I would not send my hand against the king’s son; for in our ears the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, saying: “Take care, whoever you are, of the young man, of Absalom.”note
Y el hombre dijo a Joab: «Aunque yo estuviera pesando en mis palmas mil de plata, no extendería mi mano contra el hijo del rey; porque a nuestros oídos ordenó el rey a ti y a Abisai y a Itai, diciendo: “Cuídense, quienquiera que sean, del joven, de Absalón”.
13
אוֹ-עָשִׂיתִי בנפשו בְנַפְשִׁי שֶׁקֶר, וְכָל-דָּבָר לֹא-יִכָּחֵד מִן-הַמֶּלֶךְ; וְאַתָּה, תִּתְיַצֵּב מִנֶּגֶד.
Or I would have dealt falsely against my own life — and no matter is hidden from the king — and you yourself would have stood aloof.’note
O yo habría obrado con falsedad contra mi propia vida — y ninguna cosa se esconde del rey — y tú mismo te habrías puesto aparte».
14
וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב, לֹא-כֵן אֹחִילָה לְפָנֶיךָ; וַיִּקַּח שְׁלֹשָׁה שְׁבָטִים בְּכַפּוֹ, וַיִּתְקָעֵם בְּלֵב אַבְשָׁלוֹם--עוֹדֶנּוּ חַי, בְּלֵב הָאֵלָה.
And Joab said: ‘I will not linger like this before you.’ And he took three rods in his hand, and thrust them into the heart of Absalom, while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth.note
Y Joab dijo: «No me detendré así delante de ti». Y tomó tres varas en su mano, y las clavó en el corazón de Absalón, estando él aún vivo en el corazón del terebinto.
15
וַיָּסֹבּוּ עֲשָׂרָה נְעָרִים, נֹשְׂאֵי כְּלֵי יוֹאָב; וַיַּכּוּ אֶת-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וַיְמִתֻהוּ.
And ten young men, bearers of Joab’s weapons, surrounded and struck Absalom, and put him to death.note
Y diez jóvenes, portadores de las armas de Joab, rodearon a Absalón y lo hirieron, y le dieron muerte.
16
וַיִּתְקַע יוֹאָב, בַּשֹּׁפָר, וַיָּשָׁב הָעָם, מִרְדֹף אַחֲרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל:  כִּי-חָשַׂךְ יוֹאָב, אֶת-הָעָם.
And Joab blew the horn, and the people turned back from pursuing after Israel; for Joab held back the people.note
Y Joab tocó el cuerno, y el pueblo volvió de perseguir a Israel; porque Joab detuvo al pueblo.
17
וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת-אַבְשָׁלוֹם, וַיַּשְׁלִכוּ אֹתוֹ בַיַּעַר אֶל-הַפַּחַת הַגָּדוֹל, וַיַּצִּבוּ עָלָיו גַּל-אֲבָנִים, גָּדוֹל מְאֹד; וְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל--נָסוּ, אִישׁ לְאֹהָלָו.
And they took Absalom and threw him into the great pit in the forest, and set up over him a very great heap of stones; and all Israel fled, each to his tent.note
Y tomaron a Absalón y lo echaron en el bosque, en la gran fosa, y levantaron sobre él un montón de piedras muy grande; y todo Israel huyó, cada uno a su tienda.
18
וְאַבְשָׁלֹם לָקַח, וַיַּצֶּב-לוֹ בְחַיָּו אֶת-מַצֶּבֶת אֲשֶׁר בְּעֵמֶק-הַמֶּלֶךְ--כִּי אָמַר אֵין-לִי בֵן, בַּעֲבוּר הַזְכִּיר שְׁמִי; וַיִּקְרָא לַמַּצֶּבֶת, עַל-שְׁמוֹ, וַיִּקָּרֵא לָהּ יַד אַבְשָׁלוֹם, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.  {ס}
Now Absalom in his lifetime had taken and set up for himself the pillar that is in the king’s valley; for he said: ‘I have no son, to keep my name in remembrance’; and he called the pillar by his own name, and it is called Absalom’s hand to this day.note
Y Absalón, en vida, había tomado y levantado para sí la columna que está en el valle del rey; porque dijo: «No tengo hijo, para que se recuerde mi nombre»; y llamó a la columna con su propio nombre, y se la llama la mano de Absalón hasta hoy.
19
וַאֲחִימַעַץ בֶּן-צָדוֹק, אָמַר, אָרוּצָה נָּא, וַאֲבַשְּׂרָה אֶת-הַמֶּלֶךְ:  כִּי-שְׁפָטוֹ יְהוָה, מִיַּד אֹיְבָיו.
And Ahimaaz son of Zadok said: ‘Let me run, please, and bring the king tidings: that Jehovah has judged him from the hand of his enemies.’note
Y Ahimaas hijo de Sadoc dijo: «Déjame correr, por favor, y llevar al rey la noticia de que Jehová lo ha juzgado de la mano de sus enemigos».
20
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ יוֹאָב, לֹא אִישׁ בְּשֹׂרָה אַתָּה הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, וּבִשַּׂרְתָּ, בְּיוֹם אַחֵר; וְהַיּוֹם הַזֶּה לֹא תְבַשֵּׂר, כִּי-עַל-   כֵּן בֶּן-הַמֶּלֶךְ מֵת.
And Joab said to him: ‘You are not a man of tidings today; you shall bring tidings another day; but today you shall not bring tidings, because the king’s son is dead.’note
Y Joab le dijo: «Hoy no eres hombre de noticias; llevarás noticias otro día; pero hoy no llevarás noticias, porque el hijo del rey ha muerto».
21
וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב לַכּוּשִׁי, לֵךְ הַגֵּד לַמֶּלֶךְ אֲשֶׁר רָאִיתָה; וַיִּשְׁתַּחוּ כוּשִׁי לְיוֹאָב, וַיָּרֹץ.
And Joab said to the Cushite: ‘Go, tell the king what you have seen.’ And the Cushite bowed down to Joab, and ran.note
Y Joab dijo al cusita: «Ve, di al rey lo que has visto». Y el cusita se postró ante Joab, y corrió.
22
וַיֹּסֶף עוֹד אֲחִימַעַץ בֶּן-צָדוֹק, וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל-יוֹאָב, וִיהִי מָה, אָרֻצָה-נָּא גַם-אָנִי אַחֲרֵי הַכּוּשִׁי; וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב, לָמָּה-זֶּה אַתָּה רָץ בְּנִי, וּלְכָה, אֵין-בְּשׂוֹרָה מֹצֵאת.
And Ahimaaz son of Zadok added, and said to Joab: ‘Come what may, let me run too, please, after the Cushite.’ And Joab said: ‘Why is it that you run, my son, when there is no tidings to be found?’note
Y Ahimaas hijo de Sadoc volvió a decir a Joab: «Pase lo que pase, déjame correr yo también tras el cusita, por favor». Y Joab dijo: «¿Por qué corres, hijo mío, si no hay noticia que se halle?»
23
וִיהִי-מָה אָרוּץ, וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ רוּץ; וַיָּרָץ אֲחִימַעַץ דֶּרֶךְ הַכִּכָּר, וַיַּעֲבֹר אֶת-הַכּוּשִׁי.
‘Come what may, I will run.’ And he said to him: ‘Run.’ And Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain, and passed the Cushite.note
«Pase lo que pase, correré». Y le dijo: «Corre». Y Ahimaas corrió por el camino de la llanura, y pasó al cusita.
24
וְדָוִד יוֹשֵׁב, בֵּין-שְׁנֵי הַשְּׁעָרִים; וַיֵּלֶךְ הַצֹּפֶה אֶל-גַּג הַשַּׁעַר, אֶל-הַחוֹמָה, וַיִּשָּׂא אֶת-עֵינָיו וַיַּרְא, וְהִנֵּה-אִישׁ רָץ לְבַדּוֹ.
And David was sitting between the two gates; and the watchman went up to the roof of the gate, to the wall, and lifted his eyes and saw, and look, a man running alone.note
Y David estaba sentado entre las dos puertas; y el centinela subió al terrado de la puerta, a la muralla, y alzó los ojos y vio, y mira, un hombre que corría solo.
25

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 2 Samuel 18 David musters the army — three thirds, “I will surely go out”, and the people who will not let him

Commanders of thousands and of hundreds. “And David mustered the people who were with him, and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds”: “commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds” stands in three verses. They are Deuteronomy 1:15, where Moses appoints them, 1 Samuel 22:7, where Saul asks his Benjamites whether the son of Jesse will make all of them “rulers of thousands and rulers of hundreds” (both now on these pages), and this one. Saul’s question was meant to be answered no, and here David does it for his own men. The Hebrew verb is pakad. The ASV has “numbered the people”. The NIV has “mustered the men”. The Douay has “having reviewed his people”. The TLB has “regimental colonels and company commanders”.

A third each, and the king going out. “A third under the hand of Joab, and a third under the hand of Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab’s brother, and a third under the hand of Ittai the Gittite”: the foreigner from Gath who came with six hundred men and swore to follow the king wherever he would be (2 Samuel 15:19–22, now on these pages) commands a third beside Joab and his brother. Then the king says, in the Hebrew with the doubled verb, yatso etse, “going out I will go out”. The ASV has “I will surely go forth with you myself also”. The NIV has “I myself will surely march out with you”. The Douay has “I also will go forth with you”. Hushai’s counsel to Absalom had been that the prince go into the battle himself (2 Samuel 17:11, now on these pages); here David proposes to do the same, and his people refuse.

For now — or “you are”. “For now — like us, ten thousand — and now it is good that you be to us a help from the city”: the Masoretic text has attah spelled with an ayin, “now”, and the word for “you” is the same but for its first letter, an aleph. Every English version on the page reads “you”. The ASV has “thou art worth ten thousand of us”. The NIV has “you are worth ten thousand of us”. The NWT 1984 has “you are worth ten thousand of us”. The TLB has “You are worth ten thousand of us”. The Douay has “thou alone art accounted for ten thousand”. Rahlfs’ Greek has it too, hoti sy hōs hēmeis deka chiliades, “for you are like us, ten thousand”. This translation prints the word the Hebrew has, with dashes, and leaves the reading the versions share to the note. For “they will not set their heart upon us” the NWT 1984 has “they would not set heart upon us” and the ASV has “they will not care for us”. “A help from the city”: the written form is la‘azir and the reading is la‘azor, “to help”, and this translation follows the pointed reading.

What is good in your eyes. “What is good in your eyes I will do”: the ASV has “What seemeth you best I will do”. The NIV has “I will do whatever seems best to you”. The NWT 1984 has “Whatever seems good in YOUR eyes I shall do”. The man who would go out in person stands “by the side of the gate” and watches his people go by “by hundreds and by thousands”, and he will be at a gate again when the news comes (verse 24).

Verses 5–8 · 2 Samuel 18 “Gently for me, for the young man, for Absalom” — the king’s charge, the forest of Ephraim, and the forest that devoured

Gently for me. “Gently for me, for the young man, for Absalom”: le‘at li lanna‘ar le-Avshalom, three phrases that narrow from “me” to “the young man” to the name. “For the young man, for Absalom” stands in three verses: this charge, and the king’s two questions in verses 29 and 32, in the same words. The ASV has “Deal gently for my sake with the young man, even with Absalom”. The NIV has “Be gentle with the young man Absalom for my sake”. The TLB has “For my sake, deal gently with young Absalom”. The NWT 1984 has “Deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom”. The Douay has “Save me the boy Absalom”. The word is na‘ar, a rank and not an age: Ziba, who has fifteen sons (2 Samuel 9:10), is called one too (16:1, both now on these pages), and Absalom has three sons and a daughter (14:27). “And all the people heard”: the charge is given in the hearing of everyone, and a soldier will say so in verse 12.

The forest of Ephraim. “And the battle was in the forest of Ephraim”: the page names no site and does not say why a forest east of the Jordan, where Absalom’s army has camped in Gilead (2 Samuel 17:26, now on these pages), carries that tribe’s name. The ASV has “in the forest of Ephraim”, and so does the Douay with “in the forest of Ephraim”.

A great slaughter. “And the people of Israel were struck down there before the servants of David; and the slaughter there that day was great — twenty thousand”: the Hebrew noun is magefah, and it is the word Hushai used for the rumor he told Absalom to fear, “There has been a slaughter among the people who follow Absalom” (2 Samuel 17:9, now on these pages). The ASV has “a great slaughter there that day”. The NIV has “the casualties that day were great”. The NWT 1984 has “the slaughter there turned out to be great”. The Douay has “a great slaughter was made”.

The forest devoured. “And the forest devoured more of the people than the sword devoured that day”: the sword that devours is Abner’s, “Shall the sword devour for ever?” (2 Samuel 2:26), and David’s message to Joab about Rabbah, “the sword devours in this way and in that” (2 Samuel 11:25, both now on these pages). The ASV has “the forest devoured more people that day than the sword devoured”. The NIV has “the forest swallowed up more men that day than the sword”. The TLB has “more men disappeared in the forest than were killed”. The NWT 1984 has “the forest did more in eating up the people than the sword did”. The Douay has “the forest consumed”. For the battle “scattered over the face of all the land” the written form is nifetsot and the reading is nafotset, and this translation follows the pointed reading. The ASV has “spread over the face of all the country” and the Douay has “scattered over the face of all the country”.

Verses 9–15 · 2 Samuel 18 Absalom in the terebinth — his head and not his hair, a man who would not, three rods, and ten young men

His head, not his hair. “And his head was caught fast in the terebinth, and he was given between the heavens and the earth”: the Hebrew says rosh, “head”, and nothing in the chapter mentions hair. The NIV has “Absalom’s hair got caught in the tree”. The TLB has “his hair caught in the branches”. The ASV has “his head caught hold of the oak”. The NWT 1984 has “his head got caught fast”. The Douay has “his head stuck in the oak”. The hair is the story of 2 Samuel 14:26 (now on these pages), where it is weighed at two hundred shekels, and it is the reader who brings it here. The tree is the elah, the terebinth under which the angel of Jehovah sat at Ophrah (Judges 6:11, now on these pages). The ASV has “a great oak” and the NWT 1984 has “a massive big tree”. For what happens to him the ASV has “he was taken up between heaven and earth”, the NIV has “left hanging in midair”, and the Douay has “he hung between the heaven and the earth”; the Hebrew is the passive vayyuttan, “he was given”. “The mule that was under him passed on”: the verb is avar, the one used in 17:24 of Absalom crossing the Jordan.

A man who would not. “Look, I saw Absalom hanging in a terebinth”: the man who tells Joab is not named, and when Joab says he would have owed him “ten of silver and one belt”, he answers that he would not send his hand against the king’s son for a thousand. The ASV has “ten pieces of silver, and a girdle”. The NIV has “ten shekels of silver and a warrior’s belt”, where the Hebrew says only “ten of silver”. The NWT 1984 has “ten pieces of silver and a belt”. The Douay has “ten sicles of silver, and belt”. The king’s charge in his words is shimru-mi bannar be-Avshalom, with a word mi that means “who”. The ASV has “Beware that none touch the young man Absalom”. The NWT 1984 has “WATCH, whoever”. The NIV has “Protect the young man Absalom for my sake”. The Douay has “Save me the boy Absalom”. In verse 13 the written form is benafsho, “against his life”, and the reading is benafshi, “against my life”, and this translation follows the pointed reading. The ASV has “against his life”. The NWT 1984 has “against his soul”. The NIV has “put my life in jeopardy”. The Douay has “against my own life”. The soldier’s whole argument is that Joab would have stood back, titytsev minneged, and Joab does not argue the point.

Three rods. “And Joab said: ‘I will not linger like this before you.’ And he took three rods in his hand, and thrust them into the heart of Absalom, while he was still alive in the heart of the terebinth”: the Hebrew is shelosha shevatim, and shevet is a rod, a staff, a ruler’s sceptre, and a tribe. It does not say what the weapon was. The ASV has “three darts”. The NIV has “three javelins”. The TLB has “three daggers”. The Douay has “three lances”. The NWT 1984 has “three shafts”. It is the word of the “rod of men” that God says he will use on David’s son in 2 Samuel 7:14 (now on these pages). For Joab’s words the ASV has “I may not tarry thus with thee”. The NIV has “I’m not going to wait like this for you”. The NWT 1984 has “Let me not hold myself up this way before you!”. The noun lev, “heart”, stands twice, and it is the heart that Absalom stole from the men of Israel (2 Samuel 15:6, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 keeps both with “in the heart of the big tree”, and the ASV has “in the midst of the oak”. Joab is the son of Zeruiah whom David said was too hard for him (2 Samuel 3:39, now on these pages).

Ten young men. “And ten young men, bearers of Joab’s weapons, surrounded and struck Absalom, and put him to death”: the soldier had been offered ten of silver, and ten young men do what he would not. The ASV has “ten young men that bare Joab’s armor compassed about and smote Absalom”. The NIV has “ten of Joab’s armor-bearers surrounded Absalom”. The king’s order was heard by all the people, and Joab breaks it in front of the soldier who would not; the page gives the king’s command in verse 5, the soldier’s refusal in 12, and the killing in 14 and 15, and adds no comment.

Verses 16–18 · 2 Samuel 18 Joab’s horn, a great heap of stones in the pit, and the pillar called Absalom’s hand

Joab blew the horn. “And Joab blew the horn, and the people turned back from pursuing after Israel; for Joab held back the people”: Joab stops a pursuit with the horn as he did at the end of the chase of Abner, “and all the people stood still, and they pursued Israel no more” (2 Samuel 2:28, now on these pages); here the noun is written without the vav that it has there. The ASV has “blew the trumpet”. The NWT 1984 has “blew the horn”. The ASV has “held back the people”, and the NIV has “halted them”. At Gibeon the horn stopped the pursuit of Abner’s men and here it stops the pursuit of Absalom’s; both times the page calls the pursued “Israel”.

A great heap of stones. “They took Absalom and threw him into the great pit in the forest, and set up over him a very great heap of stones; and all Israel fled, each to his tent”: “a great heap of stones” stands in three verses: Joshua 7:26, over Achan, and 8:29, over the king of Ai (both now on these pages), and this one. In both Joshua verses the heap is there “until this day”; here that phrase is given to the pillar of the next verse and not to the heap. The ASV has “raised over him a very great heap of stones”. The NIV has “piled up a large heap of rocks over him”. The NWT 1984 has “a very big pile of stones”. The TLB has “a great heap of stones”. For the people the ASV has “fled every one to his tent”. The NIV has “fled to their homes”. The Douay has “fled to their own dwellings”.

The king’s valley and Absalom’s hand. “Now Absalom in his lifetime had taken and set up for himself the pillar that is in the king’s valley”: “the king’s valley” stands in two verses: Genesis 14:17 (now on these pages), where the king of Sodom meets Abram at “the Valley of Shaveh (that is, the King’s Valley)”, and this one. The ASV has “in the king’s dale”. The NWT 1984 has “in the Low Plain of the King”. The NIV has “in the King’s Valley”. “I have no son, to keep my name in remembrance”: the ASV has “I have no son to keep my name in remembrance”, the NIV has “I have no son to carry on the memory of my name”, and the TLB has “I have no sons to carry on my name”. Three sons were born to him (2 Samuel 14:27, now on these pages), and the page does not say what became of them. The pillar is called yad Avshalom. The ASV has “Absalom’s monument”. The NIV has “Absalom’s Monument”. The Douay has “the hand of Absalom”, the Hebrew word, and Rahlfs’ Greek has Cheir Abessalōm. Saul also set up a “hand” for himself at Carmel (1 Samuel 15:12, now on these pages), and Shimei had said that Jehovah gave the kingdom “into the hand of Absalom” (2 Samuel 16:8, now on these pages). The word runs through the chapter: the thirds are “under the hand” of their commanders (verse 2), the king stands “by the side”, the Hebrew yad, of the gate (4), the soldier will not “send my hand” (12), and Ahimaaz blesses Jehovah for the men who “lifted their hand” (28).

Verses 19–23 · 2 Samuel 18 Ahimaaz asks to run — “Jehovah has judged him”, a man of tidings, the Cushite, and the way of the plain

Judged him. “Let me run, please, and bring the king tidings: that Jehovah has judged him from the hand of his enemies”: the verb is shafat, “judged”. The ASV has “Jehovah hath avenged him of his enemies”. The NIV has “the Lord has vindicated him”. The TLB has “the Lord has saved him from his enemy Absalom”. The Douay has “the Lord hath done judgment for him”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah has judged him [to free him]”. The Cushite will use the same verb in verse 31. Joab’s answer is “You are not a man of tidings today”, and his reason is that “the king’s son is dead”. The ASV has “Thou shalt not be the bearer of tidings this day”. The NIV has “You are not the one to take the news today”. The NWT 1984 has “You are not a man of news this day”. The Douay has “Thou shalt not be the messenger this day”. Joab and the soldier say “the king’s son”, ben-hammelekh (verses 12 and 20); the king says “the young man”.

The Cushite. “Go, tell the king what you have seen”: hakkushi, with the article, an unnamed man. The NIV has “a Cushite”. The TLB has “a man from Cush”. The Douay has “Chusai”, the same name it printed for Hushai in the last chapter, and Rahlfs’ Greek does the same, writing Chousi both for Hushai (17:5) and for the Cushite (18:22), where the Hebrew has two names. Joab sends the man who saw it, and he bows to Joab and runs.

No tidings to be found. “Come what may, let me run too, please, after the Cushite”: the Hebrew vihi mah is said twice, in verses 22 and 23. Joab’s “Why is it that you run, my son, when there is no tidings to be found?” has a hard last word, motset, “finding”. The NWT 1984 has “when there is no news being found for you”. The ASV has “seeing that thou wilt have no reward for the tidings”. The NIV has “You don’t have any news that will bring you a reward”. The TLB has “There is no further news to send”. The Douay has “thou wilt not be the bearer of good tidings”. The noun is besorah, “tidings”, and in 2 Samuel 4:10 (now on these pages) it is the reward David says he should have given the man who thought he was “a bringer of good news”, so the ASV and the NIV read Joab’s word the same way.

The way of the plain. “And Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain, and passed the Cushite”: “the way of the plain”, derekh hakkikkar, stands only here. The kikkar is the round plain of the Jordan (see kikkar). The ASV has “the way of the Plain”. The NIV has “by way of the plain”. The NWT 1984 has “the way of the District”. The Douay has “by a nearer way”. The TLB has “took a shortcut across the plain”. Rahlfs’ Greek does not translate it and writes Kechar. The one who was sent second, and who had nothing to say worth a reward, arrives first.

Verses 24–27 · 2 Samuel 18 The watchman and the king — a man running alone, “tidings in his mouth”, and “a good man”

Between the two gates. “And David was sitting between the two gates; and the watchman went up to the roof of the gate, to the wall, and lifted his eyes and saw, and look, a man running alone”: the king who stood “by the side of the gate” in verse 4 now sits in it. The ASV has “between the two gates”. The NIV has “between the inner and outer gates”. The ASV has “a man running alone” and the NWT 1984 has “a man running by himself”.

Alone. “If he is alone, there are tidings in his mouth”: levaddo, “alone”, stands three times in three verses (24, 25, 26), and in 2 Samuel 17:2 (now on these pages) it was the word of Ahithophel’s plan to strike “the king alone”. The king reads the news from the running, and the page does not explain his rule. The ASV has “If he be alone, there is tidings in his mouth”. The NIV has “If he is alone, he must have good news”, where the Hebrew has only “tidings”. The NWT 1984 has “If he is by himself, there is news in his mouth”. The Douay has “good tidings in his mouth”. For the second runner the ASV has “He also bringeth tidings”, the NWT 1984 has “This one also is a news bearer”, and the Douay has “He also is a good messenger”.

A good man. “And the watchman said: ‘I see the running of the first is like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok.’ And the king said: ‘This is a good man, and he comes with good tidings’”: the Hebrew says tov twice, a good man and good tidings, and the king reads one from the other. The ASV has “He is a good man, and cometh with good tidings”. The NWT 1984 has “with good news he should come”. The ASV has “the running of the foremost” and the NIV has “the first one runs like Ahimaaz”. The same Ahimaaz’s companion Jonathan will be greeted in nearly the same words by another man who wants good news, when Adonijah says to him “Come in, for you are a worthy man and bring good news” (1 Kings 1:42, now on these pages), and what Jonathan brings is the end of Adonijah’s feast.

Verses 28–32 · 2 Samuel 18 “Is it peace for the young man?” — Ahimaaz’s “Peace!”, the great tumult, and the Cushite’s “be as the young man”

Peace! “And Ahimaaz called, and said to the king: ‘Peace!’ and bowed down to the king with his face to the ground”: shalom. The ASV has “All is well”. The NIV has “All is well!”. The NWT 1984 has “It is well!”. The Douay has “God save thee, O king”. His blessing is for Jehovah “who has shut up the men who lifted their hand against my lord the king”, siggar. The ASV has “delivered up”. The NIV has “delivered up”. The NWT 1984 has “surrendered”. The Douay has “shut up”, the Hebrew verb. The TLB has “destroyed”.

Is it peace for the young man? “And the king said: ‘Is it peace for the young man, for Absalom?’”: “peace for the young man”, shalom lanna‘ar, stands in two verses, here and in verse 32, and the king asks nothing about the battle. The NIV has “Is the young man Absalom safe?”. The ASV has “Is it well with the young man Absalom?”. Ahimaaz answers “I saw the great tumult when Joab sent the king’s servant, and your servant; and I did not know what”. The ASV has “I saw a great tumult, but I knew not what it was”. The NIV has “I saw great confusion”. The NWT 1984 has “I saw the great commotion”. The Douay has “I know nothing else”. Joab had told him in verse 20 that the king’s son was dead, and the page lets the reader weigh “I did not know what”. The king says “Turn aside, stand here”, and he stands still.

Be as the young man. “Let my lord the king receive tidings; for Jehovah has judged for you today from the hand of all who rose up against you”: the Cushite has the verb shafat that Ahimaaz had in verse 19. The ASV has “Tidings for my lord the king”. The NIV has “hear the good news”. The NWT 1984 has “Let my lord the king accept news”. The Douay has “I bring good tidings”. The king asks the same question, and the answer is a wish: “May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who have risen up against you for evil, be as the young man”. The ASV has “be as that young man is”. The NIV has “be like that young man”. The NWT 1984 has “become as the young man”. The TLB has “May all of your enemies be as that young man is!”. The Cushite puts Absalom among the king’s enemies without saying that he is dead, in the king’s own words, “the young man”.

Where the chapter ends. The English versions number the king’s cry “O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom!” as 2 Samuel 18:33. In the Hebrew it is 19:1, and this translation follows the Hebrew, so that the chapter ends with the Cushite’s answer and the king’s cry opens the next.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds”; “going out I will go out”; the Masoretic “for now” in verse 3; “Gently for me, for the young man, for Absalom”; “the forest devoured”; “his head” and not his hair; “the terebinth”; “ten of silver and one belt”; “Take care, whoever you are”; “three rods”; “the heart of Absalom” and “the heart of the terebinth”; “a great heap of stones”; “the king’s valley”; “Absalom’s hand”; “a man of tidings”; “the Cushite”; “the way of the plain”; “alone”; “Peace!”; “shut up”; and “Is it peace for the young man”; with the pointed readings “to help”, “scattered” and “my own life” and the written forms in the notes.

Echoes paid. Saul’s “rulers of thousands and rulers of hundreds” and Moses’ commanders (1 Samuel 22:7; Deuteronomy 1:15); Ittai’s oath (2 Samuel 15:19–22); Hushai’s going into the battle and his slaughter (2 Samuel 17:11, 9); the sword that devours (2 Samuel 2:26; 11:25); Joab’s horn (2 Samuel 2:28); the sons of Zeruiah too hard for David (2 Samuel 3:39); the weighed hair, the three sons and Tamar (2 Samuel 14:26–27) and the stolen heart (2 Samuel 15:6); the rod of men (2 Samuel 7:14); the terebinth at Ophrah (Judges 6:11); the heaps over Achan and the king of Ai (Joshua 7:26; 8:29); the King’s Valley (Genesis 14:17); Saul’s monument (1 Samuel 15:12) and the kingdom given “into the hand of Absalom” (2 Samuel 16:8); the reward for tidings (2 Samuel 4:10); Ahimaaz and Jonathan (2 Samuel 17:17–21); Ziba’s fifteen sons (2 Samuel 9:10); and Adonijah’s greeting to Jonathan (1 Kings 1:42).

Echoes planted. The king’s cry in the chamber over the gate, in the Hebrew 19:1 and 19:5, and the victory turned to mourning (2 Samuel 19:3); Joab’s rebuke that the king has shamed the faces of his servants and must “speak to the heart” of them (2 Samuel 19:6–8); the king sitting in the gate while Israel “had fled every man to his tent” (2 Samuel 19:9); Amasa in Joab’s place (2 Samuel 19:14); Shimei at the Jordan (2 Samuel 19:17–24); Barzillai and Chimham (2 Samuel 19:32–40); and Joab’s horn at the end of Sheba’s revolt (2 Samuel 20:22). None of these is translated here yet.
