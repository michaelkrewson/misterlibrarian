David speaks to Jehovah the words of a song in the day Jehovah delivered him from the palm of all his enemies and from the palm of Saul. Jehovah is his crag, stronghold, rock, shield, horn of salvation and high tower; the breakers of death, the torrents of worthlessness, the cords of Sheol and the snares of death surrounded him, and from his temple Jehovah heard his cry. The earth shakes and the foundations of the heavens tremble; smoke, fire and coals come from his nostrils and mouth; he rides a cherub on the wings of the wind, thunders from heaven, scatters his enemies with arrows and lightning, and lays bare the channels of the sea. He draws David out of many waters and brings him to a broad place because he delighted in him; David speaks of his righteousness and the cleanness of his hands. Jehovah is loyal with the loyal and twisted with the crooked, saves the afflicted and brings down the haughty, and is David’s lamp; by him David runs at a troop and leaps a wall. He trains David’s hands for war, gives him the shield of his salvation, and David pursues his enemies and finishes them; they cry and are not answered. He is delivered from the contentions of his people and kept as head of nations, and the sons of the stranger cringe. Jehovah lives; David gives thanks among the nations, and the song ends on a tower of salvation for his king, his anointed, David and his seed, forever.
After the wars of the last chapter, in David’s old age: a long song looking back over the years of his flight from Saul and his wars.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 2 Samuel 22 A song “in the day Jehovah delivered him” — crag, stronghold, the horn of salvation, and a Psalm that opens differently

The day of the song. “And David spoke to Jehovah the words of this song in the day that Jehovah delivered him from the palm of all his enemies, and from the palm of Saul”: the noun is kaf, “palm”, twice, and Psalm 18, where the same song stands, has the second as yad, “hand”: miyyad Sha’ul. The ASV has “out of the hand of all his enemies, and out of the hand of Saul”. The NIV has “David sang to the Lord the words of this song”. The NWT 1984 has “out of the palm of all his enemies and out of Saul’s palm”. The Douay has “in the day that the Lord delivered him”. The Psalm’s heading is another one altogether, “For the Leader. A Psalm of David the servant of Jehovah”, and Psalm 18:2 has a line this song lacks, “I love you, Jehovah, my strength”. The song comes after the wars of the last chapter, and the verse does not say which day it means.

Crag and stronghold. “Jehovah is my crag, and my stronghold, and my deliverer for me”: the three names are sal’i, metsudati and mefalleti li (see sela and metsudah). The ASV has “Jehovah is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer, even mine”. The NIV has “The Lord is my rock, my fortress and my deliverer”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah is my crag and my stronghold”. The Douay has “The Lord is my rock, and my strength, and my saviour”. Rahlfs’ Greek has petra mou kai ochyrōma mou. They are the names of the places David hid from Saul: “he went down to the crag” (1 Samuel 23:25) and “stayed in the wilderness, in the strongholds” (23:14, both now on these pages).

Six titles. “My God is my rock; in him I take refuge; my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower, and my refuge, my savior — from violence you save me”: the rock here is tsur (see tsur), the shield is magen, and the horn is the qeren that Hannah lifted (1 Samuel 2:1, now on these pages; see qeren). The ASV has “my shield, and the horn of my salvation, my high tower, and my refuge”. The NIV has “my shield and the horn of my salvation”. The NWT 1984 has “my secure height”. The Douay has “God is my strong one, in him will I trust”. The last clause, “my savior — from violence you save me”, is in this song and not in Psalm 18:3, which ends at “my high tower” (see chamas).

The praised one. “I call on Jehovah, the praised one; and from my enemies I am saved”: the word is meholal, “praised”, placed before the name. The ASV has “who is worthy to be praised”. The NIV has “who is worthy of praise”. The NWT 1984 has “On the One to be praised, Jehovah, I shall call”. The Douay has “I will call on the Lord who is worthy to be praised”. The verbs are imperfects that the versions put in the present, the past or the future, and the notes that follow keep the tense of each verse as the Hebrew allows. “Enemies” stands in verse 1 and again here, and it is the word of the song’s last verses.

Verses 5–7 · 2 Samuel 22 Breakers of death, torrents of worthlessness, the cords of Sheol — and a cry that reaches the temple

Torrents of worthlessness. “For the breakers of death surrounded me; the torrents of worthlessness terrified me”: nachalei beliyaal (see beliyaal), the word of Shimei’s “man of worthlessness” (2 Samuel 16:7) and of Sheba, “a man of worthlessness” (2 Samuel 20:1, both now on these pages). The ASV has “the floods of ungodliness made me afraid”. The NIV has “the torrents of destruction overwhelmed me”. The NWT 1984 has “flash floods of good-for-nothing [men]”. The Douay has “the floods of Belial have made me afraid”. Rahlfs’ Greek has cheimarroi anomias, “torrents of lawlessness”. The Hebrew does not say whether it is men or the name of a power that is being compared to a flood.

Cords and snares. “The cords of Sheol went around me; the snares of death confronted me”: chevlei She’ol (see sheol) and mokeshei mavet. The ASV has “The cords of Sheol were round about me”. The NIV has “The cords of the grave coiled around me”. The NWT 1984 has “The snares of death confronted me”. The Douay has “The cords of hell compassed me”. Rahlfs’ Greek has ōdines thanatou, “pangs of death”. The verb “confronted” is qiddemuni, and the same verb is said of the enemies in verse 19: they “confronted me in the day of my calamity”.

From his temple. “In my distress I called on Jehovah, and to my God I called; and he heard my voice from his temple, and my cry was in his ears”: hekhal, a palace or temple, is the word of the house at Shiloh in Samuel; here it is the place from which he hears, and the song gives it no earthly address. The ASV has “he heard my voice out of his temple”. The NIV has “From his temple he heard my voice”. The NWT 1984 has “Then out of his temple he heard my voice”. The Douay has “he will hear my voice out of his temple”. Psalm 18:7 adds “before him” to the cry, “my cry before him came into his ears”; this song has no such words.

Waters. The song’s first danger is water: breakers and torrents here, “a gathering of waters” in the cloud of verse 12, “the channels of the sea” in verse 16, and “many waters” out of which he is drawn in verse 17. The enemies of verse 1 do not appear again until verse 4 and then in verse 18, and between them the poem speaks in the language of the sea, of Sheol and of fire.

Verses 8–16 · 2 Samuel 22 Jehovah comes down — the shaking earth, smoke and coals, a cherub and the wings of the wind, thunder, arrows, and the sea laid bare

The earth shook. “And the earth shook and quaked; the foundations of the heavens trembled; and they shook, because he was angry”: the Hebrew says “foundations of the heavens”, moserot hashamayim, where Psalm 18:8 has “foundations of the mountains”, moserei harim. The ASV has “The foundations of heaven quaked”. The NIV has “the foundations of the heavens shook”. The NWT 1984 has “The foundations of the heavens themselves became agitated”. The Douay has “the foundations of the mountains were moved”, the reading of the Psalm. Rahlfs’ Greek has ta themelia tou ouranou. The verb of the first clause is written vattitga’ash and read vayyitga’ash. Deborah’s song has the same trembling when Jehovah marches from Seir: “the earth trembled, the heavens dripped” (Judges 5:4–5, now on these pages). Verse 9 has af, “nostril”, the word that also means anger: smoke from the nostrils, fire from the mouth, coals blazing.

A cherub, and the wings of the wind. “And he rode on a cherub, and flew; and he was seen on the wings of the wind”: the last verb is vayyera, “he was seen”, where Psalm 18:11 has vayyede, with a dalet for the resh, which the versions take as “swooped” or “soared”. The ASV has “he was seen upon the wings of the wind”. The NIV has “he soared on the wings of the wind”. The NWT 1984 has “he was visible upon the wings of a spirit”. The Douay has “slid upon the wings of the wind”. The word ruach is both wind and spirit (see ruach), and the NWT chooses the second. The cloud is a covering: the ASV has “darkness pavilions round about him”, the NIV “darkness his canopy around him”, the NWT 1984 “a darkness around him as booths”, the Hebrew sukkot; “a gathering of waters” is chashrat mayim, whose first word is uncertain.

Thunder, arrows, confusion. “Jehovah thundered from the heavens, and the Most High gave his voice… And he sent arrows, and scattered them; lightning, and threw them into confusion”: the verbs are yar’em, “thunders”, and vayyehom, “threw into confusion”, and they stand together at the Philistines’ defeat at Samuel’s sacrifice: “Jehovah thundered with a great voice that day against the Philistines, and threw them into confusion” (1 Samuel 7:10), and Hannah’s “against them he thunders in the heavens” (1 Samuel 2:10, both now on these pages). “And threw them into confusion”, vayhummem, stands in four verses: Joshua 10:10, 1 Samuel 7:10, this one, and Psalm 18:15; in this verse it is the written form, and the reading is vayyahom. The ASV has “Jehovah thundered from heaven” and “lightning, and discomfited them”. The NIV has “The Lord thundered from heaven” and “scattered the enemy”. The NWT 1984 has “From heaven Jehovah began to thunder” and “that he might throw them into confusion”. The Douay has “lightning, and consumed them”. Psalm 18:13–14 add “hailstones and coals of fire” to the thunder, and this song does not.

The channels of the sea. “And the channels of the sea were seen; the foundations of the world were laid bare, at the rebuke of Jehovah, at the blast of the breath of his nostrils”: afiqei yam, and tevel, the world (see tevel). The ASV has “the channels of the sea appeared”. The NIV has “The valleys of the sea were exposed”. The NWT 1984 has “the streambeds of the sea became visible”. The Douay has “the overflowings of the sea appeared”. The breath of his nostrils is the breath at which “the waters piled up” in the song at the sea (Exodus 15:8, now on these pages). In Psalm 18:15 it is “at your rebuke, Jehovah, at the blast of the breath of your nostrils”: the Psalm speaks to him, and this song speaks of him.

Verses 17–25 · 2 Samuel 22 Drawn out of many waters — a broad place, “he delighted in me”, and the cleanness of David’s hands

Drew me out. “He sent from on high, he took me; he drew me out of many waters”: the verb is yamsheni, from mashah (see mashah), the verb of Moses’ name: “Because I drew him out of the water” (Exodus 2:10, now on these pages). The ASV has “he drew me out of many waters”. The NIV has “he drew me out of deep waters”. The NWT 1984 has “drawing me out of great waters”. The Douay has “drew me out of many waters”. The verbs of the verse, “sent”, “took”, “drew”, are the verbs of a rescue, and the shelf puts them in the present, the past or the future, for the Hebrew has the imperfect.

My support. “They confronted me in the day of my calamity, but Jehovah was my support”: mish’an, a staff to lean on. The ASV has “But Jehovah was my stay”. The NIV has “but the Lord was my support”. The NWT 1984 has “But Jehovah became my support”. The Douay has “the Lord became my stay”. “He delivered me from my strong enemy, from those who hated me, for they were too strong for me” (the ASV has “for they were too mighty for me”): the enemy is one and strong, “those who hated me” are many, and neither is named.

He delighted in me. “And he brought me out to a broad place; he rescued me, because he delighted in me”: the verb is chafets (see chafets), the verb of Saul’s servants’ “the king delights in you” (1 Samuel 18:22) and of the young man’s “whoever delights in Joab” (2 Samuel 20:11), and of David’s own “I have no delight in you” that he feared Jehovah might say (2 Samuel 15:26, all now on these pages). The ASV has “he delighted in me”. The NIV has “because he delighted in me”. The NWT 1984 has “because he had found delight in me”. The Douay has “because I pleased him”. The broad place is merchav, of the root of the “broad place of Beth-shan” (2 Samuel 21:12).

Righteousness and clean hands. “Jehovah rewarded me according to my righteousness; according to the cleanness of my hands he repaid me… And I was blameless toward him, and I kept myself from my iniquity”: the ASV has “Jehovah rewarded me according to my righteousness”. The NIV has “the Lord has dealt with me according to my righteousness”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah rewards me according to my righteousness”. The Douay has “The Lord will reward me according to my justice”. In verse 24 the ASV has “I kept myself from mine iniquity”, the NIV “kept myself from sin” and the NWT 1984 “I will keep myself from error on my part”. The words are the words of tsidqah and tamim; the song does not mention Bathsheba or Uriah, and David had said “I have sinned against Jehovah” and been told “Jehovah also has taken away your sin” (2 Samuel 12:13, now on these pages). The page records both texts and does not reconcile them. Psalm 18:21–25 has the same words.

Verses 26–31 · 2 Samuel 22 With the loyal you are loyal — the crooked, the afflicted and the haughty, “you are my lamp”, and a wall leapt

Loyal, blameless, pure. “With the loyal you show yourself loyal; with the blameless hero you show yourself blameless; with the pure you show yourself pure; and with the crooked you show yourself twisted”: chasid (see chasid), gibbor tamim, navar and ikkesh. Psalm 18:26 has gever tamim, “a blameless man”, where this song has gibbor, “hero” (see gibbor). The ASV has “With the perfect man thou wilt show thyself perfect”. The NIV has “to the blameless you show yourself blameless”. The NWT 1984 has “With the faultless, mighty one”. The Douay has “with the valiant perfect”. For the last clause the ASV has “with the perverse thou wilt show thyself froward”, the NIV “to the devious you show yourself shrewd”, the NWT 1984 “with the crooked one you will act as silly”, and the Douay “with the perverse thou wilt be perverted”; the Hebrew verb here is not the Psalm’s.

The afflicted and the haughty. “And an afflicted people you save, and your eyes are on the haughty; you bring them down”: the Psalm has “haughty eyes you bring low”, einayim ramot, and here the eyes are Jehovah’s. The ASV has “the afflicted people thou wilt save”. The NIV has “You save the humble”. The NWT 1984 has “the humble people you will save”. The Douay has “the poor people thou wilt save”. The word “blameless”, tamim, stands in four verses of this poem: 24, 26, 31 and 33.

My lamp. “For you are my lamp, Jehovah; and Jehovah lightens my darkness”: the Hebrew is attah niri, “you are my lamp”, where Psalm 18:28 has “you light my lamp”. The ASV has “For thou art my lamp, O Jehovah”. The NIV has “the Lord turns my darkness into light”. The NWT 1984 has “For you are my lamp, O Jehovah”. The Douay has “For thou art my lamp, O Lord”. Rahlfs’ Greek has lychnos. David’s men swore in the last chapter that he would not “put out the lamp of Israel” (2 Samuel 21:17, now on these pages), and the word here is spelled differently (see ner).

A troop, a wall, a word refined. “For by you I run at a troop; by my God I leap a wall”: the ASV has “do I leap over a wall”, the NIV “with my God I can scale a wall”, the NWT 1984 “I can run against a marauder band” and the Douay “I will leap over the wall”. “God — his way is blameless; the word of Jehovah is refined; he is a shield to all who take refuge in him”: the ASV has “The word of Jehovah is tried”, the NIV “his way is perfect”, the NWT 1984 “The saying of Jehovah is a refined one” and the Douay “the word of the Lord is tried by fire”. The verb is tserufah, smelted. The shield and the refuge of verse 3 come back in verse 31.

Verses 32–37 · 2 Samuel 22 Who is God besides Jehovah — a strong fortress, feet like hinds, a bow of bronze, and “your gentleness”

Besides Jehovah. “For who is God besides Jehovah? And who is a rock besides our God?”: the Hebrew has El and mibbal’adei, where Psalm 18:32 has Eloah and zulati. The ASV has “who is God, save Jehovah”. The NIV has “For who is God besides”. The NWT 1984 has “For who is a God besides Jehovah”. The Douay has “Who is God but the Lord”. It is Hannah’s “there is none holy like Jehovah, for there is none besides you; and there is no rock like our God” (1 Samuel 2:2, now on these pages).

A strong fortress, or one who arms me. “God is my strong fortress, and he makes my way blameless”: this song has ma’uzzi chayil, “my fortress, strength”, where Psalm 18:33 has hame’azreni chayil, “who girds me with strength”. The ASV has “God is my strong fortress”. The NIV has “It is God who arms me with strength”. The NWT 1984 has “The [true] God is my strong fortress”. The Douay has “God who hath girded me with strength”. Rahlfs’ Greek has ho ischyros ho krataiōn me dynamei, “the strong one who strengthens me with power”. The verb of the second clause is uncertain, and the word “my way” is written darko, “his way”, and read darki, “my way”.

Hinds, high places, a bow. “He makes my feet like those of hinds, and sets me upon my high places; he teaches my hands for war, and my arms bend a bow of bronze”: the word for “my feet” is written raglav, “his feet”, and read ragli, “my feet”. The ASV has “He maketh his feet like hinds’ feet”, which follows the written word. The NIV has “He makes my feet like the feet of a deer”. The NWT 1984 has “Making my feet like those of the hinds”. The Douay has “Making my feet like the feet of harts”. For the bow the ASV has “a bow of brass”, the NIV “a bow of bronze” and the NWT 1984 “a bow of copper”. The song of the Bow was the lament David taught Judah (2 Samuel 1:18, now on these pages).

Your gentleness. “You have given me the shield of your salvation, and your gentleness makes me great; you make my steps broad under me, and my ankles have not slipped”: the word is anotekha, and the shelf cannot agree on it. The ASV has “thy gentleness hath made me great”. The NIV has “your help has made me great”. The NWT 1984 has “it is your humility that makes me great”. The Douay has “thy mildness hath multiplied me”. Rahlfs’ Greek has hypakoē sou, “your obedience”. Psalm 18:35 adds “your right hand supports me”, which this song does not have. The form tachteni, “under me”, stands in three verses, all in this poem: 37, 40 and 48.

Verses 38–46 · 2 Samuel 22 Pursued and finished — enemies who cried and were not answered, dust and street mud, “the contentions of my people”, and the sons of the stranger

Pursued and finished. “I pursued my enemies and destroyed them; and I did not turn back until they were finished”: the verb of the second clause is ashmidem, “I destroyed them”, where Psalm 18:38 has ashigem, “I overtook them”. The ASV has “I have pursued mine enemies, and destroyed them”. The NIV has “I pursued my enemies and crushed them”. The NWT 1984 has “I will pursue my enemies, that I may annihilate them”. The Douay has “I will pursue after my enemies, and crush them”. The nouns are the verbs of the finished: kalotam, “until they were finished”, and in verse 39 va’akhallem, “and I finished them”. The sentence puts the whole of it in David’s mouth.

No savior. “And my enemies — you gave me the back of their necks; those who hated me — I cut them off. They looked, but there was no savior; to Jehovah, but he did not answer them”: the ASV has “Thou hast also made mine enemies turn their backs unto me”, the NIV “You made my enemies turn their backs in flight” and the NWT 1984 “you will certainly give me the back of their neck”. In verse 42 the ASV has “They looked, but there was none to save”, the NIV “They cried for help”, the NWT 1984 “They cry for help, but there is no savior” and the Douay “They shall cry, and there shall be none to save”. The verb here is yish’u, “they looked”; Psalm 18:42 has yeshav’u, “they cried”. “Jehovah did not answer him” is said of Saul when he asked (1 Samuel 28:6, now on these pages).

Dust, and the contentions of my people. “And I ground them like the dust of the earth; like the mud of the streets I crushed them… And you delivered me from the contentions of my people; you kept me as head of nations; a people I had not known served me”: the ASV has “Then did I beat them small as the dust of the earth”, the NIV “I beat them as fine as the dust of the earth” and the NWT 1984 “I shall pound them fine like the dust of the earth”; Psalm 18:42 has “dust before the wind”. In verse 44 the ASV has “Thou also hast delivered me from the strivings of my people”, the NIV “You have delivered me from the attacks of the peoples”, the NWT 1984 “you will provide me escape from the faultfinding of my people” and the Douay “Thou wilt save me from the contradictions of my people”. The Hebrew is rivei ammi, “my people”, and Psalm 18:43 has am, “a people”. No nation is named, and the verse does not say which quarrels of his own people it means.

The sons of the stranger. “The sons of the stranger cringe to me; at the hearing of the ear they obey me. The sons of the stranger fade, and come trembling out of their strongholds”: the verb is yitkachashu, from the root of “lie” (see kachash). The ASV has “The foreigners shall submit themselves unto me”. The NIV has “foreigners cower before me”. The NWT 1984 has “Foreigners themselves will come cringing to me”. The Douay has “The sons of the stranger will resist me”. The last clause has the ASV “shall come trembling out of their close places”, the NIV “they come trembling from their strongholds” and the NWT 1984 “they will come quaking out from their bulwarks”.

Verses 47–51 · 2 Samuel 22 Jehovah lives — vengeances given, the man of violences, thanks among the nations, and “a tower of salvation” for his king, his anointed

Jehovah lives. “Jehovah lives, and blessed be my rock; and exalted be God, the rock of my salvation”: chai Yehovah, the oath-formula used as a cry. The ASV has “Jehovah liveth”. The NIV has “The Lord lives! Praise be to my Rock!”. The NWT 1984 has “Jehovah is living; and blessed be my Rock”. The Douay has “The Lord liveth, and my God is blessed”. This song has Elohei tsur yish’i, “the God of the rock of my salvation”, and Psalm 18:46 has Elohei yish’i, “the God of my salvation”.

Vengeances, and the man of violences. “The God who gives me vengeances, and brings peoples down under me, and who brings me out from my enemies; you lift me above those who rise against me; from the man of violences you deliver me”: the noun is nekamot, “vengeances”, and “violences” is chamasim in the plural, where Psalm 18:49 has the singular (see chamas). The ASV has “the God that executeth vengeance for me” and “Thou deliverest me from the violent man”. The NIV has “He is the God who avenges me” and “from a violent man you rescued me”. The NWT 1984 has “the Giver of acts of vengeance to me” and “From the man of violent deeds you will deliver me”. The Douay has “God who giveth me revenge” and “from the wicked man thou shalt deliver me”. “From violence you save me” closed verse 3; the song names no man.

Among the nations. “Therefore I give thanks to you, Jehovah, among the nations; and to your name I sing”: the ASV has “Therefore I will give thanks unto thee, O Jehovah, among the nations”. The NIV has “I will sing the praises of your name”. The NWT 1984 has “That is why I shall thank you, O Jehovah, among the nations”. The Douay has “Therefore will I give thanks to thee”. Paul quotes the line to say the nations might glorify God: “Therefore I will acknowledge you among the nations, and sing to your name” (Romans 15:9, now on these pages).

A tower of salvation. “A tower of salvation is he to his king; and he does kindness to his anointed, to David and to his seed, forever”: the first word is written magdil, “he makes great”, and read migdol, “a tower of”, and this translation follows the reading. The ASV has “Great deliverance giveth he to his king”. The NIV has “He gives his king great victories”. The NWT 1984 has “The One doing great acts of salvation for his king”. The Douay has “Giving great salvation to his king”. Rahlfs’ Greek has megalynōn, “making great”, and Psalm 18:51 has the written form. “His king” and “his anointed” are the closing words of Hannah’s song (1 Samuel 2:10), and “to David and to his seed forever” is the promise of Nathan’s word (2 Samuel 7:12, 16, both now on these pages; see chesed).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the palm of all his enemies”; “my crag” and “my rock”; “my stronghold”; “the horn of my salvation”; “my high tower”; “the praised one”; “the breakers of death”; “torrents of worthlessness”; “the cords of Sheol”; “confronted”; “from his temple”; “the foundations of the heavens”; “a cherub”; “the wings of the wind”; “threw them into confusion”; “the channels of the sea”; “drew me out”; “my support”; “a broad place”; “he delighted in me”; “my righteousness” and “the cleanness of my hands”; “blameless”; “loyal”; “the afflicted” and “the haughty”; “my lamp”; “refined”; “besides Jehovah”; “my strong fortress”; “my high places”; “your gentleness”; “finished”; “the contentions of my people”; “the sons of the stranger”; “vengeances”; “the man of violences”; “a tower of salvation”; and “his anointed”; with the written forms in the notes.

Echoes paid. Hannah’s song, its horn, its thunder, its “there is none besides you”, and its “his king … his anointed” (1 Samuel 2:1, 2, 10); the thunder and confusion at Samuel’s sacrifice (1 Samuel 7:10); Deborah’s trembling earth (Judges 5:4–5); the breath of Jehovah’s nostrils at the sea (Exodus 15:8); the drawing out of Moses (Exodus 2:10); the crag and the strongholds of David’s hiding (1 Samuel 23:14, 25); Shimei’s and Sheba’s “worthlessness” (2 Samuel 16:7; 20:1); Saul’s unanswered asking (1 Samuel 28:6); the delight that Saul’s servants, Joab’s young man and David himself spoke of (1 Samuel 18:22; 2 Samuel 20:11; 15:26); David’s “I have sinned” and the sin taken away (2 Samuel 12:13); the lamp of Israel (2 Samuel 21:17); the song of the Bow (2 Samuel 1:18); the broad place of Beth-shan (2 Samuel 21:12); the promise of a seed forever (2 Samuel 7:12, 16); and Paul’s quotation (Romans 15:9).

Echoes planted. The same song as Psalm 18, with its heading and the lines each text lacks (Psalm 18); “thunder” and “confusion” at Gibeon in Joshua’s day (Joshua 10:10); David’s last words and the names of his mighty men (2 Samuel 23:1–39, now on these pages); the lamp of David (1 Kings 11:36); and the “sweet singer of Israel” (2 Samuel 23:1, now on these pages). None of the others is translated here yet.
