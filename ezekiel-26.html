Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–6 · Ezekiel 26:1–6 Tyre says ‘Aha’

⚠ The date. The eleventh year and the first of the month; no month is named. The NIV has “In the eleventh month of the twelfth year”.

⭐ Tyre. Tyre was a trading city on the coast, with an island and a mainland side. Its king sent David cedar and craftsmen (2 Samuel 5:11). This chapter and the next two are chiefly about it. The cry ‘Aha’ is the one Ammon raised at 25:3.

⚠ The doors of the peoples. ‘Doors’ is plural and ‘she is broken’ singular; the speaker means Jerusalem. ‘It has swung round’ is feminine singular, like ‘is broken’, so it may also be read ‘she has turned’. The KJV has “she is broken that was the gates of the people”, the NIV “The gate to the nations is broken”.

⭐ I am against you. Said to Jerusalem at 5:8. Here the answer to Tyre comes in the sea’s own shape: nations like waves.

⭐ A bare rock. The bare rock where Jerusalem set the blood (24:7, 8). The words tzechiach sela stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: those two, and verses 4 and 14 here. The KJV has “like the top of a rock”.

⭐ Throw down. ‘Throw down’ (verses 4 and 12) and ‘break down’ (verses 9 and 12) are the two verbs of 16:39.

⭐ Nets. Rock where nets are spread to dry. Such a spreading place is spoken of again at 47:10, with fishermen.

⭐ Plunder, and daughters. ‘Plunder to the nations’, as of Ammon at 25:7. The ‘daughters in the field’ are usually taken as the mainland towns that depend on the city; the NIV has “settlements on the mainland”, the NWT 1984 “dependent towns”.

Verses 7–14 · Ezekiel 26:7–14 Nebuchadrezzar from the north

⭐ The name. Nebuchadrezzar is named here for the first time in Ezekiel; the book has said ‘the king of Babylon’. Ezekiel spells it with r, as at Jeremiah 25:9; elsewhere the Bible also has Nebuchadnezzar.

⭐ King of kings. In Hebrew melekh melakhim stands in this one verse; Daniel 2:37 has the title in Aramaic, said to the same king.

⭐ The siege. The siege wall and ramp of 4:2 and 21:27, and the large shield of 23:24.

⚠ Siege engine. The word in verse 9, qobollo, is hard; it is not the ‘battering rams’ of 4:2. The KJV has “engines of war”, the NIV “the blows of his battering rams”. ‘With his swords’: the KJV has “with his axes”.

⭐ A multitude. Shifat, which stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible. ‘A multitude of camels will cover you’ is the good news of Isaiah 60:6; here a multitude of horses covers Tyre with dust.

⚠ Two words for dust. The horses raise abaq (verse 10), fine dust in the air. The city’s own aphar is scraped off (verse 4) and put in the water (verse 12). ‘Quake’ (verse 10) has the root of the ‘quaking’ of 12:18.

⚠ Standing stones. Matzevot, as at 2 Kings 3:2, here ‘of your power’. The ASV has “the pillars of thy strength”, the Douay “thy famous statues”, the KJV “thy strong garrisons”. The ‘desirable’ houses of verse 12 have the root of ‘the desire of your eyes’ (24:16).

⭐ He, then they. Verses 7 to 11 speak of one king; at verse 12 the verbs turn plural.

⭐ Songs and lyres. Kinnor, printed ‘lyre’ since Genesis 4:21; the KJV and ASV have “harps”. Of Babylon the great, Revelation 18:22 says that the sound of harpists will never be heard in her again.

Verses 15–18 · Ezekiel 26:15–18 the princes of the sea mourn

⭐ Islands. Iyyim, islands and coasts, as at Isaiah 41:1. ‘Wounded’ in verse 15 is the word printed ‘slain’ at 11:6; these are still groaning. ‘The killing’ has the root of ‘kill’ in verses 6, 8 and 11.

⭐ Down from their thrones. The robes and embroidered work come off, and they put on trembling, as the prince is clothed with desolation at 7:27. ‘Appalled’ is the verb of 4:17.

⭐ A dirge. Qinah, as at 19:1. It opens with ‘how’ and measures what Tyre was: peopled from the sea, praised, strong, a terror. The laments over Babylon in Revelation 18 take up these chapters.

⚠ Her inhabitants. The last words of verse 17 say that they put their terror on all ‘her inhabitants’; whose is not clear. The ASV has “on all that dwelt there”.

⚠ Alarmed. In verse 18 ‘tremble’ is the verb of verse 16; ‘are dismayed’ is another, printed so at Exodus 15:15 and ‘tremble’ of the people’s hands at 7:27. This translation closes the dirge after verse 18.

Verses 19–21 · Ezekiel 26:19–21 down to the pit

⭐ The deep. Tehom, the deep of Genesis 7:11, with the many waters. The sea that made Tyre is brought up over it.

⭐ Those who go down to the pit. Bor, a cistern or pit, here the place of the dead. This chapter has the words twice in verse 20, and they return in chapters 31 and 32. ‘The land of the lower parts’ is not the word for ‘the deep’; Psalm 63:10 speaks of ‘the lower parts of the earth’, and the phrase here returns at 32:18 and 32:24. ‘Be inhabited’ has the plain verb ‘dwell’, as in ‘make you dwell’.

⚠ Glory in the land of the living. The Hebrew has ‘and I will set glory’, with God speaking; the verse does not say where the glory goes beyond ‘in the land of the living’. The NIV reads the end of the verse otherwise: “you will not return or take your place in the land of the living”.

⚠ Terrors. The word is plural, ‘terrors I will make you’. It is not the ‘terror’ of verse 17. A like close ends two later words, 27:36 (now on these pages) and 28:19.

⭐ Never found. Revelation 18:21 says of Babylon that she will never be found again.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “bare rock” and “spreading place for nets” at 4–5 and 14; “throw down” at 4 and 12 and “break down” at 9 and 12; “dust” at 4, 10 and 12; “sound” at 10, 13 and 15; “laid waste” at 2 and 19; and “those who go down to the pit” twice in 20.

Echoes paid. The ‘Aha’ of 25:3; the bare rock of 24:7; the siege wall of 4:2; the dirge of 19:1.

Echoes planted. Tyre’s trade is listed at 27:12 (now on these pages); the pit again at 31:14.

Ezekiel 27, now on these pages: a dirge for Tyre, the ship.
