The end of the vision: at the east gate Ezekiel prophesies against twenty-five men and Pelatiah dies; he is told that the scattered exiles will be gathered and given one heart and a new spirit; the glory goes up from the city to the mountain on its east; and he is brought back to Chaldea and tells the exiles what he was shown.
Closes the vision that began at 8:1, dated there to the sixth year, sixth month, fifth day.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · Ezekiel 11:1–4 twenty-five men at the east gate — the pot and the flesh

⭐ The east gate. The vision that began at 8:1 comes to the east gate of 10:19. Twenty-five men are there. The text does not link them with the ‘about twenty-five’ of 8:16. Jaazaniah here is son of Azzur, a different father from the Jaazaniah of 8:11. The two named are sarei ha’am, ‘chiefs of the people’. The name Pelatiah is built on the root of ‘escape’ (peletah).

⚠ Houses. What these men say in verse 3 has been read three ways. The ASV has a statement, “The time is not near to build houses”; the KJV splits it, “It is not near; let us build houses”; the Douay asks, “Were not houses lately built?” This translation keeps the statement. Jeremiah 29:5 (now on these pages) tells the exiles in Babylon to build houses; the verse here does not mention that letter.

⭐ Pot and flesh. The city is the sir, a cooking pot, and they are the meat inside: whether as the part that is kept safe or the part that is chosen, the words do not say. Jeremiah 1:13 saw a boiling pot with the same noun. The TLB turns the image into “an iron shield”.

Verses 5–12 · Ezekiel 11:5–12 the spirit falls — whose pot, whose flesh — judged on the border

⭐ The spirit falls. The verb is the one of the hand that fell at 8:1, here with ‘the spirit of Jehovah’; the verb is feminine and the ‘he said’ masculine. “The things that come up in your spirit” stands in this one verse. The ASV has “the things that come into your mind”.

⭐ The saying turned. Verse 7 takes their image and gives it back: the flesh in the pot is the slain they have put there, and they themselves will be brought out. Verse 9 says vehotseti, ‘and I will bring out’; verse 7 has hotsi, without that first-person ending, and this translation takes it as a passive. The versions have ‘I will bring’ or ‘you shall be brought’: the KJV “I will bring you forth”, the ASV “ye shall be brought forth”. They have feared the sword, and the sword comes (verse 8). Verses 9–11 give them to strangers, with acts of judgment (shefatim), on the border of Israel.

⚠ One ‘not’. Verse 11 has a single Hebrew ‘not’, in its first clause. The second, ‘you flesh in its midst’, has none, and ‘nor’ carries it over, as the ASV does (“neither shall ye be the flesh in the midst thereof”). It can also be read as a plain statement, that they will be flesh in it.

⭐ The judgments of the nations. Verse 12 turns the sentence of 5:7: there the people had not done according to the judgments of the nations around them; here they have. The Hebrew joins this verse with asher, ‘whose’ or ‘in that’; ‘for’ is how the versions take it. “And according to the judgments of the nations” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, those two.

Verse 13 · Ezekiel 11:13 Pelatiah dies — “a full end”

⭐ A death in the vision. While he is prophesying, Pelatiah dies. The verse says no more than that. Ezekiel falls on his face and cries ‘Ah, Lord Jehovah!’, as at 9:8. There the question carried the Hebrew question prefix; here there is none, so the words could also be read as a cry, ‘you are making a full end’. This translation asks, as the shelf does. The ASV has “wilt thou make a full end of the remnant of Israel?” and the NIV “Will you completely destroy the remnant of Israel?” ‘What remains’ is she’erit; kalah, a full end, is the word of Zephaniah 1:18 and of the promise at Jeremiah 30:11 that Jehovah would not make a full end of his own people.

Verses 14–16 · Ezekiel 11:14–16 your brothers — “get far from Jehovah” — a little sanctuary

⭐ Your brothers. The word that follows is about the exiles. They are ‘your brothers, the men of your redemption’ (ge’ullah, goel), those with whom a kinsman’s duty lies. The phrase stands in this one verse. The ASV has “the men of thy kindred” and the NWT 1984 “the men concerned with your right to repurchase”.

⚠ Get far. The Jerusalem inhabitants say to them rachaqu, ‘get far’, a command; the NIV turns it into a statement, “They are far away from the Lord”. The claim is about land: ‘to us this land is given’, as Edom would claim it in 36:5. Jeremiah 2:5 had said it was the fathers who went far from Jehovah.

⭐ A little sanctuary. “A little sanctuary” (miqdash me’at) stands in this one verse (miqdash). God has put them far off, and yet has been to them a sanctuary where they are. The phrase can also mean ‘for a little while’: the KJV has “a little sanctuary”, the ASV “a sanctuary for a little while”. It comes in the same vision in which the glory leaves the temple.

Verses 17–21 · Ezekiel 11:17–21 gathered — one heart and a new spirit — my people, their God

⭐ One heart. The promise is the one 36:26–28 sets out at length, with one difference to notice: here it is one heart (lev echad) and there a new heart. “The heart of stone” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and 36:26, and “a heart of flesh” in the same two (lev). Jeremiah 24:7 promises a heart to know Jehovah, with the same words of covenant: they will be my people, and I will be their God (compare Exodus 6:7, said there to ‘you’). The NIV has “an undivided heart”.

⚠ You and them. Verse 19 says ‘I will give them one heart’ and then ‘a new spirit I will put within you’, plural, before returning to ‘their flesh’. The NIV evens it out: “put a new spirit in them”. This translation keeps the change.

⚠ The second heart. Verse 21 is hard: ‘to the heart of their detestable things’ their heart walks. The ASV keeps “walketh after the heart of their detestable things”; the NIV has “devoted to their vile images”. Their way comes back on their head, the words of 9:10.

Verses 22–25 · Ezekiel 11:22–25 the glory on the mountain — carried back — told to the exiles

⭐ Out of the city. The cherubim lift their wings as at 10:19, and the glory goes up from the midst of the city and stands on the mountain on its east. The verse does not name the mountain.

⭐ Back to Chaldea. Spirit lifts the prophet up as at 3:14 and brings him to the exiles, ‘in the vision’, and the vision goes up from him. The noun is the one 8:4 prints as ‘appearance’. He tells the exiles all that Jehovah has shown him. The vision that opened at 8:1 closes here. Verse 25 ends with a paragraph break.

⭐ The return. Ezekiel 43:2 has the glory coming back from the way of the east.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “pot” and “flesh” in verses 3, 7 and 11 (and ‘flesh’ again at 19); “judge”, “judgments” and “acts of judgment” kept apart; “gather” and “assemble” for the two verbs of verse 17; and “exiles” at verses 24 and 25.

Echoes paid. The judgments of the nations of 5:7; and the east gate and wings of chapter 10.

Echoes planted. The heart and spirit that 36:26 promises; the new heart and new spirit that 18:31 tells the people to make for themselves; and the glory’s return in chapter 43.
