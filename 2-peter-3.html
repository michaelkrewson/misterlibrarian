Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · “The second letter” — a reminder, the prophets and the apostles

“This is now, beloved, the second letter I am writing to you; in both of them I am stirring up your sincere mind by way of reminder.” Which letter was the first, the verse does not say; most readers take it to be 1 Peter, and others an earlier letter now lost (reported, not checked here). “Stirring up … by way of reminder” repeats 2 Peter 1:13, “to stir you up by way of reminder,” word for word. Eilikrinēs, “sincere,” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is Paul’s “that you may be sincere and without stumbling” at Philippians 1:10. “Mind” is dianoia, the “loins of your mind” of 1 Peter 1:13 (dictionary). The KJV reads “I stir up your pure minds by way of remembrance”; the NWT 1984 reads “I am arousing YOUR clear thinking faculties by way of a reminder.”

“To remember the words spoken beforehand by the holy prophets, and the commandment of the Lord and Savior given through your apostles.” The prophets are the ones of 2 Peter 1:19–21, “the prophetic word made more sure.” Jude has the same call with the apostles alone: “remember the words spoken beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ” (Jude 1:17). The Greek strings three genitives together, “the of-your-apostles commandment of-the-Lord-and-Savior,” and this translation takes the apostles as the ones through whom the Lord’s commandment came. The Received Text reads “us” for “your”: the KJV reads “the commandment of us the apostles of the Lord and Saviour”; the ASV reads “the commandments of the Lord and Saviour through your apostles.”

Verses 3–4 · “Where is the promise of his presence?” — mockers in the last days

“Knowing this first, that in the last days mockers will come with mockery, walking according to their own desires.” “Knowing this first” repeats 2 Peter 1:20. “In the last days” is the phrase of Peter’s Pentecost sermon at Acts 2:17 and of 2 Timothy 3:1. Empaiktēs, “mocker,” stands in two verses (dictionary). The other is Jude’s “In the last time there will be mockers, following after their own ungodly desires” (Jude 1:18). Empaigmonē, “mockery,” stands in one verse, and this translation keeps the doubling, “mockers with mockery”; the Byzantine text does not have “with mockery.” The ASV reads “mockers shall come with mockery”; the NWT 1984 reads “there will come ridiculers with their ridicule”; the KJV reads “there shall come in the last days scoffers.”

⚠ “And saying, ‘Where is the promise of his presence? For since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of creation.’” “Presence” is parousia, as in “the presence of the Lord has drawn near” at James 5:8 and “so will the presence of the Son of Man be” at Matthew 24:37 (dictionary); at 2 Peter 1:16 this translation printed “the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.” Who “the fathers” are, readings differ: the patriarchs of Israel, so that the mockers mean nothing has changed since the world began, or the first generation of believers, so that they mean the promised presence has not come while those who heard it have died. This translation does not choose. “Fell asleep” is the verb of “those who have fallen asleep” at 1 Thessalonians 4:15. “From the beginning of creation” is Jesus’s phrase at Mark 10:6 and Mark 13:19. The KJV reads “Where is the promise of his coming?”; the NWT 1984 reads “Where is this promised presence of his?”; the NIV reads “Where is this ‘coming’ he promised?”

Verses 5–7 · Out of water and through water — the flood, and the present heavens “stored up for fire”

“For, wanting it so, they overlook this: that heavens existed from long ago, and an earth held together out of water and through water by the word of God.” “Wanting it so” is thelontas: either they choose to overlook it, or it is their own wish that hides it from them. The KJV reads “For this they willingly are ignorant of”; the ASV reads “For this they willfully forget”; the NWT 1984 reads “according to their wish, this fact escapes their notice.” “From long ago” is the ekpalai of 2 Peter 2:3. The picture is Genesis 1: the deep and the waters at Genesis 1:2, and the vault that divided the waters at Genesis 1:6–9; “by the word of God” is the “set in order by God’s word” of Hebrews 11:3. “Held together” is synistēmi, the verb of “in him all things hold together” at Colossians 1:17.

“Through which the world of that time was flooded with water and perished.” “Through which” is plural in the printed text, and may mean the water and the word, or the waters above and below; the 28th Nestle-Aland edition reads the singular, “through which,” the word. Genesis has both waters: “all the springs of the great deep burst open, and the floodgates of the sky were opened” (Genesis 7:11). Kataklyzō, “flood,” stands in one verse; its noun is the “flood” of 2 Peter 2:5. “Perished” is apollymi, the verb of “destruction” at 2 Peter 2:1. The KJV reads “Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished”; the NWT 1984 reads “and by those [means] the world of that time suffered destruction when it was deluged with water.”

“But the present heavens and earth, by the same word, have been stored up for fire, being kept for the day of judgment and destruction of ungodly people.” Thēsaurizō, “stored up,” in this form stands in one verse; the verb is the “storing up for yourself wrath” of Romans 2:5. “Being kept” is tēreō, as at 2 Peter 2:4, 2:9 and 2:17. After the flood God said, “never again will there be a flood to ruin the earth” (Genesis 9:11); the next judgment in this letter is fire. The ASV reads “have been stored up for fire, being reserved against the day of judgment and destruction of ungodly men”; the KJV reads “are kept in store, reserved unto fire.”

Verses 8–9 · “One day is like a thousand years” — Psalm 90, and a Lord who is patient

“But do not let this one thing escape your notice, beloved: that with the Lord one day is like a thousand years, and a thousand years like one day.” “Escape your notice” is lanthanō, the verb of verse 5, now turned on the readers. The saying draws on Psalm 90:4, which the Greek numbers 89:4: “a thousand years in your eyes are like yesterday when it has passed” (Hebrew and Greek checked; Psalm 90 yet to come in this translation). The psalm has only one half; the letter adds the other, “a thousand years like one day.” The KJV reads “one day is with the Lord as a thousand years”; the NWT 1984 reads “one day is with Jehovah as a thousand years.”

⚠ “The Lord is not slow about his promise, as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing any to perish but all to come to repentance.” Who “the Lord” is in verses 8–10 the chapter leaves open, and this translation keeps “Lord”: the psalm behind verse 8 speaks to God, and verse 12 says “the day of God”; but verse 15 takes up this verse’s patience as “the patience of our Lord,” and in this letter “our Lord” is Jesus Christ (2 Peter 1:16, 3:18). The NWT 1984 prints “Jehovah” in verses 8, 9, 10 and 12. Bradynō, “be slow,” stands in two verses; the other is Paul’s “if I am delayed” at 1 Timothy 3:15. “Is patient” is makrothymeō, the “Be patient … until the presence of the Lord” of James 5:7 (dictionary), and the noun is the “patience of God” that waited in the days of Noah at 1 Peter 3:20. “Not wishing any to perish but all to come to repentance” stands beside “who wants all people to be saved” at 1 Timothy 2:4 and “the kindness of God is leading you to repentance” at Romans 2:4 (dictionary). Whether “any” and “all” reach every person or the “you” toward whom God is patient is argued between traditions, and this translation does not choose. The Byzantine text reads “toward us”: the KJV reads “longsuffering to us-ward”; the ASV reads “longsuffering to you-ward.”

Verse 10 · ⚠ “The earth and the works in it will be found” — burned up, found, or not found?

“But the day of the Lord will come like a thief, in which the heavens will pass away with a roar, and the elements will burn and be dissolved.” “Like a thief” is Paul’s “the day of the Lord comes just like a thief in the night” at 1 Thessalonians 5:2, Jesus’s householder at Matthew 24:43, and “I will come like a thief” at Revelation 3:3; the Byzantine text adds “in the night.” “The day of the Lord” is the prophets’ “day of Jehovah” (Joel’s at Acts 2:20), and this translation keeps “Lord” here as at 1 Thessalonians 5:2. “The heavens will pass away” is Jesus’s “Heaven and earth will pass away” (Matthew 24:35). Rhoizēdon, “with a roar,” stands in one verse; it is the rush of something moving fast through the air. “Elements” is stoicheia, the “elements of the world” of Galatians 4:3 and the “first elements” of Hebrews 5:12 (dictionary); whether the elements here are the stuff of the world or the bodies of the sky, readings differ. Kausoō, “burn,” stands in two verses, this one and verse 12.

⚠ “And the earth and the works in it will be found.” The last verb is one of the hardest readings in the New Testament. The printed text, with Westcott and Hort, Tregelles and the 27th Nestle-Aland edition, reads heurethēsetai, “will be found.” The Byzantine text and the Received Text read katakahēsetai, “will be burned up,” and the Latin reads exurentur, “will be burned up.” The 28th Nestle-Aland edition prints ouch heurethēsetai, “will not be found,” with a “not” found in only a few early translations (reported, not checked here). “Will be found” is read two ways: found out, laid bare before the judge, as “each one’s work will become visible; for the Day will show it” (1 Corinthians 3:13); or found still standing after the burning. This translation prints the reading of the text and does not choose between its senses. The KJV reads “the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up”; the Douay reads “shall be burnt up”; the NWT 1984 reads “earth and the works in it will be discovered”; the NIV reads “the earth and everything done in it will be laid bare.”

Verses 11–13 · “Hastening the day of God” — and new heavens and a new earth

“Since all these things are thus to be dissolved, what sort of people ought you to be in holy conduct and godliness, waiting for and hastening the presence of the day of God.” “What sort of” is potapos, the disciples’ “What sort of man is this?” at Matthew 8:27. “Holy conduct” is the “be holy in all your conduct” of 1 Peter 1:15; “godliness” is the eusebeia of 2 Peter 1:3 and 1:6. “Waiting for” is prosdokaō, three times in four verses (12, 13, 14). ⚠ “Hastening” is speudō, the verb of “Hurry and get out of Jerusalem” at Acts 22:18: either the readers hasten the day, bringing it nearer by the repentance verse 9 waits for, or they eagerly long for it. Readings differ. The KJV reads “Looking for and hasting unto the coming of the day of God”; the ASV reads “earnestly desiring the coming of the day of God”; the NWT 1984 reads “keeping close in mind the presence of the day of Jehovah,” where the Greek has “God”; the Douay reads “the day of the Lord,” from the Latin.

“Because of which the heavens will be set on fire and dissolved, and the elements will burn and melt!” Tēkō, “melt,” stands in one verse; it is the verb of the Greek of Isaiah 34:4, “all the powers of the heavens will melt,” where the Hebrew reads “all the host of heaven will rot away” (both checked; Isaiah 34 yet to come in this translation). The KJV reads “the heavens being on fire shall be dissolved, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat.”

“But according to his promise we are waiting for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells.” The promise is Isaiah’s: “For look, I create new heavens and a new earth” (Isaiah 65:17, Hebrew and Greek checked, with the same pair at 66:22). John sees it at Revelation 21:1, “a new heaven and a new earth.” Epangelma, “promise,” stands in two verses, both in this letter; the other is “the precious and very great promises” of 2 Peter 1:4 (dictionary). “New” is kainos (dictionary). The NWT 1984 reads “and in these righteousness is to dwell”; the Douay reads “in which justice dwelleth.”

Verses 14–16 · “Our beloved brother Paul” — some things hard to understand, and “the rest of the Scriptures”

“Therefore, beloved, since you are waiting for these things, be diligent to be found by him without spot and without blemish, in peace.” “Be diligent” is spoudazō, as at 2 Peter 1:10 and 1:15. “Without spot and without blemish” is the reverse of the false teachers of 2 Peter 2:13, “spots and blemishes,” and of the Lamb “without blemish or spot” at 1 Peter 1:19. Aspilos, “without spot,” stands in four verses, among them “to keep oneself unspotted from the world” at James 1:27. Amōmētos, “without blemish,” stands in one verse (dictionary). The KJV reads “that ye may be found of him in peace, without spot, and blameless”; the NWT 1984 reads “spotless and unblemished and in peace.”

“And regard the patience of our Lord as salvation, just as our beloved brother Paul also wrote to you, according to the wisdom given him.” “The patience of our Lord” takes up verse 9. Outside Acts and Paul’s own letters, the New Testament names Paul only here (encyclopedia). Which letter of Paul’s the readers had, the verse does not say; readers often point to Romans 2:4, on God’s patience leading to repentance. “According to the wisdom given him” is close to Paul’s own “According to the grace of God given to me” at 1 Corinthians 3:10. The KJV reads “account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation”; the NIV reads “Bear in mind that our Lord’s patience means salvation.”

⚠ “As he does in all his letters too, speaking in them of these things. In them are some things hard to understand, which the untaught and unstable twist, as they do the rest of the Scriptures too, to their own destruction.” “All his letters” supposes a gathering of Paul’s letters known to the readers. Dysnoētos, “hard to understand,” stands in one verse, and so do amathēs, “untaught,” and streblō, “twist,” a word for wrenching a limb on the rack. “Unstable” is the astēriktos of 2 Peter 2:14 (dictionary). “The rest of the Scriptures” sets Paul’s letters beside the Scriptures, as 1 Timothy 5:18 gives a saying of Jesus after “the Scripture says”; whether graphai here already means sacred Scripture or simply “writings,” readings differ, and 2 Timothy 3:16 has its own note on the word. The KJV reads “which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures”; the NWT 1984 reads “which the untaught and unsteady are twisting”; the NIV reads “which ignorant and unstable people distort.”

Verses 17–18 · “Grow in the grace and knowledge” — firm footing, and glory to Jesus Christ

“You then, beloved, knowing this beforehand, be on your guard, so that you are not carried away with the error of the unprincipled and fall from your own firm footing.” “Knowing beforehand” is proginōskō, which in Romans 11:2 and 1 Peter 1:20 is God’s or Christ’s foreknowledge and here is the readers’. “Carried away with” is synapagō, as “even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy” at Galatians 2:13. “The unprincipled” are the athesmoi of 2 Peter 2:7 (dictionary), and “error” is the planē of 2 Peter 2:18. “Fall from” is ekpiptō, as in “you have fallen away from grace” at Galatians 5:4 (dictionary). Stērigmos, “firm footing,” stands in one verse; it is the third word of one root in this letter, after “established in the truth” (2 Peter 1:12) and “unstable” (2:14, 3:16). The KJV reads “being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness”; the NWT 1984 reads “the error of the law-defying people”; the Douay reads “the error of the unwise,” from the Latin.

“But grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be the glory, both now and to the day of eternity. Amen.” The letter ends where it began, in “grace” and “knowledge”: “may grace and peace be multiplied to you in the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord” (2 Peter 1:2) (dictionary). Here the knowledge is gnōsis (dictionary). The glory is given to Jesus Christ, as it is at Revelation 1:6. “The day of eternity” is hēmera aiōnos, “the day of the age,” a phrase this letter alone has in the New Testament. Westcott and Hort and the 28th Nestle-Aland edition do not have “Amen.” The KJV reads “To him be glory both now and for ever. Amen”; the NWT 1984 and the Douay read “the day of eternity.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “by way of reminder,” as at 1:13; “sincere,” as at Philippians 1:10; “mockers with mockery”; “presence,” as at James 5:8; “from long ago,” as at 2:3; “held together,” as at Colossians 1:17; “stored up,” as at Romans 2:5; “Lord” in verses 8–10, with the readings in the note; “slow,” as at 1 Timothy 3:15; “like a thief,” as at 1 Thessalonians 5:2; “elements,” as at Galatians 4:3; “will be found,” as the printed text has it; “hastening”; “new heavens and a new earth,” as at Isaiah 65:17; “without spot and without blemish,” against 2:13; “untaught and unstable”; “twist”; “the unprincipled,” as at 2:7; “firm footing”; and “the day of eternity.”

Echoes paid. “By way of reminder” from 2 Peter 1:13; the prophets of 2 Peter 1:19; Jude’s mockers of Jude 1:18; “from long ago” from 2 Peter 2:3; the flood of 2 Peter 2:5; things “kept” for judgment from 2 Peter 2:9; the promises of 2 Peter 1:4; spots and blemishes from 2 Peter 2:13, reversed; the unstable of 2 Peter 2:14; the unprincipled of 2 Peter 2:7; established from 2 Peter 1:12; and grace and knowledge from 2 Peter 1:2, at the end as at the start.

The end of the letter. 2 Peter opened with the knowledge of Jesus Christ and a ladder of virtues, met the false teachers in chapter 2, and answers the mockers here with a God who is patient, a day that will come like a thief, and new heavens and a new earth. The new heaven and new earth come back in Revelation 21:1.

Next in this book: none; 2 Peter ends here.
