At Shechem Joshua gathers all the tribes and their leaders. He recites what God has done from Terah to the Amorites, sets before the people the choice of whom to serve, and they answer that they will serve Jehovah. He makes a covenant, writes the words in the book of the law of God, and sets a great stone under the oak by the sanctuary as a witness. Joshua dies at a hundred and ten and is buried at Timnath-serah; the bones of Joseph are buried at Shechem, and Eleazar at the hill of Phinehas.
The last chapter of the book, and the nearest to a date the book gives: the generation that outlived Joshua is the one that served Jehovah, and Judges 2 describes the next generation. Like the earlier chapters it gives no year. The early/late conquest question applies unchanged (c. 1406 BC on the early date this project foregrounds, c. 1230-1210 BC on the late).

1
וַיֶּאֱסֹף יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶת-כָּל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, שְׁכֶמָה; וַיִּקְרָא לְזִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וּלְרָאשָׁיו, וּלְשֹׁפְטָיו וּלְשֹׁטְרָיו, וַיִּתְיַצְּבוּ, לִפְנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים.
And Joshua gathered all the tribes of Israel to Shechem, and called for the elders of Israel, and for its heads, its judges and its officers; and they presented themselves before God.note
Y Josué reunió a todas las tribus de Israel en Siquem, y llamó a los ancianos de Israel, a sus jefes, a sus jueces y a sus oficiales; y se presentaron delante de Dios.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶל-כָּל-הָעָם, כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, בְּעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר יָשְׁבוּ אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם מֵעוֹלָם, תֶּרַח אֲבִי אַבְרָהָם וַאֲבִי נָחוֹר; וַיַּעַבְדוּ, אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים.
And Joshua said to all the people: Thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel: Your fathers dwelt of old beyond the River, Terah the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor; and they served other gods.note
Y Josué dijo a todo el pueblo: Así dice Jehová, el Dios de Israel: Sus padres habitaron desde antiguo al otro lado del río: Téraj, padre de Abraham y padre de Najor; y sirvieron a otros dioses.
3
וָאֶקַּח אֶת-אֲבִיכֶם אֶת-אַבְרָהָם, מֵעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר, וָאוֹלֵךְ אֹתוֹ, בְּכָל-אֶרֶץ כְּנָעַן; וארב וָאַרְבֶּה, אֶת-זַרְעוֹ, וָאֶתֶּן-לוֹ, אֶת-יִצְחָק.
And I took your father Abraham from beyond the River, and led him through all the land of Canaan, and multiplied his seed, and gave him Isaac.note
Y tomé a su padre Abraham del otro lado del río, y lo conduje por toda la tierra de Canaán, y multipliqué su descendencia, y le di a Isaac.
4
וָאֶתֵּן לְיִצְחָק, אֶת-יַעֲקֹב וְאֶת-עֵשָׂו; וָאֶתֵּן לְעֵשָׂו אֶת-הַר שֵׂעִיר, לָרֶשֶׁת אוֹתוֹ, וְיַעֲקֹב וּבָנָיו, יָרְדוּ מִצְרָיִם.
And I gave Isaac Jacob and Esau; and I gave Esau the mountain of Seir, to possess it; and Jacob and his children went down into Egypt.note
Y di a Isaac a Jacob y a Esaú; y a Esaú le di el monte de Seir para que lo poseyera; y Jacob y sus hijos descendieron a Egipto.
5
וָאֶשְׁלַח אֶת-מֹשֶׁה וְאֶת-אַהֲרֹן, וָאֶגֹּף אֶת-מִצְרַיִם, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתִי, בְּקִרְבּוֹ; וְאַחַר, הוֹצֵאתִי אֶתְכֶם.
And I sent Moses and Aaron, and I plagued Egypt, according to what I did in its midst; and afterward I brought you out.note
Envié a Moisés y a Aarón, e herí a Egipto con lo que hice en medio de él; y después los saqué a ustedes.
6
וָאוֹצִיא אֶת-אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם מִמִּצְרַיִם, וַתָּבֹאוּ הַיָּמָּה; וַיִּרְדְּפוּ מִצְרַיִם אַחֲרֵי אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם, בְּרֶכֶב וּבְפָרָשִׁים--יַם-סוּף.
And I brought your fathers out of Egypt, and you came to the sea; and the Egyptians pursued your fathers with chariots and horsemen to the Red Sea.note
Saqué a sus padres de Egipto, y llegaron al mar; y los egipcios persiguieron a sus padres con carros y jinetes hasta el Mar de los Juncos.
7
וַיִּצְעֲקוּ אֶל-יְהוָה, וַיָּשֶׂם מַאֲפֵל בֵּינֵיכֶם וּבֵין הַמִּצְרִים וַיָּבֵא עָלָיו אֶת-הַיָּם וַיְכַסֵּהוּ, וַתִּרְאֶינָה עֵינֵיכֶם, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-עָשִׂיתִי בְּמִצְרָיִם; וַתֵּשְׁבוּ בַמִּדְבָּר, יָמִים רַבִּים.
When they cried out to Jehovah, he put darkness between you and the Egyptians, and brought the sea upon them and covered them; and your eyes saw what I did in Egypt; and you dwelt in the wilderness many days.note
Y clamaron a Jehová, y él puso tinieblas entre ustedes y los egipcios, e hizo venir sobre ellos el mar y los cubrió; y sus ojos vieron lo que hice en Egipto; y habitaron en el desierto muchos días.
8
ואבאה וָאָבִא אֶתְכֶם, אֶל-אֶרֶץ הָאֱמֹרִי הַיּוֹשֵׁב בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן, וַיִּלָּחֲמוּ, אִתְּכֶם; וָאֶתֵּן אוֹתָם בְּיֶדְכֶם וַתִּירְשׁוּ אֶת-אַרְצָם, וָאַשְׁמִידֵם מִפְּנֵיכֶם.
And I brought you into the land of the Amorites, who dwelt beyond the Jordan; and they fought with you, and I gave them into your hand, and you possessed their land, and I destroyed them before you.note
Y los llevé a la tierra de los amorreos, que habitaban al otro lado del Jordán; y ellos pelearon contra ustedes, pero los entregué en su mano, y ustedes poseyeron su tierra, y yo los destruí delante de ustedes.
9
וַיָּקָם בָּלָק בֶּן-צִפּוֹר מֶלֶךְ מוֹאָב, וַיִּלָּחֶם בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיִּשְׁלַח, וַיִּקְרָא לְבִלְעָם בֶּן-בְּעוֹר--לְקַלֵּל אֶתְכֶם.
Then Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, arose and fought against Israel; and he sent and called Balaam son of Beor to curse you.note
Entonces Balac hijo de Zipor, rey de Moab, se levantó y peleó contra Israel; y envió a llamar a Balaam hijo de Beor, para que los maldijera.
10
וְלֹא אָבִיתִי, לִשְׁמֹעַ לְבִלְעָם; וַיְבָרֶךְ בָּרוֹךְ אֶתְכֶם, וָאַצִּל אֶתְכֶם מִיָּדוֹ.
But I would not listen to Balaam; so he blessed you, blessing you again and again; and I delivered you out of his hand.note
Pero no quise escuchar a Balaam; así que él los bendijo, bendiciéndolos una y otra vez; y yo los libré de su mano.
11
וַתַּעַבְרוּ אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן, וַתָּבֹאוּ אֶל-יְרִיחוֹ, וַיִּלָּחֲמוּ בָכֶם בַּעֲלֵי-יְרִיחוֹ הָאֱמֹרִי וְהַפְּרִזִּי וְהַכְּנַעֲנִי וְהַחִתִּי וְהַגִּרְגָּשִׁי, הַחִוִּי וְהַיְבוּסִי; וָאֶתֵּן אוֹתָם, בְּיֶדְכֶם.
And you crossed the Jordan and came to Jericho, and the lords of Jericho fought against you, the Amorite, the Perizzite, the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Girgashite, the Hivite and the Jebusite; and I gave them into your hand.note
Y pasaron el Jordán y llegaron a Jericó, y pelearon contra ustedes los señores de Jericó, el amorreo, el ferezeo, el cananeo, el hitita, el gergeseo, el heveo y el jebuseo; y yo los entregué en su mano.
12
וָאֶשְׁלַח לִפְנֵיכֶם, אֶת-הַצִּרְעָה, וַתְּגָרֶשׁ אוֹתָם מִפְּנֵיכֶם, שְׁנֵי מַלְכֵי הָאֱמֹרִי: לֹא בְחַרְבְּךָ, וְלֹא בְקַשְׁתֶּךָ.
And I sent the hornet before you, and it drove them out from before you, the two kings of the Amorites; not by your sword, nor by your bow.note
Y envié delante de ustedes la avispa, que los expulsó de delante de ustedes, a los dos reyes de los amorreos; no con tu espada ni con tu arco.
13
וָאֶתֵּן לָכֶם אֶרֶץ אֲשֶׁר לֹא-יָגַעְתָּ בָּהּ, וְעָרִים אֲשֶׁר לֹא-בְנִיתֶם, וַתֵּשְׁבוּ, בָּהֶם; כְּרָמִים וְזֵיתִים אֲשֶׁר לֹא-נְטַעְתֶּם, אַתֶּם אֹכְלִים.
And I gave you a land on which you had not labored, and cities which you did not build, and you dwell in them; of vineyards and olive groves which you did not plant, you eat.note
Y les di una tierra en la que ustedes no trabajaron, y ciudades que no edificaron, y ustedes habitan en ellas; de viñas y olivares que no plantaron, ustedes comen.
14
וְעַתָּה יְראוּ אֶת-יְהוָה, וְעִבְדוּ אֹתוֹ--בְּתָמִים וּבֶאֱמֶת; וְהָסִירוּ אֶת-אֱלֹהִים, אֲשֶׁר עָבְדוּ אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם בְּעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר וּבְמִצְרַיִם, וְעִבְדוּ, אֶת-יְהוָה.
Now therefore fear Jehovah and serve him in wholeness and in truth; and put away the gods which your fathers served beyond the River and in Egypt, and serve Jehovah.note
Ahora, pues, teman a Jehová y sírvanle con integridad y con verdad; quiten los dioses a los que sirvieron sus padres al otro lado del río y en Egipto, y sirvan a Jehová.
15
וְאִם רַע בְּעֵינֵיכֶם לַעֲבֹד אֶת-יְהוָה, בַּחֲרוּ לָכֶם הַיּוֹם אֶת-מִי תַעֲבֹדוּן--אִם אֶת-אֱלֹהִים אֲשֶׁר-עָבְדוּ אֲבוֹתֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר בעבר מֵעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר, וְאִם אֶת-אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱמֹרִי אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם יֹשְׁבִים בְּאַרְצָם; וְאָנֹכִי וּבֵיתִי, נַעֲבֹד אֶת-יְהוָה. {פ}
And if it seems evil to you to serve Jehovah, choose this day whom you will serve, whether the gods your fathers served beyond the River, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land you dwell; but as for me and my house, we will serve Jehovah.note
Y si les parece malo servir a Jehová, escojan hoy a quién van a servir: si a los dioses que sirvieron sus padres al otro lado del río, o a los dioses de los amorreos en cuya tierra habitan; pero yo y mi casa serviremos a Jehová.
16
וַיַּעַן הָעָם, וַיֹּאמֶר, חָלִילָה לָּנוּ, מֵעֲזֹב אֶת-יְהוָה--לַעֲבֹד, אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים.
The people answered and said: Far be it from us to forsake Jehovah and serve other gods.note
Y el pueblo respondió: Lejos de nosotros dejar a Jehová para servir a otros dioses.
17
כִּי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ, הוּא הַמַּעֲלֶה אֹתָנוּ וְאֶת-אֲבוֹתֵינוּ מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם מִבֵּית עֲבָדִים; וַאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה לְעֵינֵינוּ, אֶת-הָאֹתוֹת הַגְּדֹלוֹת הָאֵלֶּה, וַיִּשְׁמְרֵנוּ בְּכָל-הַדֶּרֶךְ אֲשֶׁר הָלַכְנוּ בָהּ, וּבְכֹל הָעַמִּים אֲשֶׁר עָבַרְנוּ בְּקִרְבָּם.
For Jehovah our God is he who brought us and our fathers up from the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery, and who did those great signs before our eyes, and kept us through all the way we went and among all the peoples through whom we passed.note
Porque Jehová nuestro Dios es el que nos sacó a nosotros y a nuestros padres de la tierra de Egipto, de la casa de servidumbre, y el que hizo delante de nuestros ojos aquellas grandes señales, y nos guardó por todo el camino por el que anduvimos y entre todos los pueblos por medio de los cuales pasamos.
18
וַיְגָרֶשׁ יְהוָה אֶת-כָּל-הָעַמִּים, וְאֶת-הָאֱמֹרִי יֹשֵׁב הָאָרֶץ--מִפָּנֵינוּ; גַּם-אֲנַחְנוּ נַעֲבֹד אֶת-יְהוָה, כִּי-הוּא אֱלֹהֵינוּ.
And Jehovah drove out before us all the peoples, even the Amorites who lived in the land; therefore we too will serve Jehovah, for he is our God.note
Y Jehová expulsó de delante de nosotros a todos los pueblos, y también a los amorreos que habitaban la tierra; por eso también nosotros serviremos a Jehová, porque él es nuestro Dios.
19
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶל-הָעָם, לֹא תוּכְלוּ לַעֲבֹד אֶת-יְהוָה--כִּי-אֱלֹהִים קְדֹשִׁים, הוּא: אֵל-קַנּוֹא הוּא, לֹא-יִשָּׂא לְפִשְׁעֲכֶם וּלְחַטֹּאותֵיכֶם.
And Joshua said to the people: You cannot serve Jehovah, for he is a holy God; he is a jealous God; he will not bear your rebellion or your sins.note
Y Josué dijo al pueblo: No podrán servir a Jehová, porque él es un Dios santo; es un Dios celoso; no perdonará su rebelión ni sus pecados.
20
כִּי תַעַזְבוּ אֶת-יְהוָה, וַעֲבַדְתֶּם אֱלֹהֵי נֵכָר; וְשָׁב וְהֵרַע לָכֶם וְכִלָּה אֶתְכֶם, אַחֲרֵי אֲשֶׁר-הֵיטִיב לָכֶם.
If you forsake Jehovah and serve foreign gods, then he will turn and do you evil, and consume you, after he has done you good.note
Si abandonan a Jehová y sirven a dioses extranjeros, él se volverá y les hará mal, y los consumirá, después de haberles hecho bien.
21
וַיֹּאמֶר הָעָם, אֶל-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ: לֹא, כִּי אֶת-יְהוָה נַעֲבֹד.
And the people said to Joshua: No; but we will serve Jehovah.note
Y el pueblo dijo a Josué: No, sino que serviremos a Jehová.
22
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶל-הָעָם, עֵדִים אַתֶּם בָּכֶם, כִּי-אַתֶּם בְּחַרְתֶּם לָכֶם אֶת-יְהוָה, לַעֲבֹד אוֹתוֹ; וַיֹּאמְרוּ, עֵדִים.
And Joshua said to the people: You are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen Jehovah, to serve him. And they said: We are witnesses.note
Y Josué dijo al pueblo: Ustedes son testigos contra ustedes mismos de que han escogido para sí a Jehová, para servirle. Y ellos dijeron: Testigos somos.
23
וְעַתָּה, הָסִירוּ אֶת-אֱלֹהֵי הַנֵּכָר אֲשֶׁר בְּקִרְבְּכֶם; וְהַטּוּ, אֶת-לְבַבְכֶם, אֶל-יְהוָה, אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
Now therefore put away the foreign gods that are among you, and incline your heart to Jehovah, the God of Israel.note
Ahora, pues, quiten los dioses extranjeros que hay entre ustedes, e inclinen su corazón a Jehová, Dios de Israel.
24
וַיֹּאמְרוּ הָעָם, אֶל-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ: אֶת-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ נַעֲבֹד, וּבְקוֹלוֹ נִשְׁמָע.
And the people said to Joshua: We will serve Jehovah our God, and we will hearken to his voice.note
Y el pueblo dijo a Josué: A Jehová nuestro Dios serviremos, y a su voz obedeceremos.
25
וַיִּכְרֹת יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בְּרִית לָעָם, בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא; וַיָּשֶׂם לוֹ חֹק וּמִשְׁפָּט, בִּשְׁכֶם.
So Joshua made a covenant with the people that day, and set for them a statute and an ordinance at Shechem.note
Así que Josué hizo pacto con el pueblo aquel día, y les dio un estatuto y una ordenanza en Siquem.
26
וַיִּכְתֹּב יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, בְּסֵפֶר תּוֹרַת אֱלֹהִים; וַיִּקַּח, אֶבֶן גְּדוֹלָה, וַיְקִימֶהָ שָּׁם, תַּחַת הָאַלָּה אֲשֶׁר בְּמִקְדַּשׁ יְהוָה. {פ}
And Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God; and he took a great stone and set it up there, under the oak that was by the sanctuary of Jehovah.note
Y Josué escribió estas palabras en el libro de la ley de Dios; y tomó una piedra grande, y la levantó allí, debajo de la encina que estaba junto al santuario de Jehová.
27
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶל-כָּל-הָעָם, הִנֵּה הָאֶבֶן הַזֹּאת תִּהְיֶה-בָּנוּ לְעֵדָה--כִּי-הִיא שָׁמְעָה אֵת כָּל-אִמְרֵי יְהוָה, אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר עִמָּנוּ; וְהָיְתָה בָכֶם לְעֵדָה, פֶּן-תְּכַחֲשׁוּן בֵּאלֹהֵיכֶם.
And Joshua said to all the people: Behold, this stone shall be a witness against us; for it has heard all the words of Jehovah which he spoke to us; it shall be a witness against you, lest you deny your God.note
Y Josué dijo a todo el pueblo: Miren, esta piedra será testigo contra nosotros; porque ella ha oído todas las palabras que Jehová nos habló; será, pues, testigo contra ustedes, para que no nieguen a su Dios.
28
וַיְשַׁלַּח יְהוֹשֻׁעַ אֶת-הָעָם, אִישׁ לְנַחֲלָתוֹ. {פ}
So Joshua sent the people away, each to his inheritance.note
Y Josué despidió al pueblo, cada cual a su heredad.
29
וַיְהִי, אַחֲרֵי הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, וַיָּמָת יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בִּן-נוּן, עֶבֶד יְהוָה--בֶּן-מֵאָה וָעֶשֶׂר, שָׁנִים.
And it came to pass after these things that Joshua son of Nun, the servant of Jehovah, died, being a hundred and ten years old.note
Y aconteció después de estas cosas que murió Josué hijo de Nun, siervo de Jehová, a la edad de ciento diez años.
30
וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אֹתוֹ בִּגְבוּל נַחֲלָתוֹ, בְּתִמְנַת-סֶרַח אֲשֶׁר בְּהַר-אֶפְרָיִם, מִצְּפוֹן, לְהַר-גָּעַשׁ.
And they buried him in the border of his inheritance at Timnath-serah, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, north of the mountain of Gaash.note
Y lo sepultaron en el territorio de su heredad, en Timnat-sera, que está en la montaña de Efraín, al norte del monte Gaas.
31
וַיַּעֲבֹד יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-יְהוָה, כֹּל יְמֵי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ; וְכֹל יְמֵי הַזְּקֵנִים, אֲשֶׁר הֶאֱרִיכוּ יָמִים אַחֲרֵי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, וַאֲשֶׁר יָדְעוּ אֵת כָּל-מַעֲשֵׂה יְהוָה, אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה לְיִשְׂרָאֵל.
And Israel served Jehovah all the days of Joshua, and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua and who had known all the work Jehovah had done for Israel.note
Israel sirvió a Jehová todos los días de Josué, y todos los días de los ancianos que sobrevivieron a Josué y que habían conocido toda la obra que Jehová había hecho por Israel.
32
וְאֶת-עַצְמוֹת יוֹסֵף אֲשֶׁר-הֶעֱלוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל מִמִּצְרַיִם, קָבְרוּ בִשְׁכֶם, בְּחֶלְקַת הַשָּׂדֶה אֲשֶׁר קָנָה יַעֲקֹב מֵאֵת בְּנֵי-חֲמוֹר אֲבִי-שְׁכֶם, בְּמֵאָה קְשִׂיטָה; וַיִּהְיוּ לִבְנֵי-יוֹסֵף, לְנַחֲלָה.
And the bones of Joseph, which the sons of Israel brought up from Egypt, they buried at Shechem, in the plot of ground that Jacob bought from the sons of Hamor, father of Shechem, for a hundred pieces; and it became an inheritance of the sons of Joseph.note
Y los huesos de José, que los hijos de Israel habían subido de Egipto, los sepultaron en Siquem, en la parcela de tierra que Jacob compró a los hijos de Hamor, padre de Siquem, por cien monedas; y pasó a ser herencia de los hijos de José.
33
וְאֶלְעָזָר בֶּן-אַהֲרֹן, מֵת; וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אֹתוֹ, בִּגְבַעַת פִּינְחָס בְּנוֹ, אֲשֶׁר נִתַּן-לוֹ, בְּהַר אֶפְרָיִם. {ש}
And Eleazar son of Aaron died; and they buried him at the hill of his son Phinehas, which was given to him in the hill country of Ephraim.note
Y murió Eleazar hijo de Aarón; y lo sepultaron en el collado de su hijo Finees, que le había sido dado en la montaña de Efraín.
Verses 1–2 · The last assembly at Shechem, Joshua 24:1–2: all the tribes, the same four offices as Joshua 23:2, and a history told from beyond the River

★★★ Shechem is the place of the last assembly, and the book has been there before. The name stands at the head of v1 and again at v25. Joshua had set up the altar and the stones of the law near the same region (Joshua 8:30–35, already on these pages), and Jacob had bought land there (Genesis 33:18–20, already on these pages).

★★ The officers are the same four as in Joshua 23:2. Elders, heads, judges and officers; the list repeats Joshua’s last speech, and the audience is the nation’s whole responsible body. The verb yatsev, ‘presented themselves’, says that they stood before God, in the presence of the sanctuary.

★ V2 uses the prophetic formula ‘Thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel’, and it puts the history in the mouth of God. The speech tells the nation its own origin: the fathers “dwelt beyond the River” and “served other gods”. ‘Beyond the River’ is Mesopotamia, the country of Terah and Nahor.

Verses 3–8 · The recital of God’s acts, Joshua 24:3–8: from Terah to the Amorites in one long sentence of ‘I took’, ‘I gave’, ‘I sent’

★★ The recital is built on the first person, the voice of God speaking through Joshua. Each clause begins va-ani, ‘and I’: I took, led, multiplied, gave, sent, plagued, brought, destroyed. The chapter’s history is one long sentence whose subject is God, and the people are the objects of his actions.

★ Esau gets the mountain of Seir, and Jacob goes down into Egypt. The verb yarad (‘went down’) is the one Genesis 46:3 uses for the descent into Egypt (already on these pages). Esau’s holding is Seir (Genesis 36, already on these pages), and the verse gives it as a gift, not a conquest.

★★ The Red Sea is ‘the sea of reeds’, and the recital puts it in one clause with the pursuit. Yam-suf is the Hebrew name for the body of water crossed in Exodus 14 (already on these pages). The chapter names the sea and the pursuit together, and does not narrate the crossing itself.

Verses 9–13 · Balak, Balaam, Jericho and the hornet, Joshua 24:9–13: the blessings that were not curses, and the hornet that drove the kings out

★★ Balak tries to curse Israel and Jehovah turns the curse into blessing. V10 says Balaam “blessed you, blessing you again and again”, The blessings Balaam gave are the ones of Numbers 23–24 (already on these pages), where the oracles are blessings the king did not want. The chapter reports the outcome, not the oracles.

★★ The hornet is a noun for an insect, and the spelling stands in thirteen verses of the Hebrew Bible. Tsirah appears in Exodus 23:28 and Deuteronomy 7:20 (both on these pages), and here at v12; the other occurrences include place names such as Zorah (Joshua 15:33, already on these pages), so the count is not a count of hornets. The verse says the hornet drove the two Amorite kings out, ‘not by your sword nor by your bow’.

★ The nations list in v11 has seven names. The Amorite, the Perizzite, the Canaanite, the Hittite, the Girgashite, the Hivite and the Jebusite stand together, and the verse gives Jericho as the place of the battle.

Verses 14–15 · Fear, serve in wholeness and truth, and ‘as for me and my house’, Joshua 24:14–15: the choice is made by the people, but the verb is Joshua’s

★★★ ‘In wholeness and in truth’ is one phrase, and it stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. Betamim uve-emet appears only here, checked consonantally across the whole corpus. The two nouns are the book’s own pair for sincere, whole service, and the translation keeps them as two words rather than one. The NWT 1984 reads ‘in faultlessness and in truth’, the NWT 2013 ‘with integrity and faithfulness’, and the TNM 2019 ‘integridad y fidelidad’; the KJV and the ASV both read ‘in sincerity and in truth’, which is closest to the Hebrew pair.

★ V15 is the most quoted sentence of the chapter, and its last clause is Joshua’s own. ‘As for me and my house, we will serve Jehovah’ is a choice made in the first person by the speaker, and the verb is ‘we will serve’. The people will choose in v21–24; Joshua has already chosen for his household.

Verses 16–18 · The people’s answer, Joshua 24:16–18: ‘far be it from us’ again, and the list of what God did

★★ V16 repeats the phrase of Joshua 22:29, ‘far be it from us’. The formula chalilah lanu is the eastern tribes’ denial in the altar dispute (already on these pages). Here the people use it to deny the charge that they would forsake Jehovah.

★★ V17 is the people’s creed, and it is the shortest history in the book. It has the Exodus, the house of slavery, the signs, and the preservation through the wilderness and the nations. It does not mention Sinai or the law; the people’s confession is the exodus.

Verses 19–20 · ‘A jealous God’, Joshua 24:19–20: Joshua tests the vow, and the word qanno stands in two verses

★★★ ‘He is a jealous God’ stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible. The NWT 1984 reads ‘a God exacting exclusive devotion’, the NWT 2013 ‘a God who requires exclusive devotion’, and the KJV and ASV keep ‘a jealous God’; the translation keeps the Hebrew word, as the note on it explains. The participle qanno is in Joshua 24:19 and Nahum 1:2, checked consonantally. The divine name ‘jealous’ is the book’s own version of the second commandment’s ‘jealous God’ (Exodus 20:5, already on these pages).

★ Joshua says the people cannot serve Jehovah, and then tells them what he will do if they try to serve other gods. V19 is a challenge, and v20 is the consequence: he will turn and do evil, and consume them, after he has done them good. The sentence is the covenant curse in one clause.

Verses 21–24 · The people’s pledge, Joshua 24:21–24: witnesses against themselves, and the verb hitti ‘incline’

★★ ‘You are witnesses against yourselves’ uses the witness word of Joshua 22. Edim is the word for a witness, and the book uses it in Joshua 22:22–34 (already on these pages) of the altar that was called Witness. Here the people are the witnesses, and the witness is the choice they have made.

★ V23 asks them to incline their hearts, and the verb is the one for bending a bow or a path. The verb hitti is used of turning the heart toward God, and the translation keeps ‘incline’ rather than ‘turn’, which is the verb the Hebrew uses.

Verses 25–28 · The covenant at Shechem, the book, and the great stone, Joshua 24:25–28: a witness the people can see

★★★ Joshua makes a covenant, sets a statute and an ordinance, and writes the words in ‘the book of the law of God’. The phrase sefer torat Elohim, ‘the book of the law of God’, is the chapter’s own name for the law (Joshua 23:6 uses sefer torat Moshe, already on these pages). The chapter writes the covenant down and sets a stone beside the book, so the nation has two witnesses.

★★ The stone is a witness that ‘has heard’, and Joshua gives it a speech. V27 says the stone ‘has heard all the words of Jehovah’, and it will be a witness against the people if they deny their God. The idea of a stone that hears is the book’s own witness language.

★★ The stone stands under the oak by the sanctuary of Jehovah. The word elah, ‘oak’, is the tree of the sanctuary here; Genesis 12:6 has the oak of Moreh, a different word, at the place of Abraham’s first altar in the land (already on these pages). The place is a sacred tree by the sanctuary, and the stone is set under it.

Verses 29–33 · The three deaths, Joshua 24:29–33: Joshua at 110, Joseph’s bones at Shechem, Eleazar at the hill of Phinehas

★★★ Joshua dies at a hundred and ten, the same age Joseph is given in Genesis 50:26 (already on these pages). The book does not comment on the match. The age is given once, and the burial place is named by its border: Timnath-serah, which Joshua 19:50 gave him as his inheritance (already on these pages).

★★ Joseph’s bones are buried at Shechem, in the plot Jacob bought. Genesis 33:19 says Jacob bought the field from the sons of Hamor (already on these pages), and the chapter says the bones were brought up from Egypt, which fulfils Genesis 50:25 and Exodus 13:19 (both already on these pages). The verse is the book’s own closing of the patriarchal story.

★ Eleazar dies and is buried at the hill of his son Phinehas, which was given to him in Ephraim. The hill is named Gibeat Pinchas here, and the verse says it was given to Eleazar’s son. The chapter ends on a priest’s son’s hill, not on a king’s city, and that is the book’s own last word.

Verses 28–30 · Each man to his inheritance, Joshua 24:28–30: the people sent home, and Joshua’s own burial by his border

★★ V28 sends the people home, each to his inheritance. The phrase ‘each to his inheritance’ echoes the division of the land (Joshua 19:51, already on these pages), and the verse sends the people back to the holdings they were given.

Verses 31–33 · The generation that served, Joshua 24:31: ‘the elders who outlived Joshua’, and the last words of the book

★★ Israel served Jehovah all the days of Joshua and of the elders who outlived him. The verse measures the faithfulness of the generation by the leaders who had known the work of Jehovah. Judges 2:7 (already on these pages) repeats the same measure, and then the next generation does not know (Judges 2:10).

Echoes paid. The witness stone of v27 pays off the witness altar of Joshua 22 (already on these pages). The ‘jealous God’ of v19 is the second commandment of Exodus 20:5, and the people’s ‘far be it from us’ of v16 pays off Joshua 22:29. The hornet of v12 is the one Exodus 23:28 promised.

Echoes planted. Judges 2:7–10 (already on these pages) picks up the generation that outlived Joshua. Joseph’s bones return in the first chapter after the book.

Decisions. The chapter has no verse gap. The translation keeps the first-person divine recital in v2–13 as a continuous sentence, and keeps betamim uve-emet as two words. Yam-suf is given as the Red Sea, with the note that the phrase means ‘sea of reeds’. Tsirah is ‘hornet’, with the note that the spelling also covers place names.

Library. New entries are needed in both languages: the encyclopedia entries for Abraham, Nahor, Beor, the Amorites, the Canaanites, Seir, and the Gaash and Gibeath-phinehas places; the dictionary entries for tsirah, chok and qadosh. Each entry the notes link to must exist before the chapter ships.

⚠ A note on order: Joshua stands on these pages at chapters 1–24. The book is complete, and the next book is Judges, where Judges 7 and beyond remain open.
