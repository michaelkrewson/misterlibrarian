1 Corinthians 14 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
Διώκετε τὴν ἀγάπην, ζηλοῦτε δὲ τὰ πνευματικά, μᾶλλον δὲ ἵνα προφητεύητε.
Pursue love, and desire the spiritual gifts, but especially that you may prophesy.note
Sigan el amor; y deseen con celo los dones espirituales, pero sobre todo que profeticen.
2
ὁ γὰρ λαλῶν γλώσσῃ οὐκ ἀνθρώποις λαλεῖ ἀλλὰ θεῷ, οὐδεὶς γὰρ ἀκούει, πνεύματι δὲ λαλεῖ μυστήρια·
For the one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to people but to God, for no one understands him; he speaks mysteries in the Spirit.note
Porque el que habla en lengua no habla a hombres sino a Dios, pues nadie lo entiende; en el espíritu habla misterios.
3
ὁ δὲ προφητεύων ἀνθρώποις λαλεῖ οἰκοδομὴν καὶ παράκλησιν καὶ παραμυθίαν.
But the one who prophesies speaks to people for building up, encouragement, and comfort.note
Pero el que profetiza habla a los hombres para edificación, exhortación y consuelo.
4
ὁ λαλῶν γλώσσῃ ἑαυτὸν οἰκοδομεῖ· ὁ δὲ προφητεύων ἐκκλησίαν οἰκοδομεῖ.
The one who speaks in a tongue builds up himself, but the one who prophesies builds up the congregation.note
El que habla en lengua se edifica a sí mismo, pero el que profetiza edifica la congregación.
5
θέλω δὲ πάντας ὑμᾶς λαλεῖν γλώσσαις, μᾶλλον δὲ ἵνα προφητεύητε· μείζων δὲ ὁ προφητεύων ἢ ὁ λαλῶν γλώσσαις, ἐκτὸς εἰ μὴ διερμηνεύῃ ἵνα ἡ ἐκκλησία οἰκοδομὴν λάβῃ.
I want all of you to speak in tongues, but even more that you prophesy. The one who prophesies is greater than the one who speaks in tongues, unless he interprets, so that the congregation may receive building up.note
Quisiera que todos ustedes hablaran en lenguas, pero prefiero que profeticen. El que profetiza es mayor que el que habla en lenguas, a menos que interprete, para que la congregación reciba edificación.
6
Νῦν δέ, ἀδελφοί, ἐὰν ἔλθω πρὸς ὑμᾶς γλώσσαις λαλῶν, τί ὑμᾶς ὠφελήσω, ἐὰν μὴ ὑμῖν λαλήσω ἢ ἐν ἀποκαλύψει ἢ ἐν γνώσει ἢ ἐν προφητείᾳ ἢ ἐν διδαχῇ;
But now, brothers, if I come to you speaking in tongues, what will I profit you unless I speak to you by way of a revelation, or knowledge, or prophecy, or teaching?note
Ahora bien, hermanos, si yo fuera a ustedes hablando en lenguas, ¿qué les aprovecharía, si no les hablara con revelación, o con conocimiento, o con profecía, o con enseñanza?
7
ὅμως τὰ ἄψυχα φωνὴν διδόντα, εἴτε αὐλὸς εἴτε κιθάρα, ἐὰν διαστολὴν τοῖς φθόγγοις μὴ δῷ πῶς γνωσθήσεται τὸ αὐλούμενον ἢ τὸ κιθαριζόμενον;
Even lifeless things that make a sound, such as a flute or a harp, if they do not make a distinction in the notes, how will what is played on the flute or the harp be known?note
Aun las cosas sin vida que producen sonido, como la flauta o la cítara: si no dan distinción a los sonidos, ¿cómo se sabrá lo que se toca con la flauta o con la cítara?
8
καὶ γὰρ ἐὰν ἄδηλον φωνὴν σάλπιγξ δῷ, τίς παρασκευάσεται εἰς πόλεμον;
And if the trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will prepare for battle?note
Y si la trompeta da un sonido incierto, ¿quién se preparará para la batalla?
9
οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς διὰ τῆς γλώσσης ἐὰν μὴ εὔσημον λόγον δῶτε, πῶς γνωσθήσεται τὸ λαλούμενον; ἔσεσθε γὰρ εἰς ἀέρα λαλοῦντες.
So also with you: if you do not speak through the tongue words that are easily understood, how will what is said be known? You will be speaking into the air.note
Así también ustedes: si por la lengua no dan palabra clara, ¿cómo se sabrá lo que se dice? Estarán hablando al aire.
10
τοσαῦτα εἰ τύχοι γένη φωνῶν εἰσιν ἐν κόσμῳ, καὶ οὐδὲν ἄφωνον·
There are, perhaps, so many kinds of sounds in the world, and none of them is without meaning.note
Hay, quizá, tantas clases de voces en el mundo, y ninguna carece de significado.
11
ἐὰν οὖν μὴ εἰδῶ τὴν δύναμιν τῆς φωνῆς, ἔσομαι τῷ λαλοῦντι βάρβαρος καὶ ὁ λαλῶν ἐν ἐμοὶ βάρβαρος.
If then I do not know the meaning of the sound, I will be a foreigner to the one speaking, and the one speaking will be a foreigner to me.note
Si, pues, no conozco el sentido de la voz, seré extranjero para el que habla, y el que habla será extranjero para mí.
12
οὕτως καὶ ὑμεῖς, ἐπεὶ ζηλωταί ἐστε πνευμάτων, πρὸς τὴν οἰκοδομὴν τῆς ἐκκλησίας ζητεῖτε ἵνα περισσεύητε.
So also with you: since you are zealous for spiritual gifts, seek to abound in them for the building up of the congregation.note
Así también ustedes, puesto que anhelan los dones espirituales, procuren abundar en ellos para la edificación de la congregación.
13
Διὸ ὁ λαλῶν γλώσσῃ προσευχέσθω ἵνα διερμηνεύῃ.
Therefore let the one who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.note
Por lo tanto, el que habla en lengua ore para poder interpretar.
14
ἐὰν γὰρ προσεύχωμαι γλώσσῃ, τὸ πνεῦμά μου προσεύχεται, ὁ δὲ νοῦς μου ἄκαρπός ἐστιν.
For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.note
Porque si oro en lengua, mi espíritu ora, pero mi mente no produce fruto.
15
τί οὖν ἐστιν; προσεύξομαι τῷ πνεύματι, προσεύξομαι δὲ καὶ τῷ νοΐ· ψαλῶ τῷ πνεύματι, ψαλῶ δὲ καὶ τῷ νοΐ·
What then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the mind also; I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the mind also.note
¿Qué hay, pues? Oraré con el espíritu, pero también oraré con la mente; cantaré con el espíritu, pero también cantaré con la mente.
16
ἐπεὶ ἐὰν εὐλογῇς πνεύματι, ὁ ἀναπληρῶν τὸν τόπον τοῦ ἰδιώτου πῶς ἐρεῖ τὸ Ἀμήν ἐπὶ τῇ σῇ εὐχαριστίᾳ; ἐπειδὴ τί λέγεις οὐκ οἶδεν·
Otherwise, if you bless with the spirit, how will the one who occupies the place of an outsider say “Amen” to your thanksgiving, since he does not know what you are saying?note
De otro modo, si bendices con el espíritu, ¿cómo dirá «Amén» a tu acción de gracias el que ocupa el lugar del que no sabe, puesto que no sabe lo que dices?
17
σὺ μὲν γὰρ καλῶς εὐχαριστεῖς, ἀλλʼ ὁ ἕτερος οὐκ οἰκοδομεῖται.
You give thanks well, but the other person is not built up.note
Tú das gracias bien, pero el otro no es edificado.
18
εὐχαριστῶ τῷ θεῷ πάντων ὑμῶν μᾶλλον γλώσσαις λαλῶ·
I thank God that I speak in tongues more than all of you.note
Doy gracias a Dios porque hablo en lenguas más que todos ustedes.
19
ἀλλὰ ἐν ἐκκλησίᾳ θέλω πέντε λόγους τῷ νοΐ μου λαλῆσαι, ἵνα καὶ ἄλλους κατηχήσω, ἢ μυρίους λόγους ἐν γλώσσῃ.
But in the congregation I would rather speak five words with my mind, so that I may also instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue.note
Pero en la congregación prefiero decir cinco palabras con mi mente, para instruir también a otros, a diez mil palabras en lengua.
20
Ἀδελφοί, μὴ παιδία γίνεσθε ταῖς φρεσίν, ἀλλὰ τῇ κακίᾳ νηπιάζετε, ταῖς δὲ φρεσὶν τέλειοι γίνεσθε.
Brothers, do not be children in your thinking; be infants in evil, but in your thinking be mature.note
Hermanos, no sean niños en su modo de pensar; sean niños en cuanto a la maldad, pero sean maduros en su modo de pensar.

21
ἐν τῷ νόμῳ γέγραπται ὅτι Ἐν ἑτερογλώσσοις καὶ ἐν χείλεσιν ἑτέρων λαλήσω τῷ λαῷ τούτῳ, καὶ οὐδʼ οὕτως εἰσακούσονταί μου, λέγει κύριος.
In the Law it is written: “By people of other tongues and by the lips of others I will speak to this people, and even so they will not listen to me,” says the Lord.note
En la Ley está escrito: «Hablaré a este pueblo en lenguas extranjeras y con labios de extraños, y ni aun así me escucharán», dice el Señor.

Por tanto, las lenguas son una señal no para los creyentes, sino para los incrédulos; pero la profecía no es para los incrédulos, sino para los creyentes.

So if the whole congregation comes together in one place and all speak in tongues, and outsiders or unbelievers come in, will they not say that you are out of your mind?note
Así que, si toda la congregación se reúne en un mismo lugar y todos hablan en lenguas, y entran personas comunes o incrédulos, ¿no dirán que ustedes están fuera de sí?
24
ἐὰν δὲ πάντες προφητεύωσιν, εἰσέλθῃ δέ τις ἄπιστος ἢ ἰδιώτης, ἐλέγχεται ὑπὸ πάντων, ἀνακρίνεται ὑπὸ πάντων,
But if all prophesy, and an unbeliever or an outsider comes in, he is convicted by all and judged by all.note
Pero si todos profetizan y entra un incrédulo o una persona común, es reprendido por todos y examinado por todos.
25
τὰ κρυπτὰ τῆς καρδίας αὐτοῦ φανερὰ γίνεται, καὶ οὕτως πεσὼν ἐπὶ πρόσωπον προσκυνήσει τῷ θεῷ, ἀπαγγέλλων ὅτι Ὄντως ὁ θεὸς ἐν ὑμῖν ἐστιν.
The secrets of his heart are laid bare, and so he will fall on his face and worship God, declaring that God is truly among you.note
Los secretos de su corazón quedan al descubierto; y así, cayendo sobre su rostro, adorará a Dios y declarará que Dios verdaderamente está entre ustedes.
26
Τί οὖν ἐστιν, ἀδελφοί; ὅταν συνέρχησθε, ἕκαστος ψαλμὸν ἔχει, διδαχὴν ἔχει, ἀποκάλυψιν ἔχει, γλῶσσαν ἔχει, ἑρμηνείαν ἔχει· πάντα πρὸς οἰκοδομὴν γινέσθω.
What then, brothers? When you come together, each one has a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, an interpretation. Let all things be done for building up.note
¿Qué hay, pues, hermanos? Cuando se reúnan, cada uno tiene un salmo, tiene una enseñanza, tiene una revelación, tiene una lengua, tiene una interpretación. Hágase todo para edificación.

εἴτε γλώσσῃ τις λαλεῖ, κατὰ δύο ἢ τὸ πλεῖστον τρεῖς, καὶ ἀνὰ μέρος, καὶ εἷς διερμηνευέτω·
If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be by two or at most three, one after another, and let one interpret.note
Si alguien habla en lengua, que sean dos o tres como máximo, y por turno; y que uno interprete.
28

Pero si no hay intérprete, que guarde silencio en la congregación y hable consigo mismo y con Dios.

Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others weigh what is said.note
Que hablen dos o tres profetas, y que los demás juzguen.

Pero si a otro que está sentado se le revela algo, que se calle el primero.

For you can all prophesy, one by one, so that all may learn and all may be encouraged.note

Verse 1 · love first, and then the gifts, and most of all prophecy

“Pursue love.” diōkete tēn agapēn, pursue love. The verb is the verb of chasing something hard to catch, and the object is the love that chapter 13 describes. The verb zēloō, to desire earnestly, returns in the next clause for the spiritual gifts; the sentence sets the two in order. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Pursue love, yet keep striving for the spiritual gifts, but preferably that you may prophesy.” The ASV “Follow after love; yet desire earnestly spiritual gifts” keeps the same order, and the KJV “Follow after charity” uses the older word for love.

Verses 2–5 · tongues to God and prophecy to people, building up the congregation

“Speaks in a tongue.” glōssē, a tongue or language (glōssa). The speaker addresses God, and the hearers do not understand; the sentence says he speaks mysteries in the spirit (mystērion). Paul does not say what the language is, and the word can name a human tongue or an ecstatic speech; the sentence does not settle the question, and the translation keeps “tongue” for the Greek noun. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “For he that speaks in a tongue speaks, not to men, but to God.”

“Building up.” oikodomē, building up (oikodomē), the word of 8:1 and 3:10. The prophet builds up the congregation; the tongue builds up the speaker. The verse uses one word for both, and the difference is who receives the building. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “The one who speaks in a tongue builds up himself, but the one who prophesies builds up a congregation.” The ASV “he that speaketh in a tongue edifieth himself; but he that prophesieth edifieth the church” prints “church” for ekklēsia.

“Unless he interprets.” ektos ei mē diermēneuē. The verb diermēneuō, to interpret, is the verb of carrying a meaning across (hermēneia). The sentence makes the tongue equal to prophecy when it is interpreted. The shelf: the KJV “greater is he that prophesieth than he that speaketh with tongues, except he interpret, that the church may receive edifying,” and the NWT 1984 “unless, in fact, he translates, that the congregation may receive upbuilding.”

Verses 6–12 · the flute, the trumpet, the speech that is understood, and the foreigner

“Revelation, knowledge, prophecy, or teaching.” apokalypsei, gnōsei, prophēteia, didachē. The four words are a list of ways in which a speaker might help the hearers. The word didachē is the word for teaching (didachē), and prophēteia is the word for speaking forth (prophēteia). The shelf: the NWT 2013 “what good would I do you unless I spoke to you either with a revelation or with knowledge or with a prophecy or with a teaching?”

“Flute, harp, trumpet.” The three instruments are the examples, and the point is the distinctness of the sound. A flute or harp that does not distinguish its notes cannot be known; a trumpet with an unclear call does not prepare for battle. The word diastolē is the interval between notes. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “It is the same with the inanimate things that produce sound, whether a flute or a harp. Unless there is an interval to the tones, how can what is being played on the flute or on the harp be recognized?” The ASV “if they give not a distinction in the sounds” keeps the same sense.

“A foreigner.” barbaros, a foreigner, one whose speech sounds like noise to the hearer (barbaros). The speaker and the listener are foreigners to each other if the sense is missing. The shelf: the ASV “If then I know not the meaning of the voice, I shall be to him that speaketh a barbarian, and he that speaketh will be a barbarian unto me.”

Verses 13–19 · praying with the spirit and with the mind, and five words that instruct

“Pray that he may interpret.” proseuchesthō hina diermēneuē. The prayer is for the power to interpret. The verse tells the tongue speaker to ask for the gift that makes the speech useful to others. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “let the one who speaks in a tongue pray that he may interpret.”

“My spirit prays, but my mind is unfruitful.” to pneuma mou proseuchetai, ho de nous mou akarpos estin. The word nous, mind (nous), is the faculty that understands, and the word akarpos is unfruitful. The sentence says the spirit may pray while the mind has no yield. Paul then says he will do both: pray and sing with the spirit and with the mind. The shelf: the ASV “For if I pray in a tongue, my spirit prayeth, but my understanding is unfruitful.”

“Five words with my mind.” pente logous tō noi mou, five words with the mind. The sentence sets the count of words against the ten thousand in a tongue, and the point is teaching: the five instruct others. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “in a congregation I would rather speak five words with my mind, that I might also instruct others, than ten thousand words in a tongue.” The ASV “I had rather speak five words with my understanding” uses “understanding” for the same noun.

Verses 20–25 · children in thinking, the Law, tongues as a sign, and the unbeliever who is convicted

“Do not be children in your thinking.” mē paidia ginesthe tais phresin. The word phrēn, thinking, is the word for understanding. Paul asks the readers to be infants in badness and mature in thinking, which is the contrast of the chapter. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Brothers, do not become young children in your understanding, but be young children as to badness; and become full-grown in your understanding.”

“In the Law it is written.” The quotation is from Isaiah 28:11–12, which Paul calls the Law; the words are about speech in a foreign tongue that the people will not hear. The sentence uses the quotation to say that tongues are a sign, not for believers but for unbelievers. Paul’s word for the Scripture here is nomos, Law, which the translation keeps. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “In the Law it is written: “‘With the tongues of foreigners and with the lips of strangers I will speak to this people, and even then they will refuse to listen to me,’ says Jehovah.”” The Greek has legei kyrios, says the Lord; the NWT prints the name Jehovah, which the translation does not, because the Greek word here is not a name.

“Tongues are a sign.” eis sēmeion, for a sign (sēmeion). The sentence says the tongue is a sign for the unbeliever and prophecy for the believer. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Therefore, tongues are not a sign for the believers but for the unbelievers, whereas prophecy is not for the unbelievers but for the believers.” The ASV “tongues are for a sign, not to them that believe, but to the unbelieving” keeps the same order.

“Will they not say that you are out of your mind?” mainesthe, you are mad. The outsider reads the congregation as mad, and then the prophecy convicts him and he worships God. The shelf: the ASV “will they not say that ye are mad?”; the KJV “will they not say that ye are mad?”

Verses 26–33 · each has a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, or an interpretation, and God is not of disorder

“Each one has a psalm.” hekastos psalmon echei. The list of five items is the list of how the congregation speaks: a psalm, a teaching, a revelation, a tongue, an interpretation. The word psalmos is a song sung to a stringed instrument. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “What is to be done, then, brothers? When you come together, one has a psalm, another has a teaching, another has a revelation, another has a tongue, and another has an interpretation. Let all things take place for building up.”

“Two or at most three.” kata dyo ē to pleiston treis, by two or at most three. The number limits the tongue speakers, one after another, and one interprets. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “let it be limited to two or three at the most, and in turns, and someone must interpret.”

“God is not of disorder but of peace.” akatastasias, disorder, the word of confusion. The sentence gives the reason for the order and then places it in all the congregations of the saints. The shelf: the ASV “for God is not a God of confusion, but of peace. As in all the churches of the saints,”; the KJV “For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.”

Verses 34–35 · the women and the congregation: the text, the reading, and the misuse

“Let the women keep silent.” Hai gynaikes en tais ekklēsiais sigatōsan. The verb sigaō is to be silent, and the sentence says that women are not permitted to speak in the congregations and are to be in subjection. These verses have been used for centuries to bar women from speaking in worship altogether. The misreading is in the scope of the sentence: the same letter takes for granted that women pray and prophesy in the congregation in 11:5, and the instruction here concerns speech in the assembly, the question-and-answer that the next verse describes. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “let the women keep silent in the congregations, for it is not permitted for them to speak. Rather, let them be in subjection, as the Law also says.” The KJV “Let your women keep silence in the churches” prints “churches” for ekklēsiais.

“Ask their husbands at home.” en oikō tous idious andras eperōtatōsan, let them ask their own husbands at home. The verse answers a question about learning, and gives the setting for it. The verb eperōtaō is to question, and the shame is the word aischros, disgraceful. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “If they want to learn something, let them ask their husbands at home, for it is disgraceful for a woman to speak in the congregation.” The TNM 1987 “porque es vergonzoso que una mujer hable en la congregación.”

The textual position. Some early witnesses, among them Codex Bezae (D), place verses 34–35 after verse 40, and the translation prints them where the main text places them. The note records that the position is contested and does not settle it. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “que las mujeres se queden calladas en las congregaciones, porque no se les permite hablar.”

Verses 36–40 · the word of God, the Lord’s command, and order

“Did the word of God come out from you?” ē aph hymōn ho logos tou theou exēlthen, did the word of God come out from you. The sentence closes the discussion with two questions, each expecting no. The shelf: the KJV “What? came the word of God out from you? or came it unto you only?”

“The Lord’s command.” kyriou estin, it is the Lord’s. The word entolē is the command in the translation. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “he must acknowledge that the things I am writing to you are the Lord’s commandment.”

“Let him go unrecognized.” agnoeitai, is not recognized. The passive verb is the same root as agnoein, to be ignorant. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But if anyone disregards this, he will be disregarded.” The ASV “But if any man is ignorant, let him be ignorant.”

“Decently and in order.” euschēmonōs kai kata taxin, decently and by arrangement. The adverb is the word for what is becoming, and the arrangement is the word for a rank or ordering. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But let all things take place decently and by arrangement.” The KJV “Let all things be done decently and in order.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “congregation” for ekklēsia at 14:4, 14:5, 14:12, 14:19, 14:23, 14:28, 14:33, 14:34 and 14:35, the fixed rendering of every NT chapter; “building up” for oikodomē at 14:3–5, 14:12, 14:26, the word of 8:1 and 10:23; “sign” for sēmeion at 14:22; “mind” for nous at 14:14–19; “disorder” for akatastasia at 14:33, the word of the chapter’s peace.

Echoes paid. The gifts of 12:1–31, now the speech gifts ranked (14:1–5); the building up of 8:1 and 10:23, now the purpose that orders every speech (14:26); the congregation of 11:18, now the setting for speech and silence (14:4–35); the sign of 1:22, now the tongue as a sign for unbelievers (14:22); the mind of 2:16, now the mind that must be fruitful (14:14–15); and the peace of 7:15, now God as the God of peace (14:33).

Echoes planted. Love (14:1) is the subject of chapter 13, which comes before this one; its last verses name love as the greatest (13:13). The mystery of 14:2 returns in chapter 15, where Paul calls the change of the dead a mystery (15:51). The Lord’s coming (11:26) and the body (12:27) are the frame of the last chapters.

Next in this book: 1 Corinthians 15, already on these pages. Paul turns from speech in the congregation to the resurrection of the dead.
