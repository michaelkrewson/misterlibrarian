The nations Jehovah left are used to test Israel. Israel intermarries with them and serves their gods. Othniel, Ehud and Shamgar deliver the land in turn, against a king of Aram, a king of Moab, and six hundred Philistines.
Undated within the chapter. It follows directly on from Judges 2, so it takes the same undated window the library gives that chapter.

Judges 3 — The Mister Translation

1
וְאֵלֶּה הַגּוֹיִם אֲשֶׁר הִנִּיחַ יְהוָה, לְנַסּוֹת בָּם אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר לֹא-יָדְעוּ, אֵת כָּל-מִלְחֲמוֹת כְּנָעַן.
These are the nations Jehovah left, to test Israel by them: all those who had not known any of the wars of Canaan.note
Estas son las naciones que Jehová dejó para probar con ellas a Israel: todos los que no habían conocido ninguna de las guerras de Canaán.
2
רַק, לְמַעַן דַּעַת דֹּרוֹת בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, לְלַמְּדָם, מִלְחָמָה--רַק אֲשֶׁר-לְפָנִים, לֹא יְדָעוּם.
Only so that the generations of the sons of Israel might know, and be taught war, those who had known nothing of it before.note
Solo para que las generaciones de los hijos de Israel supieran, y aprendieran la guerra: aquellos que antes nada de ella habían conocido.
3
חֲמֵשֶׁת סַרְנֵי פְלִשְׁתִּים, וְכָל-הַכְּנַעֲנִי וְהַצִּידֹנִי, וְהַחִוִּי, יֹשֵׁב הַר הַלְּבָנוֹן--מֵהַר בַּעַל חֶרְמוֹן, עַד לְבוֹא חֲמָת.
The five lords of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived on Mount Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon to the entrance of Hamath.note
Los cinco señores de los filisteos, todos los cananeos, los sidonios y los heveos que habitaban el monte Líbano, desde el monte Baal-hermón hasta la entrada de Jamat.
4
וַיִּהְיוּ, לְנַסּוֹת בָּם אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל--לָדַעַת, הֲיִשְׁמְעוּ אֶת-מִצְוֺת יְהוָה, אֲשֶׁר-צִוָּה אֶת-אֲבוֹתָם, בְּיַד-מֹשֶׁה.
They were there to test Israel by them, to learn whether Israel would listen to the commandments of Jehovah that he had given their fathers through Moses.note
Estaban allí para probar con ellas a Israel, para saber si escucharían los mandamientos de Jehová que él había dado a sus padres por medio de Moisés.
5
וּבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, יָשְׁבוּ בְּקֶרֶב הַכְּנַעֲנִי, הַחִתִּי וְהָאֱמֹרִי וְהַפְּרִזִּי, וְהַחִוִּי וְהַיְבוּסִי.
The sons of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.note
Los hijos de Israel habitaron entre los cananeos, los hititas, los amorreos, los ferezeos, los heveos y los jebuseos.
6
וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת-בְּנוֹתֵיהֶם לָהֶם לְנָשִׁים, וְאֶת-בְּנוֹתֵיהֶם נָתְנוּ לִבְנֵיהֶם; וַיַּעַבְדוּ, אֶת-אֱלֹהֵיהֶם.
They took their daughters for themselves as wives, gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.note
Tomaron sus hijas para sí como esposas, dieron sus propias hijas a sus hijos y sirvieron a sus dioses.
7
וַיַּעֲשׂוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה, וַיִּשְׁכְּחוּ אֶת-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיהֶם; וַיַּעַבְדוּ אֶת-הַבְּעָלִים, וְאֶת-הָאֲשֵׁרוֹת.
The sons of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, forgot Jehovah their God, and served the Baals and the Asherahs.note
Los hijos de Israel hicieron lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová, olvidaron a Jehová su Dios y sirvieron a los baales y a las asherot.
8
וַיִּחַר-אַף יְהוָה, בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיִּמְכְּרֵם בְּיַד כּוּשַׁן רִשְׁעָתַיִם, מֶלֶךְ אֲרַם נַהֲרָיִם; וַיַּעַבְדוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-כּוּשַׁן רִשְׁעָתַיִם, שְׁמֹנֶה שָׁנִים.
Jehovah's anger burned against Israel, and he sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim; and the sons of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years.note
La ira de Jehová se encendió contra Israel, y los vendió en manos de Cusán-risataim, rey de Aram-naharaim; y los hijos de Israel sirvieron a Cusán-risataim ocho años.
9
וַיִּזְעֲקוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל-יְהוָה, וַיָּקֶם יְהוָה מוֹשִׁיעַ לִבְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וַיֹּשִׁיעֵם--אֵת עָתְנִיאֵל בֶּן-קְנַז, אֲחִי כָלֵב הַקָּטֹן מִמֶּנּוּ.
The sons of Israel cried to Jehovah, and Jehovah raised up a deliverer for them, who saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.note
Cuando los hijos de Israel clamaron a Jehová, Jehová levantó para ellos un libertador que los salvó: Otniel hijo de Cenaz, hermano menor de Caleb.
10
וַתְּהִי עָלָיו רוּחַ-יְהוָה, וַיִּשְׁפֹּט אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיֵּצֵא לַמִּלְחָמָה, וַיִּתֵּן יְהוָה בְּיָדוֹ אֶת-כּוּשַׁן רִשְׁעָתַיִם מֶלֶךְ אֲרָם; וַתָּעָז יָדוֹ, עַל כּוּשַׁן רִשְׁעָתָיִם.
The spirit of Jehovah came upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and Jehovah gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram into his hand, and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim.note
El espíritu de Jehová vino sobre él, y juzgó a Israel; salió a la guerra, y Jehová entregó en su mano a Cusán-risataim, rey de Aram; y su mano prevaleció sobre Cusán-risataim.
11
וַתִּשְׁקֹט הָאָרֶץ, אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה; וַיָּמָת, עָתְנִיאֵל בֶּן-קְנַז.
The land had rest forty years. Then Othniel son of Kenaz died.note
La tierra reposó cuarenta años. Luego murió Otniel hijo de Cenaz.
12
וַיֹּסִפוּ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, לַעֲשׂוֹת הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה; וַיְחַזֵּק יְהוָה אֶת-עֶגְלוֹן מֶלֶךְ-מוֹאָב, עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, עַל כִּי-עָשׂוּ אֶת-הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
The sons of Israel again did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah; and Jehovah strengthened Eglon king of Moab against Israel, because they had done what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah.note
Los hijos de Israel volvieron a hacer lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová; y Jehová fortaleció a Eglón rey de Moab contra Israel, porque habían hecho lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová.
13
וַיֶּאֱסֹף אֵלָיו, אֶת-בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן וַעֲמָלֵק; וַיֵּלֶךְ, וַיַּךְ אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַיִּירְשׁוּ, אֶת-עִיר הַתְּמָרִים.
He gathered to himself the sons of Ammon and Amalek; he went and struck Israel, and they took the City of Palms.note
Éste reunió consigo a los hijos de Amón y de Amalec; fue, hirió a Israel y tomaron la ciudad de las palmeras.
14
וַיַּעַבְדוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶת-עֶגְלוֹן מֶלֶךְ-מוֹאָב, שְׁמוֹנֶה עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה.
The sons of Israel served Eglon king of Moab eighteen years.note
Los hijos de Israel sirvieron a Eglón rey de Moab dieciocho años.
15
וַיִּזְעֲקוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶל-יְהוָה, וַיָּקֶם יְהוָה לָהֶם מוֹשִׁיעַ אֶת-אֵהוּד בֶּן-גֵּרָא בֶּן-הַיְמִינִי, אִישׁ אִטֵּר יַד-יְמִינוֹ; וַיִּשְׁלְחוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל בְּיָדוֹ מִנְחָה, לְעֶגְלוֹן מֶלֶךְ מוֹאָב.
When the sons of Israel cried to Jehovah, Jehovah raised up for them a deliverer, Ehud son of Gera the Benjaminite, a man left-handed. The sons of Israel sent tribute by his hand to Eglon king of Moab.note
Cuando los hijos de Israel clamaron a Jehová, Jehová les levantó un libertador: Ehúd hijo de Guerá, benjaminita, hombre zurdo. Los hijos de Israel enviaron tributo por medio de él a Eglón rey de Moab.
16
וַיַּעַשׂ לוֹ אֵהוּד חֶרֶב, וְלָהּ שְׁנֵי פֵיוֹת--גֹּמֶד אָרְכָּהּ; וַיַּחְגֹּר אוֹתָהּ מִתַּחַת לְמַדָּיו, עַל יֶרֶךְ יְמִינוֹ.
Ehud made himself a sword with two edges, a cubit long, and strapped it under his clothing on his right thigh.note
Ehúd se hizo una espada de dos filos, de un codo de largo, y se la ciñó bajo la ropa, sobre el muslo derecho.
17
וַיַּקְרֵב, אֶת-הַמִּנְחָה, לְעֶגְלוֹן, מֶלֶךְ מוֹאָב; וְעֶגְלוֹן, אִישׁ בָּרִיא מְאֹד.
He brought the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Now Eglon was a very fat man.note
Llevó el tributo a Eglón rey de Moab. Y Eglón era un hombre muy gordo.
18
וַיְהִי כַּאֲשֶׁר כִּלָּה, לְהַקְרִיב אֶת-הַמִּנְחָה; וַיְשַׁלַּח, אֶת-הָעָם, נֹשְׂאֵי, הַמִּנְחָה.
When he had finished bringing the tribute, he sent away the people who had carried the tribute.note
Cuando terminó de entregar el tributo, despidió a la gente que había llevado el tributo.
19
וְהוּא שָׁב, מִן-הַפְּסִילִים אֲשֶׁר אֶת-הַגִּלְגָּל, וַיֹּאמֶר, דְּבַר-סֵתֶר לִי אֵלֶיךָ הַמֶּלֶךְ; וַיֹּאמֶר הָס--וַיֵּצְאוּ מֵעָלָיו, כָּל-הָעֹמְדִים עָלָיו.
But he himself turned back from the carved stones near Gilgal and said: "I have a secret word for you, O king." And the king said, "Silence!" and all who stood by him went out from him.note
Pero él mismo regresó desde las piedras talladas junto a Guilgal, y dijo: «Tengo una palabra secreta para ti, oh rey». Y el rey dijo: «¡Silencio!». Y todos los que estaban junto a él salieron de su presencia.
20
וְאֵהוּד בָּא אֵלָיו, וְהוּא-יֹשֵׁב בַּעֲלִיַּת הַמְּקֵרָה אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ לְבַדּוֹ, וַיֹּאמֶר אֵהוּד, דְּבַר-אֱלֹהִים לִי אֵלֶיךָ; וַיָּקָם, מֵעַל הַכִּסֵּא.
Ehud came to him while he was sitting alone in his cool upper room. Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you." And he rose from his seat.note
Ehúd llegó a él mientras estaba sentado solo en su aposento de verano. Y Ehúd dijo: «Tengo una palabra de Dios para ti». Y él se levantó de su asiento.
21
וַיִּשְׁלַח אֵהוּד, אֶת-יַד שְׂמֹאלוֹ, וַיִּקַּח אֶת-הַחֶרֶב, מֵעַל יֶרֶךְ יְמִינוֹ; וַיִּתְקָעֶהָ, בְּבִטְנוֹ.
Ehud reached out with his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh, and thrust it into his belly.note
Ehúd extendió su mano izquierda, tomó la espada del muslo derecho y se la hundió en el vientre.
22
וַיָּבֹא גַם-הַנִּצָּב אַחַר הַלַּהַב, וַיִּסְגֹּר הַחֵלֶב בְּעַד הַלַּהַב--כִּי לֹא שָׁלַף הַחֶרֶב, מִבִּטְנוֹ; וַיֵּצֵא, הַפַּרְשְׁדֹנָה.
The hilt went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not draw the sword out of his belly; and the dung came out.note
La empuñadura entró tras la hoja, y la grasa cerró sobre la hoja, porque no sacó la espada de su vientre; y salió el excremento.
23
וַיֵּצֵא אֵהוּד, הַמִּסְדְּרוֹנָה; וַיִּסְגֹּר דַּלְתוֹת הָעֲלִיָּה, בַּעֲדוֹ--וְנָעָל.
Ehud went out into the porch, shut the doors of the upper room behind him, and locked them.note
Entonces Ehúd salió al pórtico, cerró tras de sí las puertas del aposento y les echó llave.
24
וְהוּא יָצָא, וַעֲבָדָיו בָּאוּ, וַיִּרְאוּ, וְהִנֵּה דַּלְתוֹת הָעֲלִיָּה נְעֻלוֹת; וַיֹּאמְרוּ, אַךְ מֵסִיךְ הוּא אֶת-רַגְלָיו בַּחֲדַר הַמְּקֵרָה.
When he had gone out, his servants came, saw that the doors of the upper room were locked, and said, "He must be covering his feet in the closet of the cool room."note
Cuando él salió, llegaron sus siervos, miraron, y he aquí que las puertas del aposento estaban cerradas; y dijeron: «Sin duda está cubriendo sus pies en el aposento fresco».
25
וַיָּחִילוּ עַד-בּוֹשׁ, וְהִנֵּה אֵינֶנּוּ פֹתֵחַ דַּלְתוֹת הָעֲלִיָּה; וַיִּקְחוּ אֶת-הַמַּפְתֵּחַ, וַיִּפְתָּחוּ, וְהִנֵּה אֲדֹנֵיהֶם, נֹפֵל אַרְצָה מֵת.
They waited until they were ashamed; but when he still did not open the doors of the upper room, they took the key and opened them, and there was their lord, fallen dead on the floor.note
Esperaron hasta avergonzarse; pero como él no abría las puertas del aposento, tomaron la llave, abrieron, y he aquí que su señor yacía muerto en el suelo.
26
וְאֵהוּד נִמְלַט, עַד הִתְמַהְמְהָם; וְהוּא עָבַר אֶת-הַפְּסִילִים, וַיִּמָּלֵט הַשְּׂעִירָתָה.
Ehud escaped while they delayed. He passed beyond the carved stones and got away to Seirah.note
Ehúd escapó mientras ellos se demoraban; pasó más allá de las piedras talladas y huyó a Seirá.
27
וַיְהִי בְּבוֹאוֹ, וַיִּתְקַע בַּשּׁוֹפָר בְּהַר אֶפְרָיִם; וַיֵּרְדוּ עִמּוֹ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל מִן-הָהָר, וְהוּא לִפְנֵיהֶם.
When he came, he blew a ram's horn in the hill country of Ephraim, and the sons of Israel came down with him from the hill country, with him at their head.note
Al llegar, tocó el cuerno de carnero en el monte de Efraín; y los hijos de Israel bajaron con él del monte, y él iba delante de ellos.
28
וַיֹּאמֶר אֲלֵהֶם רִדְפוּ אַחֲרַי, כִּי-נָתַן יְהוָה אֶת-אֹיְבֵיכֶם אֶת-מוֹאָב בְּיֶדְכֶם; וַיֵּרְדוּ אַחֲרָיו, וַיִּלְכְּדוּ אֶת-מַעְבְּרוֹת הַיַּרְדֵּן לְמוֹאָב, וְלֹא-נָתְנוּ אִישׁ, לַעֲבֹר.
He said to them, "Follow me, for Jehovah has given your enemies the Moabites into your hand." They went down after him, seized the fords of the Jordan against Moab, and let no one cross.note
Y les dijo: «Síganme, porque Jehová ha entregado en sus manos a sus enemigos los moabitas». Descendieron tras él, tomaron los vados del Jordán frente a Moab, y no dejaron pasar a nadie.
29
וַיַּכּוּ אֶת-מוֹאָב בָּעֵת הַהִיא, כַּעֲשֶׂרֶת אֲלָפִים אִישׁ--כָּל-שָׁמֵן, וְכָל-אִישׁ חָיִל; וְלֹא נִמְלַט, אִישׁ.
They struck down about ten thousand Moabites at that time, every strong man and every man of valor; not one escaped.note
Hirieron entonces a unos diez mil moabitas, todos hombres robustos y valientes; no escapó ni uno.
30

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · the nations left on purpose, and the marriages that followed

The chapter opens by explaining the chapter before it. The nations of verse 1 are the ones Judges 2:21–23 (already on these pages) said Jehovah would leave, and the reason is given twice: to test Israel, and, in verse 2, so that the later generations would learn war. The Hebrew of verse 2 is rak, "only," followed by a purpose clause, and the translation keeps the purpose before the verb of learning.

"To test Israel by them." Lenasot bam et-Yisrael is the phrase from Judges 2:22, repeated word for word. The shelf is consistent on it: the NWT 1984 reads "so as by them to test Israel", and the ASV reads "to prove Israel by them". The translation keeps the same English verb both times, so a reader sees the echo.

The list is the Philistines and the northern peoples. Verse 3 names five Philistine lords (sarnei, a word for the rulers of the Philistine cities), then the Canaanites and Sidonians and Hivites up to the entrance of Hamath. The five lords are the rulers of the Philistine cities, the same people whose cities the chapter before has already named.

A verse about marriage that is also about religion. Verse 6 says they took their daughters as wives and gave their own daughters to their sons, and then the verb is vayaavdu, "served", with their gods as object. The translation keeps the two actions in one sentence, because the Hebrew does.

Verses 7–11 · Baal, the Asherahs, and the first judge

The Asherahs are named beside the Baals. Ha-asherot is the plural of asherah, a cult object or goddess connected with the Canaanite sanctuaries, and the Hebrew puts it with the Baals as the object of service. The ASV prints "the Asheroth", a transliteration. This translation keeps "Asherahs," the same kind of transliteration, since the word is a name in the Hebrew, not an ordinary noun.

"Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim." The name is the first foreign oppressor in the book. Its Hebrew is Kushan Rishatayim, usually read as "Cushan of double wickedness", and the translation keeps the name whole, because a reader who meets it again in verse 10 should recognise it. The shelf turns the place name into a region: the NWT 1984 gives "Mesopotamia", the ASV also gives "Mesopotamia", and the KJV keeps "Mesopotamia" too. The Hebrew says Aram Naharayim, "Aram of the two rivers", and the translation prints the Hebrew name as the Hebrew has it.

The first judge is the Othniel of Judges 1. The text names him as Judges 1:13 does (already on these pages): Caleb's younger brother, the man who took Kiriath-sepher. The TNM 2019 in verse 10 names him in the sentence, "El espíritu de Jehová vino sobre Otniel"; the Hebrew says only "the spirit of Jehovah came upon him", so the name is the edition's addition. The translation keeps "him."

Forty years of rest. Verse 11 gives the land rest forty years. The chapter's later rests are eighty years (verse 30) and the oppressions eight and eighteen years (verses 8 and 14).

Verses 12–14 · Eglon, Moab, and the City of Palms

The cycle begins again with the same sentence. Verse 12 repeats the wording of Judges 3:7 and 2:11: "did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah." The verb vayosifu means "they added to", so the people did the evil again, and the verse is the book's usual repetition, now with a new oppressor. The ASV reads "again", and the translation keeps that, which is the verb's sense.

"Eglon king of Moab." Eglon is a king's name here, and the translation gives it as a name.

"The City of Palms." Ir ha-temarim is the name Judges 1:16 gives to the place the Kenites came from, and the translation keeps the same English in both places. The ASV gives "city of palm-trees", and the translation uses the form of Judges 1:16.

Verses 15–18 · Ehud, the left hand, and the tribute

"A man left-handed." Ish itter yad yemino is a phrase that reads literally as "a man restrained in his right hand", and the Hebrew is ambiguous: the rare word itter is read as restrained or bound in the right hand, which makes the man left-handed. The NWT 2013 reads "left-handed", and the translation follows that reading.

"Tribute." Minchah is the same word as the grain offering of Leviticus. Here it is what the people send to the king, and the translation keeps "tribute" so that the reader does not take the word as worship. The ASV has "tribute" and the KJV "present"; the translation uses the first.

The sword has two edges. Verse 16 gives its length as a cubit, and the translation keeps the measure. The sword is hidden under his clothing on the right thigh, which is where verse 21 will draw it from.

"Now Eglon was a very fat man." The Hebrew bari me'od, "very fat," is blunt, and the verse is blunt about it. The English keeps the bluntness because the story depends on it: the sword goes into fat that closes over the blade in verse 22.

Verses 19–26 · the upper room and the locked doors

"The carved stones near Gilgal." Ha-pesilim asher et-ha-Gilgal is the phrase the translation has to decide on. Pesilim means carved images, and outside this chapter the word names images of gods (Isaiah 42:8 and 2 Chronicles 33:22, neither yet on these pages), so the reader expects a cult object; here it is a place near Gilgal, a quarry or a row of carved stones. The shelf splits three ways: the ASV reads "quarries", the NWT 2013 "carved images", and the TNM 2019 "imágenes talladas". The translation keeps "carved stones", which is the nearest reading that does not assert an idol.

"A message from God." Ehud's line in verse 20 is devar Elohim, which can mean a word from God or a message from God. The KJV keeps "a message from God." The translation keeps the same, because the lie is in the phrase and the reader should be able to hear it.

The fat closed over the blade. Verse 22 says the hilt went in after the blade, that the fat closed over it, and that he did not draw the sword out. The word parshedonah, which the translation renders "dung", occurs only in this verse in the Hebrew Bible, so its meaning is not settled by any other use. The old readings take it as dung, which is what the NWT 1984 ("fecal matter") and the TNM 2019 ("excremento") both do. The translation says "dung," and flags the word as unique here.

The servants and the locked doors. Verse 24 has the servants guess that their lord is "covering his feet" in the closet. The phrase is a euphemism for relieving oneself, and the translation keeps it, because the joke depends on the servants' delicacy: they wait until they are ashamed (verse 25) before they open the door.

Verses 27–30 · the fords of the Jordan

A horn in the hill country. The shofar is a ram's horn, and the translation keeps "ram's horn" where the Hebrew names the animal. The ASV prints "trumpet"; the translation keeps the animal, because it is what the instrument is.

"Let no one cross." Verse 28 says they seized the fords of the Jordan against Moab and lo natnu ish laavor, "they did not let a man pass over". The translation keeps the literal sense, so the reader sees that the victory is a blockade rather than a battle.

Ten thousand, and every man of valor. The number in verse 29 is the same round number as the other large figures in the book, and the verse says the dead were "every strong man and every man of valor." The translation keeps the parallel, because the verse is a count of the whole fighting force, not a sample.

Verse 31 · Shamgar, six hundred, and an oxgoad

The shortest judgeship in the book. Shamgar has no tribute, no rest, and no years given. Verse 31 says only that he struck down six hundred Philistines and "also saved Israel." The translation keeps the brevity, because the Hebrew gives no more.

An oxgoad. Malmad ha-baqar is the long stick used to drive oxen, and the translation keeps "oxgoad", the word the KJV and the ASV use. The weapon is an agricultural tool, and the verse gives no more than that.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: "to test Israel by them" for lenasot, the same phrase as Judges 2:22, kept identical so the echo is audible; "Cushan-rishathaim" kept whole, because the name is the first oppressor and should be recognised; "tribute" for minchah, not "offering", so the reader does not take the king's tax for worship; "carved stones" for pesilim, the nearest reading that does not assert an idol; and "dung" for parshedonah, flagged as a word that occurs only here.

Echoes paid. The formula "did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah" returns in verse 12 with a new oppressor, as Judges 2 said it would. The "test" of Judges 2:22 is paid in verse 4. The "left in the land" nations of Judges 2:21 are the ones this chapter lists in verses 3 and 5. Othniel, the first judge, is the man who took Achsah, Caleb's daughter, in Judges 1:13.

Echoes planted. Ehud's sword is hidden on the right thigh, and verse 21 draws it from there. The fords of the Jordan in verse 28 are the place where the victory is held.
