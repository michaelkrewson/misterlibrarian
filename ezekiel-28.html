Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–6 · Ezekiel 28:1–6 the leader of Tyre

⭐ Leader. Nagid, the word used when Saul is to be anointed (1 Samuel 9:16), not the ‘prince’ of other chapters. The KJV has “prince”, the NIV “ruler”. The chapter gives no date.

⭐ A heart grown high. ‘Haughty’ is the verb used of Sodom and her daughters at 16:50. It comes again in verses 5 and 17.

⚠ Two words for God. ‘I am a god’ and ‘not God’ use El; ‘the seat of God’ and ‘the heart of God’ use Elohim, which can also mean ‘gods’. Hebrew has no capital letters, and the versions choose differently. The boast ends at ‘the heart of the seas’, where 27:4 sets Tyre’s borders.

⚠ Daniel. The text writes the name one way and reads it another (ketiv/qere); the written form is the one met at 14:14. The NIV turns the line into a question, “Are you wiser than Daniel?” The verb behind ‘is hidden’ is not certain; it returns at 31:8 (now on these pages).

⭐ Wealth and merchandise. The words of 26:12. Here his wisdom gets the wealth, and the wealth lifts his heart. In verse 4, ‘brought’ is the same verb as ‘made’.

Verses 7–10 · Ezekiel 28:7–10 strangers with drawn swords

⭐ Strangers. The most ruthless of the nations draw swords against ‘the beauty of your wisdom’. Yifah, ‘radiance’, here and in verse 17, is not the ‘splendor’ of 27:10.

⚠ The pit. Shachat, as at 19:4, not the bor of 26:20. ‘Deaths’ is plural in verses 8 and 10.

⚠ Slain and profaned. One root can mean to pierce or to profane: the ‘slain’ of verse 8 and ‘profane’ of verses 7, 9, 16 and 18. As pointed, verse 9 is ‘those who profane you’, and the translation follows. The KJV has “him that slayeth thee”. There the boast is ‘I am God’ with Elohim, not the El of verse 2.

⭐ The uncircumcised. The word is arel, used of the Philistines in the stories of Samson and Saul.

Verses 11–13 · Ezekiel 28:11–13 in Eden, the garden of God

⭐ A second word. A dirge, now over the ‘king’ of Tyre. ‘Perfect in beauty’ takes up Tyre’s boast of 27:3.

⚠ The seal. Chotem tokhnit, ‘seal of the pattern’, is hard: as pointed the first word is ‘sealing’, and the translation reads the noun ‘seal’; tokhnit returns for the pattern of the house at 43:10. The KJV has “Thou sealest up the sum”, the NIV “the seal of perfection”.

⭐ Eden. The garden of Genesis 2:8; ‘garden of God’ comes again at 31:8 (now on these pages).

⭐ The stones. The nine stones named all stand on the high priest’s breastpiece (Exodus 28:17–20), which has twelve; the three of its third row are not here. The versions name them differently; this translation keeps the names used at Exodus. Tarshish here is a stone, the ‘beryl’ of 1:16.

⚠ Timbrels and sockets. The words are uncertain. ‘Covering’ has the root of ‘screens’ in verse 14. The KJV has “thy tabrets and of thy pipes”, the NIV “Your settings and mountings”.

Verses 14–16 · Ezekiel 28:14–16 the cherub and the stones of fire

⭐ Cherub. Keruv, the guard of Genesis 3:24. ‘Screens’ is the verb of the cherubim over the cover of the ark (Exodus 25:20).

⚠ Hard words. The opening ‘you’ is spelled like a word for ‘with’, and mimshach, here ‘anointed’, stands only in this verse. The Douay has “stretched out”.

⭐ Stones of fire. The words avne esh stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, verses 14 and 16. They are not the ‘fiery stones’ of Isaiah 54:12, which are another phrase.

⭐ Blameless. The word used of Noah (Genesis 6:9); and the violence that fills him is the word of Genesis 6:11. ‘They filled’ is plural; no subject is named.

⚠ Who is spoken to. The passage names its subject: the king of Tyre (verse 12). It speaks of him with the garden, the cherub and the mountain of God. Many readers have taken it to tell also of a fall of Satan; the verses themselves do not name Satan.

Verses 17–19 · Ezekiel 28:17–19 ashes on the ground

⭐ Beauty and radiance. What made him haughty is what ruined his wisdom. ‘Ruined’ is the root shachat, printed ‘destroy’ at 26:4. ‘Made you perish’ (verse 16) is the verb of 25:16.

⚠ Tense. The verbs are past in form. The KJV turns some of them into the future (“I will cast thee to the ground”).

⭐ Sanctuaries. Plural: by his injustice in trade he profaned them. The fire comes out of his own midst, and he ends as ashes.

⭐ The close. The words of 27:35 and 36, said over Tyre, are said again over its king.

Verses 20–24 · Ezekiel 28:20–24 against Sidon

⭐ Sidon. Tyre’s neighbour, whose men rowed for her (27:8). ‘I am against you’ was said to Tyre (26:3).

⭐ Glory and holiness. ‘I will get glory’ is the verb spoken over Pharaoh (Exodus 14:4); being shown holy and glorified stand together at Leviticus 10:3. The speech turns from ‘you’ to ‘her’ in the middle of verse 22. In verse 23 the word for ‘will fall’ has an unusual form; the KJV has “shall be judged”.

⭐ Two thorns. The verse has two words, printed alike here: sillon, met in the plural at 2:6, and qots, the thorns of Genesis 3:18. ‘Malignant’ is the word of the skin disease at Leviticus 13:52. Israel was warned of thorns in its sides at Numbers 33:55, in other words.

⭐ Despise. The root printed ‘scorn’ at 25:6.

Verses 25–26 · Ezekiel 28:25–26 Israel dwells securely

⭐ A turn. After the words on the neighbours, a word for Israel, with the gathering of 11:17 and 20:41.

⭐ Jacob’s ground. 37:25 speaks of ‘the land that I gave to my servant Jacob’; the word here is ‘ground’.

⭐ Houses and vineyards. Compare Jeremiah 29:5, where the exiles are told to build houses and plant gardens in Babylon. ‘Securely’ is said twice; compare 34:25 and 28.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “haughty” at 2, 5 and 17; “merchandise” at 5, 16 and 18; “radiance” at 7 and 17; “stones of fire” at 14 and 16; and “they will know that I am” at 22, 23, 24 and 26.

Echoes paid. The Daniel of 14:14; the heart of the seas of 27:4; the close of 27:36.

Echoes planted. The garden of God again at 31:8 (now on these pages); Pharaoh next, at 29:3 (now on these pages).

Ezekiel 29, now on these pages: against Pharaoh and Egypt.
