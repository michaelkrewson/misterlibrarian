🕰
Moses continues the review with the second and last Transjordan king, Og of Bashan, defeated in a battle quoted almost verbatim from Numbers 21. Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh are granted the conquered territory east of the Jordan, on condition that their armed men cross first to help take the rest; Moses then recounts his own plea to cross the Jordan himself, its refusal, and the charge he receives to strengthen Joshua for the crossing instead.
Same setting as chapters 1-2 — the plains of Moab, 'in the fortieth year' (1:3) — recounting the conquest of Bashan, which itself took place shortly before this address, in the final weeks of the fortieth year, on the early-date chronology this project foregrounds (c. 1406 BC).

Deuteronomy 3 — The Mister Translation

1
וַנֵּפֶן וַנַּעַל, דֶּרֶךְ הַבָּשָׁן; וַיֵּצֵא עוֹג מֶלֶךְ-הַבָּשָׁן לִקְרָאתֵנוּ הוּא וְכָל-עַמּוֹ, לַמִּלְחָמָה--אֶדְרֶעִי.
Then we turned and went up by the way of Bashan. And Og the king of Bashan came out against us, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei.note
Entonces nos volvimos y subimos por el camino de Basán. Y Og, el rey de Basán, salió a nuestro encuentro, él y todo su pueblo, para pelear en Edrei.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֵלַי, אַל-תִּירָא אֹתוֹ--כִּי בְיָדְךָ נָתַתִּי אֹתוֹ וְאֶת-כָּל-עַמּוֹ, וְאֶת-אַרְצוֹ; וְעָשִׂיתָ לּוֹ--כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ לְסִיחֹן מֶלֶךְ הָאֱמֹרִי, אֲשֶׁר יוֹשֵׁב בְּחֶשְׁבּוֹן.
And Jehovah said to me, ‘Do not fear him, for I have given him and all his people and his land into your hand, and you shall do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who dwelt in Heshbon.’note
Y Jehová me dijo: «No le tengas miedo, porque he entregado en tu mano a él, a todo su pueblo y su tierra, y le harás tal como hiciste a Sehón, rey de los amorreos, que habitaba en Hesbón».
3
וַיִּתֵּן יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ בְּיָדֵנוּ, גַּם אֶת-עוֹג מֶלֶךְ-הַבָּשָׁן--וְאֶת-כָּל-עַמּוֹ; וַנַּכֵּהוּ, עַד-בִּלְתִּי הִשְׁאִיר-לוֹ שָׂרִיד.
So Jehovah our God gave into our hand Og also, the king of Bashan, and all his people, and we struck him down until he had no survivor remaining.note
Y Jehová nuestro Dios entregó también en nuestra mano a Og, el rey de Basán, y a todo su pueblo, y lo derrotamos hasta no dejarle sobreviviente alguno.
4
וַנִּלְכֹּד אֶת-כָּל-עָרָיו, בָּעֵת הַהִוא--לֹא הָיְתָה קִרְיָה, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-לָקַחְנוּ מֵאִתָּם: שִׁשִּׁים עִיר כָּל-חֶבֶל אַרְגֹּב, מַמְלֶכֶת עוֹג בַּבָּשָׁן.
And we captured all his cities at that time. There was not a city that we did not take from them — sixty cities, all the region of Argob, the kingdom of Og in Bashan.note
Y tomamos todas sus ciudades en aquel tiempo. No hubo ciudad que no tomáramos de ellos — sesenta ciudades, toda la región de Argob, el reino de Og en Basán.
5
כָּל-אֵלֶּה עָרִים בְּצֻרֹת, חוֹמָה גְבֹהָה--דְּלָתַיִם וּבְרִיחַ: לְבַד מֵעָרֵי הַפְּרָזִי, הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד.
All these were fortified cities, with high walls, gates, and bars, besides a great many unwalled towns.note
Todas estas eran ciudades fortificadas, con muros altos, puertas y barras, aparte de muchísimos pueblos sin muro.
6
וַנַּחֲרֵם אוֹתָם--כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂינוּ, לְסִיחֹן מֶלֶךְ חֶשְׁבּוֹן: הַחֲרֵם כָּל-עִיר מְתִם, הַנָּשִׁים וְהַטָּף.
And we devoted them to destruction, as we did to Sihon king of Heshbon, devoting every city to destruction — the men, the women, and the children.note
Y las destruimos por completo, tal como hicimos con Sehón, rey de Hesbón, destruyendo por completo cada ciudad — los hombres, las mujeres y los niños.
7
וְכָל-הַבְּהֵמָה וּשְׁלַל הֶעָרִים, בַּזּוֹנוּ לָנוּ.
But all the livestock and the spoil of the cities we took as plunder for ourselves.note
Pero todo el ganado y el despojo de las ciudades lo tomamos como botín para nosotros.
8
וַנִּקַּח בָּעֵת הַהִוא, אֶת-הָאָרֶץ, מִיַּד שְׁנֵי מַלְכֵי הָאֱמֹרִי, אֲשֶׁר בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן--מִנַּחַל אַרְנֹן, עַד-הַר חֶרְמוֹן.
So we took the land at that time out of the hand of the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan, from the brook Arnon to Mount Hermon—note
Así tomamos la tierra en aquel tiempo de mano de los dos reyes de los amorreos que estaban al otro lado del Jordán, desde el arroyo Arnón hasta el monte Hermon—
9
צִידֹנִים יִקְרְאוּ לְחֶרְמוֹן, שִׂרְיֹן; וְהָאֱמֹרִי, יִקְרְאוּ-לוֹ שְׂנִיר.
(the Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir) —note
(los sidonios llaman a Hermon Sirión, y los amorreos lo llaman Senir) —
10
כֹּל עָרֵי הַמִּישֹׁר, וְכָל-הַגִּלְעָד וְכָל-הַבָּשָׁן, עַד-סַלְכָה, וְאֶדְרֶעִי--עָרֵי מַמְלֶכֶת עוֹג, בַּבָּשָׁן.
all the cities of the tableland, and all Gilead, and all Bashan, as far as Salcah and Edrei, cities of the kingdom of Og in Bashan.note
todas las ciudades de la meseta, y todo Galaad, y todo Basán, hasta Salcá y Edrei, ciudades del reino de Og en Basán.
11
כִּי רַק-עוֹג מֶלֶךְ הַבָּשָׁן, נִשְׁאַר מִיֶּתֶר הָרְפָאִים--הִנֵּה עַרְשׂוֹ עֶרֶשׂ בַּרְזֶל, הֲלֹה הִוא בְּרַבַּת בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן: תֵּשַׁע אַמּוֹת אָרְכָּהּ, וְאַרְבַּע אַמּוֹת רָחְבָּהּ--בְּאַמַּת-אִישׁ.
For only Og king of Bashan remained of the remnant of the Rephaim. Look — his bed was a bed of iron. Is it not in Rabbah of the sons of Ammon? Nine cubits was its length, and four cubits its width, by the cubit of a man.note
Porque solo Og, rey de Basán, quedaba del resto de los refaítas. Mira — su cama era una cama de hierro. ¿No está en Rabá de los hijos de Amón? Nueve codos era su longitud, y cuatro codos su anchura, según el codo de un hombre.
12
וְאֶת-הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת יָרַשְׁנוּ, בָּעֵת הַהִוא; מֵעֲרֹעֵר אֲשֶׁר-עַל-נַחַל אַרְנֹן, וַחֲצִי הַר-הַגִּלְעָד וְעָרָיו--נָתַתִּי, לָראוּבֵנִי וְלַגָּדִי.
And this land we took in possession at that time: from Aroer, which is by the brook Arnon, and half the hill country of Gilead, with its cities, I gave to the Reubenites and the Gadites.note
Y esta tierra tomamos en posesión en aquel tiempo: desde Aroer, que está junto al arroyo Arnón, y la mitad de la región montañosa de Galaad, con sus ciudades, di a los rubenitas y a los gaditas.
13
וְיֶתֶר הַגִּלְעָד וְכָל-הַבָּשָׁן, מַמְלֶכֶת עוֹג--נָתַתִּי, לַחֲצִי שֵׁבֶט הַמְנַשֶּׁה: כֹּל חֶבֶל הָאַרְגֹּב לְכָל-הַבָּשָׁן, הַהוּא יִקָּרֵא אֶרֶץ רְפָאִים.
And the rest of Gilead, and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og, I gave to the half tribe of Manasseh — all the region of Argob, all that Bashan, which used to be called the land of the Rephaim.note
Y el resto de Galaad, y todo Basán, el reino de Og, di a la media tribu de Manasés — toda la región de Argob, todo aquel Basán, que solía llamarse la tierra de los refaítas.
14
יָאִיר בֶּן-מְנַשֶּׁה, לָקַח אֶת-כָּל-חֶבֶל אַרְגֹּב, עַד-גְּבוּל הַגְּשׁוּרִי, וְהַמַּעֲכָתִי; וַיִּקְרָא אֹתָם עַל-שְׁמוֹ אֶת-הַבָּשָׁן חַוֺּת יָאִיר, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
Jair son of Manasseh took all the region of Argob, as far as the border of the Geshurites and the Maacathites, and called Bashan by his own name, Havvoth-jair, to this day.note
Jair, hijo de Manasés, tomó toda la región de Argob, hasta el límite de los guesuritas y los maacatitas, y llamó a Basán por su propio nombre, Havot-jaír, hasta hoy.
15
וּלְמָכִיר, נָתַתִּי אֶת-הַגִּלְעָד.
And to Machir I gave Gilead.note
Y a Maquir di Galaad.
16
וְלָראוּבֵנִי וְלַגָּדִי נָתַתִּי מִן-הַגִּלְעָד, וְעַד-נַחַל אַרְנֹן, תּוֹךְ הַנַּחַל, וּגְבֻל--וְעַד יַבֹּק הַנַּחַל, גְּבוּל בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן.
And to the Reubenites and the Gadites I gave from Gilead as far as the brook Arnon, the middle of the brook being the border, as far as the Jabbok, the brook that is the border of the sons of Ammon;note
Y a los rubenitas y a los gaditas di desde Galaad hasta el arroyo Arnón, con el medio del arroyo como límite, hasta el Jaboc, el arroyo que es el límite de los hijos de Amón;
17
וְהָעֲרָבָה, וְהַיַּרְדֵּן וּגְבֻל--מִכִּנֶּרֶת, וְעַד יָם הָעֲרָבָה יָם הַמֶּלַח, תַּחַת אַשְׁדֹּת הַפִּסְגָּה, מִזְרָחָה.
the Arabah also, with the Jordan as the border, from Chinnereth as far as the sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, under the slopes of Pisgah eastward.note
el Arabá también, con el Jordán como límite, desde Cineret hasta el mar del Arabá, el mar Salado, bajo las laderas del Pisgá hacia el oriente.
18
וָאֲצַו אֶתְכֶם, בָּעֵת הַהִוא לֵאמֹר: יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, נָתַן לָכֶם אֶת-הָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת לְרִשְׁתָּהּ--חֲלוּצִים תַּעַבְרוּ לִפְנֵי אֲחֵיכֶם בְּנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, כָּל-בְּנֵי-חָיִל.
And I commanded you at that time, saying, ‘Jehovah your God has given you this land to possess it. You shall cross over armed before your brothers, the sons of Israel, all the men of valor.note
Y les mandé en aquel tiempo, diciendo: «Jehová su Dios les ha dado esta tierra para que la posean. Cruzarán armados delante de sus hermanos, los hijos de Israel, todos los hombres valientes.
19
רַק נְשֵׁיכֶם וְטַפְּכֶם, וּמִקְנֵכֶם, יָדַעְתִּי, כִּי-מִקְנֶה רַב לָכֶם--יֵשְׁבוּ, בְּעָרֵיכֶם, אֲשֶׁר נָתַתִּי, לָכֶם.
Only your wives, and your children, and your livestock — I know that you have much livestock — shall remain in your cities that I have given you,note
Solo sus esposas, y sus niños, y su ganado —yo sé que tienen mucho ganado— se quedarán en las ciudades que les he dado,
20
עַד אֲשֶׁר-יָנִיחַ יְהוָה לַאֲחֵיכֶם, כָּכֶם, וְיָרְשׁוּ גַם-הֵם, אֶת-הָאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם נֹתֵן לָהֶם בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן; וְשַׁבְתֶּם, אִישׁ לִירֻשָּׁתוֹ, אֲשֶׁר נָתַתִּי, לָכֶם.
until Jehovah gives rest to your brothers, as he has to you, and they also possess the land that Jehovah your God is giving them beyond the Jordan; then you shall return, each man to his possession, which I have given you.’note
hasta que Jehová dé descanso a sus hermanos, como se lo ha dado a ustedes, y ellos también posean la tierra que Jehová su Dios les da al otro lado del Jordán; entonces volverán, cada uno a su posesión, que les he dado».
21
וְאֶת-יְהוֹשׁוּעַ צִוֵּיתִי, בָּעֵת הַהִוא לֵאמֹר: עֵינֶיךָ הָרֹאֹת, אֵת כָּל-אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם לִשְׁנֵי הַמְּלָכִים הָאֵלֶּה--כֵּן-יַעֲשֶׂה יְהוָה לְכָל-הַמַּמְלָכוֹת, אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה עֹבֵר שָׁמָּה.
And I commanded Joshua at that time, saying, ‘Your eyes have seen all that Jehovah your God has done to these two kings. So will Jehovah do to all the kingdoms into which you are crossing.note
Y mandé a Josué en aquel tiempo, diciendo: «Tus ojos han visto todo lo que Jehová su Dios ha hecho a estos dos reyes. Así hará Jehová a todos los reinos por donde vas a pasar.
22
לֹא, תִּירָאוּם: כִּי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, הוּא הַנִּלְחָם לָכֶם.
You shall not fear them, for Jehovah your God, he is the one fighting for you.’note
No les tengan miedo, porque Jehová su Dios es quien pelea por ustedes».
23
וָאֶתְחַנַּן, אֶל-יְהוָה, בָּעֵת הַהִוא, לֵאמֹר.
And I pleaded with Jehovah at that time, saying,note
Y supliqué a Jehová en aquel tiempo, diciendo:
24
אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה, אַתָּה הַחִלּוֹתָ לְהַרְאוֹת אֶת-עַבְדְּךָ, אֶת-גָּדְלְךָ, וְאֶת-יָדְךָ הַחֲזָקָה--אֲשֶׁר מִי-אֵל בַּשָּׁמַיִם וּבָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר-יַעֲשֶׂה כְמַעֲשֶׂיךָ וְכִגְבוּרֹתֶךָ.
‘O Lord Jehovah, you have begun to show your servant your greatness and your strong hand — for what god is there in heaven or on earth who can do deeds like yours, and mighty acts like yours?note
«Oh Señor Jehová, tú has comenzado a mostrarle a tu siervo tu grandeza y tu mano fuerte — porque ¿qué dios hay en el cielo o en la tierra que pueda hacer obras como las tuyas, y proezas como las tuyas?
25
אֶעְבְּרָה-נָּא, וְאֶרְאֶה אֶת-הָאָרֶץ הַטּוֹבָה, אֲשֶׁר, בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן: הָהָר הַטּוֹב הַזֶּה, וְהַלְּבָנֹן.
Let me cross over, please, and see the good land that is beyond the Jordan, this good hill country, and Lebanon.’note
Déjame cruzar, por favor, y ver la buena tierra que está al otro lado del Jordán, esta buena región montañosa, y el Líbano».
26
וַיִּתְעַבֵּר יְהוָה בִּי לְמַעַנְכֶם, וְלֹא שָׁמַע אֵלָי; וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֵלַי, רַב-לָךְ--אַל-תּוֹסֶף דַּבֵּר אֵלַי עוֹד, בַּדָּבָר הַזֶּה.
But Jehovah was angry with me for your sakes, and would not listen to me, and Jehovah said to me, ‘Enough from you — never speak to me again of this matter.note
Pero Jehová se enojó conmigo por causa de ustedes, y no me escuchó, y Jehová me dijo: «Basta ya —nunca me vuelvas a hablar de este asunto.
27
עֲלֵה רֹאשׁ הַפִּסְגָּה, וְשָׂא עֵינֶיךָ יָמָּה וְצָפֹנָה וְתֵימָנָה וּמִזְרָחָה--וּרְאֵה בְעֵינֶיךָ: כִּי-לֹא תַעֲבֹר, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן הַזֶּה.
Go up to the top of Pisgah, and lift up your eyes westward, and northward, and southward, and eastward, and see it with your eyes, for you shall not cross over this Jordan.note
Sube a la cima del Pisgá, y alza tus ojos hacia el occidente, y el norte, y el sur, y el oriente, y mírala con tus ojos, porque no cruzarás este Jordán.
28
וְצַו אֶת-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, וְחַזְּקֵהוּ וְאַמְּצֵהוּ: כִּי-הוּא יַעֲבֹר, לִפְנֵי הָעָם הַזֶּה, וְהוּא יַנְחִיל אוֹתָם, אֶת-הָאָרֶץ אֲשֶׁר תִּרְאֶה.
But charge Joshua, and encourage him, and strengthen him, for he shall cross over before this people, and he shall give them the land that you see to inherit.’note

Translator's Notes

Verses 1–3 · Og comes out to battle — and the retelling quotes rather than narrates

★ This chapter does not paraphrase Numbers — for three verses it quotes it, almost word for word. Compare Numbers 21:33-35 (already on these pages): “And they turned and went up by the way of Bashan, and Og king of Bashan went out against them, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei. And Jehovah said to Moses, Do not fear him, for I have given him into your hand…” Deuteronomy 3:1-2 repeats the same verb, the same place names, the same divine speech — the only change is grammatical person: Numbers’ third-person “they” and “against them” become Moses’ own first-person “we” and “against us,” and “to Moses” drops out because Moses is the one now speaking. This is tighter than 2:1’s own citation of Numbers 14:25 — there the verb forms shifted; here whole clauses survive unchanged. ⚠ Worth noticing what the retelling does with SPACE, not just words: Numbers gives Sihon twelve verses and Og three, both inside one chapter (21:21-35); Deuteronomy gives Sihon a full chapter and now gives Og one of his own. Expansion, not compression, is this book’s method.

“Also” (v3, gam) does real work. Jehovah gave Sihon into Israel’s hand in the last chapter; here he gives Og ‘also’ — the same conjunction the Sihon entry already calls out: the two kings “become a standing pair in Israel’s liturgy of remembrance.” Nothing in this chapter has to argue for the pairing; the single word does it.

Verses 4–7 · sixty walled cities, and the same verb used on Sihon

Where Sihon’s war stayed vague, Og’s is counted. Sixty fortified cities, “with high walls, gates, and bars,” a whole named region — Argob — “besides a great many unwalled towns.” Deuteronomy rarely gives an enemy this much specificity; Argob will recur twice more in this very chapter (vv13-14, see n12) as the region Jair conquers and renames, so the number seeded here is not decoration — it is the object the rest of the chapter keeps returning to.

“As we did to Sihon king of Heshbon” (v6) makes explicit what 2:34’s own cherem left implicit. The two wars are not merely similar in outcome; the text says so, in so many words, inside the account itself — the second king is destroyed BECAUSE that is what was already done to the first.

Verses 8–11 · a mountain with three names, and the last of the giants

★ The mountain has three names, one per nation that named it — the same device this book has just spent a whole chapter running on a PEOPLE, now run on a PLACE. “The Sidonians call Hermon Sirion, and the Amorites call it Senir” (v9) is 2:11’s Rephaim/Emim/Zamzummim pattern in miniature — three peoples, three labels, one referent — except there the category was giants and the parenthetical explained a PEOPLE’s vanishing; here it explains nothing but naming itself. Hermon keeps its own name in this translation, as Rephaim did, because the shelf’s three names for the same summit are not a puzzle to be resolved but a fact about how many nations bordered it.

⚠ “His bed was a bed of iron” (v11) is where the shelf actually disagrees, not just on wording but on what kind of object this is. KJV, RV 1909 and RV60 all resolve Rephaim to ‘giants’/‘gigantes’ here, exactly as they did at 2:11; ASV, the NWT and the TNM keep it transliterated, exactly as this translation does — the identical split, verified fresh rather than assumed to carry over. The stranger divergence is the OBJECT itself: the NWT 1984 renders eresh ‘his BIER,’ not his bed — eresh is an ordinary word for a couch elsewhere (Psalm 6:6, Amos 6:4), and ‘bier’ quietly imports a funerary reading the Hebrew does not itself require. The NIV hedges the other way, translating min-barzel not as the bed’s material but its trim — ‘decorated with iron’ — which reads like an attempt to make a thirteen-foot solid-iron bedframe less physically absurd. This translation keeps it flat: a bed of iron, full stop, and lets the note carry what the shelf is actually negotiating — whether the object is a real bed, a black-basalt sarcophagus mistaken for metal by its colour, or a dolmen, a debate already flagged in this project’s own Og entry and not settled here either. Nine cubits by four is, on the common eighteen-inch cubit, roughly four metres long and under two wide — large by any accounting, whatever it was.

★ “The last of the Rephaim” closes a thread that has run since Genesis. Genesis 14:5 (already on these pages) named the Rephaim among the peoples Chedorlaomer’s coalition struck in Abram’s own lifetime; Deuteronomy 2:11, 20 traced the same umbrella name to two more nations, under two more local labels. Og is not merely another casualty — he is stated outright to be the LAST one, the final bearer of a name that has been outliving the people it covers since before Israel existed. The category survives the man by exactly as long as this verse.

Verses 12–17 · the allotment, and a refrain that reaches a third name

The allotment formalizes a conquest Numbers already narrated informally. Numbers 32:39-42 (already on these pages) has Machir’s clan simply GO and TAKE Gilead — only afterward does Numbers add one clipped sentence, ‘Moses gave Gilead to Machir,’ nothing like the elaborate covenant Reuben and Gad struck a few verses earlier in that same chapter. Jair and Nobah, capturing and renaming their own conquests two verses later, don’t even get that much — Numbers never mentions Moses for either one. Here Moses issues the whole allotment BY LAW, retroactively: Reuben and Gad take the southern half (from Aroer to half of Gilead), and the rest of Gilead with all Bashan — Og’s whole kingdom, Argob’s sixty cities included — goes to the half tribe of Manasseh. The facts on the ground do not change; only the voice that authorizes them does, the same retelling habit n18 traces in the very next verses.

★ “To this day” (v14) is this book’s refrain reaching a third link. Genesis 19:37-38 closed Moab and Ben-ammi’s naming with it; 2:22 closed the Horites’ displacement by Esau’s sons with the identical phrase. Now Jair renames Bashan’s captured villages Havvoth-jair, ‘the tent-villages of Jair,’ and the book reaches for the same words a third time — a formula this translation has now watched recur across three separate chapters, always marking something that has outlasted the generation that made it so.

Vv16-17’s border list — Gilead, the Arnon, the Jabbok, the Arabah, the Jordan, Chinnereth, the Salt Sea, the slopes of Pisgah — is written in a different register from the narrative around it. No verb of conquest, no divine speech, just points on a line, the same flat legal cadence Numbers 34 (already on these pages) already used to fix Canaan’s own border for the nine and a half tribes still to cross — a genre this book reaches for whenever land, not narrative, is the point.

Verses 18–20 · the bargain of Numbers 32, reissued as a command

Moses folds a negotiated covenant into a direct order. Numbers 32 (already on these pages) narrated this as a human bargain struck between Moses and the tribes — that chapter’s own note calls it “a covenant with no divine speech in it.” Here, in Moses’ retelling, the same terms arrive as something he “commanded… at that time” — the negotiation smoothed into a command, the same way 2:12’s own narrator-voice-from-the-future was left as an honest seam rather than resolved. Deuteronomy’s retelling keeps doing this: not changing what happened, but changing whose voice carries it.

★ “Each man to his possession” (v20) is the third time this chapter alone uses the word Deuteronomy 2 built its whole argument around. There, yerushah named what four foreign nations already held — Edom, Moab, Ammon, and Israel itself in prospect. Here it names, for the first time, the actual land Reuben and Gad are about to occupy: the word that described what everyone else already had now describes what Israel itself finally holds.

Verses 21–22 · the first charge to Joshua, and an old battle-promise renewed

“Jehovah your God, he is the one fighting for you” is not a new promise — it is Exodus 14:14 (already on these pages), forty years later. At the sea, Moses told a frightened, cornered people, “Jehovah will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace” — the same verb, the same preposition. What steadied Israel against Egypt’s chariots now steadies Joshua against “all the kingdoms” still to come.

This is Joshua’s SECOND naming in this book, and still not his real commissioning. 1:38 already named him “the one to be strengthened” (chazek oto); here he is told, secondhand through Moses, only that his eyes have seen what Jehovah did to two kings. The actual charge — the moment strengthening happens rather than being merely promised — waits seven verses, until Moses has asked for something else first and been refused it.

Verses 23–27 · the plea, the refusal, and a word this chapter has been building toward

★ “For your sakes” (v26) is a citation this book already made of ITSELF before this chapter existed. 1:38’s own note named this verse by number in advance: “Here, three times in Deuteronomy (1:37, 3:26, 4:21), Moses says he was excluded because of them” — set against Numbers 20:12’s own account, where the fault is squarely Moses’ own, striking a rock he was told to speak to. That note offered three readings and chose none; this chapter is simply the second of the three predicted appearances arriving on schedule, and nothing here resolves what 1:38 left open. What IS new: the exact idiom Jehovah uses to cut the conversation off — rav lakh, ‘enough from you’ — is the dictionary’s own rav lakhem entry, promised to land here since Numbers 16: the same two-word idiom Korah once threw at Moses and Aaron (‘YOU have too much’) becomes, singular now and from God, Moses’ own sentence. A phrase that once accused him of taking too much authority now tells him he has said too much about a door already closed.

★ “You have BEGUN to show your servant your greatness” (v24) uses the exact root 2:24-31’s own note counted three times and explicitly flagged ‘worth watching in Deuteronomy 3’ — chalal, ‘to begin,’ the word that framed Sihon’s conquest as a first installment rather than a completed grant. It returns here a fourth time, reversed: instead of God announcing a beginning TO Israel, Moses throws the word back AT God, as leverage — you have begun, so let me see it through. The very word that kept promising more is what the request stakes itself on, and the request still fails.

Pisgah is a down payment, not the view itself. Moses is sent up “the top of Pisgah” — the same ridge that already showed Balaam the whole camp of Israel from outside (Numbers 23:14, already on these pages) — to look in every direction and see the land he will not enter. The Pisgah entry already promises a fuller vision at Deuteronomy 34:1 (not yet on these pages), where Moses is shown the whole land before he dies; this chapter is the first, partial look, granted specifically because the fuller request was refused.

Verses 28–29 · two roots spoken together for the first time, and a valley finally named

★ V28 is where chazak and amats finally stand in one sentence — the payoff of two separate plants, four verses and one whole chapter apart. 1:38 already used chazak alone of Joshua (‘Strengthen him’); 2:30 used amats alone of Sihon’s hardened heart (‘strengthened’). Both roots genuinely mean something close to ‘be/make strong,’ which is exactly the problem this verse creates: reusing ‘strengthen’ for both, now that they sit side by side, would erase the fact that two different Hebrew words are doing the work. This translation follows the whole English shelf’s own long-settled solution — KJV, ASV and the NWT all read ‘encourage him, and strengthen him’ here, in that order, and RV60 independently reaches the same split in Spanish, ‘anímalo, y fortalécelo’ — assigning chazak ‘encourage’ and keeping amats at its own established ‘strengthen,’ consistent with 2:30. Chazak is the root that shifts; the dictionary link is what keeps the thread visible in English even while the gloss moves.

⚠ This is NOT the same construction as the phrase this pairing will become. Here both verbs are transitive, aimed AT Joshua by someone else — God commanding Moses to strengthen and embolden him. Joshua 1:6-9 (already on these pages) fixes the same two roots into a single self-addressed idiom, ‘be strong and resolute,’ spoken straight to Joshua rather than done to him — first by Moses at Deuteronomy 31:6-7 (not yet on these pages), then four times over by God and by Israel itself once Joshua actually leads. Three grammatical shapes, three English solutions, one Hebrew pairing running underneath all of them.

Beth-peor (v29) has been the camp’s address the whole time without being named this plainly. Israel has been parked in this valley since the Baal-peor crisis (Numbers 25, already on these pages) and Balaam’s oracles before it; this is the first verse in this book to give the site its own name rather than describe it. ⚠ The Douay-Rheims alone declines to transliterate it — ‘the temple of Phogor,’ translating beth outright rather than leaving it as a place-name — the same choice this translation itself makes one book earlier, at Genesis 28:17 (already on these pages), where Jacob’s own beit Elohim becomes ‘the house of God’ before he turns the phrase into the fixed name Bethel two verses later. ★ The valley will not let go of this chapter’s subject: Deuteronomy 34:6 (not yet on these pages) reports Moses buried ‘in the valley… opposite Beth-peor,’ his grave unmarked to this day — the same site that here watches him refused the crossing will, by that chapter, be where the refusal becomes permanent.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English agree on 29 verses, with no versification shift at this chapter’s boundary (checked directly against Mechon-Mamre’s own verse count, unlike the real +1 offset found at the Exodus 21/22 seam). The rendering to flag is “encourage” for chazak and “strengthen” for amats at v28 — see n28 above for why two roots this translation has each independently glossed ‘strengthen’ on their own needed to separate once they finally share a sentence. Elsewhere the established renderings hold: Jehovah for the divine name, possession for yerushah, and cherem ‘devoted to destruction.’ The Masoretic text marks one setumah, after v22, and closes the chapter with a petuchah after v29 — the break falls exactly at the seam between the two royal wars and the personal plea that follows, which the Masoretic tradition also marks with its own named division: verses 23-29 open Parashat Va’etchanan, the week’s second Torah portion, beginning at the exact point Moses turns from reporting battles to begging for something for himself.

Library. One new dictionary entry in both languages: chalal, ‘to begin’ — the root Deuteronomy 2’s own closing note promised to watch for here, now on its fourth and most consequential use. One existing dictionary entry extended in both languages: amats, its second appearance and the full translation-choice discussion at v28. One existing dictionary entry gained its first Spanish edition: chazak, touched by v28 alongside amats but never before translated. Three new encyclopedia entries in both languages: Hermon, the mountain with three names; Argob, Og’s sixty walled cities; and Beth-peor, named outright for the first time at v29. Three existing encyclopedia entries gained their first Spanish edition here, touched by this chapter but not previously translated: Gilead, Reuben, and Rabbah (Chinnereth, the Salt Sea and the Jordan already had Spanish editions). Five existing encyclopedia entries extended, each in both languages: Og and the Rephaim (their own forward-citations of this chapter turned into real links), Jair (likewise), Joshua (his first commission in this book), and Manasseh and Ephraim (the formal Transjordan grant). One pre-existing dead link, in the Machir entry, was also corrected to point at the right slug — found while extending a neighbouring entry, not created by this chapter. A second pre-existing defect, unrelated to this chapter's own content, was found and fixed in passing: the Spanish Anakim entry had been silently duplicated by a dictionary-key collision, with the older (Numbers 13) version quietly shadowing the newer (Deuteronomy 2) one at runtime — the two are now merged into one entry.

Echoes paid. Numbers 21:33-35’s Og narrative, quoted almost verbatim at vv1-3. Genesis 14:5 and Deuteronomy 2:11, 20’s whole Rephaim thread, closing at v11 with the category’s last bearer. 1:38’s “strengthen him,” paid at v28 alongside 2:30’s amats. 1:37’s own forward-citation of “3:26,” arriving on schedule at v26, together with the rav lakhem entry’s promised return from Numbers 16. 2:24-31’s own “worth watching in Deuteronomy 3” for the chalal root, paid and reversed at v24. Exodus 14:14’s battle-promise, renewed at v22. And Numbers 32’s covenant, folded into command at vv18-20.

Patterns worth carrying forward. First: this chapter runs Deuteronomy 2’s three-names-one-thing device on a different scale — a mountain instead of a people — which suggests the pattern is a habit of this book’s retelling voice, not a coincidence tied to giant-clans specifically; worth watching for a third instance. Second: chazak and amats have now been glossed three different ways across three appearances (“strengthen” alone, “strengthened” alone, “encourage…strengthen” together) with a fourth — “be strong and resolute” — already live at Joshua 1; the dictionary link, not the English surface, is what a reader should trust to track the root. Third: Deuteronomy keeps quietly converting Numbers’ narrated events into Moses’ own commanded speech — the Reuben/Gad covenant (n18) is the second instance this translation has flagged, after 2:12’s narrator-voice seam; a third would make it a confirmed habit rather than a pair of coincidences.

Echoes planted. Deuteronomy 34:1, where Pisgah’s partial view (v27) becomes the full one; Deuteronomy 34:6, where the valley opposite Beth-peor (v29) becomes Moses’ own unmarked grave; and Deuteronomy 4:1-4, already glimpsed while checking this chapter’s own forward boundary — the same Baal-peor this chapter’s closing verse camps beside becomes, one chapter later, the example Moses reaches for first.
