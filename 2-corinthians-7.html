The same letter, written from Macedonia after Titus arrived with news from Corinth. Paul calls the Corinthians to cleanse themselves from every defilement, tells them he has no wrong to answer for, and describes the comfort Titus brought with their longing and zeal. He does not regret the letter that grieved them, and he distinguishes godly sorrow, which leads to repentance, from the sorrow of the world, which produces death.
Undated by the text: the chapter names no year. It continues the letter of chapters 2 to 6 without a break.

2 Corinthians 7 — The Mister Translation

1
ταύτας οὖν ἔχοντες τὰς ἐπαγγελίας, ἀγαπητοί, καθαρίσωμεν ἑαυτοὺς ἀπὸ παντὸς μολυσμοῦ σαρκὸς καὶ πνεύματος, ἐπιτελοῦντες ἁγιωσύνην ἐν φόβῳ θεοῦ.
Therefore, since we have these promises, beloved ones, let us cleanse ourselves of every defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God.note
Por lo tanto, amados, ya que tenemos estas promesas, limpiémonos de todo lo que contamina el cuerpo y el espíritu, perfeccionando nuestra santidad con el temor de Dios.
2
Χωρήσατε ἡμᾶς· οὐδένα ἠδικήσαμεν, οὐδένα ἐφθείραμεν, οὐδένα ἐπλεονεκτήσαμεν.
Make room for us in your hearts. We have wronged no one, corrupted no one, taken advantage of no one.note
Hagan lugar para nosotros en sus corazones. A nadie hemos perjudicado, a nadie hemos corrompido, a nadie hemos explotado.
3
πρὸς κατάκρισιν οὐ λέγω, προείρηκα γὰρ ὅτι ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ἡμῶν ἐστε εἰς τὸ συναποθανεῖν καὶ συζῆν.
I do not say this to condemn you; I have said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.note
No digo esto para condenarlos; ya les he dicho que los llevamos en el corazón, para morir juntos y para vivir juntos.
4
πολλή μοι παρρησία πρὸς ὑμᾶς, πολλή μοι καύχησις ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν· πεπλήρωμαι τῇ παρακλήσει, ὑπερπερισσεύομαι τῇ χαρᾷ ἐπὶ πάσῃ τῇ θλίψει ἡμῶν.
I speak with great boldness toward you, and great is my boasting about you. I am filled with comfort; I overflow with joy in all our affliction.note
Les hablo con toda libertad y me siento muy orgulloso de ustedes. Estoy lleno de consuelo; rebosa mi alegría en medio de todos nuestros sufrimientos.
5
Καὶ γὰρ ἐλθόντων ἡμῶν εἰς Μακεδονίαν οὐδεμίαν ἔσχηκεν ἄνεσιν ἡ σὰρξ ἡμῶν, ἀλλʼ ἐν παντὶ θλιβόμενοι— ἔξωθεν μάχαι, ἔσωθεν φόβοι—
For even when we came into Macedonia, our flesh had no relief, but we were afflicted on every side: fights outside, fears within.note
Porque cuando llegamos a Macedonia, nuestra carne no tuvo alivio, sino que en todo fuimos afligidos: luchas por fuera, temores por dentro.
6
ἀλλʼ ὁ παρακαλῶν τοὺς ταπεινοὺς παρεκάλεσεν ἡμᾶς ὁ θεὸς ἐν τῇ παρουσίᾳ Τίτου·
But God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus,note
Pero Dios, que consuela a los abatidos, nos consoló con la llegada de Tito;
7
οὐ μόνον δὲ ἐν τῇ παρουσίᾳ αὐτοῦ, ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν τῇ παρακλήσει ᾗ παρεκλήθη ἐφʼ ὑμῖν, ἀναγγέλλων ἡμῖν τὴν ὑμῶν ἐπιπόθησιν, τὸν ὑμῶν ὀδυρμόν, τὸν ὑμῶν ζῆλον ὑπὲρ ἐμοῦ, ὥστε με μᾶλλον χαρῆναι.
and not only by his coming, but also by the comfort with which he was comforted in you, as he told us of your longing, your sorrow, and your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced even more.note
y no solo con su llegada, sino también con el consuelo que él recibió de ustedes, pues nos contó de su anhelo, de su dolor y de su celo por mí, de modo que me alegré aún más.
8
ὅτι εἰ καὶ ἐλύπησα ὑμᾶς ἐν τῇ ἐπιστολῇ, οὐ μεταμέλομαι· εἰ καὶ μετεμελόμην (βλέπω ὅτι ἡ ἐπιστολὴ ἐκείνη εἰ καὶ πρὸς ὥραν ἐλύπησεν ὑμᾶς),
Even if I made you sorry with my letter, I do not regret it; though I did regret it, because I see that the letter made you sorry, though only for a while,note
Porque aunque los entristecí con mi carta, no me arrepiento; aunque sí me arrepentí, pues veo que aquella carta los entristeció, aunque fuera por un tiempo breve,
cross-refs⤷ Hebrews 7:21
9
νῦν χαίρω, οὐχ ὅτι ἐλυπήθητε, ἀλλʼ ὅτι ἐλυπήθητε εἰς μετάνοιαν, ἐλυπήθητε γὰρ κατὰ θεόν, ἵνα ἐν μηδενὶ ζημιωθῆτε ἐξ ἡμῶν.
now I rejoice, not because you were made sorry, but because you were made sorry into repentance; for you were sorrowed in a godly way, so that you suffered loss from us in nothing.note
ahora me alegro, no porque hayan sido entristecidos, sino porque fueron entristecidos para arrepentimiento; pues fueron entristecidos según Dios, para que de ninguna manera salieran perjudicados por nosotros.
10
ἡ γὰρ κατὰ θεὸν λύπη μετάνοιαν εἰς σωτηρίαν ἀμεταμέλητον ἐργάζεται ἡ δὲ τοῦ κόσμου λύπη θάνατον κατεργάζεται.
For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, without regret; but worldly sorrow produces death.note
Porque la tristeza según Dios produce arrepentimiento para salvación, sin lamento; pero la tristeza del mundo produce muerte.
11
ἰδοὺ γὰρ αὐτὸ τοῦτο τὸ κατὰ θεὸν λυπηθῆναι πόσην κατειργάσατο ὑμῖν σπουδήν, ἀλλὰ ἀπολογίαν, ἀλλὰ ἀγανάκτησιν, ἀλλὰ φόβον, ἀλλὰ ἐπιπόθησιν, ἀλλὰ ζῆλον, ἀλλὰ ἐκδίκησιν· ἐν παντὶ συνεστήσατε ἑαυτοὺς ἁγνοὺς εἶναι τῷ πράγματι.
For see what earnestness this godly sorrow produced in you: clearing of yourselves, indignation, fear, longing, zeal, and vindication. In everything you showed yourselves pure in this matter.note
Porque miren qué diligencia produjo en ustedes esta misma tristeza según Dios: defensa, indignación, temor, anhelo, celo y justicia. En todo se mostraron puros en este asunto.
12
ἄρα εἰ καὶ ἔγραψα ὑμῖν, οὐχ ἕνεκεν τοῦ ἀδικήσαντος, οὐδὲ ἕνεκεν τοῦ ἀδικηθέντος, ἀλλʼ 1ἕνεκεν τοῦ φανερωθῆναι τὴν σπουδὴν ὑμῶν τὴν ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν πρὸς ὑμᾶς ἐνώπιον τοῦ θεοῦ.
So although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did wrong, nor for the one who was wronged, but so that your earnestness for us might be made clear to you before God.note
Así que, aunque les escribí, no fue por causa del que cometió la falta ni del que la sufrió, sino para que se hiciera evidente ante Dios la diligencia que ustedes tienen por nosotros.
13
διὰ τοῦτο παρακεκλήμεθα. Ἐπὶ δὲ τῇ παρακλήσει ἡμῶν περισσοτέρως μᾶλλον ἐχάρημεν ἐπὶ τῇ χαρᾷ Τίτου, ὅτι ἀναπέπαυται τὸ πνεῦμα αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ πάντων ὑμῶν·
That is why we have been comforted. And besides our comfort, we rejoiced even more at the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you.note
Por eso hemos sido consolados. Y además de nuestro consuelo, nos alegramos mucho más por el gozo de Tito, porque todos ustedes le han devuelto la tranquilidad.
14
ὅτι εἴ τι αὐτῷ ὑπὲρ ὑμῶν κεκαύχημαι, οὐ κατῃσχύνθην, ἀλλʼ ὡς πάντα ἐν ἀληθείᾳ ἐλαλήσαμεν ὑμῖν, οὕτως καὶ ἡ καύχησις ἡμῶν ἡ ἐπὶ Τίτου ἀλήθεια ἐγενήθη.
For if I boasted to him about you, I was not put to shame; but just as everything we said to you was true, so also our boasting before Titus has proved true.note
Porque si de algo me había jactado con él de ustedes, no quedé en vergüenza; sino que, como todo lo que les dijimos fue verdad, así también resultó verdad nuestro gloriarnos ante Tito.
15
καὶ τὰ σπλάγχνα αὐτοῦ περισσοτέρως εἰς ὑμᾶς ἐστιν ἀναμιμνῃσκομένου τὴν πάντων ὑμῶν ὑπακοήν, ὡς μετὰ φόβου καὶ τρόμου ἐδέξασθε αὐτόν.
And his affection for you is even greater as he remembers the obedience of you all, how you received him with fear and trembling.note
Y su afecto por ustedes es aún mayor al recordar la obediencia de todos ustedes, cómo lo recibieron con temor y temblor.
16
χαίρω ὅτι ἐν παντὶ θαρρῶ ἐν ὑμῖν.
I rejoice that in everything I can have confidence in you.note
Me alegro de que en todo puedo confiar en ustedes.
Verse 1 · these promises, and cleansing from every defilement
“These promises.” tautas oun echontes tas epangelias, having therefore these promises. The promises are the ones quoted at 6:16–18, and the verse opens with oun, therefore, to tie the two chapters. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Therefore, since we have these promises, beloved ones, let us cleanse ourselves of every defilement of flesh and spirit.” The KJV “let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit.”

Verses 2–4 · make room for us, the denials, and in your hearts to die together
“Make room for us.” chōrēsate hēmas, make room for us. The verb is the one for making space, as for a crowd or a container. Three denials follow, each with a verb in the aorist: we wronged no one, corrupted no one, took advantage of no one. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Make room for us in your hearts. We have wronged no one, we have corrupted no one, we have taken advantage of no one.” The ASV “Open your hearts to us: we wronged no man, we corrupted no man, we took advantage of no man.”

“In your hearts to die together and live together.” eis to synapothanein kai syzēn, to die together and to live together. The sentence says he said this before, and it denies that he speaks to condemn. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “you are in our hearts to die together and to live together.” The KJV “that ye are in our hearts to die and live with you.”

Verses 5–7 · our flesh had no relief in Macedonia, and the God who comforts the downcast
“Our flesh had no relief.” ouk eschēken anesin hē sarx hēmōn, our flesh had no relief. The verse names what they felt when they came into Macedonia, and then names the two kinds of trouble, outside and inside. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “there were fights on the outside, fears within.” The ASV “without were fightings, within were fears.”

“The God who comforts the downcast.” ho parakalōn tous tapeinous, the one who comforts the lowly. The verb parakaleō is the verb of comfort and appeal that runs through the letter, from 1:3 on. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But God, who comforts those who are downhearted, comforted us by the presence of Titus.” The KJV “Nevertheless God, that comforteth those that are cast down, comforted us by the coming of Titus.”

Verses 8–12 · the letter that grieved them, sorrow in a godly way, and the list of what it produced
“I made you sorry with my letter.” ei kai elypēsa hymas en tē epistolē, even if I grieved you in the letter. The verb metamelomai is to regret; the sentence says he did regret it at first, then says he does not. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For even if I saddened you by my letter, I do not regret it.” The KJV “For though I made you sorry with a letter, I do not repent, though I did repent.”

“Sadness in a godly way.” hē kata theon lypē, sorrow according to God, against hē tou kosmou lypē, the sorrow of the world. The first produces repentance that leads to salvation and leaves no regret; the second produces death. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For sadness in a godly way produces repentance leading to salvation, leaving no regret; but the sadness of the world produces death.” The ASV “For godly sorrow worketh repentance unto salvation, a repentance which bringeth no regret: but the sorrow of the world worketh death.”

“What earnestness.” Verse 11 lists what the sorrow produced: six nouns, each introduced by alla, but, after the word for earnestness. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “yes, clearing of yourselves, yes, indignation, yes, fear, yes, earnest desire, yes, zeal, yes, righting of the wrong!” The KJV “yea, what clearing of yourselves, yea, what indignation, yea, what fear, yea, what vehement desire, yea, what zeal, yea, what revenge!”

Verses 13–16 · the joy of Titus refreshed, fear and trembling, and confidence in you
“The joy of Titus.” epi tē charā Titou, at the joy of Titus. The verb is anapauō, to give rest, and the sentence says his spirit was refreshed by all of them. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “because all of you refreshed his spirit.” The ASV “because his spirit hath been refreshed by you all.”

“With fear and trembling.” meta phobou kai tromou, with fear and trembling, the words of how the Corinthians received Titus. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “how you received him with fear and trembling.” The KJV “how with fear and trembling ye received him.”

“Confidence in you.” charō hoti en panti tharrō en hymin, I rejoice that in everything I am confident in you. The verb is the one for courage at 5:6 and 5:8, where it is said of Paul and his companions. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “I rejoice that in every way I may have confidence in you.” The KJV “I rejoice therefore that I have confidence in you in all things.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “comfort” for parakaleō at 7:6 and 7:7; “sorrow” for lypē in both of its kinds at 7:9–11, as the Greek uses one noun for the godly and the worldly; “confidence” for tharrō at 7:16.

Echoes paid. The comfort of 1:3–7 and 6:1 returns as the comfort Titus brings (7:6–7); the letter that grieved them (7:8) is the letter that 2:4 describes; the boasting over the Corinthians returns at 7:14.

Echoes planted. The collection for the saints that the next chapters take up begins with Titus’s report of Macedonian generosity; the chapter ends on confidence, and the next pages carry the matter on.
