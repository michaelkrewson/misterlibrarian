Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · “The main point” — a high priest seated in the heavens, and the true tent “which Jehovah pitched”

“Now the main point in what we are saying is this: we have such a high priest, who sat down at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens.” Kephalaion, “main point,” stands in two verses: here, and the tribune’s “I bought this citizenship for a large sum” at Acts 22:28 (dictionary). The word means the head of a matter, or the sum of money it adds up to, and the shelf divides between the two. “Sat down at the right hand” is the line of Psalm 110:1, already said of the Son at Hebrews 1:3, “sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high.” Megalōsynē, “Majesty,” stands in three verses: Hebrews 1:3, here, and Jude 1:25 (dictionary); in Hebrews it is a reverent way of naming God. The KJV reads “Now of the things which we have spoken this is the sum”; the ASV reads “the chief point is this”; the NWT 1984 reads “Now as to the things being discussed this is the main point.”

“A minister of the sanctuary and of the true tent, which Jehovah pitched, not a human being.” Leitourgos, “minister,” stands in five verses, among them the angels as God’s “ministers” at Hebrews 1:7 and the rulers as “God’s public servants” at Romans 13:6 (dictionary). “The sanctuary” is a Greek plural, “the holy [places]” or “the holy [things],” and readers take it either way. “True” is alēthinos, as in “the true vine” (dictionary): real, as against a copy. Pēgnymi, “to pitch” a tent, stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). “Which the Lord pitched” is word for word the Greek of Numbers 24:6, “like tents which the Lord pitched” (checked), where the Hebrew, as this translation’s Numbers reads it, has “like aloes Jehovah has planted”; here the Greek reads kyrios, and the one who pitches the tent is God, so this translation prints “Jehovah.” Robinson–Pierpont has “and not a human being” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “which the Lord pitched, and not man”; the Douay reads “A minister of the holies”; the NWT 1984 reads “which Jehovah put up, and not man.”

Verses 3–6 · “A copy and shadow of the heavenly things” — “according to the pattern,” and the mediator of a better covenant

“For every high priest is appointed to offer both gifts and sacrifices … Now if he were on earth, he would not even be a priest, since there are those who offer the gifts according to the law.” The first clause repeats Hebrews 5:1, “to offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins.” On earth the priesthood belonged to the sons of Aaron, and Hebrews 7:14 has said that the Lord’s tribe was Judah. The present tense, “there are those who offer,” is read by some as a sign that sacrifice was still being offered at Jerusalem when the letter was written, and by others as the present tense of the Law’s own text, since the writer speaks of the tent and never names the temple. Robinson–Pierpont has “For if” and “the priests who offer” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “seeing that there are priests that offer gifts according to the law”; the ASV reads “he would not be a priest at all”; the NIV reads “for there are already priests who offer the gifts prescribed by the law.”

“They serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things, just as Moses was warned when he was about to complete the tent: ‘See,’ he says, ‘that you make everything according to the pattern that was shown to you on the mountain.’” Hypodeigma stands in six verses; at Hebrews 4:11 it is the “example” of disobedience, and at John 13:15 the “example” of the washed feet, and here a “copy” (dictionary). “Shadow” is skia, as at Colossians 2:17, “a shadow of the things to come” (dictionary). “Was warned” is chrēmatizō, the verb of the magi “warned in a dream” at Matthew 2:12 and of Noah “warned about what could not yet be seen” at Hebrews 11:7 (dictionary). The quotation is Exodus 25:40, which this translation renders from the Hebrew “according to their pattern, which is being shown to you on the mountain”; the Greek of Exodus has no “everything,” and Hebrews adds it (checked). Stephen uses the same word at Acts 7:44, “according to the pattern he had seen” (dictionary). The KJV reads “Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things”; the ASV reads “who serve that which is a copy and shadow of the heavenly things”; the NWT 1984 reads “rendering sacred service in a typical representation and a shadow of the heavenly things.”

“But as it is, he has obtained a ministry as much more excellent as he is also the mediator of a better covenant, which has been enacted on better promises.” Diaphōroteros, “more excellent,” stands in two verses: the “more excellent” name of Hebrews 1:4, and here. Leitourgia, “ministry,” stands in six verses, among them Zechariah’s days of “service” in the temple at Luke 1:23 (dictionary). Mesitēs, “mediator,” stands in six verses, among them Galatians 3:19, the law given “by the hand of a mediator,” and 1 Timothy 2:5, “one mediator between God and humans” (dictionary). “Enacted” is the verb of Hebrews 7:11, where the people “received the law”: the better covenant is legislated, as the first one was. The KJV reads “But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry”; the ASV reads “which hath been enacted upon better promises”; the NWT 1984 reads “which has been legally established upon better promises.”

Verses 7–9 · “Faultless … finding fault” — Jeremiah’s new covenant, and “I neglected them”

“For if that first one had been faultless, no place would have been sought for a second. For finding fault with them, he says.” The Greek says only “that first one”; “covenant” is understood from verse 6. “Faultless” and “finding fault” are one root, amemptos and memphomai, and this translation keeps the pair; elsewhere amemptos is “blameless,” as at Philippians 3:6 (dictionary). Memphomai, “to find fault,” stands in two verses: “Why does he still find fault?” at Romans 9:19, and here (dictionary). The printed text has “them” as the object of the fault-finding, with Westcott–Hort and Nestle–Aland 28; Tregelles and Robinson–Pierpont have “finding fault, he says to them” (the apparatus), and on that reading the fault can be the covenant’s rather than the people’s. This page does not choose. The KJV reads “For if that first covenant had been faultless”; the Douay reads “For if that former had been faultless,” without “covenant”; the NIV reads “But God found fault with the people.”

“Look — days are coming, says Jehovah, when I will conclude a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.” The quotation, verses 8–12, is Jeremiah 31:31–34 in English Bibles, 31:30–33 in the Hebrew numbering this translation’s Jeremiah follows (31:30–33), and 38:31–34 in the Greek Bible. Where the Hebrew “cuts” a covenant (dictionary) and the Greek of Jeremiah says “I will covenant,” Hebrews has synteleō, “I will conclude, accomplish” (checked). “Says Jehovah” is the Hebrew ne’um YHVH, “declares Jehovah,” and the Greek reads kyrios; this translation prints “Jehovah” for the God of Israel in a quotation. The phrase “new covenant” with kainē stands in five verses: the cup at Luke 22:20 and 1 Corinthians 11:25, 2 Corinthians 3:6, here, and 9:15 (dictionary). The KJV reads “Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, when I will make a new covenant”; the NWT 1984 reads “and I will conclude with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah a new covenant”; the Douay reads “and I will perfect unto the house of Israel.”

⚠ “Because they did not continue in my covenant, and I neglected them, says Jehovah.” Here Hebrews follows the Greek of Jeremiah, “and I neglected them” (checked), where the Hebrew, as this translation’s Jeremiah reads it, has “though I was a husband to them” (Jeremiah 31:31). How the Greek translators came to read the line differently is explained in more than one way (reported, not checked here); this page reports the difference and does not settle it. Ameleō, “to neglect,” stands in four verses, and it turns back the warning of Hebrews 2:3, “if we neglect so great a salvation” (dictionary). Emmenō, “to continue in,” stands in four verses, among them “to remain in the faith” at Acts 14:22 and “everyone who does not abide by all the things written” at Galatians 3:10 (dictionary). “Took them by the hand” is the verb of Hebrews 2:16 (dictionary). The KJV reads “and I regarded them not, saith the Lord”; the NWT 1984 reads “so that I stopped caring for them”; the NIV reads “and I turned away from them.”

Verses 10–12 · “I will write them on their hearts” — “Know Jehovah,” and sins remembered no more

“For this is the covenant that I will covenant with the house of Israel after those days, says Jehovah: putting my laws into their mind, I will also write them on their hearts; and I will be their God, and they will be my people.” “The covenant that I will covenant” keeps the Greek’s noun and verb from one root. The Hebrew has “my law,” torah, in the singular, “within them”; the Greek of Jeremiah and Hebrews have “my laws” in the plural, “into their mind” (checked). “Mind” is dianoia (dictionary). “Write on” is epigraphō, the verb of Mark 15:26, where “the inscription of the charge against him was written”; its noun is the “inscription” on the coin at Matthew 22:20 (dictionary). The law that Moses received on tablets of stone is here written on the heart, as Paul puts it at 2 Corinthians 3:3, “not on stone tablets but on tablets of human hearts.” The KJV reads “I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts”; the ASV reads “And on their heart also will I write them”; the NWT 1984 reads “For this is the covenant that I shall covenant with the house of Israel.”

“And they will not teach, each one his fellow citizen and each one his brother, saying, ‘Know Jehovah,’ because they will all know me, from the least to the greatest of them.” Politēs, “fellow citizen,” stands in four verses, among them Paul’s “a citizen of a city not without distinction” at Acts 21:39 (dictionary). The Textus Receptus printed “neighbour,” plēsion, and the KJV follows it (checked in the printed text); the Byzantine text has “fellow citizen,” as the Greek of Jeremiah does. “Know Jehovah” is the Hebrew “Know YHVH,” with kyrios in the Greek. Robinson–Pierpont has “from the least of them” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “for all shall know me, from the least to the greatest”; the ASV reads “From the least to the greatest of them”; the NWT 1984 reads “teach each one his fellow citizen and each one his brother.”

“For I will be merciful to their unrighteous deeds, and their sins I will remember no more.” Hileōs, “merciful,” stands in two verses: Peter’s “God forbid, Lord!” at Matthew 16:22, literally “[God be] merciful to you,” and here (dictionary). It belongs with hilaskomai, the tax collector’s “God, be merciful to me” at Luke 18:13 and the “make atonement” of Hebrews 2:17 (dictionary). The Hebrew has “I will forgive their guilt” (Jeremiah 31:33). Robinson–Pierpont adds “and their lawless deeds” (the apparatus), which the KJV follows. The KJV reads “For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more”; the NWT 1984 reads “I shall by no means call their sins to mind anymore”; the NIV reads “For I will forgive their wickedness.”

Verse 13 · “He has made the first one old” — growing old, aging, and near to vanishing

“In saying ‘new,’ he has made the first one old; and what is growing old and aging is near to vanishing.” Palaioō, “to make old, to wear out,” stands in three verses: “purses that do not wear out” at Luke 12:33, the heavens that “will all wear out like a garment” at Hebrews 1:11, and here, twice (dictionary). Gēraskō, “to grow old,” stands in two verses: “when you grow old” at John 21:18, and here (dictionary). Aphanismos, “vanishing,” stands in one verse (dictionary). The verse does not use the phrase “old covenant,” which stands at 2 Corinthians 3:14; it says “the first.” Readers take “the first” as the covenant of Sinai as a whole, or as the Levitical priesthood and its sacrifices, the subject of chapters 7 to 10; and the quotation itself makes the new covenant “with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah.” This page does not choose. The KJV reads “Now that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away”; the ASV reads “In that he saith, A new covenant he hath made the first old”; the NWT 1984 reads “he has made the former one obsolete.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “the main point,” the word of Acts 22:28’s “large sum”; “the Majesty,” as at 1:3; “minister,” as at 1:7; “tent,” as at Acts 7:44; “which Jehovah pitched,” the words of the Greek of Numbers 24:6; “copy and shadow”; “warned,” as at 11:7; “pattern,” as at Exodus 25:40 and Acts 7:44; “more excellent,” as at 1:4; “mediator”; “enacted,” the verb of 7:11; “faultless … finding fault,” one root kept; “Look —”; “conclude” a covenant; “says Jehovah”; “I neglected them,” the verb of 2:3; “the covenant that I will covenant”; “laws” in the plural; “fellow citizen”; “Know Jehovah”; “merciful”; and “made old,” the verb of 1:11.

Echoes paid. The high priest who sat down at the right hand of Hebrews 1:3; the ministers of Hebrews 1:7; the tents which the Lord pitched of Numbers 24:6; gifts and sacrifices from Hebrews 5:1; the pattern of Exodus 25:40 and Acts 7:44; the shadow of Colossians 2:17; the more excellent name of Hebrews 1:4; the mediator of Galatians 3:19 and 1 Timothy 2:5; a better covenant from Hebrews 7:22; Why does he still find fault? from Romans 9:19; Jeremiah’s new covenant at Jeremiah 31:30; the cup of the new covenant at Luke 22:20; neglect from Hebrews 2:3; tablets of human hearts from 2 Corinthians 3:3; be merciful to me from Luke 18:13; and wear out like a garment from Hebrews 1:11.

Echoes planted. The “copy” at 9:23; the “shadow” at 10:1; the “mediator of a new covenant” at 9:15 and 12:24; and the same quotation from Jeremiah, shortened, at 10:16–17 (all yet to come in this translation).

Next in this book: Hebrews 9. The first covenant’s tent and its furniture — the lampstand, the table, the golden altar, the ark — and the high priest’s yearly entry “not without blood”; then Christ entering “once for all” with his own blood; a covenant that, like a will, takes effect at a death; and “without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.”
