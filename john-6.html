Jesus feeds five thousand from a boy's five loaves and two fish, then withdraws when the crowd tries to make him king by force. That night he walks to the disciples across the water. The next day, at Capernaum, he delivers the Bread of Life discourse — 'I am the bread of life... whoever comes to me shall not hunger' — language so hard that many of his own disciples turn back and no longer walk with him.
c. AD 29, near a Passover (v4), corresponding to the same feeding and water-walking told in Matthew 14 and Mark 6 — John's extended discourse afterward is unique to his Gospel.

John 6 — The Mister Translation

1
Μετὰ ταῦτα ἀπῆλθεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς πέραν τῆς θαλάσσης τῆς Γαλιλαίας τῆς Τιβεριάδος.
After this Jesus went away to the other side of the Sea of Galilee, that is, of Tiberias.note
Después de esto, Jesús se fue al otro lado del mar de Galilea, que es el de Tiberias.
2
ἠκολούθει δὲ αὐτῷ ὄχλος πολύς, ὅτι ἐθεώρουν τὰ σημεῖα ἃ ἐποίει ἐπὶ τῶν ἀσθενούντων.
And a great crowd was following him, because they saw the signs that he was doing on those who were sick.note
Y le seguía una gran multitud, porque veían las señales que hacía en los enfermos.
3
ἀνῆλθεν δὲ εἰς τὸ ὄρος Ἰησοῦς, καὶ ἐκεῖ ἐκάθητο μετὰ τῶν μαθητῶν αὐτοῦ.
And Jesus went up on the mountain, and there he sat with his disciples.note
Y subió Jesús al monte, y se sentó allí con sus discípulos.
4
ἦν δὲ ἐγγὺς τὸ πάσχα, ἡ ἑορτὴ τῶν Ἰουδαίων.
Now the Passover, the feast of the Jews, was near.note
Y estaba cerca la pascua, la fiesta de los judíos.
5
ἐπάρας οὖν τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς ὁ Ἰησοῦς καὶ θεασάμενος ὅτι πολὺς ὄχλος ἔρχεται πρὸς αὐτὸν λέγει πρὸς Φίλιππον· Πόθεν ἀγοράσωμεν ἄρτους ἵνα φάγωσιν οὗτοι;
Then Jesus, lifting up his eyes and seeing that a great crowd was coming to him, said to Philip, ‘Where are we to buy bread, that these people may eat?’note
Entonces Jesús, alzando los ojos y viendo que una gran multitud venía a él, dijo a Felipe: «¿Dónde compraremos pan para que coman estos?»
6
τοῦτο δὲ ἔλεγεν πειράζων αὐτόν, αὐτὸς γὰρ ᾔδει τί ἔμελλεν ποιεῖν.
But he said this to test him, for he himself knew what he was about to do.note
Pero decía esto para probarlo, porque él sabía lo que iba a hacer.
7
ἀπεκρίθη αὐτῷ Φίλιππος· Διακοσίων δηναρίων ἄρτοι οὐκ ἀρκοῦσιν αὐτοῖς ἵνα ἕκαστος βραχύ τι λάβῃ.
Philip answered him, ‘Two hundred denarii worth of bread would not be enough for each of them to get a little.’note
Felipe le respondió: «Doscientos denarios de pan no bastarían para que cada uno de ellos tomase un poco.»
8
λέγει αὐτῷ εἷς ἐκ τῶν μαθητῶν αὐτοῦ, Ἀνδρέας ὁ ἀδελφὸς Σίμωνος Πέτρου·
One of his disciples, Andrew, Simon Peter’s brother, said to him,note
Uno de sus discípulos, Andrés, hermano de Simón Pedro, le dijo:
9
Ἔστιν παιδάριον ὧδε ὃς ἔχει πέντε ἄρτους κριθίνους καὶ δύο ὀψάρια· ἀλλὰ ταῦτα τί ἐστιν εἰς τοσούτους;
‘There is a young boy here who has five barley loaves and two small fish, but what are these for so many?’note
«Aquí hay un muchacho que tiene cinco panes de cebada y dos pececillos; ¿pero qué es esto para tantos?»
10
εἶπεν ὁ Ἰησοῦς· Ποιήσατε τοὺς ἀνθρώπους ἀναπεσεῖν. ἦν δὲ χόρτος πολὺς ἐν τῷ τόπῳ. ἀνέπεσαν οὖν οἱ ἄνδρες τὸν ἀριθμὸν ὡς πεντακισχίλιοι.
Jesus said, ‘Make the people sit down.’ Now there was much grass in the place. So the men sat down, in number about five thousand.note
Jesús dijo: «Haced recostar a la gente.» Había mucha hierba en aquel lugar; se recostaron, pues, los hombres, en número como de cinco mil.
11

Then Jesus took the loaves, and having given thanks, he distributed them to those reclining; so also of the fish, as much as they wanted.note
Y tomó Jesús los panes, y habiendo dado gracias, los repartió a los que estaban recostados; asimismo de los peces, cuanto querían.
12
ὡς δὲ ἐνεπλήσθησαν λέγει τοῖς μαθηταῖς αὐτοῦ· Συναγάγετε τὰ περισσεύσαντα κλάσματα, ἵνα μή τι ἀπόληται.
And when they were filled, he said to his disciples, ‘Gather up the fragments left over, so that nothing may be lost.’note
Y cuando se saciaron, dijo a sus discípulos: «Recoged los pedazos que sobraron, para que no se pierda nada.»
13
συνήγαγον οὖν, καὶ ἐγέμισαν δώδεκα κοφίνους κλασμάτων ἐκ τῶν πέντε ἄρτων τῶν κριθίνων ἃ ἐπερίσσευσαν τοῖς βεβρωκόσιν.
So they gathered them up, and filled twelve baskets with fragments from the five barley loaves, left over by those who had eaten.note
Los recogieron, pues, y llenaron doce cestas de pedazos de los cinco panes de cebada, que sobraron a los que habían comido.
14
οἱ οὖν ἄνθρωποι ἰδόντες ὃ ἐποίησεν σημεῖον ἔλεγον ὅτι Οὗτός ἐστιν ἀληθῶς ὁ προφήτης ὁ ἐρχόμενος εἰς τὸν κόσμον.
When the people saw the sign that he had done, they said, ‘This is truly the prophet who is to come into the world.’note
Al ver la gente la señal que había hecho, decían: «Este es verdaderamente el profeta que había de venir al mundo.»
15
Ἰησοῦς οὖν γνοὺς ὅτι μέλλουσιν ἔρχεσθαι καὶ ἁρπάζειν αὐτὸν ἵνα ποιήσωσιν βασιλέα ἀνεχώρησεν πάλιν εἰς τὸ ὄρος αὐτὸς μόνος.
Jesus therefore, knowing that they were about to come and seize him to make him king, withdrew again to the mountain by himself alone.note
Sabiendo Jesús que iban a venir para apoderarse de él y hacerlo rey, volvió a retirarse al monte él solo.
16
Ὡς δὲ ὀψία ἐγένετο κατέβησαν οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ τὴν θάλασσαν,
Now when evening came, his disciples went down to the sea,note
Al atardecer, descendieron sus discípulos al mar,
17
καὶ ἐμβάντες εἰς πλοῖον ἤρχοντο πέραν τῆς θαλάσσης εἰς Καφαρναούμ. καὶ σκοτία ἤδη ἐγεγόνει καὶ οὔπω ἐληλύθει πρὸς αὐτοὺς ὁ Ἰησοῦς,
and getting into a boat, they were going over the sea to Capernaum. It was now dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them.note
y entrando en una barca, se dirigían al otro lado del mar hacia Capernaum. Ya había oscurecido, y Jesús no había venido a ellos.
18
ἥ τε θάλασσα ἀνέμου μεγάλου πνέοντος διεγείρετο
The sea was rising because a strong wind was blowing.note
Y el mar se embravecía porque soplaba un gran viento.
19
ἐληλακότες οὖν ὡς σταδίους εἴκοσι πέντε ἢ τριάκοντα θεωροῦσιν τὸν Ἰησοῦν περιπατοῦντα ἐπὶ τῆς θαλάσσης καὶ ἐγγὺς τοῦ πλοίου γινόμενον, καὶ ἐφοβήθησαν.
When they had rowed about twenty-five or thirty stadia, they saw Jesus walking on the sea and coming near the boat, and they were afraid.note
Cuando habían remado como veinticinco o treinta estadios, vieron a Jesús que andaba sobre el mar y se acercaba a la barca, y tuvieron miedo.
20
ὁ δὲ λέγει αὐτοῖς· Ἐγώ εἰμι, μὴ φοβεῖσθε.
But he said to them, ‘It is I; do not be afraid.’note
Pero él les dijo: «Yo soy; no temáis.»
21
ἤθελον οὖν λαβεῖν αὐτὸν εἰς τὸ πλοῖον, καὶ εὐθέως ἐγένετο τὸ πλοῖον ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς εἰς ἣν ὑπῆγον.
Then they were willing to take him into the boat, and immediately the boat was at the land to which they were going.note
Entonces quisieron recibirlo en la barca, y en seguida la barca llegó a la tierra adonde iban.
22

On the next day the crowd that stood on the other side of the sea saw that there had been only one boat there, and that Jesus had not entered the boat with his disciples, but that his disciples had gone away alone.note
Al día siguiente, la multitud que estaba al otro lado del mar vio que no había allí sino una barca, y que Jesús no había entrado en ella con sus discípulos, sino que sus discípulos se habían ido solos.
23
ἀλλὰ ἦλθεν πλοιάρια ἐκ Τιβεριάδος ἐγγὺς τοῦ τόπου ὅπου ἔφαγον τὸν ἄρτον εὐχαριστήσαντος τοῦ κυρίου.
Other boats from Tiberias came near the place where they had eaten the bread after the Lord had given thanks.note
Pero otras barcas habían llegado de Tiberias, cerca del lugar donde habían comido el pan después de haber dado gracias el Señor.
24

So when the crowd saw that Jesus was not there, nor his disciples, they themselves got into the boats and went to Capernaum, seeking Jesus.note
Cuando vio, pues, la gente que Jesús no estaba allí, ni sus discípulos, entraron ellos mismos en las barcas y fueron a Capernaum, buscando a Jesús.
25

Y hallándolo al otro lado del mar, le dijeron: «Rabino, ¿cuándo llegaste acá?»

Jesús les respondió: «De cierto, de cierto os digo, me buscáis no porque habéis visto señales, sino porque comisteis el pan y os saciasteis.
27

Trabajad no por la comida que perece, sino por la comida que a vida eterna permanece, la cual el Hijo del Hombre os dará; porque a este señaló Dios el Padre.»
28

Then they said to him, ‘What must we do, to be working the works of God?’note
Entonces le dijeron: «¿Qué debemos hacer para poner por obra las obras de Dios?»
29
ἀπεκρίθη ὁ Ἰησοῦς καὶ εἶπεν αὐτοῖς· Τοῦτό ἐστιν τὸ ἔργον τοῦ θεοῦ ἵνα πιστεύητε εἰς ὃν ἀπέστειλεν ἐκεῖνος.
Jesus answered and said to them, ‘This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent.’note
Respondió Jesús y les dijo: «Esta es la obra de Dios, que creáis en el que él ha enviado.»
30
εἶπον οὖν αὐτῷ· Τί οὖν ποιεῖς σὺ σημεῖον, ἵνα ἴδωμεν καὶ πιστεύσωμέν σοι; τί ἐργάζῃ;
So they said to him, ‘Then what sign do you do, that we may see and believe you? What work do you perform?note
Le dijeron entonces: «¿Qué señal, pues, haces tú, para que veamos y te creamos? ¿Qué obra haces?
31

Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, just as it is written, “He gave them bread out of heaven to eat.”’note
Nuestros padres comieron el maná en el desierto, como está escrito: «Pan del cielo les dio a comer.»»

Porque el pan de Dios es aquel que descendió del cielo y da vida al mundo.»

They said to him, ‘Lord, give us this bread always.’note
Le dijeron: «Señor, danos siempre este pan.»

Jesús les dijo: «Yo soy el pan de vida; el que a mí viene, nunca tendrá hambre, y el que en mí cree, no tendrá sed jamás.

Mas os he dicho que aunque me habéis visto, no creéis.
37

All that the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never cast out,note
Todo lo que el Padre me da, vendrá a mí; y al que a mí viene, no lo echo fuera,

porque he descendido del cielo, no para hacer mi voluntad, sino la voluntad del que me envió.

And this is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but should raise it up at the last day.note
Y esta es la voluntad del que me envió: que de todo lo que me diere, no pierda yo nada, sino que lo resucite en el día postrero.
40

Porque esta es la voluntad de mi Padre: que todo aquel que ve al Hijo, y cree en él, tenga vida eterna; y yo lo resucitaré en el día postrero.»

Murmuraban entonces de él los judíos, porque había dicho: «Yo soy el pan que descendió del cielo.»

Y decían: «¿No es este Jesús, el hijo de José, cuyo padre y madre nosotros conocemos? ¿Cómo, pues, dice este: «Del cielo he descendido»?»

Ninguno puede venir a mí, si el Padre que me envió no lo trajere; y yo lo resucitaré en el día postrero.

ἔστιν γεγραμμένον ἐν τοῖς προφήταις· Καὶ ἔσονται πάντες διδακτοὶ θεοῦ· πᾶς ὁ ἀκούσας παρὰ τοῦ πατρὸς καὶ μαθὼν ἔρχεται πρὸς ἐμέ
It is written in the prophets, “And they will all be taught by God.” Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me —note
Escrito está en los profetas: «Y serán todos enseñados por Dios.» Así, todo aquel que oyó al Padre y aprendió de él, viene a mí —
46
οὐχ ὅτι τὸν πατέρα ἑώρακέν τις εἰ μὴ ὁ ὢν παρὰ τοῦ θεοῦ, οὗτος ἑώρακεν τὸν πατέρα.
not that anyone has seen the Father except him who is from God; he has seen the Father.note
no que alguno haya visto al Padre, sino aquel que vino de Dios; este ha visto al Padre.

Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever believes has eternal life.note
De cierto, de cierto os digo: El que cree en mí tiene vida eterna.

I am the bread of life.note
Yo soy el pan de vida.

Este es el pan que desciende del cielo, para que el que de él come, no muera.

I am the living bread that came down out of heaven; if anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever; and the bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh.’note
Yo soy el pan vivo que descendió del cielo; si alguno comiere de este pan, vivirá para siempre; y el pan que yo daré es mi carne, la cual yo daré por la vida del mundo.»
52
Ἐμάχοντο οὖν πρὸς ἀλλήλους οἱ Ἰουδαῖοι λέγοντες· Πῶς δύναται οὗτος ἡμῖν δοῦναι τὴν σάρκα αὐτοῦ φαγεῖν;
The Jews then quarreled among themselves, saying, ‘How can this man give us his flesh to eat?’note
Entonces los judíos contendían entre sí, diciendo: «¿Cómo puede este darnos a comer su carne?»
53

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–15 · the sign, and the crown they tried to force on him

“Now the Passover, the feast of the Jews, was near” (v4) is one of only a handful of explicit Passover markers in this Gospel (2:13, 6:4, and 11:55/12:1 before the final one at 13:1) — the seam that lets readers count roughly three years of ministry between the first Passover in Jerusalem and the last. It also puts the feast that will define the discourse to come — a meal, bread, a body given — on the calendar before a single loaf is broken.

⚠ The crowd’s two responses to the sign (vv14-15) run in the same direction and overshoot it: ‘the prophet who is to come’ draws on Deuteronomy 18:15’s promised prophet like Moses (the same text Peter will cite directly in Acts 3), and from there it is a short step to trying to make him king by force — a title Jesus withdraws from rather than accepts, alone, up a mountain, exactly where this same Gospel will later have him accept a very different kind of kingship (18:36-37).

Verses 16–21 · ‘it is I’ — on a lake in a storm

“It is I; do not be afraid” (v20) translates egō eimi, the same two words this Gospel elsewhere lets stand absolutely, without a predicate, as a claim of divine self-identification (8:58, 18:5-6). In this verse’s own context it is simply the ordinary way a Greek speaker would say ‘it’s me’ — a frightened boat crew needs identification, not theology, in the dark. ⚠ Whether John means his readers to hear the deeper register too, on a lake at night with the wind against them, is a real question the grammar alone cannot answer; this Gospel has already shown a habit of letting ordinary words carry a second, heavier sense (4:10’s ‘living water,’ 3:3’s ‘born again/from above’), which is the whole reason the question is worth asking here at all.

Verses 22–29 · a boat-count that does not add up

The crowd’s arithmetic (vv22-24) is oddly precise for a detail that does no narrative work except to make a theological point: they counted the boats, know Jesus did not leave in one, and still cannot explain how he is now in Capernaum — the walking on water is confirmed backhandedly, by people who never saw it happen and are only totting up what does not fit.

“Do not work for the food that perishes” (v27) redirects a crowd that came back for more bread, not more meaning — the same crowd Jesus had just told, bluntly, that they missed the sign entirely and only wanted a second lunch (v26). "This is the work of God, that you believe" (v29) answers their own question about "works" (plural, v28) with a single verb: not a checklist, one act.

Verses 30–35 · manna demanded, and the first ‘I am’ with a name attached

⚠ The crowd asks for a sign after already receiving one, and reaches for the same precedent Israel’s own history supplies: manna in the wilderness (v31, quoting Psalm 78:24, itself recalling Exodus 16 — the chapter that spent its own six notes on exactly this bread, including the pun buried in its Hebrew name, man, ‘what is it?’). Jesus’s answer corrects the attribution before it corrects anything else: not Moses who gave it, but the Father, and not in the past tense — ‘my Father gives you the true bread’ (v32), present tense, still happening.

“I am the bread of life” (v35) is the first of this Gospel’s seven egō eimi sayings built with a stated predicate — the bread of life, the light of the world, the door, the good shepherd, the resurrection and the life, the way/truth/life, the true vine — a series this chapter opens and the rest of the book completes one at a time.

Verses 36–44 · raised up at the last day — a refrain begins

⚠ “Raise him up at the last day” (v39) is the first of four nearly identical occurrences of this exact clause in this one chapter (v39, v40, v44, and again at v54) — a refrain repeated more densely here than anywhere else in the Gospel, closing four separate movements of the discourse on the same note, as if the sermon keeps returning to the one thing it will not let the crowd forget.

The Jews’ grumbling (v41, egogguzon) uses the identical root that fell nine times across Exodus 16 for Israel’s complaint against Moses over — pointedly — the very same bread. A crowd fed miraculously grumbles about the one who fed them; the pattern repeats across fourteen centuries with the identical verb. ⚠ The same shelf split recurs here that this translation flagged in Exodus 16: KJV and ASV both keep ‘murmured’; NIV and ESV both modernize to ‘grumbled.’

Verses 45–51 · living bread, and the word that turns the crowd

“They will all be taught by God” (v45) draws on Isaiah 54:13’s promise of a restored Jerusalem where every child is God’s own pupil — cited here as already underway, in the middle of an argument about bread, to the people the prophecy was made to.

⚠ The turn is v51’s last clause. Everything before it can be heard as a fairly conventional metaphor — bread standing for teaching, wisdom, the Torah itself, images already at home in Jewish thought. ‘The bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh’ will not resolve into metaphor that easily, and the text marks the change: it is exactly here, at this sentence, that the audience stops merely grumbling (v41) and starts fighting each other over what he could possibly mean (v52).

Verses 52–59 · a verb that gets cruder as the offense grows

⚠ The Greek changes its verb for ‘eat’ at v54, and does not change back. Every occurrence of eating up to this point uses phagein/esthiō, the ordinary, colorless verb for eating (vv5, 23, 26, 31, 49-53). From v54 on, six times running (vv54, 56, 57, 58), John switches to trōgō — a coarser, more physical word, properly ‘to gnaw, to munch,’ used elsewhere of animals feeding. This translation renders it gnaws to keep the harder word audible where English versions almost universally flatten both Greek verbs to the same ‘eat.’ The shift in vocabulary tracks the shift in offense exactly: the more literal and physical the language gets, the more of the audience it loses.

The scene is anchored, almost as an afterthought, to a real place: ‘in the synagogue … in Capernaum’ (v59) — the town this Gospel has already made Jesus’s base of operations (2:12), now the site of his hardest teaching.

Verses 60–66 · the hard saying, and the first walking away

⚠ This is the moment the note at the end of chapter 5 already named as waiting here: the first point in this Gospel where some of Jesus’s own disciples — not outsiders, not hostile Pharisees, people who had been walking with him — hear him out and turn back (v66). Nothing is retracted or softened to keep them; v61-65 restates the same claim in harder terms, not easier ones, ending on the same predestination-adjacent line that opened this whole section (v44, v65: no one comes unless given/drawn by the Father).

“This is a hard saying” (v60, sklēros ho logos houtos) is the disciples’ own verdict, not the narrator’s; sklēros is the word for something literally hard or harsh — the same root behind English ‘sclerosis.’ ‘Does this offend you?’ (v61, skandalizei) names the reaction precisely: not confusion but scandal, a stumbling block they trip over rather than a puzzle they cannot solve.

Verses 67–71 · to whom shall we go — and a title the shelf can’t agree on

“Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life” (v68) is not an answer to the hard teaching — Peter does not claim to understand it any better than the disciples who left. It is a confession that there is nowhere else worth going, offered by someone who is staying without having resolved the difficulty.

⚠ V69’s title for Jesus is a genuine textual crux, not just a translation choice. KJV reads ‘that Christ, the Son of the living God’ — matching Peter’s famous confession at Matthew 16:16 almost word for word. ASV, NIV and ESV all read instead ‘the Holy One of God,’ the earlier and better-attested reading — and the harder one to explain as a later change, since a scribe harmonizing toward the Synoptics’ famous line is a far likelier accident than one harmonizing away from it. RV60 follows the Byzantine reading, ‘el Cristo, el Hijo del Dios viviente’; NVI reads ‘el Santo de Dios.’ This translation follows the earlier text.

The chapter that opened with bread enough for five thousand closes on one man revealed as a devil (v70) — chosen, present, still one of the Twelve, named only in the narrator’s own aside (v71) rather than by Jesus in the moment.

Patterns worth carrying forward.

⚠ Renderings chosen. Trōgō (vv54, 56, 57, 58) rendered gnaws rather than folded into the same ‘eat’ used for phagein earlier in the chapter, so the Greek's own escalation stays visible in English. Egō eimi (v20, v35, v48, v51) kept as the plain ‘I am,’ the predicate present where the Greek has one and absent where it does not, rather than smoothing every instance to the same register. And ‘the Holy One of God’ (v69) follows the earlier text against the Byzantine ‘Christ, the Son of the living God,’ noted in full in the verse's own note rather than silently adopted.

Echoes paid. The disciples turning back (v66) is exactly what chapter 5's own closing note named as waiting in this chapter, by number, before it was translated. The manna of Exodus 16, cited directly at v31 and answered at v49-51 with living bread that does not leave its eaters dead in the wilderness. And gogguzō, grumbling (v41, v43, v61) — the identical root Israel used against Moses over the same substance, fourteen centuries earlier.

Echoes planted. The seven egō eimi-with-predicate sayings, opened here at v35 with bread and completed one at a time through the rest of the book. ‘Raised up at the last day’ (vv39, 40, 44, 54), a refrain this Gospel will return to at 11:24. And Judas, named for the first time as the one who will betray him (v71) — a fact the reader now carries into every scene he appears in from here on.

⚠ Left open on purpose. Whether egō eimi at v20 is meant to carry its heavier, absolute sense or is simply idiomatic self-identification in the dark — the library states the question and does not resolve it by translation choice alone. And exactly how literally the ‘eating’ of vv53-58 was meant — sacramental, purely figurative, or something this chapter's own audience was never meant to fully resolve either, since many of them left rather than ask.

Next installment in this book, whenever it is translated: John 7, where Jesus teaches at the Feast of Tabernacles and the crowd divides over whether he is the Christ — the same division, in miniature, that this chapter's ending already showed in full.
