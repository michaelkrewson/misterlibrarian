Jeroboam’s son Abijah falls sick, and his wife goes in disguise to Ahijah at Shiloh, who is blind and knows her by the sound of her feet. He gives her the sentence on the house of Jeroboam, and the boy dies as she reaches the threshold at Tirzah. Jeroboam dies after twenty-two years. Rehoboam reigns in Judah, and Judah builds high places; Shishak of Egypt comes up against Jerusalem and takes the treasures and the shields of gold, and bronze shields replace them.
The one date is the fifth year of Rehoboam (1 Kings 14:25), about 925 BC on the usual reckoning. The sickness of Abijah is dated only ‘at that time’.

בָּעֵת הַהִיא, חָלָה אֲבִיָּה בֶן-יָרָבְעָם.
At that time Abijah son of Jeroboam fell sick.note
En aquel tiempo enfermó Abías hijo de Jeroboam.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר יָרָבְעָם לְאִשְׁתּוֹ, קוּמִי נָא וְהִשְׁתַּנִּית, וְלֹא יֵדְעוּ, כִּי-אתי אַתְּ אֵשֶׁת יָרָבְעָם; וְהָלַכְתְּ שִׁלֹה, הִנֵּה-שָׁם אֲחִיָּה הַנָּבִיא--הוּא-דִבֶּר עָלַי לְמֶלֶךְ, עַל-הָעָם הַזֶּה.
And Jeroboam said to his wife: “Arise, please, and disguise yourself, so that they will not know that you are the wife of Jeroboam, and go to Shiloh. Look, Ahijah the prophet is there; he is the one who spoke of me as king over this people.note
Y Jeroboam dijo a su esposa: «Levántate, por favor, y disfrázate, para que no sepan que tú eres la esposa de Jeroboam, y ve a Silo. Mira, allí está Ahías el profeta; él es el que habló de mí como rey sobre este pueblo.
3
וְלָקַחַתְּ בְּיָדֵךְ עֲשָׂרָה לֶחֶם וְנִקֻּדִים, וּבַקְבֻּק דְּבַשׁ--וּבָאת אֵלָיו; הוּא יַגִּיד לָךְ, מַה-יִּהְיֶה לַנָּעַר.
And take in your hand ten loaves, and crumb-cakes, and a flask of honey, and go to him; he will tell you what will happen to the boy.”note
Y toma en tu mano diez panes, y tortas de migas, y un frasco de miel, y ve a él; él te dirá lo que le va a pasar al muchacho».
4
וַתַּעַשׂ כֵּן, אֵשֶׁת יָרָבְעָם, וַתָּקָם וַתֵּלֶךְ שִׁלֹה, וַתָּבֹא בֵּית אֲחִיָּה; וַאֲחִיָּהוּ לֹא-יָכֹל לִרְאוֹת, כִּי קָמוּ עֵינָיו מִשֵּׂיבוֹ.  {פ}
And Jeroboam’s wife did so; she arose and went to Shiloh, and came to the house of Ahijah. Now Ahijah could not see, for his eyes were set because of his age.note
Y la esposa de Jeroboam lo hizo así; se levantó y fue a Silo, y llegó a la casa de Ahías. Y Ahías no podía ver, porque sus ojos se habían quedado fijos a causa de su vejez.
5
וַיהוָה אָמַר אֶל-אֲחִיָּהוּ, הִנֵּה אֵשֶׁת יָרָבְעָם בָּאָה לִדְרֹשׁ דָּבָר מֵעִמְּךָ אֶל-בְּנָהּ כִּי-חֹלֶה הוּא--כָּזֹה וְכָזֶה, תְּדַבֵּר אֵלֶיהָ; וִיהִי כְבֹאָהּ, וְהִיא מִתְנַכֵּרָה.
And Jehovah had said to Ahijah: “Look, the wife of Jeroboam is coming to inquire of you about her son, for he is sick. Thus and thus you shall speak to her. And when she comes, she will be making herself unrecognizable.”note
Y Jehová había dicho a Ahías: «Mira, la esposa de Jeroboam viene a consultarte acerca de su hijo, porque está enfermo. Así y así le hablarás. Y cuando llegue, estará haciéndose irreconocible».
6
וַיְהִי כִשְׁמֹעַ אֲחִיָּהוּ אֶת-קוֹל רַגְלֶיהָ, בָּאָה בַפֶּתַח, וַיֹּאמֶר, בֹּאִי אֵשֶׁת יָרָבְעָם; לָמָּה זֶּה, אַתְּ מִתְנַכֵּרָה, וְאָנֹכִי, שָׁלוּחַ אֵלַיִךְ קָשָׁה.
And when Ahijah heard the sound of her feet as she came in at the doorway, he said: “Come in, wife of Jeroboam. Why are you making yourself unrecognizable? And I am sent to you with a hard thing.note
Y cuando Ahías oyó el ruido de sus pies al entrar ella por la puerta, dijo: «Entra, esposa de Jeroboam. ¿Por qué te haces irreconocible? Y yo soy enviado a ti con algo duro.
7
לְכִי אִמְרִי לְיָרָבְעָם, כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, יַעַן אֲשֶׁר הֲרִמֹתִיךָ, מִתּוֹךְ הָעָם; וָאֶתֶּנְךָ נָגִיד, עַל עַמִּי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
Go, say to Jeroboam: ‘Thus says Jehovah the God of Israel: Because I lifted you up from among the people, and made you leader over my people Israel,note
Ve, di a Jeroboam: “Así dice Jehová el Dios de Israel: Por cuanto te alcé de en medio del pueblo, y te hice caudillo sobre mi pueblo Israel,
8
וָאֶקְרַע אֶת-הַמַּמְלָכָה מִבֵּית דָּוִד, וָאֶתְּנֶהָ לָךְ; וְלֹא-הָיִיתָ כְּעַבְדִּי דָוִד, אֲשֶׁר שָׁמַר מִצְוֺתַי וַאֲשֶׁר-הָלַךְ אַחֲרַי בְּכָל-לְבָבוֹ, לַעֲשׂוֹת, רַק הַיָּשָׁר בְּעֵינָי.
and tore the kingdom from the house of David and gave it to you; yet you have not been like my servant David, who kept my commandments and walked after me with all his heart, to do only what was right in my eyes,note
y rasgué el reino de la casa de David y te lo di; y tú no has sido como mi siervo David, que guardó mis mandamientos y anduvo en pos de mí con todo su corazón, para hacer solamente lo recto a mis ojos,
9
וַתָּרַע לַעֲשׂוֹת, מִכֹּל אֲשֶׁר-הָיוּ לְפָנֶיךָ; וַתֵּלֶךְ וַתַּעֲשֶׂה-לְּךָ אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים וּמַסֵּכוֹת, לְהַכְעִיסֵנִי, וְאֹתִי הִשְׁלַכְתָּ, אַחֲרֵי גַוֶּךָ.
but you have done evil more than all who were before you, and you have gone and made for yourself other gods and molten images to provoke me to anger, and me you have cast behind your back;note
sino que has hecho el mal más que todos los que hubo antes de ti, y has ido y te has hecho otros dioses e imágenes fundidas para provocarme a ira, y a mí me has echado detrás de tu espalda;
10
לָכֵן, הִנְנִי מֵבִיא רָעָה אֶל-בֵּית יָרָבְעָם, וְהִכְרַתִּי לְיָרָבְעָם מַשְׁתִּין בְּקִיר, עָצוּר וְעָזוּב בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל; וּבִעַרְתִּי אַחֲרֵי בֵית-יָרָבְעָם, כַּאֲשֶׁר יְבַעֵר הַגָּלָל עַד-תֻּמּוֹ.
therefore, look, I am bringing evil upon the house of Jeroboam, and I will cut off from Jeroboam anyone who urinates against the wall, whether shut up or set free in Israel; and I will burn out after the house of Jeroboam, as one burns out dung until it is gone.note
por eso, mira, yo traigo el mal sobre la casa de Jeroboam, y cortaré de Jeroboam a todo el que orine contra la pared, sea encerrado o libre en Israel; y quemaré tras la casa de Jeroboam, como se quema el estiércol hasta que se acaba.
11
הַמֵּת לְיָרָבְעָם בָּעִיר, יֹאכְלוּ הַכְּלָבִים, וְהַמֵּת בַּשָּׂדֶה, יֹאכְלוּ עוֹף הַשָּׁמָיִם:  כִּי יְהוָה, דִּבֵּר.
Whoever of Jeroboam’s dies in the city the dogs shall eat, and whoever dies in the field the birds of the heavens shall eat; for Jehovah has spoken.’note
Al de Jeroboam que muera en la ciudad, se lo comerán los perros, y al que muera en el campo, se lo comerán las aves de los cielos; porque Jehová ha hablado”.
12
וְאַתְּ קוּמִי, לְכִי לְבֵיתֵךְ; בְּבֹאָה רַגְלַיִךְ הָעִירָה, וּמֵת הַיָּלֶד.
And you, arise, go to your house. When your feet come into the city, the child will die.note
Y tú, levántate, vete a tu casa. Cuando tus pies entren en la ciudad, el niño morirá.
13
וְסָפְדוּ-לוֹ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְקָבְרוּ אֹתוֹ--כִּי-זֶה לְבַדּוֹ, יָבֹא לְיָרָבְעָם אֶל-קָבֶר:  יַעַן נִמְצָא-בוֹ דָּבָר טוֹב, אֶל-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--בְּבֵית יָרָבְעָם.
And all Israel will mourn for him and bury him; for this one alone of Jeroboam’s will come to a grave, because in him something good toward Jehovah the God of Israel has been found in the house of Jeroboam.note
Y todo Israel hará duelo por él y lo sepultará; porque solo este, de los de Jeroboam, entrará en sepultura, por cuanto en él se ha hallado algo bueno para con Jehová el Dios de Israel, en la casa de Jeroboam.
14
וְהֵקִים יְהוָה לוֹ מֶלֶךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֲשֶׁר יַכְרִית אֶת-בֵּית יָרָבְעָם זֶה הַיּוֹם; וּמֶה, גַּם-עָתָּה.
And Jehovah will raise up for himself a king over Israel who will cut off the house of Jeroboam. This is the day; and what? Even now.note
Y Jehová levantará para sí un rey sobre Israel que cortará la casa de Jeroboam. Este es el día; ¿y qué? Ya ahora.
15
וְהִכָּה יְהוָה אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, כַּאֲשֶׁר יָנוּד הַקָּנֶה בַּמַּיִם, וְנָתַשׁ אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל מֵעַל הָאֲדָמָה הַטּוֹבָה הַזֹּאת אֲשֶׁר נָתַן לַאֲבוֹתֵיהֶם, וְזֵרָם מֵעֵבֶר לַנָּהָר:  יַעַן, אֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ אֶת-אֲשֵׁרֵיהֶם--מַכְעִיסִים, אֶת-יְהוָה.
And Jehovah will strike Israel, as the reed sways in the water; and he will uproot Israel from upon this good ground that he gave to their fathers, and will scatter them beyond the River, because they made their Asherahs, provoking Jehovah to anger.note
Y Jehová herirá a Israel, como se mece la caña en el agua; y arrancará a Israel de sobre este buen suelo que dio a sus padres, y los esparcirá más allá del Río, por cuanto hicieron sus Aserás, provocando a ira a Jehová.
16
וְיִתֵּן, אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל--בִּגְלַל חַטֹּאות יָרָבְעָם, אֲשֶׁר חָטָא, וַאֲשֶׁר הֶחֱטִיא, אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And he will give up Israel because of the sins of Jeroboam, which he sinned and which he made Israel sin.”note
Y entregará a Israel por causa de los pecados de Jeroboam, que él pecó y que hizo pecar a Israel».
17
וַתָּקָם אֵשֶׁת יָרָבְעָם, וַתֵּלֶךְ וַתָּבֹא תִרְצָתָה; הִיא בָּאָה בְסַף-הַבַּיִת, וְהַנַּעַר מֵת.
And Jeroboam’s wife arose and went, and came to Tirzah. As she came to the threshold of the house, the boy died.note
Y la esposa de Jeroboam se levantó y se fue, y llegó a Tirsa. Al llegar ella al umbral de la casa, el muchacho murió.
18
וַיִּקְבְּרוּ אֹתוֹ וַיִּסְפְּדוּ-לוֹ, כָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל:  כִּדְבַר יְהוָה אֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר, בְּיַד-עַבְדּוֹ אֲחִיָּהוּ הַנָּבִיא.
And they buried him, and all Israel mourned for him, according to the word of Jehovah which he spoke by the hand of his servant Ahijah the prophet.note
Y lo sepultaron, y todo Israel hizo duelo por él, conforme a la palabra de Jehová que él habló por mano de su siervo Ahías el profeta.
19
וְיֶתֶר דִּבְרֵי יָרָבְעָם, אֲשֶׁר נִלְחַם וַאֲשֶׁר מָלָךְ:  הִנָּם כְּתוּבִים, עַל-סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים--לְמַלְכֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And the rest of the acts of Jeroboam, how he fought and how he reigned, look, they are written in the book of the acts of the days of the kings of Israel.note
Y lo demás de los hechos de Jeroboam, cómo peleó y cómo reinó, mira, está escrito en el libro de los hechos de los días de los reyes de Israel.
20
וְהַיָּמִים אֲשֶׁר מָלַךְ יָרָבְעָם, עֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁתַּיִם שָׁנָה; וַיִּשְׁכַּב, עִם-אֲבֹתָיו, וַיִּמְלֹךְ נָדָב בְּנוֹ, תַּחְתָּיו.  {פ}
And the days that Jeroboam reigned were twenty-two years; and he lay down with his fathers, and Nadab his son reigned in his place.note
Y los días que Jeroboam reinó fueron veintidós años; y se acostó con sus padres, y Nadab su hijo reinó en su lugar.
21
וּרְחַבְעָם, בֶּן-שְׁלֹמֹה, מָלַךְ, בִּיהוּדָה; בֶּן-אַרְבָּעִים וְאַחַת שָׁנָה רְחַבְעָם בְּמָלְכוֹ וּשְׁבַע עֶשְׂרֵה שָׁנָה מָלַךְ בִּירוּשָׁלִַם, הָעִיר אֲשֶׁר-בָּחַר יְהוָה לָשׂוּם אֶת-שְׁמוֹ שָׁם מִכֹּל שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְשֵׁם אִמּוֹ, נַעֲמָה הָעַמֹּנִית.
And Rehoboam son of Solomon reigned in Judah. Rehoboam was forty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city that Jehovah had chosen out of all the tribes of Israel to put his name there. And his mother’s name was Naamah the Ammonite.note
Y Roboam hijo de Salomón reinó en Judá. Roboam tenía cuarenta y un años cuando comenzó a reinar, y reinó diecisiete años en Jerusalén, la ciudad que Jehová había escogido de entre todas las tribus de Israel para poner allí su nombre. Y el nombre de su madre era Naama la amonita.
22
וַיַּעַשׂ יְהוּדָה הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה; וַיְקַנְאוּ אֹתוֹ, מִכֹּל אֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ אֲבֹתָם, בְּחַטֹּאתָם, אֲשֶׁר חָטָאוּ.
And Judah did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah; and they roused him to jealousy more than all that their fathers had done, with their sins that they sinned.note
Y Judá hizo lo malo a los ojos de Jehová; y lo hicieron sentir celos más que todo lo que habían hecho sus padres, con sus pecados que pecaron.
23
וַיִּבְנוּ גַם-הֵמָּה לָהֶם בָּמוֹת וּמַצֵּבוֹת, וַאֲשֵׁרִים, עַל כָּל-גִּבְעָה גְבֹהָה, וְתַחַת כָּל-עֵץ רַעֲנָן.
For they too built for themselves high places and standing stones and Asherahs on every high hill and under every leafy tree.note
Porque también ellos se edificaron lugares altos y piedras alzadas y Aserás sobre toda colina alta y debajo de todo árbol frondoso.
24
וְגַם-קָדֵשׁ, הָיָה בָאָרֶץ:  עָשׂוּ, כְּכֹל הַתּוֹעֲבֹת הַגּוֹיִם, אֲשֶׁר הוֹרִישׁ יְהוָה, מִפְּנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {פ}
And there was also the consecrated man in the land. They did according to all the abominations of the nations that Jehovah had dispossessed from before the children of Israel.note
Y hubo también el hombre consagrado en la tierra. Hicieron conforme a todas las abominaciones de las naciones que Jehová había desalojado de delante de los hijos de Israel.
25
וַיְהִי בַּשָּׁנָה הַחֲמִישִׁית, לַמֶּלֶךְ רְחַבְעָם; עָלָה שושק שִׁישַׁק מֶלֶךְ-מִצְרַיִם, עַל-יְרוּשָׁלִָם.
And it was in the fifth year of King Rehoboam that Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem.note
Y en el año quinto del rey Roboam, Sisac rey de Egipto subió contra Jerusalén.
26
וַיִּקַּח אֶת-אֹצְרוֹת בֵּית-יְהוָה, וְאֶת-אוֹצְרוֹת בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ, וְאֶת-הַכֹּל, לָקָח; וַיִּקַּח אֶת-כָּל-מָגִנֵּי הַזָּהָב, אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה שְׁלֹמֹה.
And he took the treasures of the house of Jehovah and the treasures of the king’s house; he took it all. And he took all the shields of gold that Solomon had made.note
Y tomó los tesoros de la casa de Jehová y los tesoros de la casa del rey; lo tomó todo. Y tomó todos los escudos de oro que Salomón había hecho.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 1 Kings 14:1–4 Jeroboam’s wife in disguise — ten loaves, a flask of honey, and a prophet who cannot see

Two tellings. The Greek Bible tells this story in another place and another way. In the Vaticanus tradition, verses 1–20 are not here at all; the story stands earlier, among the long additions after 12:24, and differs in its details. There the mother has a name, Ano, the sister of Shishak’s wife; the prophet is sixty years old; and a servant is sent out to meet her (see the note at 1 Kings 12, now on these pages). Editions that print verses 1–20 in this place take them from the Alexandrinus. This page translates the Hebrew and reports the rest.

He sends to Shiloh. Jeroboam has two calves of gold, a house of high places and priests of his own making (1 Kings 12:28–31, now on these pages), and when his son falls sick he sends to none of them. He sends to the old prophet who gave him the kingdom. Abijah means “my father is Jah”, so the son of the calf-maker carries the divine name, and it stands one letter away from Ahijah, “my brother is Jah”. “That they will not know”: the Hebrew has the scroll’s atti with the reading at, an older spelling of “you” said to a woman; the meaning is the same. The verb for “disguise” is shanah, to change. She is to change herself so as to deceive a man who, verse 4 tells us, cannot see her.

Ten loaves and a flask. A present for a prophet is expected (1 Samuel 9:7, now on these pages), and this one is a farm wife’s, not a queen’s. “Crumb-cakes” is niqqudim: as a word for food it stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the other two are the Gibeonites’ bread, “dry and crumbled” (Joshua 9:5, 12, now on these pages), which is also bread brought in order to deceive. The word is uncertain. The KJV, the Geneva and the Douay have “cracknels”, the ASV and the NIV “cakes”, the NWT 1984 “sprinkled cakes”, and the TLB, with some confidence, “fig bars”. The “flask” is baqbuq, the vessel Jeremiah will break (Jeremiah 19:1, now on these pages).

“His eyes were set”. “Ahijah could not see, for his eyes were set because of his age”: the Hebrew is the phrase used of Eli, also at Shiloh, also unable to see (1 Samuel 4:15, now on these pages). The verb is qum, to rise or stand, the same verb as “Arise” in verse 2 and “she arose” in this one: she rises, and his eyes have stood still. The ASV and the KJV have “were set”, the NWT 1984 “had set”, the NIV “his sight was gone”, and the Douay “were dim”.

Verses 5–6 · 1 Kings 14:5–6 “Making herself unrecognizable” — the sound of her feet, and a hard thing

“Unrecognizable”. “She will be making herself unrecognizable”: the participle mitnakker stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, verses 5 and 6 here. The form of the verb is the one for Joseph before his brothers, who “made himself unrecognizable to them, and spoke harshly with them” (Genesis 42:7, now on these pages), and the same two words come together here: she is mitnakkerah and the message is qashah. In Genesis the man who hides is the man who knows. Here the one who hides is the one who is known. The verb belongs to nakar, to recognize. The NWT 1984 has “making herself unrecognizable”, and this page follows it, as Genesis 42:7 does. The ASV and the KJV have “feign herself to be another woman”, and the NIV “pretend to be someone else”.

The sound of her feet. “Thus and thus you shall speak to her”: the reader is not told what. A blind man has been told who is coming, and he hears her in the doorway and calls her by the one title she came to hide: “Come in, wife of Jeroboam.” The feet come back in verse 12.

“A hard thing”. “I am sent to you with a hard thing”: the Hebrew has only the adjective, qashah, “hard”, and it is the word for how Rehoboam answered the people (1 Kings 12:13, now on these pages). The kingdom was divided on a hard answer, and now the king who got the ten tribes receives one. The KJV and the ASV have “heavy tidings”, the NWT 1984 “a severe message”, and the NIV “bad news”.

Verses 7–11 · 1 Kings 14:7–11 The sentence on the house of Jeroboam — “cast me behind your back”, “shut up or set free”, and burned out like dung

What was offered. “I lifted you up from among the people, and made you leader”: the word is nagid, the title given to Saul (1 Samuel 9:16, now on these pages). “And tore the kingdom”: qara, the verb of the cloak (1 Kings 11:30–31, now on these pages), spoken by the same man. He had offered Jeroboam “a faithful house” if he would keep the commandments “as David my servant did” (11:38, now on these pages), and the condition is now read back to him as a verdict.

“Other gods”. Jeroboam presented the calves as “your gods” who brought Israel up from Egypt (1 Kings 12:28, now on these pages), and Ahijah calls them “other gods and molten images”. The noun for the images is the one of the “molten calf” (Exodus 32:4, now on these pages). The adjective in “other gods” is plural in the Hebrew. The ASV, the KJV and the NIV have “other gods”, the NWT 1984 has “another god”, and the TLB has “gold calves”, which says what the verse means and not what it says.

“Behind your back”. “And me you have cast behind your back”: the Hebrew puts “me” first. “Behind your back” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; in Ezekiel, of Jerusalem, “you have forgotten me and cast me behind your back” (Ezekiel 23:35); and in Hezekiah’s song, of God, “you have cast all my sins behind your back” (Isaiah 38:17). The NIV softens it to “turned your back on me”. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 keep the throw.

Every male. The phrase mashtin be-qir stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible. This is the first of the four in Kings, each aimed at a king’s house, and David swore the same oath against Nabal and did not carry it out (1 Samuel 25:22, now on these pages). The KJV, the Geneva and the Douay keep it as “pisseth against the wall”, the NWT 1984 as “urinating against a wall”, the ASV has “man-child”, and the NIV “every last male”. The pair atsur ve-azuv stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, and it is not known what it means. This page keeps its rendering at Deuteronomy 32:36 (now on these pages), “shut up or set free”. The pair plainly means “everyone”. Which two kinds of people it names is open: the ASV has “him that is shut up and him that is left at large”, the NIV “slave or free”, and the NWT 1984 “a helpless and worthless one”.

Burned out like dung. “I will burn out after the house of Jeroboam, as one burns out dung until it is gone”: the verb is ba’ar, the verb of the formula “you shall burn out the evil from your midst” (Deuteronomy 13:6, now on these pages), and here the evil to be burned out is a king’s family. Dung was fuel (Ezekiel 4:12, 15). The shelf splits on the verb: the NIV has “burn up” and “burns dung”, while the ASV has “sweepeth away dung”, the KJV “taketh away dung”, and the NWT 1984 “clears away the dung”. The word for dung, galal, shares its letters with gillulim, the word for idols that this book will use (1 Kings 15:12). Whether that is more than a coincidence of letters is not something the verse says.

The dogs. “The dogs shall eat”: to lie unburied was the last disgrace, and the full sentence, dogs in the city and birds in the field, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all with the same shape: here, on Jeroboam’s house; on Baasha’s (1 Kings 16:4); and on Ahab’s (1 Kings 21:24). The man who carries out this sentence will have it passed on himself.

Verses 12–16 · 1 Kings 14:12–16 “Something good toward Jehovah” — the only grave, “and what? Even now”, and a reed in the water

Her own feet. “When your feet come into the city, the child will die”: he heard her feet at his door, and now they are the clock. The boy will live for exactly as long as it takes his mother to walk home, and the verse tells her so before she starts. Jeroboam says “the boy”, na’ar, and the prophet says “the child”, yeled.

A grave as a mercy. “This one alone of Jeroboam’s will come to a grave”: the man of God from Judah was told that his corpse would not reach the grave of his fathers (1 Kings 13:22, now on these pages), and it is the same word. In this house, to die young and be buried is the good news. “Something good toward Jehovah”: davar tov, and the text does not say what it was. The ASV and the KJV have “some good thing toward”, the NIV “anything good”, and the Douay “a good word from the Lord”, following the Latin. The TLB has “the only good thing”.

“This is the day; and what? Even now.” The closing words u-meh gam attah stand in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and how to read them, and the two words before them, is uncertain. This page leaves the Hebrew as broken as it is. The ASV and the KJV have “that day: but what? even now”, the Geneva “what? yea, even now”, and the NWT 1984 “the said day, and what if right now?”. The NIV has a smooth sentence, “Even now this is beginning to happen”, which is a guess, and the TLB stops before the words. The king is not named. He will be Baasha (1 Kings 15:27–29).

The reed and the River. “As the reed sways in the water”: the prophet moves from one house to the whole north. “He will uproot Israel” is natash, the verb of Deuteronomy’s warning (Deuteronomy 29:27, EN 28, now on these pages). “This good ground” is the language of Joshua’s farewell (Joshua 23:13, 15, now on these pages). “Beyond the River” is where Joshua said Israel’s fathers lived, serving other gods, before Abraham was taken from there (Joshua 24:2–3, now on these pages), so the sentence sends them back to where the story began, and it will happen (2 Kings 17:6). The ASV and the NWT 1984 have “the River”, and the NIV names it, “the Euphrates River”. “Their Asherahs”: the people’s, not only the king’s. The ASV has “Asherim”, the NIV “Asherah poles”, the NWT 1984 “sacred poles”, and the KJV “groves”.

The refrain begins. “The sins of Jeroboam, which he sinned and which he made Israel sin”: this is the first time the sentence is heard. Hecheti et Yisrael, “made Israel sin”, stands in eighteen verses of the Hebrew Bible, nearly all of it the judgment on one northern king after another, who are measured against this man.

Verses 17–20 · 1 Kings 14:17–20 Tirzah — a death at the threshold, a lost book of the kings of Israel, and no burial for Jeroboam

Tirzah. “And came to Tirzah”: the last the reader heard, Jeroboam was building Shechem and Penuel (1 Kings 12:25, now on these pages), and the text does not say when he moved. Tirzah is in the list of Joshua’s conquered kings (Joshua 12:24, now on these pages), and it will be the seat of the next kings of Israel (1 Kings 15:33).

The threshold. “As she came to the threshold of the house, the boy died”: Ahijah said “into the city”, and the narrator follows her to the door. She reaches the threshold, and he is dead. The KJV has “the threshold of the door”, and the ASV and the NWT 1984 “the threshold of the house”, which is the Hebrew. “According to the word of Jehovah”: the verse notes the fulfilment at once. The rest of the sentence takes longer (1 Kings 15:29).

A book we do not have. “The book of the acts of the days of the kings of Israel”: the phrase “book of the acts of the days” stands in thirty-seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, thirty-three of them in Kings, and the first are these, verses 19 and 29. It is not the book we call Chronicles, though that is the Hebrew name of Chronicles too; it is a source the writer of Kings could send a reader to, and it has not survived. The ASV and the KJV have “the book of the chronicles”, the NIV “the book of the annals”, the Douay “the book of the words of the days”, and the NWT 1984 “the book of the affairs of the days”. “How he fought”: a reign of twenty-two years, and the fighting is left to another book. This chapter has given more words to a sick boy than to all of it.

No burial. “And he lay down with his fathers, and Nadab his son reigned in his place”: compare verse 31, where Rehoboam lay down with his fathers “and was buried”. For Jeroboam the second half is missing, and that is in keeping with what Ahijah said in verse 13. The sentence is the shorter one the book sometimes uses, and the absence may mean nothing, but it stands. Nadab’s turn is short (1 Kings 15:25–28).

Verses 21–24 · 1 Kings 14:21–24 Rehoboam and Naamah the Ammonite — roused to jealousy, and the word the KJV rendered “sodomites”

Forty-one years old. The story goes back to Judah. Solomon reigned forty years (1 Kings 11:42, now on these pages), so a son of forty-one was born before his father took the throne, and the “young men” of Shechem were not young (1 Kings 12:8, now on these pages). The Greek Bible gives two answers. At this verse it agrees with the Hebrew, forty-one and seventeen; in the additions after 12:24 of the Vaticanus tradition it says sixteen and twelve, and calls his mother Naanan, the daughter of Ana son of Naas, king of Ammon. “The city that Jehovah had chosen” is the language of 1 Kings 11:36 (now on these pages).

Naamah the Ammonite. “Naamah the Ammonite” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: twice in this chapter, verses 21 and 31, and once in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 12:13). From here on, Kings gives the name of the mother of nearly every king of Judah, and hers comes first and comes twice. She is one of the Ammonite women of 1 Kings 11:1 (now on these pages), whose god Solomon bowed to (11:33). Deuteronomy says that an Ammonite shall not enter the assembly of Jehovah (Deuteronomy 23:4, now on these pages), and every later king of the house of David descends from this woman. The verse makes no comment on that.

“Roused him to jealousy”. “And Judah did what was evil”: the Hebrew has the people. The Greek Bible has “Roboam did evil”, and Chronicles also has “he” (2 Chronicles 12:14). The readings differ, and the plural verbs that follow fit the Hebrew. Vayqan’u oto is the verb of the Song of Moses, “They roused me to jealousy with a no-god” (Deuteronomy 32:21, now on these pages). The ASV and the KJV have “provoked him to jealousy”, the NWT 1984 “incited him to jealousy”, and the Geneva and the Douay lose the jealousy with “provoked him”. “They too”: the Hebrew is emphatic, and the south has been told at length what the north has done. “Under every leafy tree” is the place Deuteronomy ordered destroyed (Deuteronomy 12:2, now on these pages), and “standing stones” and “Asherahs” are the things of Deuteronomy 7:5 (now on these pages).

⚠ The “sodomites” of the KJV. The Hebrew is the singular qadesh, “a consecrated man”, from qadosh, “holy”, and this page keeps its rendering at Deuteronomy 23:18 (now on these pages), where the word has a full note. The KJV, the ASV and the Geneva print “sodomites”. That word has been read as the Bible naming and condemning men who have sex with men, in a verse that says nothing of Sodom or of any partner; the word describes a man attached to a shrine. Translations and commentaries have long taken him for a cult prostitute: the NIV has “male shrine prostitutes” and the NWT 1984 “the male temple prostitute”. That is a reading the word itself does not settle, and the evidence for it is disputed. The Douay has “the effeminate”, following the Latin. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has syndesmos, “conspiracy”, a different thought altogether, which may reflect a different Hebrew word.

Verses 25–28 · 1 Kings 14:25–28 Shishak king of Egypt and Sheshonq I at Karnak — the shields of gold, and bronze in their place

Shishak. “Shishak king of Egypt came up against Jerusalem”: he is the man who sheltered Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:40, now on these pages). The scroll writes the name Shushaq and the reading is Shishaq; the spelling with the vav stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The Greek has Sousakim. Kings gives five years, one verse and no reason. Chronicles gives the reason, a faithless Judah; twelve hundred chariots and sixty thousand horsemen; fortified cities taken; and the prophet Shemaiah (2 Chronicles 12:2–8).

Sheshonq I at Karnak. He is usually identified with the Egyptian king Sheshonq I. That identification comes from outside the text, and it is the common view: the names are close, and the king left a record of a campaign into Canaan, carved at the temple of Amun at Karnak, listing more than a hundred and fifty places, and a piece of a stela with his name was found at Megiddo. Those are reported here from the published descriptions, not read. The list as it survives has a good many gaps. It does not name Jerusalem, Rehoboam or Jeroboam, and scholars explain that silence in different ways: the name may be lost, a payment may have spared the city, or the campaign may not have gone as the Bible tells it. This page does not decide. On the usual reckoning all of this is about 925 BC.

“He took it all”. “The shields of gold that Solomon had made”: the phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 12:9). These are the three hundred of 1 Kings 10:17 (now on these pages), three minas each, put in the House of the Forest of Lebanon, and the word is magen. All that gold lasted five years into his son’s reign. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition adds the golden spears that David took from Hadadezer’s servants, and at 2 Samuel 8:7 it says that Shishak carried those off. The Hebrew has neither remark (2 Samuel 8:7, now on these pages).

Bronze. “And King Rehoboam made shields of bronze in their place”: the procession goes on, and only the metal has changed. Nechoshet is bronze; the NIV has “bronze”, the ASV “brass”, and the NWT 1984 “copper”. “The runners” are ratsim, the guard that Saul ordered to kill the priests (1 Samuel 22:17, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “runners”, and this page follows it; the ASV, the KJV and the NIV have “guard”, and the Douay “shieldbearers”. “The runners’ chamber”, ta ha-ratsim, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 12:11). The shields go out for the walk to the house of Jehovah and are locked up again. Solomon’s stayed in the House of the Forest of Lebanon, and these are kept in a guardroom.

Verses 29–31 · 1 Kings 14:29–31 “All the days” — war to the end, Naamah again, and Abijam, whom Chronicles calls Abijah

War “all the days”. “And there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days”: Shemaiah stopped the war at the start, and the people listened (1 Kings 12:24, now on these pages). So either that held for a while or this is a different war, and the text does not say. The sentence returns, oddly, in the reign of Rehoboam’s son (1 Kings 15:6). The KJV has “all their days”, the ASV and the NIV “continually” or “continual warfare”, and the NWT 1984 “always”.

Abijah and Abijam. “And Abijam his son reigned in his place”: the Hebrew here is Aviyam. Chronicles calls the same king Abijah (2 Chronicles 12:16; 13:1), the Greek here has Abiou, and Matthew’s list has Abia (Matthew 1:7, now on these pages). The ASV and the KJV have “Abijam”, the Douay “Abiam”, and the NIV “Abijah”, which follows Chronicles and not this verse. So the chapter that opened on one Abijah, the heir of the north, who died a boy, closes on another, the heir of the south. And it repeats the name of his grandmother, “Naamah the Ammonite”, in the middle of the sentence, between the burial and the heir. The Greek as printed from the Vaticanus has no second mention.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “making herself unrecognizable” for mitnakkerah, as in Genesis 42:7; “a hard thing” for qashah, as in 1 Kings 12:13; “his eyes were set”, as in 1 Samuel 4:15; “crumb-cakes” for niqqudim; “leader” for nagid; “tore” for qara; “burn out” for ba’ar, as in Deuteronomy 13:6; “anyone who urinates against the wall”; “shut up or set free” for atsur ve-azuv, as in Deuteronomy 32:36; “ground” for adamah; “the River”; “roused him to jealousy”, as in Deuteronomy 32:21; “the consecrated man” for qadesh, as in Deuteronomy 23:18; “standing stones” and “Asherahs”; “runners” for ratsim; and “Abijam”, as the Hebrew has it.

Echoes paid. Ahijah and the torn cloak (1 Kings 11:29–39); the faithful house (1 Kings 11:38); the calves, the high places and the priests (1 Kings 12:28–31); Shishak (1 Kings 11:40); Rehoboam’s hard answer (1 Kings 12:13); Rehoboam’s age (1 Kings 12:8); the grave of the fathers (1 Kings 13:22); the shields of gold (1 Kings 10:17; 2 Samuel 8:7); the Ammonite women (1 Kings 11:1, 33); Joseph unrecognizable (Genesis 42:7); Eli’s eyes (1 Samuel 4:15); the Gibeonites’ bread (Joshua 9:5, 12); David’s oath against Nabal (1 Samuel 25:22); burning out the evil (Deuteronomy 13:6); shut up or set free (Deuteronomy 32:36); uprooted (Deuteronomy 29:27); the good ground (Joshua 23:13, 15); beyond the River (Joshua 24:2–3); the assembly and the Ammonite (Deuteronomy 23:4); the consecrated man (Deuteronomy 23:18); jealousy (Deuteronomy 32:21); the leafy tree (Deuteronomy 12:2); and the runners (1 Samuel 22:17).

Echoes planted. Nadab and Baasha, and the end of the house of Jeroboam (1 Kings 15:25–29); the consecrated men cleared out, and the gillulim (1 Kings 15:12); the same sentence on Baasha and on Ahab (1 Kings 16:4; 21:24); the north carried off (2 Kings 17:6); “behind your back” (Isaiah 38:17; Ezekiel 23:35); and the Chronicler’s Shishak (2 Chronicles 12:2–16). None is translated here yet.

Next in this book: 1 Kings 15 — Abijam reigns three years; Asa clears out the consecrated men and removes Maacah from being queen mother; he pays Ben-hadad with what is left in the treasuries; and Baasha kills Nadab and strikes down all the house of Jeroboam, according to the word of Ahijah.
