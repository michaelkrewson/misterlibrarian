Jesus, 'the apostle and high priest of our confession,' set beside Moses: Moses faithful in God's house as an attendant, Christ as a Son over it; then Psalm 95, 'Today, if you hear his voice,' read as a warning from the wilderness generation who provoked God at Massah and Meribah and did not enter his rest.
Before AD 70, most likely c. AD 60-69, continuing the same letter as chapter 1 (see that note).

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · “The apostle and high priest of our confession” — and faithful “to the one who appointed him”

“Holy brothers, companions in a heavenly calling, consider the apostle and high priest of our confession, Jesus.” Metochos, “partner, companion,” stands in six verses: the fishermen’s “partners” in the other boat at Luke 5:7, the Son anointed “beyond your companions” at Hebrews 1:9, here, verse 14, Hebrews 6:4, and 12:8 (dictionary). This translation prints “companions” here and in verse 14, as in chapter 1: the Son had companions, and the readers are now called his. “Apostle” is apostolos, “one sent,” a title the Gospels and letters give to the men Jesus sends; here it is given to Jesus, who in John’s Gospel says “As the Father has sent me, I also send you” (John 20:21) (dictionary). “High priest” picks up Hebrews 2:17 (dictionary). Homologia, “confession,” stands in six verses, among them “you confessed the good confession” at 1 Timothy 6:12 (dictionary). The Byzantine text reads “Christ Jesus” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “consider the Apostle and High Priest of our profession, Christ Jesus”; the ASV reads “consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession, even Jesus”; the NWT 1984 reads “consider the apostle and high priest whom we confess.”

⚠ “Who is faithful to the one who appointed him, as Moses also was in his house.” “Appointed” is poieō, “to make,” in the sense it has in the Hebrew and Greek of 1 Samuel 12:6, “Jehovah, who made Moses and Aaron,” that is, set them in office (both checked). The verse was argued over in the fourth-century debates about whether the Son was created, with “made” read as creation by one side and as appointment by the other (reported here, not checked). The line about Moses is Numbers 12:7, “Not so my servant Moses: in all my house he is trusted,” where the Hebrew ne’eman is “trusted, faithful” (dictionary). The printed Greek, Holmes’s, has “in his house”; Westcott–Hort, Tregelles, Nestle–Aland 28 and Robinson–Pierpont have “in all his house,” as verse 5 has it (the apparatus). The KJV reads “Who was faithful to him that appointed him”; the Douay reads “Who is faithful to him that made him”; the NWT 1984 reads “He was faithful to the One that made him such.”

Verses 3–6 · Moses the attendant, the Son over the house — “and we are that house, if…”

“Counted worthy of more glory than Moses, by as much as the one who built the house has more honor than the house.” “Built” is kataskeuazō, “to build, to prepare, to fit out,” the verb of Noah, who “built an ark” at Hebrews 11:7, and of the messenger who “will prepare your way” at Matthew 11:10 (dictionary). Verse 4 sets the builder of every house beside “the one who built all things,” God; readers take the builder of verse 3 as the Son, through whom God “made the ages” (Hebrews 1:2), or as God himself, with the comparison running between the Son and Moses as members of the house. The KJV reads “For this man was counted worthy of more glory than Moses”; the ASV reads “by so much as he that built the house hath more honor than the house”; the NWT 1984 reads “he who constructs it has more honor than the house.”

“Moses was faithful in all his house as an attendant, for a testimony to the things that were going to be spoken.” Therapōn, “attendant,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary); it is the word of the Greek of Numbers 12:7, “my attendant Moses,” where the Hebrew has ‘eved, “servant” (both checked). It is a free servant’s word, different from doulos, “slave” (dictionary). Moses’ faithfulness is “a testimony to the things that were going to be spoken”: the letter reads the Law as pointing past itself. The same pairing of a faithful priest and a house stands in 1 Samuel 2:35, where the Hebrew promises “a faithful priest” and “a faithful house,” ne’eman both times (checked). The KJV reads “And Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant”; the NWT 1984 reads “And Moses as an attendant was faithful”; the TLB reads “but he was only a servant.”

⚠ “But Christ as a Son over his house; and we are that house, if we hold fast our boldness and the boast of our hope.” “His house” is read as God’s house, the house of Numbers 12:7, or as Christ’s own. The printed text has hos, “which [house] we are”; Westcott–Hort, Tregelles, Nestle–Aland 28 and Robinson–Pierpont have hou, “whose house we are”; and Westcott–Hort, Tregelles and Robinson–Pierpont add “firm to the end,” which Nestle–Aland 28 and the printed text leave out here and have in verse 14 (the apparatus). “Boldness” is parrēsia, the word of Acts 4:13, “seeing the boldness of Peter and John” (dictionary); “boast” is kauchēma (dictionary). “If” makes the belonging conditional, and readers divide: some take the holding fast as the evidence that one belongs, others as a condition one can fail to meet. This page does not choose. The KJV reads “whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end”; the Douay reads “which house are we”; the NWT 1984 reads “our freeness of speech and our boasting over the hope.”

Verses 7–11 · Psalm 95 — “Today, if you hear his voice”: the Provocation and the Testing, and “my rest”

“As the Holy Spirit says, ‘Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts.’” The quotation is Psalm 95:7–11 (94 in the Greek Psalter), and the letter gives it to the Holy Spirit as the one speaking now, “says,” in the present. “Today” is sēmeron (dictionary), and the letter will make the word the subject of verses 13 and 15. “Harden” is sklērynō (dictionary). The KJV reads “To day if ye will hear his voice”; the ASV reads “To-day if ye shall hear his voice”; the NWT 1984 reads “Today if YOU people listen to his own voice.”

⚠ “As at the Provocation, on the day of the Testing in the wilderness.” The Hebrew of the psalm names two places, “as at Meribah, as on the day of Massah in the wilderness” (checked): the place of Exodus 17:7, named Massah, “Testing,” and Meribah, “Strife” (dictionary; encyclopedia). The Greek translates the names. Parapikrasmos, “provocation, embitterment,” stands in two verses, here and verse 15 (dictionary); “the Testing” is peirasmos, the word the Greek of Exodus 17:7 uses for the name Massah (checked). This translation capitalizes both, so that they read as the names they stand for. The KJV reads “as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness”; the NWT 1984 reads “as on the occasion of causing bitter anger”; the NIV reads “as you did in the rebellion.”

⚠ “And saw my works for forty years. Therefore I was disgusted with this generation.” In the Hebrew of the psalm, “forty years” belongs to the disgust, “they saw my work; forty years I loathed a generation,” and the Greek Psalter keeps that order (both checked). Hebrews attaches the forty years to “saw my works” and adds “therefore,” so that the years of seeing become the reason for the anger; in verse 17 it puts the forty years back with the anger, “with whom was he disgusted for forty years?” Prosōchthizō, “to be disgusted, to loathe,” stands in two verses, here and verse 17 (dictionary). Dokimasia, “scrutiny, putting to the proof,” stands in one verse; the Byzantine text has the verb instead, “tested me, proved me” (the apparatus). The printed text has “this generation”; the Byzantine text and the Greek Psalter have “that generation.” The KJV reads “When your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years”; the NWT 1984 reads “For this reason I became disgusted with this generation”; the NIV reads “though for forty years they saw what I did.”

⚠ “As I swore in my anger, ‘They shall not enter my rest.’” The Hebrew is an oath formula, im yevo’un, “if they enter …,” with the curse left unsaid, and the Greek keeps it word for word, ei eiseleusontai (both checked). It means “they shall not enter,” and this translation prints that, as it does with the same oath in Numbers 14:23 and 14:30. “Rest” is katapausis, for the Hebrew menuchah (dictionary; dictionary); in Deuteronomy the rest is the land, “the rest and the inheritance” (Deuteronomy 12:9), and chapter 4 of the letter takes the word further. Katapausis stands in eight verses: Acts 7:49, “what is the place of my rest?”, and seven in Hebrews 3–4. The Geneva keeps the oath’s form: “If they shall enter into my rest”; so does the Douay: “If they shall enter into my rest”; the KJV reads “They shall not enter into my rest.”

Verses 12–15 · “An evil heart of unbelief” — “as long as it is called Today,” and “the beginning of our confidence”

“Watch out, brothers, that there is not in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in falling away from the living God.” Apistia, “unbelief,” stands in eleven verses, here and verse 19 among them (dictionary). “Falling away” is aphistēmi, the verb of Luke 8:13, “in a time of testing they fall away,” and of 1 Timothy 4:1, “some will fall away from the faith” (dictionary). “The living God” is Peter’s phrase at Matthew 16:16. The KJV reads “an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God”; the ASV reads “in falling away from the living God”; the NWT 1984 reads “a wicked heart lacking faith by drawing away from the living God.”

“Exhort one another day by day, as long as it is called ‘Today,’ so that not one of you is hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.” The psalm’s “Today” becomes a span of time: as long as the word is still being said, the warning is still open. “Exhort” is parakaleō, to call alongside, encourage, urge. “Deceitfulness” is apatē, as in “the deceitfulness of riches” at Mark 4:19. The KJV reads “But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day”; the NIV reads “so that none of you may be hardened by sin’s deceitfulness”; the NWT 1984 reads “by the deceptive power of sin.”

⚠ “For we have become companions of Christ, if indeed we hold the beginning of our confidence firm to the end.” “Confidence” is hypostasis (dictionary), which stands in five verses (hypostasis): “our confidence” at 2 Corinthians 9:4 and “this confidence of boasting” at 2 Corinthians 11:17; “the exact imprint of his substance” at Hebrews 1:3; here; and “the ground of things hoped for” at Hebrews 11:1. Here this translation prints “confidence,” the sense of the two verses in 2 Corinthians; readers who keep “substance” take it as the reality of the believers’ standing. “Firm” is bebaios, the word of Hebrews 2:2. “Companions of Christ” is read as sharing in Christ or as being his partners. The KJV reads “if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end”; the Douay reads “if we hold the beginning of his substance firm unto the end”; the NIV reads “if indeed we hold our original conviction firmly to the very end.”

“As it is said, ‘Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts as at the Provocation.’” The line is read with what comes before it (“we hold firm … as it is said”) or as the start of what follows (“As it is said … for who were they?”). The ASV reads “while it is said, To-day if ye shall hear his voice”; the NIV reads “As has just been said”; the Douay reads “harden not your hearts, as in that provocation.”

Verses 16–19 · ⚠ “Who were they?” or “some” — carcasses in the wilderness, disobedience and unbelief

⚠ “For who were they who heard and provoked? Was it not all who came out of Egypt through Moses?” The Greek letters can be read two ways, depending on the accent: tínes, “who?”, a question, or tinés, “some.” Modern editions print the question, and verses 17 and 18 are questions too. The older English versions read “some … but not all,” thinking of Caleb and Joshua, the two excepted at Numbers 14:30. Parapikrainō, “to provoke,” stands in one verse; it is the verb of the noun in verses 8 and 15. The KJV reads “For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses”; the ASV reads “For who, when they heard, did provoke? nay, did not all they that came out of Egypt by Moses?”; the NIV reads “Were they not all those Moses led out of Egypt?”

“With those who sinned, whose carcasses fell in the wilderness.” The words are those of Numbers 14:29, “In this wilderness your carcasses will fall” (dictionary). Kōlon, “limb, corpse,” stands in one verse of the New Testament; it is the word of the Greek of Numbers 14:29 (checked) (dictionary). The KJV reads “whose carcases fell in the wilderness”; the ASV reads “whose bodies fell in the wilderness”; the NIV reads “whose bodies perished in the wilderness.”

“To those who disobeyed … because of unbelief.” Verse 18 has apeitheō, “to disobey, to refuse to be persuaded” (dictionary), and verse 19 has apistia, “unbelief”: the letter puts the refusal and the lack of trust side by side and makes them one failure. This translation prints “disobeyed” here, where the next verse supplies “unbelief,” and “refused to believe” for the same verb at Hebrews 11:31. The KJV reads “but to them that believed not?”; the ASV reads “but to them that were disobedient?”; the Douay reads “but to them that were incredulous?”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “companions” for metochoi, as at 1:9; “apostle” and “high priest”; “confession,” as at 1 Timothy 6:12; “appointed,” with “made” in the note; “built,” as at 11:7; “attendant” for therapōn; “boldness,” as at Acts 4:13; “the Provocation” and “the Testing,” for Meribah and Massah; “forty years” where Hebrews puts it; “disgusted”; “They shall not enter,” with the oath in the note; “rest”; “falling away,” as at Luke 8:13; “confidence” for hypostasis, as in 2 Corinthians; “firm,” as at 2:2; “carcasses,” as at Numbers 14:29; and “disobeyed” beside “unbelief.”

Echoes paid. The companions of Hebrews 1:9; the high priest of Hebrews 2:17; the good confession of 1 Timothy 6:12; Moses trusted in all my house from Numbers 12:7; Noah who built an ark at Hebrews 11:7; boldness from Acts 4:13; Massah and Meribah from Exodus 17:7; the oath of Numbers 14:23; the rest of Deuteronomy 12:9; falling away from Luke 8:13; the deceitfulness of Mark 4:19; confidence from 2 Corinthians 9:4; firm from Hebrews 2:2; the carcasses of Numbers 14:29; and Caleb and Joshua, excepted at Numbers 14:30.

Echoes planted. “The living God” at 9:14; “confession” at 10:23 and “the living God” again at 10:31; “companions” at 12:8 and “the living God” at 12:22.

Next in this book: Hebrews 4, already on these pages. “Let us fear, then,” while “a promise of entering his rest is still left open”; the seventh day and “a sabbath rest … for the people of God”; the word of God “sharper than any two-edged sword”; and a high priest “tested in every respect in the same way, without sin.”
