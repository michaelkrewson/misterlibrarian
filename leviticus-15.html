🕰
The law of bodily discharges, closing the whole clean/unclean block that opened at Leviticus 11: an abnormal male discharge and its elaborate contact rules, an ordinary emission of semen, ordinary marital intercourse, ordinary menstruation, and an irregular or prolonged female discharge treated exactly like the man's own case. It closes on one blunt statement of stakes — die in your uncleanness by defiling the tabernacle in your midst — the reason the whole apparatus exists.
⚠ Undated within the chapter, like the rest of Leviticus, and — like Leviticus 11 and 13 — addressed to Moses AND Aaron together. This is the law Leviticus 12 (already on these pages) twice assumed its reader already knew, comparing a new mother's uncleanness to 'the days of her niddah' without ever explaining the term. Still inside the same Sinai window as the rest of this block.

Leviticus 15 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה, אֶל-מֹשֶׁה וְאֶל-אַהֲרֹן לֵאמֹר.
And Jehovah spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying:note
Y Jehová habló a Moisés y a Aarón, diciendo:
2
דַּבְּרוּ אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, וַאֲמַרְתֶּם אֲלֵהֶם: אִישׁ אִישׁ, כִּי יִהְיֶה זָב מִבְּשָׂרוֹ--זוֹבוֹ, טָמֵא הוּא.
Speak to the children of Israel, and say to them: When any man has a discharge from his body, his discharge is unclean.note
Hablen a los hijos de Israel, y díganles: Cuando un hombre tenga un flujo de su cuerpo, su flujo es impuro.
3
וְזֹאת תִּהְיֶה טֻמְאָתוֹ, בְּזוֹבוֹ: רָר בְּשָׂרוֹ אֶת-זוֹבוֹ, אוֹ-הֶחְתִּים בְּשָׂרוֹ מִזּוֹבוֹ--טֻמְאָתוֹ, הִוא.
And this shall be his uncleanness in his discharge: whether his body runs with his discharge, or his body is stopped up from his discharge, it is his uncleanness.note
Y esta será su impureza en su flujo: sea que su cuerpo destile su flujo, o que su cuerpo se cierre a su flujo, es su impureza.
4
כָּל-הַמִּשְׁכָּב, אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁכַּב עָלָיו הַזָּב--יִטְמָא; וְכָל-הַכְּלִי אֲשֶׁר-יֵשֵׁב עָלָיו, יִטְמָא.
Every bed the one with the discharge lies on shall be unclean, and everything he sits on shall be unclean.note
Toda cama en que se acueste el que tiene el flujo quedará impura, y todo objeto en que se siente quedará impuro.
5
וְאִישׁ, אֲשֶׁר יִגַּע בְּמִשְׁכָּבוֹ--יְכַבֵּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And whoever touches his bed shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y quien toque su cama lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
6
וְהַיֹּשֵׁב, עַל-הַכְּלִי, אֲשֶׁר-יֵשֵׁב עָלָיו, הַזָּב--יְכַבֵּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And whoever sits on anything the one with the discharge has sat on shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y quien se siente sobre algo en que se haya sentado el que tiene el flujo lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
7
וְהַנֹּגֵעַ, בִּבְשַׂר הַזָּב--יְכַבֵּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And whoever touches the body of the one with the discharge shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y quien toque el cuerpo del que tiene el flujo lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
8
וְכִי-יָרֹק הַזָּב, בַּטָּהוֹר--וְכִבֶּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And if the one with the discharge spits on someone who is clean, that person shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y si el que tiene el flujo escupe sobre alguien que está puro, este lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
9
וְכָל-הַמֶּרְכָּב, אֲשֶׁר יִרְכַּב עָלָיו הַזָּב--יִטְמָא.
And any saddle the one with the discharge rides shall be unclean.note
Y toda silla de montar en que cabalgue el que tiene el flujo quedará impura.
10
וְכָל-הַנֹּגֵעַ, בְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר יִהְיֶה תַחְתָּיו--יִטְמָא, עַד-הָעָרֶב; וְהַנּוֹשֵׂא אוֹתָם--יְכַבֵּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And whoever touches anything that was under him shall be unclean until evening; and whoever carries those things shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y quien toque cualquier cosa que haya estado debajo de él quedará impuro hasta el atardecer; y quien cargue esas cosas lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
11
וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר יִגַּע-בּוֹ הַזָּב, וְיָדָיו לֹא-שָׁטַף בַּמָּיִם--וְכִבֶּס בְּגָדָיו וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And whomever the one with the discharge touches, without having rinsed his hands in water, that person shall wash his clothes, and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y a quien toque el que tiene el flujo, sin haberse enjuagado las manos en agua, esa persona lavará sus vestidos, y se bañará en agua, y quedará impura hasta el atardecer.
12
וּכְלִי-חֶרֶשׂ אֲשֶׁר-יִגַּע-בּוֹ הַזָּב, יִשָּׁבֵר; וְכָל-כְּלִי-עֵץ--יִשָּׁטֵף, בַּמָּיִם.
And the earthen vessel the one with the discharge touches shall be broken; and every vessel of wood shall be rinsed in water.note
Y la vasija de barro que toque el que tiene el flujo será quebrada; y todo utensilio de madera será enjuagado en agua.
13
וְכִי-יִטְהַר הַזָּב, מִזּוֹבוֹ--וְסָפַר לוֹ שִׁבְעַת יָמִים לְטָהֳרָתוֹ, וְכִבֶּס בְּגָדָיו; וְרָחַץ בְּשָׂרוֹ בְּמַיִם חַיִּים, וְטָהֵר.
And when the one with the discharge is cleansed of his discharge, he shall count off seven days for his cleansing, and wash his clothes, and bathe his body in living water, and he shall be clean.note
Y cuando el que tiene el flujo se purifique de su flujo, contará para sí siete días para su purificación, y lavará sus vestidos, y bañará su cuerpo en agua viva, y quedará puro.

14

And on the eighth day he shall take two turtle-doves, or two young pigeons, and come before Jehovah, to the entrance of the tent of meeting, and give them to the priest.note
Y al octavo día tomará para sí dos tórtolas, o dos pichones, y vendrá delante de Jehová, a la entrada del tabernáculo de reunión, y los dará al sacerdote.
15
וְעָשָׂה אֹתָם, הַכֹּהֵן--אֶחָד חַטָּאת, וְהָאֶחָד עֹלָה; וְכִפֶּר עָלָיו הַכֹּהֵן לִפְנֵי יְהוָה, מִזּוֹבוֹ. {ס}
And the priest shall offer them, one as a sin-offering, and the other as a burnt-offering; and the priest shall make atonement for him before Jehovah for his discharge.note
Y el sacerdote los ofrecerá, uno como ofrenda por el pecado, y el otro como holocausto; y el sacerdote hará expiación por él delante de Jehová a causa de su flujo.
16
וְאִישׁ, כִּי-תֵצֵא מִמֶּנּוּ שִׁכְבַת-זָרַע--וְרָחַץ בַּמַּיִם אֶת-כָּל-בְּשָׂרוֹ, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And if an emission of semen goes out from a man, he shall bathe his whole body in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y cuando de un hombre salga una emisión de semen, bañará todo su cuerpo en agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
17
וְכָל-בֶּגֶד וְכָל-עוֹר, אֲשֶׁר-יִהְיֶה עָלָיו שִׁכְבַת-זָרַע--וְכֻבַּס בַּמַּיִם, וְטָמֵא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And every garment, and every piece of leather, that has an emission of semen on it, shall be washed with water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y toda prenda, y todo cuero, que tenga sobre sí una emisión de semen, será lavado con agua, y quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.
18
וְאִשָּׁה, אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁכַּב אִישׁ אֹתָהּ שִׁכְבַת-זָרַע--וְרָחֲצוּ בַמַּיִם, וְטָמְאוּ עַד-הָעָרֶב. {פ}
And when a man lies with a woman and there is an emission of semen, they shall both bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.note
Y cuando un hombre se acueste con una mujer y haya emisión de semen, ambos se bañarán en agua, y quedarán impuros hasta el atardecer.
19
וְאִשָּׁה כִּי-תִהְיֶה זָבָה, דָּם יִהְיֶה זֹבָהּ בִּבְשָׂרָהּ--שִׁבְעַת יָמִים תִּהְיֶה בְנִדָּתָהּ, וְכָל-הַנֹּגֵעַ בָּהּ יִטְמָא עַד-הָעָרֶב.
And when a woman has a discharge, and her discharge in her body is blood, she shall be in her monthly separation for seven days; and whoever touches her shall be unclean until evening.note
Y cuando una mujer tenga flujo, y su flujo en su cuerpo sea sangre, estará en su separación mensual siete días; y quien la toque quedará impuro hasta el atardecer.

Zav — the discharge that gets the most elaborate contact-law in the whole Torah

The Hebrew itself is a euphemism, and this translation follows it rather than resolving it: zav mibsaro, ‘flows from his flesh/body’, never names what actually discharges. NWT makes the anatomy explicit — ‘a discharge from his genital organ’ — almost certainly the correct referent, but a degree of specificity the Hebrew itself declines to state. This translation keeps ‘a discharge from his body’, matching the original’s own indirection rather than closing a gap the text leaves open. KJV and ASV both keep the older ‘issue’; NIV reads ‘an unusual bodily discharge’. On the Spanish shelf, ⚠ RV60 jumps straight to ‘flujo de semen’ at v2 itself — assuming from the first verse a specificity the chapter’s own structure argues against, since vv16–18 go on to treat seminal emission as its own separate, much milder case; NVI’s ‘flujo inusual’ keeps the ambiguity open, closer to the Hebrew.

⚠ What follows is the most elaborate contact-contamination law in the entire Torah — more extensive even than tzaraat’s own (Leviticus 13, already on these pages). A bed, a seat, a saddle, being spat on, being touched, touching what was under him, an earthen vessel (broken outright) versus a wooden one (merely rinsed) — the law tracks contact through an entire day’s furniture and transport with a thoroughness nothing else in this book matches. And when the discharge finally clears, the cleansing water is named the same way Leviticus 14 (already on these pages) already named it: mayim chayim, living water — the third chapter running to use this exact phrase for a purification, and each time this project’s own translation keeps the word every other version smooths away.

Ordinary marital sex makes both partners unclean until evening — and that is the whole point

Verse 18 is the plainest statement in the whole Torah that ritual impurity carries no moral content whatsoever: ordinary marital intercourse, exactly as intended, produces the identical ‘unclean until evening’ as touching a stranger’s sickbed. No sin-offering. No guilt. No confinement, no examination, no priest involved at all — just an evening’s wait and a bath, the same minimal consequence assigned to touching furniture two verses earlier. A married couple doing precisely what the text elsewhere commands (‘be fruitful and multiply’, Genesis 1:28, already on these pages) still crosses this law’s threshold.

⚠ If any single verse in this book proves that tame is a status of APPROACH rather than a verdict on WRONGDOING — the reading this project has argued since Leviticus 11 (already on these pages) — this is it. The same three verses treat a solitary nocturnal emission (v16) and a couple’s shared act (v18) as functionally identical: a body did what bodies do, and the sanctuary’s calendar simply needs an evening to reset before either person may approach it.

Niddah — the law Leviticus 12 already assumed its reader knew

Twice, Leviticus 12 defined a new mother’s first days by comparison — unclean ‘as in the days of her monthly separation’ — without ever explaining the comparison, because this law was the one doing the explaining, whenever a reader got here. Leviticus 12:2 and 12:5 (already on these pages) both lean on it; this is where the loan comes due.

⚠ The rule itself is unremarkable by this chapter’s own standards — seven days, the identical bed-and-seat contamination already run for the man in vv1–15, no offering required at the end. What is worth naming is the word’s afterlife outside this chapter: a euphemism here (KJV ‘her flowers’, RV60 ‘su costumbre’, both languages reaching independently for an indirect, almost domestic word) became, across centuries of interpretation far removed from this text, a byword for female uncleanness in a much harsher sense than the seven-day, evening-resolved, no-offering-needed law actually states. The chapter that follows a woman’s ordinary body with the same procedural calm it gave a man’s discharge and a couple’s marriage bed is not the source of that harsher reading. It is, if anything, the text such readings had to work hardest to get past.

The zavah — and the woman who touched a hem rather than wait to be clean

Verses 25–30 extend the rule to an irregular or prolonged discharge — bleeding outside the expected week, or bleeding that will not stop — and treat it exactly as the male zav was treated: an offering on the eighth day once it clears, sin-offering and burnt-offering both, identical to vv14–15’s own male case. This is precisely the condition the Gospels report a woman living with for twelve straight years.

⚠ ‘There was a woman who had had a flow of blood for twelve years’ (Mark 5:25, already on these pages) — this chapter’s zavah, not for days but for over a decade, with no eighth day ever arriving. By this law’s own terms she made unclean everyone she brushed against; Mark never spells that out, but says she came up behind Jesus in a dense crowd and touched the edge of his cloak, then immediately felt her own body know it was healed — before he even turned around. He does not rebuke her for the contact. He calls her ‘daughter’ and sends her away in peace. The chapter that spent thirty verses tracking exactly how far this condition’s uncleanness could spread through touch is answered, centuries later, by a story where the touch runs the other direction.

Why any of this — the one verse that states the stakes

One verse states outright why any of this exists, and the stakes are put as bluntly as this book ever states anything: ‘so that they do not die in their uncleanness, by defiling my tabernacle which is in their midst.’ Not disgust. Not shame. A warning, addressed to the whole nation at once, about approaching a dangerous holiness while carrying an unresolved status — the same danger that killed Nadab and Abihu (Leviticus 10:2, already on these pages) for approaching wrongly, now generalized to an entire people whose ordinary bodies, on ordinary days, put them exactly there.

The four-part colophon that follows (vv32–33) is the closing bracket for a much longer unit than this chapter alone: it names the man’s discharge, the emission of semen, the woman ‘unwell in her monthly separation’, the discharge of either sex, and the man who lies with an unclean woman — five categories in two verses, every one of them an ordinary, blameless, entirely human condition. Nothing in the list is a wrongdoing. Everything in the list, this chapter has just spent thirty-three verses insisting, still matters — because the sanctuary is close, and closeness has rules that have nothing to do with character.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English Leviticus 15 both run 1–33 — verified against the Masoretic text, no versification shift. Three Masoretic paragraph breaks divide it: closed after v15, open after v18, closed after v24, with a final open break closing the chapter at v33. ‘A discharge from his body’ keeps the Hebrew’s own euphemism (zav mibsaro) rather than resolving it to explicit anatomy, as NWT does. ‘The one with the discharge’ is used throughout rather than a fixed identity-noun, continuing the discipline this project kept for ‘the one with scaly disease’ across Leviticus 13–14 (already on these pages). ‘An emission of semen’ renders shikhvat zera plainly. Niddah keeps Leviticus 12’s own established ‘monthly separation’. Mayim chayim (v13) is kept as ‘living water’, matching Leviticus 14.

Echoes paid. Leviticus 12:2 and 12:5’s own niddah comparison (already on these pages), left unexplained on the promise that this chapter would explain it; mayim chayim from Leviticus 14 (already on these pages), now a third consecutive chapter; the elaborate contact-contamination structure set against Leviticus 13’s own (already on these pages); and Nadab and Abihu’s deaths at Leviticus 10:2 (already on these pages), the concrete case behind this chapter’s abstract warning.

Echoes planted. The woman with the twelve-year discharge, already told at Mark 5:25 (already on these pages), is this chapter’s own zavah (vv25–30) read forward rather than back — the rare case where the New Testament payoff was on these pages before the Torah law it depends on.
