Jehovah sends Nathan to David, who tells of a rich man with many flocks who takes a poor man’s one ewe lamb; David says the man is a son of death and must pay fourfold, and Nathan says “You are the man”; Jehovah’s word recalls the anointing and the house given, names the killing of Uriah and the taking of his wife, and promises that the sword shall never depart from David’s house and that his wives will be taken in the sight of the sun; David says “I have sinned against Jehovah”, and Nathan says Jehovah has taken away his sin but the child shall die; the child is struck and dies on the seventh day after David fasts on the ground, David rises, washes and eats, and says “I am going to him”; David comforts Bathsheba and Solomon is born, and Jehovah loves him and sends by Nathan the name Jedidiah; Joab takes the royal city of Rabbah and the city of waters and calls David to take it, David takes the crown and sets the people to the saw and the brickmold, and returns to Jerusalem.
After the siege of Rabbah begins in 2 Samuel 11:1; the chapter gives no dates beyond the child’s seven days, and Rabbah falls after Solomon’s birth.

1
וַיִּשְׁלַח יְהוָה אֶת-נָתָן, אֶל-דָּוִד; וַיָּבֹא אֵלָיו, וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ שְׁנֵי אֲנָשִׁים הָיוּ בְּעִיר אֶחָת, אֶחָד עָשִׁיר, וְאֶחָד רָאשׁ.
And Jehovah sent Nathan to David. And he came to him, and said to him: ‘There were two men in one city: the one rich, and the other poor.note
Y Jehová envió a Natán a David. Y vino a él, y le dijo: «Había dos hombres en una ciudad: el uno rico, y el otro pobre.
2
לְעָשִׁיר, הָיָה צֹאן וּבָקָר--הַרְבֵּה מְאֹד.
The rich man had very many flocks and herds;note
El rico tenía muchísimas ovejas y vacas;
3
וְלָרָשׁ אֵין-כֹּל, כִּי אִם-כִּבְשָׂה אַחַת קְטַנָּה אֲשֶׁר קָנָה, וַיְחַיֶּהָ, וַתִּגְדַּל עִמּוֹ וְעִם-בָּנָיו יַחְדָּו; מִפִּתּוֹ תֹאכַל וּמִכֹּסוֹ תִשְׁתֶּה, וּבְחֵיקוֹ תִשְׁכָּב, וַתְּהִי-לוֹ, כְּבַת.
but the poor man had nothing, except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought, and kept alive; and it grew up with him and with his children together. From his morsel it ate, and from his cup it drank, and in his bosom it lay; and it was to him like a daughter.note
pero el pobre no tenía nada, excepto una corderita, una sola, que había comprado y mantenido con vida; y creció con él y con sus hijos juntamente. De su bocado comía, y de su copa bebía, y en su seno yacía; y era para él como una hija.
4
וַיָּבֹא הֵלֶךְ, לְאִישׁ הֶעָשִׁיר, וַיַּחְמֹל לָקַחַת מִצֹּאנוֹ וּמִבְּקָרוֹ, לַעֲשׂוֹת לָאֹרֵחַ הַבָּא-לוֹ; וַיִּקַּח, אֶת-כִּבְשַׂת הָאִישׁ הָרָאשׁ, וַיַּעֲשֶׂהָ, לָאִישׁ הַבָּא אֵלָיו.
And a traveler came to the rich man, and he spared to take from his own flock and from his own herd, to prepare for the wayfarer who had come to him; and he took the poor man’s lamb, and prepared it for the man who had come to him.’note
Y vino un viajero al hombre rico, y él se abstuvo de tomar de sus ovejas y de sus vacas, para preparar al caminante que había venido a él; y tomó la corderita del hombre pobre, y la preparó para el hombre que había venido a él».
5
וַיִּחַר-אַף דָּוִד בָּאִישׁ, מְאֹד; וַיֹּאמֶר, אֶל-נָתָן, חַי-יְהוָה, כִּי בֶן-מָוֶת הָאִישׁ הָעֹשֶׂה זֹאת.
And David’s anger was greatly kindled against the man; and he said to Nathan: ‘As Jehovah lives, the man who did this is a son of death!note
Y la ira de David se encendió en gran manera contra el hombre; y dijo a Natán: «Vive Jehová, el hombre que hizo esto es hijo de muerte.
6
וְאֶת-הַכִּבְשָׂה, יְשַׁלֵּם אַרְבַּעְתָּיִם:  עֵקֶב, אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה אֶת-הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, וְעַל, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-חָמָל.  {ס}
And he shall pay for the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity.’note
Y la corderita la pagará cuatro veces, porque hizo esta cosa y porque no tuvo compasión».
7
וַיֹּאמֶר נָתָן אֶל-דָּוִד, אַתָּה הָאִישׁ;  {ס}  כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אָנֹכִי מְשַׁחְתִּיךָ לְמֶלֶךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאָנֹכִי הִצַּלְתִּיךָ, מִיַּד שָׁאוּל.
And Nathan said to David: ‘You are the man. Thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel: “I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul;note
Y dijo Natán a David: «Tú eres ese hombre. Así dice Jehová, el Dios de Israel: “Yo te ungí rey sobre Israel, y yo te libré de la mano de Saúl;
8
וָאֶתְּנָה לְךָ אֶת-בֵּית אֲדֹנֶיךָ, וְאֶת-נְשֵׁי אֲדֹנֶיךָ בְּחֵיקֶךָ, וָאֶתְּנָה לְךָ, אֶת-בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל וִיהוּדָה; וְאִם-מְעָט--וְאֹסִפָה לְּךָ, כָּהֵנָּה וְכָהֵנָּה.
and I gave you your master’s house, and your master’s wives into your bosom, and gave you the house of Israel and of Judah; and if that was too little, I would add to you more of the same, and more again.note
y te di la casa de tu señor, y las mujeres de tu señor en tu seno, y te di la casa de Israel y de Judá; y si eso fue poco, te añadiría otro tanto, y más.
9
מַדּוּעַ בָּזִיתָ אֶת-דְּבַר יְהוָה, לַעֲשׂוֹת הָרַע בעינו בְּעֵינַי, אֵת אוּרִיָּה הַחִתִּי הִכִּיתָ בַחֶרֶב, וְאֶת-אִשְׁתּוֹ לָקַחְתָּ לְּךָ לְאִשָּׁה; וְאֹתוֹ הָרַגְתָּ, בְּחֶרֶב בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן.
Why did you despise the word of Jehovah, to do what is evil in my eyes? Uriah the Hittite you struck with the sword, and his wife you took to be your wife; and him you killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon.note
¿Por qué despreciaste la palabra de Jehová, haciendo lo malo ante mis ojos? A Urías el heteo heriste a espada, y a su mujer tomaste por mujer tuya; y a él lo mataste con la espada de los hijos de Amón.
10
וְעַתָּה, לֹא-תָסוּר חֶרֶב מִבֵּיתְךָ--עַד-עוֹלָם:  עֵקֶב, כִּי בְזִתָנִי, וַתִּקַּח אֶת-אֵשֶׁת אוּרִיָּה הַחִתִּי, לִהְיוֹת לְךָ לְאִשָּׁה.  {ס}
And now the sword shall never depart from your house, because you despised me, and took the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.”note
Y ahora, la espada no se apartará jamás de tu casa, porque me despreciaste y tomaste a la mujer de Urías el heteo para que fuera tu mujer”.
11
כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה, הִנְנִי מֵקִים עָלֶיךָ רָעָה מִבֵּיתֶךָ, וְלָקַחְתִּי אֶת-נָשֶׁיךָ לְעֵינֶיךָ, וְנָתַתִּי לְרֵעֶיךָ; וְשָׁכַב עִם-נָשֶׁיךָ, לְעֵינֵי הַשֶּׁמֶשׁ הַזֹּאת.
Thus says Jehovah: “Look — I am raising up evil against you out of your own house; and I will take your wives before your eyes, and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this sun.note
Así dice Jehová: “Mira, yo levanto contra ti el mal desde tu propia casa; y tomaré tus mujeres delante de tus ojos, y las daré a tu prójimo, y él yacerá con tus mujeres a la vista de este sol.
12
כִּי אַתָּה, עָשִׂיתָ בַסָּתֶר; וַאֲנִי, אֶעֱשֶׂה אֶת-הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה, נֶגֶד כָּל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְנֶגֶד הַשָּׁמֶשׁ.  {ס}
For you did it in secret; but I will do this thing before all Israel, and before the sun.”’note
Porque tú lo hiciste en secreto; pero yo haré esta cosa delante de todo Israel y delante del sol”».
13
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-נָתָן, חָטָאתִי לַיהוָה;  {ס}  וַיֹּאמֶר נָתָן אֶל-דָּוִד, גַּם-יְהוָה הֶעֱבִיר חַטָּאתְךָ--לֹא תָמוּת.
And David said to Nathan: ‘I have sinned against Jehovah.’ And Nathan said to David: ‘Jehovah also has taken away your sin; you shall not die.note
Y dijo David a Natán: «He pecado contra Jehová». Y dijo Natán a David: «También Jehová ha quitado tu pecado; no morirás.
14
אֶפֶס, כִּי-נִאֵץ נִאַצְתָּ אֶת-אֹיְבֵי יְהוָה, בַּדָּבָר, הַזֶּה; גַּם, הַבֵּן הַיִּלּוֹד לְךָ--מוֹת יָמוּת.
But because by this thing you have given great occasion to the enemies of Jehovah to scorn, the son who is born to you shall surely die.’note
Pero, por cuanto con esta cosa has dado gran ocasión a los enemigos de Jehová para menospreciar, el hijo que te ha nacido ciertamente morirá».
15
וַיֵּלֶךְ נָתָן, אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ; וַיִּגֹּף יְהוָה, אֶת-הַיֶּלֶד אֲשֶׁר יָלְדָה אֵשֶׁת-אוּרִיָּה לְדָוִד--וַיֵּאָנַשׁ.
And Nathan went to his house. And Jehovah struck the child that Uriah’s wife bore to David; and it became gravely ill.note
Y Natán se fue a su casa. Y Jehová hirió al niño que la mujer de Urías había dado a luz a David; y enfermó gravemente.
16
וַיְבַקֵּשׁ דָּוִד אֶת-הָאֱלֹהִים, בְּעַד הַנָּעַר; וַיָּצָם דָּוִד צוֹם, וּבָא וְלָן וְשָׁכַב אָרְצָה.
And David sought God for the boy; and David fasted, and went in, and spent the night lying on the ground.note
Y David buscó a Dios por el muchacho; y ayunó David, y entró, y pasó la noche acostado en la tierra.
17
וַיָּקֻמוּ זִקְנֵי בֵיתוֹ, עָלָיו, לַהֲקִימוֹ, מִן-הָאָרֶץ; וְלֹא אָבָה, וְלֹא-בָרָא אִתָּם לָחֶם.
And the elders of his house stood over him, to raise him up from the ground; but he would not, and did not eat bread with them.note
Y se levantaron los ancianos de su casa junto a él, para levantarlo de la tierra; pero él no quiso, ni comió pan con ellos.
18
וַיְהִי בַּיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי, וַיָּמָת הַיָּלֶד; וַיִּרְאוּ עַבְדֵי דָוִד לְהַגִּיד לוֹ כִּי-מֵת הַיֶּלֶד, כִּי אָמְרוּ הִנֵּה בִהְיוֹת הַיֶּלֶד חַי דִּבַּרְנוּ אֵלָיו וְלֹא-שָׁמַע בְּקוֹלֵנוּ, וְאֵיךְ נֹאמַר אֵלָיו מֵת הַיֶּלֶד, וְעָשָׂה רָעָה.
And it happened on the seventh day that the child died. And the servants of David feared to tell him that the child was dead; for they said: ‘Look, while the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he did not listen to our voice; how then shall we say to him, The child is dead? He may do harm.’note
Y sucedió al séptimo día que murió el niño. Y los siervos de David temían decirle que el niño había muerto; porque decían: «Mira, cuando el niño aún vivía, le hablábamos, y no escuchaba nuestra voz; ¿cómo, pues, le diremos: El niño ha muerto? Podría hacer daño».
19
וַיַּרְא דָּוִד, כִּי עֲבָדָיו מִתְלַחֲשִׁים, וַיָּבֶן דָּוִד, כִּי מֵת הַיָּלֶד; וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד אֶל-עֲבָדָיו הֲמֵת הַיֶּלֶד, וַיֹּאמְרוּ מֵת.
And David saw that his servants were whispering together, and David understood that the child was dead; and David said to his servants: ‘Is the child dead?’ And they said: ‘He is dead.’note
Y vio David que sus siervos cuchicheaban entre sí, y entendió David que el niño había muerto; y dijo David a sus siervos: «¿Ha muerto el niño?» Y ellos dijeron: «Ha muerto».
20
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד מֵהָאָרֶץ וַיִּרְחַץ וַיָּסֶךְ, וַיְחַלֵּף שִׂמְלֹתָו, וַיָּבֹא בֵית-יְהוָה, וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ; וַיָּבֹא, אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ, וַיִּשְׁאַל, וַיָּשִׂימוּ לוֹ לֶחֶם וַיֹּאכַל.
And David arose from the ground, and washed, and anointed himself, and changed his garments; and he went into the house of Jehovah, and prostrated himself; and he went to his own house, and asked, and they set bread before him, and he ate.note
Y se levantó David de la tierra, y se lavó, y se ungió, y cambió sus vestidos; y entró en la casa de Jehová, y se postró; y vino a su casa, y pidió, y le pusieron pan, y comió.
21
וַיֹּאמְרוּ עֲבָדָיו אֵלָיו, מָה-הַדָּבָר הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָה; בַּעֲבוּר הַיֶּלֶד חַי, צַמְתָּ וַתֵּבְךְּ, וְכַאֲשֶׁר מֵת הַיֶּלֶד, קַמְתָּ וַתֹּאכַל לָחֶם.
And his servants said to him: ‘What is this thing that you have done? For the child, while he was alive, you fasted and wept; and when the child was dead, you rose and ate bread.’note
Y sus siervos le dijeron: «¿Qué es esto que has hecho? Por el niño, mientras vivía, ayunaste y lloraste; y cuando murió el niño, te levantaste y comiste pan».
22
וַיֹּאמֶר--בְּעוֹד הַיֶּלֶד חַי, צַמְתִּי וָאֶבְכֶּה:  כִּי אָמַרְתִּי מִי יוֹדֵעַ, יחנני וְחַנַּנִי יְהוָה וְחַי הַיָּלֶד.
And he said: ‘While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept; for I said: Who knows whether Jehovah will be gracious to me, so that the child lives?note
Y él dijo: «Mientras el niño aún vivía, ayuné y lloré; porque dije: ¿Quién sabe si Jehová tendrá compasión de mí, y el niño vivirá?
23
וְעַתָּה מֵת, לָמָּה זֶּה אֲנִי צָם--הַאוּכַל לַהֲשִׁיבוֹ, עוֹד:  אֲנִי הֹלֵךְ אֵלָיו, וְהוּא לֹא-יָשׁוּב אֵלָי.
But now he is dead; why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I am going to him, but he will not return to me.’note
Pero ahora ha muerto; ¿para qué he de ayunar? ¿Puedo yo hacerlo volver? Yo voy a él, pero él no volverá a mí».
24
וַיְנַחֵם דָּוִד, אֵת בַּת-שֶׁבַע אִשְׁתּוֹ, וַיָּבֹא אֵלֶיהָ, וַיִּשְׁכַּב עִמָּהּ; וַתֵּלֶד בֵּן, ויקרא וַתִּקְרָא אֶת-שְׁמוֹ שְׁלֹמֹה, וַיהוָה, אֲהֵבוֹ.
And David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her, and lay with her; and she bore a son, and she called his name Solomon. And Jehovah loved him;note
Y David consoló a Betsabé su mujer, y fue a ella y se acostó con ella; y ella dio a luz un hijo, y ella llamó su nombre Salomón. Y Jehová lo amó;
25
וַיִּשְׁלַח, בְּיַד נָתָן הַנָּבִיא, וַיִּקְרָא אֶת-שְׁמוֹ, יְדִידְיָהּ--בַּעֲבוּר, יְהוָה.  {פ}
and he sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and called his name Jedidiah, for the sake of Jehovah.note
y envió por mano de Natán el profeta, y llamó su nombre Jedidías, por causa de Jehová.
26
וַיִּלָּחֶם יוֹאָב, בְּרַבַּת בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן; וַיִּלְכֹּד, אֶת-עִיר הַמְּלוּכָה.
And Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and took the royal city.note
Y Joab peleaba contra Rabá de los hijos de Amón, y tomó la ciudad real.
27
וַיִּשְׁלַח יוֹאָב מַלְאָכִים, אֶל-דָּוִד; וַיֹּאמֶר נִלְחַמְתִּי בְרַבָּה, גַּם-לָכַדְתִּי אֶת-עִיר הַמָּיִם.
And Joab sent messengers to David, and said: ‘I have fought against Rabbah; I have also taken the city of waters.note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · Nathan’s parable of the ewe lamb — the poor man’s one lamb, “he spared”, “a son of death”, and fourfold

Jehovah sent Nathan. “And Jehovah sent Nathan to David”: Nathan was last on these pages telling David to do all that was in his heart (2 Samuel 7:3, already on these pages), and Jehovah is named here for the first time since the last verse of the chapter before. The NIV has “The Lord sent Nathan to David”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah proceeded to send Nathan to David”; the TLB “So the Lord sent the prophet Nathan to tell David this story”. The poor man is rash, and the NWT 1984 has “the other of little means”; the ASV “the other poor”. The Greek has ton Nathan ton prophētēn, “Nathan the prophet”.

One little ewe lamb. “But the poor man had nothing, except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought, and kept alive; and it grew up with him and with his children together. From his morsel it ate, and from his cup it drank, and in his bosom it lay; and it was to him like a daughter”: the lamb eats and drinks as the household does and lies where a child would lie, and the last words are kevat, “like a daughter”, bat being the first half of the name Bath-sheba; the parable does not say so. The ASV has “one little ewe lamb, which he had bought and nourished up”; the NIV “one little ewe lamb he had bought” and “It was like a daughter to him”; the NWT 1984 “one female lamb, a small one, that he had bought” and “he was preserving it alive”; the TLB “a little lamb he had managed to buy”; the Douay “one little ewe lamb”; the Geneva “slept in his bosom”; the KJV “lay in his bosom”. The NIV has “even slept in his arms” and the TLB “he cuddled it in his arms like a baby daughter”.

He spared. “And a traveler came to the rich man, and he spared to take from his own flock and from his own herd … and he took the poor man’s lamb”: “and he spared” (vayyachmol) stands in three verses. They are this one, 1 Samuel 15:9 (already on these pages), where Saul and the people “spared Agag, and the best of the flock”, and 2 Samuel 21:7, where David spares Mephibosheth. The rich man spares what is his and takes what is not, and in verse 6 the verb comes back in the negative, “he had no pity”. The ASV has “he spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd”; the KJV “he spared to take of his own flock and of his own herd”; the NIV “the rich man refrained from taking one of his own sheep or cattle”; the NWT 1984 “he spared taking some from his own sheep and his own cattle”; the Douay “he spared to take of his own sheep and oxen”; the Geneva “who refused to take of his own sheep”; the TLB “instead of killing a lamb from his own flocks”.

A son of death. “As Jehovah lives, the man who did this is a son of death!”: “a son of death” and “sons of death” stand in three verses. They are Saul’s word of David, “he is a son of death” (1 Samuel 20:31), David’s word of Abner and his guard, “you are sons of death” (26:16, both already on these pages), and this one. “The man who did this” is ha’ish, and Nathan’s answer is attah ha’ish, “you are the man”. The ASV has “is worthy to die”; the NWT 1984 “deserves to die”; the NIV “the man who did this must die”; the KJV “shall surely die”; the Douay “is a child of death”; the TLB “should be put to death”. The Greek has huios thanatou, “a son of death”.

Fourfold. “And he shall pay for the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity”: “fourfold” (arba’tayim) stands in one verse. The law for a stolen sheep is “four sheep for the sheep” (Exodus 21:37, already on these pages), so David judges by the book; the Greek has heptaplasiona, “sevenfold”. The ASV has “he shall restore the lamb fourfold, because he did this thing, and because he had no pity”; the NIV “He must pay for that lamb four times over” and “had no pity”; the NWT 1984 “make compensation with four” and “he did not have compassion”; the TLB “he shall repay four lambs”; the Douay “He shall restore the ewe fourfold”; the Geneva “had no pity thereof”. The sentence David passes on the rich man is the one that falls on him.

Verses 7–12 · “You are the man” — anointed, delivered, “into your bosom”, “evil in my eyes”, the sword that never departs, and wives “before the sun”

You are the man. “And Nathan said to David: ‘You are the man. Thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel: “I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul’”: the Hebrew is two words, attah ha’ish, and Jehovah’s speech begins with his own two, anokhi meshachtikha, “I anointed you”. Samuel anointed him in the midst of his brothers (1 Samuel 16:13) and the elders anointed him at Hebron (2 Samuel 5:3, both already on these pages). The NIV has “You are the man!”; the NWT 1984 “You yourself are the man!”; the TLB “You are that rich man!”; and the ASV, the KJV, the Geneva and the Douay all have “Thou art the man”. For the anointing the ASV has “I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul”; the NIV “I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul”; the NWT 1984 “I myself anointed you as king over Israel”; the TLB “I made you king of Israel and saved you from the power of Saul”.

Into your bosom. “And I gave you your master’s house, and your master’s wives into your bosom, and gave you the house of Israel and of Judah; and if that was too little, I would add to you more of the same, and more again”: “into your bosom” (bechéiqekha) stands in five verses. They are Sarai’s “I myself gave my slave-girl into your bosom” (Genesis 16:5), Moses putting his hand into it (Exodus 4:6, “into your cloak” in the site’s English), “carry them in your bosom” (Numbers 11:12, all already on these pages), this verse, and Abishag, who is to “lie in your bosom” (1 Kings 1:2, already on these pages). The poor man’s lamb lay in “his bosom” in verse 3. The text does not say which wives are meant; Saul’s concubine Rizpah is named at 2 Samuel 3:7, and David’s own wives and concubines at 5:13 (both already on these pages). The NIV has “your master’s wives into your arms”; the NWT 1984 “the wives of your lord into your bosom”; the TLB “I gave you his palace and his wives”; and the ASV and the Douay have “thy master’s wives into thy bosom”. “More of the same” is kahennah vekahennah, “like these and like these”: the ASV has “I would have added unto thee such and such things”; the NIV “I would have given you even more”; the NWT 1984 “I was willing to add to you things like these as well as other things”; the TLB “I would have given you much, much more”.

Evil in my eyes. “Why did you despise the word of Jehovah, to do what is evil in my eyes? Uriah the Hittite you struck with the sword, and his wife you took to be your wife; and him you killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon”: the written text has “in his eyes” (be’eino) and the reading tradition “in my eyes” (be’einai), so that Jehovah speaks of himself in the first person and in the third in one clause; this translation follows the reading. The ASV has “to do that which is evil in his sight”; the NIV “by doing what is evil in his eyes”; the NWT 1984 “by doing what is bad in his eyes”; the Douay “to do evil in my sight”. “Evil in the eyes” is the phrase of 11:25 and 11:27 (2 Samuel 11:27, already on these pages). To “despise the word of Jehovah” is the sin for which a soul is cut off (Numbers 15:31, already on these pages). The ASV has “Wherefore hast thou despised the word of Jehovah”; the NIV “Why did you despise the word of the Lord”; the NWT 1984 “Why did you despise the word of Jehovah”; the KJV “despised the commandment of”; the TLB “Why, then, have you despised the laws of God”. For the killing the ASV has “thou hast smitten Uriah the Hittite with the sword”; the NIV “You struck down Uriah the Hittite with the sword”; the NWT 1984 “you struck down with the sword”. The verb is hikkita, “you struck”, and the Hebrew puts it as David’s own stroke, and also “with the sword of the sons of Ammon”.

The sword shall never depart. “And now the sword shall never depart from your house, because you despised me”: the sword that “devours in this way and in that” (2 Samuel 11:25) and the sword Abner asked about, “Shall the sword devour for ever?” (2 Samuel 2:26, both already on these pages), is now fixed to David’s house, ad olam, “for ever”. The ASV has “the sword shall never depart from thy house”; the KJV “the sword shall never depart from thine house”; the NIV “the sword will never depart from your house”; the Douay “the sword shall never depart from thy house”; the NWT 1984 “a sword will not depart from your own house to time indefinite”; the TLB “murder shall be a constant threat in your family from this time on”.

Before all Israel, and before the sun. “Look — I am raising up evil against you out of your own house; and I will take your wives before your eyes, and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives in the sight of this sun. For you did it in secret; but I will do this thing before all Israel, and before the sun”: the “neighbor” is not named; the roof where David walked in 11:2 comes back with a tent on it, and what is done there is done “in the sight of all Israel” (2 Samuel 16:22, now on these pages). The ASV has “I will raise up evil against thee out of thine own house” and “in the sight of this sun”; the NIV “Out of your own household I am going to bring calamity on you” and “in broad daylight”; the NWT 1984 “under the eyes of this sun”; the Douay “in the sight of this sun”; the TLB “in public view”. For verse 12 the ASV has “before all Israel, and before the sun”; the NWT 1984 “in front of all Israel and in front of the sun”; the NIV “in broad daylight before all Israel”; the TLB “in the sight of all Israel”.

Verses 13–14 · “I have sinned against Jehovah” — “taken away your sin”, and the child who shall die

I have sinned against Jehovah. “And David said to Nathan: ‘I have sinned against Jehovah.’”: “I have sinned against Jehovah” (chatati laYHWH) stands in three verses. They are Pharaoh’s (Exodus 10:16), Achan’s (Joshua 7:20, both already on these pages), and this one. Saul had said only “I have sinned” (1 Samuel 15:24, 30; 26:21, already on these pages) and then asked to be honored before the elders; David says it in two words and asks for nothing. The ASV has “I have sinned against Jehovah”, as do the NWT 1984 and the Geneva; the TLB adds “confessed”, “‘I have sinned against the Lord,’ David confessed to Nathan”.

Taken away your sin. “Jehovah also has taken away your sin; you shall not die”: “he has taken away your sin” (he’evir chatta’tkha) stands in one verse; the verb is “cause to pass”, as in Zechariah’s “I have caused your iniquity to pass from you” (Zechariah 3:4, already on these pages). “You shall not die” answers David’s own “son of death” (verse 5), and the death goes to the child. The ASV has “Jehovah also hath put away thy sin; thou shalt not die”; the NIV “has taken away your sin”; the NWT 1984 “does let your sin pass by”; the Douay “hath taken away thy sin”; the TLB “the Lord has forgiven you”. The Greek has paregibasen to hamartēma sou, “caused your sin to pass over”.

The enemies of Jehovah. “But because by this thing you have given great occasion to the enemies of Jehovah to scorn, the son who is born to you shall surely die”: the Hebrew is ni’ets ni’atsta et oyevei YHWH, “you have utterly spurned the enemies of Jehovah”, which is hard as it stands, and the versions give it three senses. The ASV has “thou hast given great occasion to the enemies of Jehovah to blaspheme”; the Geneva “thou hast caused the enemies of the Lord to blaspheme”; the Douay “thou hast given occasion to the enemies of the Lord to blaspheme”; the TLB “you have given great opportunity to the enemies of the Lord to despise and blaspheme him”; and the NIV “you have shown utter contempt for the Lord”; the NWT 1984 “you have unquestionably treated Jehovah with disrespect”, which drop “enemies” and make Jehovah himself the object of the spurning. The Greek keeps the enemies, parōxynas tous echthrous kyriou, “you provoked the enemies of the Lord”. Mot yamut, “shall surely die”, is the form of the first warning in the garden (Genesis 2:17, already on these pages, not yet in Spanish).

Verses 15–19 · The child struck — “Uriah’s wife bore”, “sought God for the boy”, the ground, and servants whispering

Jehovah struck the child. “And Nathan went to his house. And Jehovah struck the child that Uriah’s wife bore to David; and it became gravely ill”: nagaf is the verb of defeat in chapter 10, “beaten before Israel”, and of Jehovah’s “struck the people” (Exodus 32:35, already on these pages). The mother is “Uriah’s wife”, as at the end of chapter 11. “And it became gravely ill” (vayye’anash) stands in one verse. The ASV has “Jehovah struck the child that Uriah’s wife bare unto David, and it was very sick”; the NIV “struck the child that Uriah’s wife had borne to David, and he became ill”; the NWT 1984 “proceeded to deal a blow to the child”; the TLB “made Bathsheba’s baby deathly sick”; the Douay “struck the child which the wife of Urias had borne to David”. The Greek has ethrausen, “shattered”.

Sought God for the boy. “And David sought God for the boy; and David fasted, and went in, and spent the night lying on the ground. And the elders of his house stood over him, to raise him up from the ground; but he would not, and did not eat bread with them”: “And David sought” stands in two verses, this one and the verse where he seeks the face of Jehovah in the famine (2 Samuel 21:1). Here the child is hanna’ar, “the boy”, once, and “the child” everywhere else; and God is ha’Elohim, with the article, which the NWT 1984 prints as “the [true] God”. The ASV has “David therefore besought God for the child; and David fasted, and went in, and lay all night upon the earth”; the NIV “David pleaded with God for the child”; the NWT 1984 “David began to seek the [true] God in behalf of the boy”; the TLB “David begged him to spare the child”. The Greek has ēulisthē en sakkō, “spent the night in sackcloth”, and the NIV has “lying in sackcloth on the ground”. For the elders the ASV has “the elders of his house arose, and stood beside him, to raise him up from the earth”; the NIV “The elders of his household stood beside him to get him up from the ground”; the NWT 1984 “the older men of his house stood up over him to raise him up from the earth”; the Douay “the ancients of his house came, to make him rise from the ground”.

They were afraid to tell him. “And it happened on the seventh day that the child died. And the servants of David feared to tell him that the child was dead … And David saw that his servants were whispering together, and David understood that the child was dead”: “whispering together” (mitlachashim) stands in one verse. What the servants fear, ve’asah ra’ah, is that he “will do evil”, which the versions read as harm to himself; it is the noun of verses 9 and 11. The ASV has “how will he then vex himself, if we tell him that the child is dead!”; the NIV “He may do something desperate”; the NWT 1984 “Then he will certainly do something bad”; the Douay “how much more will he afflict himself if we tell him that the child is dead?”; the Geneva “to vex him more?” For the whispering the ASV has “his servants were whispering together”; the NWT 1984 “his servants were whispering together”; the NIV “his attendants were whispering among themselves”; the KJV “his servants whispered”.

Verses 20–23 · David arose and washed — “Who knows”, “gracious to me”, and “I am going to him”

Washed, anointed, changed. “And David arose from the ground, and washed, and anointed himself, and changed his garments; and he went into the house of Jehovah, and prostrated himself; and he went to his own house, and asked, and they set bread before him, and he ate”: rachatz, “washed”, is the verb of the woman on the roof and of “wash your feet” (2 Samuel 11:2, 8, already on these pages). Naomi tells Ruth “wash, and anoint yourself” (Ruth 3:3, already on these pages). “And he changed his garments” stands in two verses, this one and Joseph’s change of clothes before Pharaoh (Genesis 41:14, already on these pages). The “house of Jehovah” is the tent David pitched for the ark (2 Samuel 6:17), a name already used of Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:7, both already on these pages). The ASV has “David arose from the earth, and washed, and anointed himself, and changed his apparel”; the NIV “After he had washed, put on lotions and changed his clothes”; the NWT 1984 “washed and rubbed himself with oil and changed his mantles”; the TLB “washed himself, brushed his hair, changed his clothes”. For the worship the ASV has “he came into the house of Jehovah, and worshipped”; the NWT 1984 “came to the house of Jehovah and prostrated himself”; the TLB “went into the Tabernacle and worshiped the Lord”; and for the meal the ASV has “when he required, they set bread before him, and he did eat”; the NIV “at his request they served him food, and he ate”.

Who knows. “For I said: Who knows whether Jehovah will be gracious to me, so that the child lives?”: “Who knows” (mi yode’a) stands in ten verses. They are this one, Ecclesiastes 3:21 (already on these pages), and eight others: Joel 2:14, Jonah 3:9, Psalm 90:11, Proverbs 24:22, Ecclesiastes 2:19; 6:12; 8:1 and Esther 4:14; in Joel and Jonah it is the hope that God may turn, and here the hope does not hold. “Gracious” is yechanneni, from chanan, the root of Hanun’s name in the chapter before (2 Samuel 10:1, already on these pages). The ASV has “Who knoweth whether Jehovah will not be gracious to me, that the child may live?”; the NIV “Who knows? The Lord may be gracious to me and let the child live”; the NWT 1984 “Who is there knowing whether Jehovah may show me favor”; the TLB “Perhaps the Lord will be gracious to me and let the child live”; the Douay “Who knoweth whether the Lord may not give him to me”. The servants ask “What is this thing that you have done?”, and David answers with what he had asked of God while the child lived.

I am going to him. “But now he is dead; why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I am going to him, but he will not return to me”: ani holekh elav, a participle, “I am going to him”, and the Hebrew does not say where. The ASV has “I shall go to him, but he will not return to me”; the NIV “I will go to him, but he will not return to me”; the NWT 1984 “I am going to him, but, as for him, he will not return to me”; the TLB “I shall go to him, but he shall not return to me”. The Greek has egō poreusomai pros auton kai autos ouk anastrepsei pros me.

Verses 24–25 · Solomon and Jedidiah — “comforted”, “she called”, “Jehovah loved him”, and “he sent by the hand of Nathan”

Comforted, and a son. “And David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and went in to her, and lay with her; and she bore a son, and she called his name Solomon. And Jehovah loved him”: nacham, “comforted”, is the verb of the comforters of chapter 10, and “his wife” is the first time she is his wife without “Uriah’s” (2 Samuel 10:2, already on these pages). “Went in to her and lay with her” is the pair of verbs of 11:4. The written text has “he called” (vayyiqra) and the reading tradition “she called” (vattiqra); this translation follows the reading, and in the Chronicles it is Jehovah who names him (1 Chronicles 22:9). Shelomoh is from shalom, “peace”, the word of David’s three “welfares” to Uriah (2 Samuel 11:7, already on these pages). The ASV has “and he called his name Solomon”; the KJV “he called his name Solomon”; the NIV “they named him Solomon”; the NWT 1984 “his name came to be called”; the TLB “gave birth to a son and named him Solomon”. For the love the ASV has “And Jehovah loved him”; the NIV “The Lord loved him”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah himself did love him”; the TLB “the Lord loved the baby”.

He sent by the hand of Nathan. “And he sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and called his name Jedidiah, for the sake of Jehovah”: “Jedidiah” (Yedidyah, “beloved of Jehovah”) stands in one verse. Who sends is not said; the nearest subject is Jehovah, and the versions divide. The ASV has “he sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet”; the NWT 1984 “he sent by means of Nathan the prophet”; the NIV “he sent word through Nathan the prophet to name him Jedidiah”; the Geneva “For the Lord had sent by Nathan the Prophet”; the Douay “he sent by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and called his name, Amiable to the Lord”; the TLB “sent congratulations and blessings through Nathan the prophet”. The Greek has Idedi and heneken kyriou, “for the Lord’s sake”. The prophet who came with the word of the child’s death comes again with a name.

Verses 26–31 · Rabbah taken — “the city of waters”, “my name be called over it”, the crown of “their king”, and the saws

The city of waters. “And Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon, and took the royal city. And Joab sent messengers to David, and said: ‘I have fought against Rabbah; I have also taken the city of waters. And now gather the rest of the people, and encamp against the city, and take it; lest I take the city, and my name be called over it’”: “the city of waters” stands in one verse, and it is probably the water supply of Rabbah. The idiom “be called over it” is the one of “the name of Jehovah is called over you” (Deuteronomy 28:10, already on these pages); Joab declines the naming of a city after himself, in the chapter where two names are given to a child. The ASV has “took the royal city” and “I have taken the city of waters”; the NIV “captured the royal citadel” and “taken its water supply”; the NWT 1984 “got to capture the city of the kingdom” and “captured the city of waters”; the Douay “laid close siege to the royal city”; the Geneva “took the city of the Kingdom”; the TLB “Rabbah and its beautiful harbor are ours!” For the name the ASV has “lest I take the city, and it be called after my name”; the NIV “Otherwise I will take the city, and it will be named after me”; the NWT 1984 “my name should not have to be called upon it”; the TLB “so that you will get the credit for the victory instead of me”; the Douay “the victory be ascribed to my name”.

The crown of their king. “And he took the crown of their king from off his head; and its weight was a talent of gold, and precious stones; and it was set on David’s head”: the pointed text reads malkam, “their king”, and the same consonants are the name of the god Milcom elsewhere; Amos, speaking of Rabbah in the same breath, says “their king will go into exile” (Amos 1:14–15, already on these pages). The Greek has Melchol tou basileōs autōn, “Milcom of their king”. The KJV has “their king’s crown”; the NIV “the crown from their king’s head”; the Geneva “their king’s crown”; the Douay “the crown of their king”; the NWT 1984 “the crown of Malcam”; the TLB “the king of Rabbah’s crown”. The stone is even yeqarah, singular and collective.

To the saw. “And he brought out the people who were in it, and put them to the saw, and to iron threshing-sledges, and to iron axes, and made them pass through the brickmold; and thus he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon”: the verbs are “put” and “made pass”, and whether these are tools of forced labor or of killing the Hebrew does not say. “The brickmold” (malben) stands in three verses, this one, Jeremiah 43:9 and Nahum 3:14; the bricks of the Egyptian service were “mortar and bricks” (Exodus 1:14, already on these pages). The Chronicles read “and cut them with saws, and with harrows of iron, and with axes” (1 Chronicles 20:3), which makes the saws a killing, and omit the brickmold. The ASV has “put them under saws, and under harrows of iron, and under axes of iron, and made them pass through the brickkiln”; the Geneva “put them under saws, and under iron harrows”; the NIV “consigning them to labor with saws and with iron picks and axes”; the NWT 1984 “he might put them at sawing stones”; the TLB “made slaves of the people of the city and made them labor with saws, picks, and axes”; the Douay “he sawed them, and drove over them chariots armed with iron”. The Greek has ethēken en tō priōni, “put in the saw”, and dieagagen autous dia tou plintheiou, “led them through the brick-kiln”. David and the people then return to Jerusalem, where the chapter began.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a son of death” as in 1 Samuel 20:31; “fourfold”; “You are the man”; “into your bosom”; “evil in my eyes”, as in 2 Samuel 11:25, 27, with the written “his” in the note; “the sword shall never depart”; “I have sinned against Jehovah”; “taken away”; “the boy” once and “the child” elsewhere; “washed” as in 2 Samuel 11:2, 8; “Who knows”; “gracious”; “she called”, as the reading tradition has it; “the city of waters”; “their king”; “the saw” and “the brickmold”.

Echoes paid. Nathan and the house promise (2 Samuel 7:3); Saul’s and David’s “son of death” (1 Samuel 20:31; 26:16); Saul’s sparing of Agag (1 Samuel 15:9); the law of the sheep (Exodus 21:37); the anointings (1 Samuel 16:13; 2 Samuel 5:3); Saul’s concubine and David’s wives (2 Samuel 3:7; 5:13); Sarai’s “into your bosom” (Genesis 16:5; Exodus 4:6; Numbers 11:12); “evil in the eyes” and the sword that devours (2 Samuel 11:25, 27; 2:26); the word despised (Numbers 15:31); Pharaoh’s and Achan’s “I have sinned against Jehovah” (Exodus 10:16; Joshua 7:20) and Saul’s plain “I have sinned” (1 Samuel 15:24, 30; 26:21); “Jehovah struck” (Exodus 32:35); “wash” and “anoint” (2 Samuel 11:2, 8; Ruth 3:3); Joseph’s change of clothes (Genesis 41:14); the tent and Shiloh’s “house of Jehovah” (2 Samuel 6:17; 1 Samuel 1:7); Hanun and “gracious” (2 Samuel 10:1); the comforters (2 Samuel 10:2); the welfares (2 Samuel 11:7); the name called over (Deuteronomy 28:10); the bricks of Egypt (Exodus 1:14); and “their king” in Amos (Amos 1:14–15); Abishag in the bosom (1 Kings 1:2); Zechariah’s “I have caused your iniquity to pass” (Zechariah 3:4); “Who knows” in Ecclesiastes 3:21; and Psalm 51, whose heading names this chapter.

Echoes planted. The wives taken “in the sight of all Israel” on the roof (2 Samuel 16:21–22, now on these pages); the sword in Amnon’s killing (2 Samuel 13:28, now on these pages) and in the battle against Absalom, where the forest devours more than the sword (2 Samuel 18:8, now on these pages), and in Sheba’s (2 Samuel 20); David’s seeking in the famine (2 Samuel 21:1) and the sparing of Mephibosheth (21:7); Solomon named by Jehovah in the Chronicles (1 Chronicles 22:9); the Chronicles’ saws (1 Chronicles 20:3); “Who knows” in Joel and Jonah (Joel 2:14; Jonah 3:9); the cause of the sword in Kings (1 Kings 2:25). None of the others is translated here yet.
