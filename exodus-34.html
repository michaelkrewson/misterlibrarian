Exodus 34 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, פְּסָל-לְךָ שְׁנֵי-לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים כָּרִאשֹׁנִים; וְכָתַבְתִּי, עַל-הַלֻּחֹת, אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים, אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ עַל-הַלֻּחֹת הָרִאשֹׁנִים אֲשֶׁר שִׁבַּרְתָּ.
And Jehovah said to Moses: Cut for yourself two tablets of stone like the first, and I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you broke.note
Y Jehová dijo a Moisés: Tállate dos tablas de piedra como las primeras, y escribiré sobre las tablas las palabras que estaban en las tablas primeras, que quebraste.
2
וֶהְיֵה נָכוֹן, לַבֹּקֶר; וְעָלִיתָ בַבֹּקֶר אֶל-הַר סִינַי, וְנִצַּבְתָּ לִי שָׁם עַל-רֹאשׁ הָהָר.
And be ready by morning, and come up in the morning to Mount Sinai, and present yourself there to me on the top of the mountain.note
Y está listo para la mañana, y sube por la mañana al monte Sinaí, y preséntate allí ante mí en la cumbre del monte.
3
וְאִישׁ לֹא-יַעֲלֶה עִמָּךְ, וְגַם-אִישׁ אַל-יֵרָא בְּכָל-הָהָר; גַּם-הַצֹּאן וְהַבָּקָר אַל-יִרְעוּ, אֶל-מוּל הָהָר הַהוּא.
And no man shall come up with you, and let no man even be seen anywhere on the mountain. Let neither flocks nor herds graze before that mountain.note
Y ningún hombre subirá contigo, y que ningún hombre sea siquiera visto en todo el monte. Que ni ovejas ni bueyes pazcan delante de aquel monte.
4
וַיִּפְסֹל שְׁנֵי-לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים כָּרִאשֹׁנִים, וַיַּשְׁכֵּם מֹשֶׁה בַבֹּקֶר וַיַּעַל אֶל-הַר סִינַי, כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוָּה יְהוָה, אֹתוֹ; וַיִּקַּח בְּיָדוֹ, שְׁנֵי לֻחֹת אֲבָנִים.
And he cut two tablets of stone like the first, and Moses rose up early in the morning and went up to Mount Sinai, as Jehovah had commanded him, and took in his hand two tablets of stone.note
Y tálló dos tablas de piedra como las primeras, y Moisés se levantó temprano por la mañana y subió al monte Sinaí, como Jehová le había mandado, y tomó en su mano dos tablas de piedra.
5
וַיֵּרֶד יְהוָה בֶּעָנָן, וַיִּתְיַצֵּב עִמּוֹ שָׁם; וַיִּקְרָא בְשֵׁם, יְהוָה.
And Jehovah descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name Jehovah.note
Y Jehová descendió en la nube, y estuvo allí con él, y proclamó el nombre Jehová.
6
וַיַּעֲבֹר יְהוָה עַל-פָּנָיו, וַיִּקְרָא, יְהוָה יְהוָה, אֵל רַחוּם וְחַנּוּן--אֶרֶךְ אַפַּיִם, וְרַב-חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת.
And Jehovah passed before him and proclaimed: Jehovah, Jehovah, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abundant in steadfast love and truth,note
Y Jehová pasó delante de él y proclamó: Jehová, Jehová, Dios misericordioso y clemente, tardo para la ira, y abundante en amor leal y verdad,
cross-refs⤷ 33:19
7
נֹצֵר חֶסֶד לָאֲלָפִים, נֹשֵׂא עָוֺן וָפֶשַׁע וְחַטָּאָה; וְנַקֵּה, לֹא יְנַקֶּה--פֹּקֵד עֲוֺן אָבוֹת עַל-בָּנִים וְעַל-בְּנֵי בָנִים, עַל-שִׁלֵּשִׁים וְעַל-רִבֵּעִים.
keeping steadfast love to the thousandth generation, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin — yet he will by no means clear the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children and upon the children’s children, to the third and to the fourth generation.note
que guarda amor leal hasta la milésima generación, que perdona la iniquidad, la transgresión y el pecado —pero de ningún modo tendrá por inocente al culpable, que visita la iniquidad de los padres sobre los hijos y sobre los hijos de los hijos, hasta la tercera y hasta la cuarta generación.
8
וַיְמַהֵר, מֹשֶׁה; וַיִּקֹּד אַרְצָה, וַיִּשְׁתָּחוּ.
And Moses quickly bowed his head to the ground and worshiped.note
Y Moisés se apresuró, e inclinó su cabeza hasta la tierra, y adoró.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר אִם-נָא מָצָאתִי חֵן בְּעֵינֶיךָ, אֲדֹנָי, יֵלֶךְ-נָא אֲדֹנָי, בְּקִרְבֵּנוּ: כִּי עַם-קְשֵׁה-עֹרֶף הוּא, וְסָלַחְתָּ לַעֲוֺנֵנוּ וּלְחַטָּאתֵנוּ וּנְחַלְתָּנוּ.
And he said: If now I have found favor in your sight, O Lord, let the Lord, I pray, go in our midst, for it is a stiffnecked people; and pardon our iniquity and our sin, and take us for your inheritance.note
Y dijo: Si ahora he hallado favor ante tus ojos, oh Señor, vaya ahora el Señor, te ruego, en medio de nosotros, porque es un pueblo de dura cerviz; y perdona nuestra iniquidad y nuestro pecado, y tómanos por tu heredad.
cross-refs⤷ 33:5
10
וַיֹּאמֶר, הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי כֹּרֵת בְּרִית, נֶגֶד כָּל-עַמְּךָ אֶעֱשֶׂה נִפְלָאֹת, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-נִבְרְאוּ בְכָל-הָאָרֶץ וּבְכָל-הַגּוֹיִם; וְרָאָה כָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר-אַתָּה בְקִרְבּוֹ אֶת-מַעֲשֵׂה יְהוָה, כִּי-נוֹרָא הוּא, אֲשֶׁר אֲנִי, עֹשֶׂה עִמָּךְ.
And he said: Behold, I make a covenant. Before all your people I will do marvels such as have not been done in all the earth, nor in any nation, and all the people among whom you live shall see the work of Jehovah, for it is a fearsome thing that I am about to do with you.note
Y dijo: He aquí, hago un pacto. Delante de todo tu pueblo haré maravillas cuales no han sido hechas en toda la tierra, ni en nación alguna, y todo el pueblo entre el cual vives verá la obra de Jehová, porque es cosa temible lo que voy a hacer contigo.
11
שְׁמָר-לְךָ--אֵת אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי, מְצַוְּךָ הַיּוֹם; הִנְנִי גֹרֵשׁ מִפָּנֶיךָ, אֶת-הָאֱמֹרִי וְהַכְּנַעֲנִי, וְהַחִתִּי וְהַפְּרִזִּי, וְהַחִוִּי וְהַיְבוּסִי.
Observe what I command you this day. Behold, I am driving out before you the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite.note
Observa lo que yo te mando hoy. He aquí, yo expulso delante de ti al amorreo, y al cananeo, y al heteo, y al ferezeo, y al heveo, y al jebuseo.
12
הִשָּׁמֶר לְךָ, פֶּן-תִּכְרֹת בְּרִית לְיוֹשֵׁב הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה, בָּא עָלֶיהָ: פֶּן-יִהְיֶה לְמוֹקֵשׁ, בְּקִרְבֶּךָ.
Take heed to yourself, lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land where you are going, lest it become a snare in your midst.note
Guárdate de hacer pacto con los habitantes de la tierra adonde vas, no sea que se vuelva lazo en medio de ti.
13
כִּי אֶת-מִזְבְּחֹתָם תִּתֹּצוּן, וְאֶת-מַצֵּבֹתָם תְּשַׁבֵּרוּן; וְאֶת-אֲשֵׁרָיו, תִּכְרֹתוּן.
But you shall tear down their altars, and smash their sacred pillars, and cut down their Asherah poles —note
Sino que derribarás sus altares, y quebrarás sus columnas sagradas, y cortarás sus aíderas —
14
כִּי לֹא תִשְׁתַּחֲוֶה, לְאֵל אַחֵר: כִּי יְהוָה קַנָּא שְׁמוֹ, אֵל קַנָּא הוּא.
for you shall bow down to no other god, for Jehovah, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God —note
porque no te inclinarás a ningún otro dios, porque Jehová, cuyo nombre es Celoso, es un Dios celoso —
15
פֶּן-תִּכְרֹת בְּרִית, לְיוֹשֵׁב הָאָרֶץ; וְזָנוּ אַחֲרֵי אֱלֹהֵיהֶם, וְזָבְחוּ לֵאלֹהֵיהֶם, וְקָרָא לְךָ, וְאָכַלְתָּ מִזִּבְחוֹ.
lest you make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and they whore after their gods and sacrifice to their gods, and one of them invites you and you eat of his sacrifice,note
no sea que hagas pacto con los habitantes de la tierra, y se prostituyan tras sus dioses y sacrifiquen a sus dioses, y alguno de ellos te invite y comas de su sacrificio,
16
וְלָקַחְתָּ מִבְּנֹתָיו, לְבָנֶיךָ; וְזָנוּ בְנֹתָיו, אַחֲרֵי אֱלֹהֵיהֶן, וְהִזְנוּ אֶת-בָּנֶיךָ, אַחֲרֵי אֱלֹהֵיהֶן.
and you take his daughters for your sons, and his daughters whore after their gods and make your sons whore after their gods.note
y tomes de sus hijas para tus hijos, y sus hijas se prostituyan tras sus dioses y hagan que tus hijos se prostituyan tras sus dioses.
17
אֱלֹהֵי מַסֵּכָה, לֹא תַעֲשֶׂה-לָּךְ.
You shall make yourself no molten gods.note
No te harás dioses de fundición.
cross-refs⤷ 32:4
18
אֶת-חַג הַמַּצּוֹת, תִּשְׁמֹר--שִׁבְעַת יָמִים תֹּאכַל מַצּוֹת אֲשֶׁר צִוִּיתִךָ, לְמוֹעֵד חֹדֶשׁ הָאָבִיב: כִּי בְּחֹדֶשׁ הָאָבִיב, יָצָאתָ מִמִּצְרָיִם.
You shall keep the feast of unleavened bread. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time in the month of Abib, for in the month of Abib you came out from Egypt.note
Guardarás la fiesta de los panes sin levadura. Siete días comerás panes sin levadura, como te mandé, en el tiempo señalado en el mes de Abib, porque en el mes de Abib saliste de Egipto.
19
כָּל-פֶּטֶר רֶחֶם, לִי; וְכָל-מִקְנְךָ תִּזָּכָר, פֶּטֶר שׁוֹר וָשֶׂה.
All that opens the womb is mine, and all your livestock that is male, the firstborn of ox and sheep.note
Todo lo que abre matriz es mío, y de todo tu ganado, los machos, las crías primerizas de buey y de oveja.
20
וּפֶטֶר חֲמוֹר תִּפְדֶּה בְשֶׂה, וְאִם-לֹא תִפְדֶּה וַעֲרַפְתּוֹ; כֹּל בְּכוֹר בָּנֶיךָ תִּפְדֶּה, וְלֹא-יֵרָאוּ פָנַי רֵיקָם.
And the firstborn of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb, and if you will not redeem it, you shall break its neck. Every firstborn of your sons you shall redeem. And none shall appear before me empty-handed.note
Y la cría primeriza de un asno la redimirás con un cordero, y si no la redimes, le quebrarás la nuca. A todo primogénito de tus hijos redimirás. Y ninguno se presentará ante mí con las manos vacías.
21
שֵׁשֶׁת יָמִים תַּעֲבֹד, וּבַיּוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי תִּשְׁבֹּת; בֶּחָרִישׁ וּבַקָּצִיר, תִּשְׁבֹּת.
Six days you shall work, but on the seventh day you shall rest; in plowing time and in harvest you shall rest.note
Seis días trabajarás, mas el séptimo día descansarás; en la siembra y en la siega descansarás.
22
וְחַג שָׁבֻעֹת תַּעֲשֶׂה לְךָ, בִּכּוּרֵי קְצִיר חִטִּים; וְחַג, הָאָסִיף--תְּקוּפַת, הַשָּׁנָה.
And you shall keep the feast of weeks, the firstfruits of the wheat harvest, and the feast of ingathering at the turn of the year.note
Y guardarás la fiesta de las semanas, de las primicias de la siega del trigo, y la fiesta de la cosecha al fin del año.
23
שָׁלֹשׁ פְּעָמִים, בַּשָּׁנָה--יֵרָאֶה, כָּל-זְכוּרְךָ, אֶת-פְּנֵי הָאָדֹן יְהוָה, אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the Lord, Jehovah, the God of Israel.note
Tres veces en el año se presentará todo varón tuyo ante el Señor, Jehová, el Dios de Israel.
24
כִּי-אוֹרִישׁ גּוֹיִם מִפָּנֶיךָ, וְהִרְחַבְתִּי אֶת-גְּבֻלֶךָ; וְלֹא-יַחְמֹד אִישׁ, אֶת-אַרְצְךָ, בַּעֲלֹתְךָ לֵרָאוֹת אֶת-פְּנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, שָׁלֹשׁ פְּעָמִים בַּשָּׁנָה.
For I will cast out nations before you and enlarge your borders, and no man shall covet your land when you go up to appear before Jehovah your God three times in the year.note
Porque yo expulsaré naciones delante de ti y ensancharé tus fronteras, y ningún hombre codiciará tu tierra cuando subas a presentarte ante Jehová tu Dios tres veces en el año.
25
לֹא-תִשְׁחַט עַל-חָמֵץ, דַּם-זִבְחִי; וְלֹא-יָלִין לַבֹּקֶר, זֶבַח חַג הַפָּסַח.
You shall not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leavened bread, and the sacrifice of the feast of the Passover shall not remain until morning.note
No ofrecerás la sangre de mi sacrificio con pan leudado, y el sacrificio de la fiesta de la pascua no quedará hasta la mañana.
26
רֵאשִׁית, בִּכּוּרֵי אַדְמָתְךָ, תָּבִיא, בֵּית יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ; לֹא-תְבַשֵּׁל גְּדִי, בַּחֲלֵב אִמּוֹ. {פ}
The best of the firstfruits of your land you shall bring to the house of Jehovah your God. You shall not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk. {פ}note
Lo mejor de las primicias de tu tierra traerás a la casa de Jehová tu Dios. No cocerás un cabrito en la leche de su madre. {פ}
cross-refs⤷ 23:19
27
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-מֹשֶׁה, כְּתָב-לְךָ אֶת-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה: כִּי עַל-פִּי הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, כָּרַתִּי אִתְּךָ בְּרִית--וְאֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And Jehovah said to Moses: Write these words for yourself, for in accordance with these words I have made a covenant with you and with Israel.note
Y Jehová dijo a Moisés: Escribe estas palabras para ti, porque conforme a estas palabras he hecho un pacto contigo y con Israel.
28
וַיְהִי-שָׁם עִם-יְהוָה, אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם וְאַרְבָּעִים לַיְלָה--לֶחֶם לֹא אָכַל, וּמַיִם לֹא שָׁתָה; וַיִּכְתֹּב עַל-הַלֻּחֹת, אֵת דִּבְרֵי הַבְּרִית--עֲשֶׂרֶת, הַדְּבָרִים.
And he was there with Jehovah forty days and forty nights. He ate no bread and drank no water. And he wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the ten words.note
Y estuvo allí con Jehová cuarenta días y cuarenta noches. No comió pan ni bebió agua. Y escribió en las tablas las palabras del pacto, las diez palabras.
cross-refs⤷ 24:18⤷ Matt 4:2
29
וַיְהִי, בְּרֶדֶת מֹשֶׁה מֵהַר סִינַי, וּשְׁנֵי לֻחֹת הָעֵדֻת בְּיַד-מֹשֶׁה, בְּרִדְתּוֹ מִן-הָהָר; וּמֹשֶׁה לֹא-יָדַע, כִּי קָרַן עוֹר פָּנָיו--בְּדַבְּרוֹ אִתּוֹ.
And when Moses came down from Mount Sinai, with the two tablets of the testimony in Moses’ hand as he came down from the mountain, Moses did not know that the skin of his face shone because he had been talking with him.note
Y cuando Moisés descendió del monte Sinaí, con las dos tablas del testimonio en la mano de Moisés al descender del monte, Moisés no sabía que la piel de su rostro resplandecía por haber hablado con él.
cross-refs⤷ 33:20
30
וַיַּרְא אַהֲרֹן וְכָל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶת-מֹשֶׁה, וְהִנֵּה קָרַן, עוֹר פָּנָיו; וַיִּירְאוּ, מִגֶּשֶׁת אֵלָיו.
And Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, and behold, the skin of his face shone, and they were afraid to come near him.note
Y Aarón y todos los hijos de Israel vieron a Moisés, y he aquí, la piel de su rostro resplandecía, y tuvieron miedo de acercarse a él.

Y Moisés los llamó, y Aarón y todos los jefes de la congregación volvieron a él, y Moisés les habló.

The promise from the last chapter, kept within nine verses

‘I will proclaim the name Jehovah before you,’ God said at the end of the last chapter (Exodus 33:19, already on these pages) — and here, with almost no delay, he does exactly that. Moses climbs the mountain a second time, tablets in hand, and Jehovah descends in the cloud and proclaims his own name aloud. Few promises in the Torah are kept this quickly, or this literally.

‘Jehovah, Jehovah, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abundant in steadfast love and truth’ (vv6–7) is the single most quoted self-description of God in the entire Hebrew Bible. Later writers reach for it constantly, sometimes leaning on its mercy and sometimes on its final clause — ‘he will by no means clear the guilty’ — depending on which half of the formula their own moment needs. Nahum 1:3, already on these pages, quotes the justice half almost verbatim against Nineveh; the same formula also shapes David’s prayers, Jonah’s complaint, and Nehemiah’s national confession, all still ahead of this project. No verse in the Torah does more theological work in fewer words. ⚠ Chesed itself splits the shelf three ways: KJV reads the plain ‘goodness’; ASV and NASB both keep the older ‘lovingkindness’; NIV and ESV modernize to ‘love’ paired with ‘faithfulness.’ This translation follows ESV’s covenantal ‘steadfast love,’ the reading that keeps both the warmth and the loyalty the Hebrew word carries together.

⚠ Moses’ response repeats his own plea from the previous chapter almost word for word: ‘if I have found favor in your sight’ and ‘it is a stiffnecked people’ (v9) are the identical words, and the identical admission, that opened and drove the whole argument of Exodus 33, already on these pages. This is the fourth and final use of ‘stiffnecked’ in three chapters — twice from God (32:9; 33:3, 5) and now once from Moses himself, turning God’s own diagnosis into the very grounds of his appeal for pardon.

A covenant remade, and a warning aimed at exactly what just happened

Every stipulation in this section reads like a direct answer to the golden calf, three chapters back. Tear down foreign altars, smash sacred pillars, cut down Asherah poles (v13); make no covenant with the nations whose gods will become a snare (vv12, 15–16); and above all, ‘you shall make yourself no molten gods’ (v17) — the identical two-word Hebrew phrase, egel massekhah’s own root, that named Aaron’s calf at Exodus 32:4, already on these pages. The renewed covenant does not simply repeat old law; it names the specific failure that broke the first covenant and forbids it again, in the same words.

⚠ ‘Jehovah, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God’ (v14) makes explicit what the Second Commandment implied but never said outright — that qanna, ‘jealous,’ is not merely an attribute God has but something close to a name he claims. The same root already described Jehovah at Exodus 20:5, already on these pages; here, for the first time, the word is not describing God’s reaction but naming who he is.

A calendar repeated almost verbatim, and the verse that built a kitchen out of six words

Nearly this entire section — the three pilgrimage feasts, the firstborn law, the sabbath rest, the ban on leavened bread with the sacrifice — already stood in the book’s first law code, at Exodus 23:14–19, already on these pages. Covenants broken and remade in the ancient Near East were often reissued with their full terms restated, not merely cross-referenced; this chapter follows that convention almost to the letter, repeating the earlier stipulations nearly word for word as part of formally renewing what the golden calf had broken.

⚠ ‘You shall not boil a young goat in its mother’s milk’ (v26) is the same six-word command, essentially unchanged, that closed the very passage this section repeats (Exodus 23:19, already on these pages) — and it will be given a third time still, at Deuteronomy 14:21, not yet on these pages. A command stated three times across the Torah is rare enough to signal its own importance, whatever its original scope; centuries of later interpretation built an entire kosher kitchen on these same six words.

A second forty days, and a face that carries what no one may look at directly

Moses spends forty days and nights on the mountain a second time (v28), fasting exactly as he did the first time the covenant was given (Exodus 24:18, already on these pages). The number repeats because the whole ascent repeats — new tablets for the ones he broke, a second forty-day audience with God, a covenant re-cut in the same shape as the one that failed.

⚠ ‘The skin of his face shone’ (v29, qaran) is the same root behind the Hebrew word for ‘horn,’ keren — a coincidence of roots that produced one of the most famous mistranslations in art history. Every major English version reads ‘shone’ or ‘radiant’ — KJV, ASV, NIV, and NASB all agree — but the Douay-Rheims, translating Jerome’s Vulgate, still reads ‘his face was HORNED.’ See the new dictionary entry for the full story of how Jerome’s Latin gave Moses horns for over a thousand years, and how Michelangelo carved the mistake in marble.

This is the answer, in the same book, to the very thing Exodus 33:20, already on these pages, said was impossible: no one can see Jehovah’s face and live. Moses does not see God’s face here either — but he stands close enough, long enough, that his OWN face begins to carry a version of what he witnessed, bright enough that Aaron and all Israel are afraid to come near him (v30). The glory Moses is refused a direct look at (33:20, 23) turns out to be catching, at one remove.

Moses veils his face only when speaking with the people, and unveils it again to speak with Jehovah (vv33–35) — the opposite of what modesty would suggest. The veil is not for God’s protection or Moses’ own; it exists entirely for the congregation, who cannot bear to look at what Moses can apparently bear to carry.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. ‘Steadfast love’ chosen for chesed (v6) over KJV’s ‘goodness’ or ASV/NASB’s ‘lovingkindness’ — see the note above for the three-way split. ‘Stiffnecked’ kept as one word for qesheh-oref (v9), the fourth and final use of the term across three chapters, matching this translation’s own earlier choices. ‘Molten gods’ kept literal for the massekhah root (v17), matching Exodus 32’s own choice for the golden calf. ‘Shone’ kept for qaran (v29) rather than the Douay-Rheims’ literal Vulgate-derived ‘horned’ — see the note above and the new dictionary entry for the mistranslation’s long afterlife in art. The chapter’s own paragraph break ({פ} at v26) marks the seam between the covenant’s terms and its writing down; v35 closes with the Torah’s own {ס}. Verse numbering follows the Hebrew exactly; Hebrew and English Exodus 34 both run 1–35.

A note on order: Exodus stands on these pages at chapters 1–35 — the covenant broken at the golden calf is now fully remade: new tablets cut, the divine name proclaimed, the law repeated, and Moses himself marked, visibly, by the encounter. Exodus 35, the people’s own gifts for the tabernacle at last brought forward, is now on these pages too. Exodus 36, the craftsmen at work and the people’s giving finally restrained, remains open, along with the rest of the book.
