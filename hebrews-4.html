The rest of Psalm 95 still open: God's own rest on the seventh day, a 'Today' spoken through David long after Joshua, 'a sabbath rest for the people of God'; the word of God sharper than any two-edged sword; and a great high priest, tested in every respect, who has passed through the heavens.
Before AD 70, most likely c. AD 60-69, continuing the same letter as chapter 1 (see that note).

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · “Let us fear” — the promise left open, and “not united by faith”

“Let us fear, then, lest, while a promise of entering his rest is still left open, any one of you should seem to have fallen short.” The “rest” is the one the psalm closed to the wilderness generation in Hebrews 3:11 (dictionary). “Fallen short” is hystereō, the verb of Romans 3:23, “all sinned and fall short of the glory of God.” “Seem” is dokeō, read as “appear” or as “be judged.” The KJV reads “Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest”; the ASV reads “any one of you should seem to have come short of it”; the NIV reads “since the promise of entering his rest still stands.”

⚠ “The word they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united by faith with those who listened.” “Had the good news proclaimed” is euangelizō: the wilderness generation had good news too, the promise of the land. “The word they heard” is ho logos tēs akoēs, “the word of hearing,” as in 1 Thessalonians 2:13 (dictionary). Then a reading that divides the editions. The printed text, with Westcott–Hort, Tregelles, Nestle–Aland 28 and Robinson–Pierpont, has synkekerasmenous, plural, “they were not united by faith with those who listened”; the apparatus gives the singular, synkekerasmenos, “it was not mixed with faith in those who heard,” to the text the NIV translators followed. The Textus Receptus and the Latin Vulgate have the singular (both checked), and the KJV follows them. Synkerannymi, “to blend, to unite,” stands in two verses: “God has composed the body” at 1 Corinthians 12:24, and here (dictionary). The KJV reads “not being mixed with faith in them that heard it”; the ASV reads “because it was not united by faith with them that heard”; the NWT 1984 reads “because they were not united by faith with those who did hear”; the NIV reads “because they did not share the faith of those who obeyed.”

Verses 3–5 · “We who have believed are entering rest” — the seventh day, and works finished from the founding of the world

“For we who have believed are entering rest, just as he has said, ‘They shall not enter my rest.’” “Are entering” is a present tense, read as the entering now under way or as a sure future spoken as present. The printed text has no article, “rest”; Westcott–Hort, Tregelles, Nestle–Aland 28 and Robinson–Pierpont have “the rest” (the apparatus). The oath of Psalm 95:11 is quoted again, in the form explained at Hebrews 3:11. “The founding of the world” is the phrase of Matthew 25:34, “the kingdom prepared for you from the founding of the world” (dictionary): the rest has been ready since creation. The KJV reads “As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest”; the ASV reads “They shall not enter into my rest”; the Douay reads “and this indeed when the works from the foundation of the world were finished.”

“He has spoken somewhere about the seventh day in this way: ‘And God rested on the seventh day from all his works.’” The quotation is Genesis 2:2, “on the seventh day he rested from all the work he had done,” in the words of the Greek, katepausen (checked); the Hebrew verb is shavat, the root of “sabbath” (dictionary). “Somewhere” is pou, as at Hebrews 2:6. Katapauō, “to rest, to give rest, to stop,” stands in four verses: Acts 14:18, where the crowds are scarcely made to stop sacrificing, and verses 4, 8 and 10 here. The two quotations sit side by side: God’s own rest on the seventh day, and the rest he swore the generation would not enter. The KJV reads “For he spake in a certain place of the seventh day on this wise”; the NIV reads “For somewhere he has spoken about the seventh day in these words”; the NWT 1984 reads “For in one place he has said of the seventh day as follows.”

Verses 6–10 · ⚠ “If Joshua had given them rest” — “Today” through David, and “a sabbath rest”

“Those who formerly had the good news proclaimed to them did not enter because of disobedience.” Apeitheia, “disobedience,” stands in seven verses, among them “the sons of disobedience” at Ephesians 2:2 (dictionary); it is the noun of the verb of Hebrews 3:18, “those who disobeyed.” The KJV reads “entered not in because of unbelief”; the ASV reads “failed to enter in because of disobedience”; the NIV reads “did not go in because of their disobedience.”

“He again sets a certain day, ‘Today,’ saying through David after so long a time.” The Greek says “in David”: in the book of Psalms, which went under David’s name, or through David as speaker. The Hebrew of Psalm 95 has no title, and the Greek Psalter heads it “of David” (both checked). “Sets” is horizō, “to mark out, to appoint,” the verb of Romans 1:4, “appointed Son of God in power,” and of Acts 17:31, “a man whom he appointed.” “After so long a time” is the argument: a “Today” spoken centuries after the wilderness means the offer was still open. The KJV reads “Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David”; the ASV reads “he again defineth a certain day, To-day, saying in David so long a time afterward”; the NIV reads “when a long time later he spoke through David.”

⚠ “For if Joshua had given them rest, he would not have spoken afterward about another day.” The Greek name is Iēsous, the Greek form of Yehoshua, and the same name as Jesus; Stephen’s “with Joshua” at Acts 7:45 has it too (encyclopedia). The verse means Joshua son of Nun: the book of Joshua says “Jehovah gave them rest all around” (Joshua 21:44) and “Jehovah your God has given rest to your brothers” (Joshua 22:4), and the Greek of Joshua 21:44 uses the verb of this verse, katepausen (checked; dictionary). The letter’s point is that the land was a rest, and not the last one. The KJV reads “For if Jesus had given them rest,” the name as the Greek spells it and as the Latin Vulgate has it (checked); so does the Geneva: “For if Jesus had given them rest”; the ASV reads “For if Joshua had given them rest.”

⚠ “So then, a sabbath rest remains for the people of God.” Sabbatismos, “a sabbath-keeping, a sabbath rest,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary); in the next verse the rest is God’s own, the seventh day of verse 4. Readers take the rest as entered now by faith, as the rest of the age to come, or as both; and they divide over what it says about keeping the weekly Sabbath. This page does not choose. The KJV reads “There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God”; the ASV reads “There remaineth therefore a sabbath rest for the people of God”; the Douay reads “a day of rest for the people of God.”

“For the one who has entered his rest has himself also rested from his works, as God did from his own.” “The one who has entered” is read as any believer who enters, or as Jesus, who has entered first. The KJV reads “hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his”; the NWT 1984 reads “has also himself rested from his own works, just as God did from his own”; the TLB reads “Christ has already entered there.”

Verses 11–13 · “Sharper than any two-edged sword” — soul and spirit, and “laid bare”

“Let us therefore do our best to enter that rest … by the same example of disobedience.” “Do our best” is spoudazō, the verb of 2 Timothy 4:9, “Do your best to come to me soon.” “Example” is hypodeigma, the word of John 13:15, “I have given you an example,” used here of a pattern to avoid (dictionary). The KJV reads “Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest”; the ASV reads “Let us therefore give diligence to enter into that rest”; the NWT 1984 reads “Let us therefore do our utmost to enter into that rest.”

⚠ “The word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, piercing as far as the division of soul and of spirit, of joints and of marrow.” “The word of God” is the word just quoted, “Today, if you hear his voice”; it is “living,” like “the living God” of Hebrews 3:12. “Two-edged” is distomos, which stands in three verses, here and the sword from the mouth of the one like a son of man at Revelation 1:16 and 2:12; “the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God” is Ephesians 6:17 (dictionary). “Division” is merismos, the “distributions” of Hebrews 2:4 (dictionary). Readers take “soul and spirit, joints and marrow” as a figure for the whole person, reached to the inmost part, or, with 1 Thessalonians 5:23, as a line that distinguishes spirit from soul; this page does not choose. The KJV reads “For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword”; the ASV reads “For the word of God is living, and active”; the NIV reads “it penetrates even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow.”

“Able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart.” Kritikos, “able to judge,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). Enthymēsis, “thought,” stands in four verses, among them Matthew 9:4, where Jesus knows “their thoughts.” The KJV reads “and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart”; the ASV reads “and quick to discern the thoughts and intents of the heart”; the NIV reads “it judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart.”

“All things are naked and laid bare to the eyes of him to whom we must give our account.” Trachēlizō, “to lay bare,” stands in one verse; it is built on trachēlos, “neck,” and the picture is read as a wrestler’s hold on the throat or as the neck of a victim bent back for the knife (both reported here, not checked) (dictionary). “To whom we must give our account” is, in the Greek, “to whom for us the logos”: the word of God in verse 12, and here the word we owe him. The KJV reads “with whom we have to do”; the NWT 1984 reads “with whom we have an accounting”; the Douay, following the Latin, reads “to whom our speech is.”

Verses 14–16 · “A great high priest” — “tested in every respect,” “without sin,” and the throne of grace

“Having, then, a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast to the confession.” The high priest of Hebrews 2:17 and Hebrews 3:1 is now “great,” and he has “passed through the heavens,” as the high priest of Leviticus went “inside the veil” once a year (Leviticus 16:2, 16:34). “The confession” is the homologia of 3:1 (dictionary). The KJV reads “that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession”; the ASV reads “who hath passed through the heavens”; the NIV reads “who has ascended into heaven.”

⚠ “One who has been tested in every respect in the same way, without sin.” Sympatheō, “to sympathize, to suffer with,” stands in two verses, here and Hebrews 10:34 (dictionary). “Tested” is peirazō, as at Hebrews 2:18; this translation prints “tested” with the sense of temptation in the dictionary (dictionary). “Without sin” is read as “yet he did not sin,” or as “tested in everything except what comes from sin”; this page does not choose. The Byzantine text has a different perfect, pepeiramenon (the apparatus). The KJV reads “but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin”; the NWT 1984 reads “one who has been tested in all respects like ourselves, but without sin”; the NIV reads “yet he did not sin.”

“Let us, then, draw near with boldness to the throne of grace, so that we may receive mercy and find grace for timely help.” “Draw near” is proserchomai, the verb of Hebrews 11:6, “whoever comes to God must believe that he exists”; it returns at 7:25 and 10:22. “Boldness” is the parrēsia of Hebrews 3:6. Boētheia, “help,” stands in two verses: the “supports” that undergird the ship at Acts 27:17, and here; it is the noun of the verb of Hebrews 2:18, “he is able to help” (dictionary). “Timely” is eukairos, the “opportune day” of Mark 6:21. The KJV reads “Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace”; the Douay reads “and find grace in seasonable aid”; the NWT 1984 reads “for help at the right time.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “rest,” as in chapter 3; “fallen short,” as at Romans 3:23; “the word they heard,” as at 1 Thessalonians 2:13; “united by faith,” the printed plural, with the singular in the note; “the founding of the world,” as at Matthew 25:34; “rested,” as at Genesis 2:2; “disobedience,” the noun of 3:18; “through David,” with “in David” in the note; “Joshua,” with Iēsous in the note; “sabbath rest” for sabbatismos; “do our best,” as at 2 Timothy 4:9; “living and active”; “division,” as at 2:4; “laid bare”; “account” for the second logos; “tested,” as at 2:18; “boldness,” as at 3:6; and “timely help.”

Echoes paid. The rest and the oath of Hebrews 3:11; fall short from Romans 3:23; the word you heard of 1 Thessalonians 2:13; the founding of the world from Matthew 25:34; God rested at Genesis 2:2; disobeyed from Hebrews 3:18; Jehovah gave them rest at Joshua 21:44; Joshua as Iēsous at Acts 7:45; the example of John 13:15; the living God of Hebrews 3:12; the sword of the Spirit of Ephesians 6:17; division from Hebrews 2:4; spirit and soul from 1 Thessalonians 5:23; the high priest of Hebrews 3:1; tested and help from Hebrews 2:18; and boldness from Hebrews 3:6.

Echoes planted. “Draw near” at 7:25 and the high priest “in the likeness of Melchizedek” at 7:15; “sympathize” at 10:34 and “draw near” again at 10:22.

Next in this book: Hebrews 5, already on these pages. Every high priest “taken from among human beings” and able to “deal gently with the ignorant and those who go astray”; Christ, who did not glorify himself, but was told “You are my Son” and “a priest forever, according to the order of Melchizedek”; “he learned obedience from what he suffered”; and readers still needing milk, not solid food.
