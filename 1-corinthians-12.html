Acerca de los dones espirituales, hermanos, no quiero que ignoren.

Pero todas estas cosas las obra el único y mismo Espíritu, repartiendo a cada uno individualmente como quiere.

Porque en un solo Espíritu todos nosotros fuimos bautizados en un solo cuerpo, sean judíos o griegos, sean esclavos o libres, y todos bebimos de un solo Espíritu.

Pues el cuerpo no es un solo miembro, sino muchos.

And if the ear should say, “Because I am not an eye, I am not part of the body,” it is not for this reason any less part of the body.note
Y si la oreja dijera: «Porque no soy ojo, no soy parte del cuerpo», no por eso deja de ser parte del cuerpo.
17
εἰ ὅλον τὸ σῶμα ὀφθαλμός, ποῦ ἡ ἀκοή; εἰ ὅλον ἀκοή, ποῦ ἡ ὄσφρησις;

Si todo el cuerpo fuera ojo, ¿dónde estaría el oído? Si todo fuera oído, ¿dónde estaría el olfato?

But now God has placed the members, each one of them, in the body just as he chose.note
Pero ahora Dios colocó los miembros, cada uno de ellos, en el cuerpo, como quiso.
19
εἰ δὲ ἦν τὰ πάντα ἓν μέλος, ποῦ τὸ σῶμα;
If they were all one member, where would the body be?note
Si todos fueran un solo miembro, ¿dónde estaría el cuerpo?
20
νῦν δὲ πολλὰ μὲν μέλη, ἓν δὲ σῶμα.
But now there are many members, yet one body.note
Pero ahora son muchos miembros, y un solo cuerpo.
21
οὐ δύναται δὲ ὁ ὀφθαλμὸς εἰπεῖν τῇ χειρί· Χρείαν σου οὐκ ἔχω, ἢ πάλιν ἡ κεφαλὴ τοῖς ποσίν· Χρείαν ὑμῶν οὐκ ἔχω·
The eye cannot say to the hand, “I have no need of you,” or again the head to the feet, “I have no need of you.”note
El ojo no puede decirle a la mano: «No te necesito»; ni la cabeza a los pies: «No los necesito».
22
ἀλλὰ πολλῷ μᾶλλον τὰ δοκοῦντα μέλη τοῦ σώματος ἀσθενέστερα ὑπάρχειν ἀναγκαῖά ἐστιν,
On the contrary, the members of the body that seem to be weaker are necessary.note
Al contrario, los miembros del cuerpo que parecen más débiles son necesarios;

And the parts of the body that we think less honorable, these we clothe with greater honor, and our unpresentable parts have greater modesty;note
Y a los que consideramos menos honorables del cuerpo, a estos les damos más honor; y nuestras partes menos decorosas tienen más decoro;
24

whereas our presentable parts have no need of it. But God has composed the body, giving greater honor to the part that lacked it,note
Mientras que nuestras partes decorosas no lo necesitan. Pero Dios compuso el cuerpo, dando más honor a la parte que carecía;

Para que no haya división en el cuerpo, sino que los miembros tengan el mismo cuidado unos por otros.

Y si un miembro sufre, todos los miembros sufren con él; y si un miembro es honrado, todos los miembros se alegran con él.
27

Ustedes son cuerpo de Cristo y miembros en particular.

And God has placed in the congregation first apostles, second prophets, third teachers; then miracles, then gifts of healing, helps, governments, and kinds of tongues.note
Y Dios puso en la congregación a unos: primero apóstoles, segundo profetas, tercero maestros; luego milagros, luego dones de sanidad, ayudas, administraciones, diversidad de lenguas.

31

But desire earnestly the greater gifts. And I will show you a still more excellent way.note
Pero deseen con celo los dones mayores. Y aún les muestro un camino más excelente.
Verses 1–3 · the spiritual things, the idols that led away, and the test of the confession

“Concerning the spiritual gifts.” Peri de tōn pneumatikōn, now about the spiritual things. The noun is plural and neuter, and it can mean gifts or the spiritual matters in general; the sentence that follows speaks of gifts, and the translation says “gifts” in verse 4. The formula I do not want you to be ignorant (ou thelō hymas agnoein) is the one of 10:1. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now concerning the spiritual gifts, brothers, I do not want you to be uninformed,” and the KJV “Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, I would not have you ignorant.”

“Jesus is cursed.” Anathema Iēsous. The verse is a test of speech: the one who speaks by God’s spirit does not say that Jesus is accursed, and saying Jesus is Lord comes only by the holy spirit. The word anathema is the word for what is devoted to destruction. The shelf: the ASV “no man speaking in the Spirit of God saith, Jesus is anathema,” and the KJV “no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed.”

Verses 4–6 · the same Spirit, the same Lord, the same God, and three kinds of variety

“Varieties of gifts.” diairesis charismatōn, a division or variety of gifts. The three nouns are the gift (charisma), the service (diakonia), and the working (energēma). Each is set beside a divine name: the same Spirit, the same Lord, the same God. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now there are different gifts, but there is the same spirit; and there are different ministries, and yet there is the same Lord; and there are different activities, and yet it is the same God who performs them all in everyone.”

“The same God who works all things in all.” ho energōn ta panta en pasin. The participle is from energeō, to work effectively. The shelf: the ASV “the same God, who worketh all things in all,” and the KJV “it is the same God which worketh all in all.”

Verses 7–11 · the manifestation for the common good, and the one Spirit who distributes

“The manifestation of the Spirit.” hē phanerōsis tou pneumatos, the making visible of the Spirit. The noun is the word for showing something plainly. The gift is the Spirit made visible in a person, and its purpose is to sympheron, what brings people together for the good; the same verb as 6:12, where the Corinthians’ slogan is answered by what is useful. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But the manifestation of the spirit is given to each one for a beneficial purpose.” The KJV “to profit withal” keeps the purpose of the gift, and the translation takes the purpose as the common good.

The nine gifts. The sentence lists a word of wisdom, a word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healing, workings of miracles, prophecy, the distinguishing of spirits, kinds of tongues, and the interpretation of tongues. The words “word of wisdom” and “word of knowledge” use the same nouns as 1:5, where the Corinthians were enriched in all speech and all knowledge. The shelf: the KJV “To one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit,” and the ASV “to another the word of knowledge, according to the same Spirit.”

“One and the same Spirit.” to hen kai to auto pneuma, the one and the same Spirit, distributing to each one as he wills. The verb diaireō is to divide or distribute, the same root as the varieties of verses 4–6. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “But all these operations the one and the same spirit performs, making a distribution to each one respectively just as it wills.”

Verses 12–13 · one body with many members, and one Spirit to drink

“The body is one and has many members.” to sōma hen estin kai mele polla echei. The word melos, member, is the word for a limb or part of the body, and Paul uses it for each person in the congregation. The sentence says that the many members are one body, and the sentence ends with so also is Christ. The shelf: the ASV “For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of the body, being many, are one body; so also is Christ.” The KJV “so also is Christ” has the same sense.

“Baptized into one body.” eis hen sōma ebaptisthēmen, we were baptized into one body. The word for drinking is potizō, to make to drink, and the sentence says that we were all made to drink one Spirit. The sentence says that the one body is entered through the one Spirit. The shelf: the ASV “For in one Spirit were we all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, whether bond or free; and were all made to drink of one Spirit.” The TNM 2019 “todos nosotros —judíos o griegos, esclavos o libres— fuimos bautizados por un solo espíritu para formar un solo cuerpo” keeps the same order.

Verses 14–16 · the body is not one member, and the foot and ear that say they are not part of it

“The body is not one member but many.” ou estin hen melos alla polla. The sentence is an argument from the body, the body of the individual made into the congregation. The foot and the ear each say that they are not part of the body; the sentence says that the saying does not make it so. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “If the foot should say: “Because I am not a hand, I am no part of the body,”” and the KJV “If the foot shall say, Because I am not the hand, I am not of the body”.

Verses 17–20 · a whole body of one sense, and God setting the members

“Where would the hearing be?” pou hē akoē, where the hearing. The question is the one the body asks of a single sense that takes the whole. The verb is ethento, God placed the members. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “If the whole body were an eye, where would the [sense of] hearing be?” and the KJV “If the whole body were an eye, where were the hearing?”

“Many members, yet one body.” nyn de polla men melē, hen de sōma. The sentence keeps the many and the one in the same clause. The shelf: the ASV “But now they are many members, but one body.”

Verses 21–24 · the eye and the hand, the weaker members, and the honor given to the lacking part

“I have no need of you.” chreian sou ouk echō, I have no need of you. The noun chreia is need. The eye cannot say this to the hand, and the head cannot say it to the feet. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “The eye cannot say to the hand: “I have no need of you”; or, again, the head [cannot say] to the feet: “I have no need of YOU.””

“Weaker, less honorable, unpresentable.” The weaker members are anagkaia, necessary, and the less honorable get timē perissoteran, more abundant honor. The word asthenestera is the word of the weak in 8:7. The shelf: the KJV “those members of the body, which seem to be more feeble, are necessary” and “upon these we bestow more abundant honour.”

“God composed the body.” ho theos synekerasen to sōma, God mixed the body together. The verb synkerannymi is the verb of compounding a mixture. The shelf: the KJV “but God hath tempered the body together,” and the ASV “but God tempered the body together.”

Verses 25–26 · no division, and the members that suffer and rejoice together

“No division in the body.” hina mē ē schisma en tō sōmati, that there should be no division in the body. The word schisma is the word of 1:10 and 11:18. The members have the same care (merimnōsi) for one another, the word of care in 7:32. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “so that there should be no division in the body, but that its members should have the same care for one another.” The ASV “that there should be no schism in the body” keeps the Greek noun.

“If one member suffers.” eite paschei hen melos synpaschei panta ta melē. The verb sympaschō is to suffer with, and synchairei, to rejoice with. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “If one member suffers, all the other members suffer with it; or if a member is glorified, all the other members rejoice with it.”

Verses 27–31 · you are the body of Christ, the order of those God set in the congregation, and the greater gifts

“You are the body of Christ.” Hymeis este sōma Christou kai melē ek merous, you are the body of Christ and members from a part. The sentence applies the body of verse 12 to the Corinthians directly. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “Now YOU are Christ’s body, and members individually.”

“In the congregation.” en tē ekklēsia, in the congregation. The word is ekklēsia, rendered “congregation” in every chapter of this translation. The order of the list is apostles, prophets, teachers, then the powers and the gifts. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “And God has set the respective ones in the congregation, first, apostles; second, prophets; third, teachers,” and the ASV “And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly teachers.” The ASV prints “church” for the same noun; the translation does not.

The questions. Verses 29 and 30 ask seven questions, each expecting no. The Greek has mē in each, and the answer the grammar invites is no in every case, so the questions say that the gifts are not all the same. The shelf: the KJV “Are all apostles? are all prophets? are all teachers? are all workers of miracles?” and the TNM 2019 “¿Verdad que no todos son apóstoles ni todos son profetas ni todos son maestros ni todos hacen milagros?”

“The greater gifts.” zēloute ta charismata ta meizona, desire the greater gifts. The verb zēloō is to desire earnestly, and verse 31 turns to a more excellent way, which is the subject of chapter 13 (13:1). The shelf: the KJV “But covet earnestly the best gifts: and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way.” The NWT 1984 “keep zealously seeking the greater gifts” keeps the present tense of the verb.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “congregation” for ekklēsia at 12:28, the fixed rendering of every NT chapter; “member” for melos at 12:12–27; “division” for schisma at 12:25, the word of 1:10 and 11:18; “gift” for charisma at 12:4–31, the word of 1:7; “working” for energēma at 12:6 and 12:10; “service” for diakonia at 12:5.

Echoes paid. The one body of 10:17, now the body of Christ with its many members (12:12–13, 12:27); the same care for one another of 11:33, now the members who care for each other (12:25); the gifts named in 1:7, now the gifts of the Spirit and their list; the knowledge of 1:5 and 8:1, now the word of knowledge (12:8); the weak of 8:7, now the weaker members that are necessary (12:22); and the division of 1:10, now the body without division (12:25).

Echoes planted. The more excellent way (12:31) returns in chapter 13, where love is that way. The greater gifts return in chapter 14, where prophecy is set over tongues. Speaking with the Spirit (12:3) returns in chapter 14 as the speech that builds up the congregation.

Next in this book: 1 Corinthians 13, already on these pages. Paul leaves the list of gifts for the way more excellent than any of them: love.
