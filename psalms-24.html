A psalm headed “of David”: the earth and all in it belong to Jehovah, founded on seas and rivers; who may go up his mountain (clean hands, a pure heart, the soul not lifted to what is false); the generation that seeks his face; and the gates and doors of long ago told to lift up their heads for the King of glory, Jehovah strong and mighty, Jehovah of hosts.
Undated. The heading names David and gives no occasion, and the psalm names no event; later readers have set it at the going up of the ark (2 Samuel 6), which is a reading and not something the psalm says. ⚠ This page sets it among the monarchy by the name of David and says no more.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Psalm 24:1–2 to Jehovah the earth — founded upon seas, established upon rivers

⭐ To Jehovah the earth. “To Jehovah the earth and its fullness” stands in this one verse, with “to Jehovah” put first. The heading, ledavid mizmor, “of David, a psalm” (mizmor), is part of verse 1 in the Hebrew and is not numbered apart. “The world and those who dwell in it” stands in two verses, here and Psalm 98:7; the world is tevel. Paul quotes the verse at 1 Corinthians 10:26 (now on these pages), and the claim is made in other words at Exodus 9:29, Exodus 19:5 and Deuteronomy 10:14 (all now on these pages). The KJV has “The earth is the Lord’s, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein”, the NIV “The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it, the world, and all who live in it”, the NWT 1984 “To Jehovah belong the earth and that which fills it, The productive land and those dwelling in it”, the TLB “The earth belongs to God! Everything in all the world is his!” and the Douay “The earth is the Lord’s and the fulness thereof: the world, and all they that dwell therein”.

⭐ Upon seas and upon rivers. “Upon seas he founded it” stands in this one verse, and “and upon rivers he establishes it” stands in this one verse, the verb being kun, to set firm; the waters of Genesis 1:9 (now on these pages) are the ground. The KJV has “he hath founded it upon the seas, and established it upon the floods”, the NIV “he founded it on the seas and established it on the waters”, the NWT 1984 “upon the seas he himself has solidly fixed it, And upon the rivers he keeps it firmly established” and the Douay “hath prepared it upon the rivers”. ⚠ The TLB has “He is the one who pushed the oceans back to let dry land appear”, which is a paraphrase of the verse and not a translation of it.

Verses 3–6 · Psalm 24:3–6 who may go up the mountain — clean hands and a pure heart — “my soul” — the generation that seeks, “Jacob”

⭐ Who may go up? “Who may go up the mountain of Jehovah” stands in this one verse, and “and who may stand in his holy place” stands in this one verse. It is the question put at a gate, and the gate of Jehovah of Psalm 118:19–20 and the gates of Isaiah 26:2 (both now on these pages) ask who may enter. The KJV has “Who shall ascend into the hill of the Lord? or who shall stand in his holy place?”, the NIV “Who may ascend the mountain of the Lord? Who may stand in his holy place?”, the NWT 1984 “Who may ascend into the mountain of Jehovah, And who may rise up in his holy place?” and the TLB “Who may climb the mountain of the Lord and enter where he lives?”.

⭐ Clean hands, a pure heart. “Clean of hands” (naqi kappayim) stands in this one verse, and “and pure of heart” stands in this one verse. The pair is taken up in the Greek of Matthew 5:8, “the pure in heart”, and at James 4:8, “cleanse your hands… purify your hearts” (both now on these pages). The KJV has “He that hath clean hands, and a pure heart”, the NIV “The one who has clean hands and a pure heart”, the NWT 1984 “Anyone innocent in his hands and clean in heart”, the Douay “The innocent in hands, and clean of heart”, the Geneva “Even he that hath innocent hands, and a pure heart” and the TLB “Only those with pure hands and hearts”.

⚠ “My soul.” “Who has not lifted up my soul to what is false” stands in this one verse: the printed Hebrew we translate from has nafshi, “my soul”, and most versions read “his soul”. The NWT 1984 keeps it, “Who has not carried My soul to sheer worthlessness”; the KJV has “who hath not lifted up his soul unto vanity”, the Geneva “which hath not lifted up his mind unto vanity” and the Douay “who hath not taken his soul in vain”, which follows the Latin. The NIV has “who does not trust in an idol or swear by a false god” and the TLB “who do not practice dishonesty and lying”, both of which interpret it. This translation keeps “my soul” because it is what is written, and says so. “And has not sworn to deceive” stands in this one verse (mirmah); the KJV has “nor sworn deceitfully” and the NWT 1984 “Nor taken an oath deceitfully”.

⭐ Blessing and righteousness. “He will carry away blessing from Jehovah” stands in this one verse, and the righteousness comes “from the God of his salvation”, which stands in this one verse (berakhah, yasha). The KJV has “He shall receive the blessing from the Lord, and righteousness from the God of his salvation”, the NIV “They will receive blessing from the Lord and vindication from God their Savior”, the NWT 1984 “He will carry away blessing from Jehovah And righteousness from his God of salvation”, the Douay “and mercy from God his Saviour” and the TLB “God’s own goodness as their blessing from him”.

⭐ The generation, and “Jacob”. “The generation of those who seek him” stands in this one verse (darash), and “those who seek your face” stands in this one verse (panim). ⚠ The Hebrew then ends with “Jacob” and nothing more, and the versions mend it three ways. The KJV has “that seek thy face, O Jacob”, the Geneva “of them that seek thy face, this is Jacob”, the NIV “who seek your face, God of Jacob”, the Douay “of them that seek the face of the God of Jacob”, the TLB “worship the God of Jacob” and the NWT 1984 “O [God of] Jacob”, with the words in brackets. This translation keeps the bare “Jacob”, either the people addressed or the God of Jacob left unsaid. The first Selah of the psalm stands here.

Verses 7–10 · Psalm 24:7–10 lift up your heads, gates — who is this King of glory? — Jehovah of hosts

⭐ Lift up your heads, gates. “Lift up your heads, gates” stands in two verses, verses 7 and 9, and “doors of long ago” stands in two verses, the same two, the doors (gates, petach) being those of olam, long-lasting time. The psalm names no occasion; later readers have heard in it the ark going up (2 Samuel 6:12–15, now on these pages), and that is a reading and not something the verses say. The KJV has “Lift up your heads, O ye gates; and be ye lift up, ye everlasting doors”, the NIV “Lift up your heads, you gates; be lifted up, you ancient doors”, the NWT 1984 “And raise yourselves up, O YOU long-lasting entrances” and the TLB “Open up, O ancient gates, and let the King of Glory in”. ⚠ The Douay has “Lift up your gates, O ye princes”, which reads the Hebrew for “your heads” as “your rulers”.

⭐ The King of glory. “The King of glory” stands in four verses, and all four are in this psalm, verses 7, 8, 9 and 10 (melekh, kavod); the Hebrew puts the article on the glory, “the King of the glory”, which the NWT 1984 renders “the glorious King”. “Who is this King of glory” stands in this one verse, and the answer is “strong and mighty”, which stands in this one verse (izuz), “Jehovah, mighty in battle”, which stands in this one verse. The KJV has “The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle”, the TLB “The Lord, strong and mighty, invincible in battle” and the NWT 1984 “Jehovah strong and mighty, Jehovah mighty in battle”.

⭐ Jehovah of hosts. The second asking has a different Hebrew, mi hu zeh, “who is he, this”, and the answer is Jehovah of hosts: “he is the King of glory” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “The Lord of hosts, he is the King of glory”, the NIV “he is the King of glory”, the NWT 1984 “Jehovah of armies” and the TLB “The Commander of all of heaven’s armies!”. The second Selah closes the psalm.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the world” for tevel; “establishes” for kun; “clean of hands” for naqi kappayim and “pure of heart” for bar levav; “my soul” for nafshi at verse 4, as written; “blessing” for berakhah and “righteousness” for tsedaqah; “doors of long ago” for pithei olam; “the King of glory” for melekh hakavod in all four verses; “Jehovah of hosts” for YHWH tsevaot; “Jehovah” for the Name, written in verses 1, 3, 5, 8 and 10.

Echoes paid. 1 Corinthians 10:26; Exodus 9:29 and 19:5; Deuteronomy 10:14; Genesis 1:9; Psalm 118:19–20; Isaiah 26:2; Matthew 5:8 and James 4:8; 2 Samuel 6:12–15.

Echoes planted. Psalm 98:7 (the other “the world and those who dwell in it”). Not translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Psalms stands on these pages at chapters 1, 8, 19, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 32, 34, 37, 40, 42, 46, 51, 56, 63, 90, 91, 100, 103, 118, 119, 121, 130, 138, 139 and 150, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Psalm 2.
