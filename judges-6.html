Midian raids Israel for seven years. An angel sits under a terebinth at Ophrah while Gideon threshes wheat in a winepress; Gideon asks for signs, and the altar named Jehovah-shalom is built. He tears down his father's altar to Baal by night, and is named Jerubbaal.
Undated within the chapter. It follows directly on from Judges 5, so it takes the same undated window the library gives the judges period.

1
וַיַּעֲשׂוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל הָרַע, בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה; וַיִּתְּנֵם יְהוָה בְּיַד-מִדְיָן, שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים.
The sons of Israel did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, and Jehovah gave them into the hand of Midian for seven years.note
Los hijos de Israel hicieron lo malo ante los ojos de Jehová, y Jehová los entregó en manos de Madián por siete años.
2
וַתָּעָז יַד-מִדְיָן, עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל; מִפְּנֵי מִדְיָן עָשׂוּ לָהֶם בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶת-הַמִּנְהָרוֹת אֲשֶׁר בֶּהָרִים, וְאֶת-הַמְּעָרוֹת, וְאֶת-הַמְּצָדוֹת.
The hand of Midian was strong against Israel; because of Midian the sons of Israel made for themselves the dens that are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds.note
La mano de Madián se fortaleció contra Israel; a causa de Madián, los hijos de Israel se hicieron guaridas en los montes, cuevas y fortalezas.
3
וְהָיָה, אִם-זָרַע יִשְׂרָאֵל--וְעָלָה מִדְיָן וַעֲמָלֵק וּבְנֵי-קֶדֶם, וְעָלוּ עָלָיו.
And so it was: whenever Israel had sown, Midian and Amalek and the sons of the east came up against them.note
Y así era: cuando Israel sembraba, subían Madián, Amalec y los hijos del oriente; subían contra ellos.
4
וַיַּחֲנוּ עֲלֵיהֶם, וַיַּשְׁחִיתוּ אֶת-יְבוּל הָאָרֶץ, עַד-בּוֹאֲךָ, עַזָּה; וְלֹא-יַשְׁאִירוּ מִחְיָה בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל, וְשֶׂה וָשׁוֹר וַחֲמוֹר.
They camped against them and destroyed the produce of the land as far as Gaza, and left no food in Israel, no sheep, ox or donkey.note
Acampaban contra ellos y destruían los frutos de la tierra hasta llegar a Gaza, y no dejaban sustento en Israel: ni oveja, ni buey, ni asno.
5
כִּי הֵם וּמִקְנֵיהֶם יַעֲלוּ וְאָהֳלֵיהֶם, יבאו וּבָאוּ כְדֵי-אַרְבֶּה לָרֹב, וְלָהֶם וְלִגְמַלֵּיהֶם, אֵין מִסְפָּר; וַיָּבֹאוּ בָאָרֶץ, לְשַׁחֲתָהּ.
For they came up with their livestock and their tents, and they came in like locusts for number; they and their camels could not be counted, and they came into the land to destroy it.note
Porque ellos subían con sus ganados y sus tiendas; venían como langostas en multitud, y sus camellos no tenían número; entraban en la tierra para destruirla.
6
וַיִּדַּל יִשְׂרָאֵל מְאֹד, מִפְּנֵי מִדְיָן; וַיִּזְעֲקוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל, אֶל-יְהוָה.
Israel was brought very low because of Midian, and the sons of Israel cried to Jehovah.note
Israel quedó muy empobrecido a causa de Madián, y los hijos de Israel clamaron a Jehová.
7
וַיְהִי, כִּי-זָעֲקוּ בְנֵי-יִשְׂרָאֵל אֶל-יְהוָה, עַל, אֹדוֹת מִדְיָן.
When the sons of Israel cried to Jehovah because of Midian,note
Cuando los hijos de Israel clamaron a Jehová a causa de Madián,
8
וַיִּשְׁלַח יְהוָה אִישׁ נָבִיא, אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם כֹּה-אָמַר יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אָנֹכִי הֶעֱלֵיתִי אֶתְכֶם מִמִּצְרַיִם, וָאֹצִיא אֶתְכֶם, מִבֵּית עֲבָדִים.
Jehovah sent them a prophet, a man, and he said to them: "Thus says Jehovah, the God of Israel: I brought you up from Egypt and brought you out of the house of slavery.note
Jehová envió a los hijos de Israel un profeta, un hombre; y les dijo: «Así dice Jehová, el Dios de Israel: Yo los hice subir de Egipto y los saqué de la casa de servidumbre.
9
וָאַצִּל אֶתְכֶם מִיַּד מִצְרַיִם, וּמִיַּד כָּל-לֹחֲצֵיכֶם; וָאֲגָרֵשׁ אוֹתָם מִפְּנֵיכֶם, וָאֶתְּנָה לָכֶם אֶת-אַרְצָם.
I delivered you out of the hand of Egypt and out of the hand of all who oppressed you; I drove them out before you and gave you their land.note
Los libré de la mano de Egipto y de la mano de todos los que los oprimían; los eché de delante de ustedes y les di su tierra.
10
וָאֹמְרָה לָכֶם, אֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם--לֹא תִירְאוּ אֶת-אֱלֹהֵי הָאֱמֹרִי, אֲשֶׁר אַתֶּם יוֹשְׁבִים בְּאַרְצָם; וְלֹא שְׁמַעְתֶּם, בְּקוֹלִי.
And I said to you: I am Jehovah your God; you shall not fear the gods of the Amorites in whose land you dwell; but you have not listened to my voice."note
Y les dije: "Yo soy Jehová su Dios; no temerán a los dioses de los amorreos, en cuya tierra habitan; pero no han escuchado mi voz."»
11
וַיָּבֹא מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה, וַיֵּשֶׁב תַּחַת הָאֵלָה אֲשֶׁר בְּעָפְרָה, אֲשֶׁר לְיוֹאָשׁ, אֲבִי הָעֶזְרִי; וְגִדְעוֹן בְּנוֹ, חֹבֵט חִטִּים בַּגַּת, לְהָנִיס, מִפְּנֵי מִדְיָן.
The angel of Jehovah came and sat under the terebinth that was in Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite; and his son Gideon was beating out wheat in the winepress, to hide it from the Midianites.note
Vino el ángel de Jehová y se sentó bajo el terebinto que estaba en Ofra, el que era de Joás el abiezerita; y su hijo Gedeón batía trigo en el lagar, para esconderlo de los madianitas.
12
וַיֵּרָא אֵלָיו, מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה; וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, יְהוָה עִמְּךָ גִּבּוֹר הֶחָיִל.
The angel of Jehovah appeared to him and said to him: "Jehovah is with you, mighty man of valour."note
Se le apareció el ángel de Jehová y le dijo: «Jehová está contigo, hombre valiente y fuerte.»
13
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו גִּדְעוֹן, בִּי אֲדֹנִי, וְיֵשׁ יְהוָה עִמָּנוּ, וְלָמָּה מְצָאַתְנוּ כָּל-זֹאת; וְאַיֵּה כָל-נִפְלְאֹתָיו אֲשֶׁר סִפְּרוּ-לָנוּ אֲבוֹתֵינוּ לֵאמֹר, הֲלֹא מִמִּצְרַיִם הֶעֱלָנוּ יְהוָה, וְעַתָּה נְטָשָׁנוּ יְהוָה, וַיִּתְּנֵנוּ בְּכַף-מִדְיָן.
Gideon said to him: "Please, my lord, if Jehovah is with us, why has all this come upon us? And where are all his wonders that our fathers told us about, saying, 'Did not Jehovah bring us up from Egypt?' But now Jehovah has forsaken us and given us into the hand of Midian."note
Gedeón le respondió: «Ah, señor mío, si Jehová está con nosotros, ¿por qué nos ha sucedido todo esto? ¿Y dónde están todas sus maravillas que nos contaron nuestros padres, diciendo: "¿No nos sacó Jehová de Egipto?"? Pero ahora Jehová nos ha desamparado y nos ha entregado en manos de Madián.»
14
וַיִּפֶן אֵלָיו, יְהוָה, וַיֹּאמֶר לֵךְ בְּכֹחֲךָ זֶה, וְהוֹשַׁעְתָּ אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל מִכַּף מִדְיָן: הֲלֹא, שְׁלַחְתִּיךָ.
Jehovah turned to him and said: "Go in this strength of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Have I not sent you?"note
Jehová se volvió hacia él y le dijo: «Ve con esta tu fuerza y salvarás a Israel de la mano de Madián. ¿No te he enviado yo?»
15
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו בִּי אֲדֹנָי, בַּמָּה אוֹשִׁיעַ אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל; הִנֵּה אַלְפִּי הַדַּל בִּמְנַשֶּׁה, וְאָנֹכִי הַצָּעִיר בְּבֵית אָבִי.
He said to him: "Please, my lord, how shall I save Israel? Look, my clan is the poorest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."note
Él le respondió: «Ah, señor mío, ¿con qué salvaré yo a Israel? Mira, mi clan es el menos importante de Manasés, y yo soy el más pequeño de la casa de mi padre.»
16
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו יְהוָה, כִּי אֶהְיֶה עִמָּךְ; וְהִכִּיתָ אֶת-מִדְיָן, כְּאִישׁ אֶחָד.
Jehovah said to him: "Surely I will be with you, and you will strike down Midian as one man."note
Y Jehová le dijo: «Ciertamente yo estaré contigo; y derrotarás a Madián como a un solo hombre.»
17
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, אִם-נָא מָצָאתִי חֵן בְּעֵינֶיךָ; וְעָשִׂיתָ לִּי אוֹת, שָׁאַתָּה מְדַבֵּר עִמִּי.
He said to him: "If now I have found favour in your eyes, then give me a sign that it is you who speak with me.note
Él le dijo: «Si ahora he hallado gracia ante tus ojos, dame una señal de que eres tú quien habla conmigo.
18
אַל-נָא תָמֻשׁ מִזֶּה, עַד-בֹּאִי אֵלֶיךָ, וְהֹצֵאתִי אֶת-מִנְחָתִי, וְהִנַּחְתִּי לְפָנֶיךָ; וַיֹּאמַר, אָנֹכִי אֵשֵׁב עַד שׁוּבֶךָ.
Please do not leave from here until I come to you and bring out my offering and set it before you." He said: "I will wait until you return."note
No te vayas de aquí hasta que yo vuelva a ti, y saque mi ofrenda y la ponga delante de ti.» Y él dijo: «Yo esperaré hasta que regreses.»
19
וְגִדְעוֹן בָּא, וַיַּעַשׂ גְּדִי-עִזִּים וְאֵיפַת-קֶמַח מַצּוֹת, הַבָּשָׂר שָׂם בַּסַּל, וְהַמָּרַק שָׂם בַּפָּרוּר; וַיּוֹצֵא אֵלָיו אֶל-תַּחַת הָאֵלָה, וַיַּגַּשׁ.
Gideon went and prepared a young goat and an ephah of flour as unleavened cakes; he put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, and brought them out to him under the terebinth and presented them.note
Gedeón fue, preparó un cabrito y una efa de harina en panes sin levadura; puso la carne en un canasto y el caldo en una olla, y se los sacó bajo el terebinto y se los presentó.
20
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו מַלְאַךְ הָאֱלֹהִים, קַח אֶת-הַבָּשָׂר וְאֶת-הַמַּצּוֹת וְהַנַּח אֶל-הַסֶּלַע הַלָּז, וְאֶת-הַמָּרַק, שְׁפוֹךְ; וַיַּעַשׂ, כֵּן.
The angel of God said to him: "Take the meat and the unleavened cakes, set them on this rock, and pour out the broth." And he did so.note
Y el ángel de Dios le dijo: «Toma la carne y los panes sin levadura, ponlos sobre esta peña, y derrama el caldo.» Y él lo hizo así.
21
וַיִּשְׁלַח מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה, אֶת-קְצֵה הַמִּשְׁעֶנֶת אֲשֶׁר בְּיָדוֹ, וַיִּגַּע בַּבָּשָׂר, וּבַמַּצּוֹת; וַתַּעַל הָאֵשׁ מִן-הַצּוּר, וַתֹּאכַל אֶת-הַבָּשָׂר וְאֶת-הַמַּצּוֹת, וּמַלְאַךְ יְהוָה, הָלַךְ מֵעֵינָיו.
The angel of Jehovah put out the tip of the staff that was in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened cakes; fire rose from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened cakes, and the angel of Jehovah was gone from his sight.note
Entonces el ángel de Jehová extendió la punta del bastón que tenía en la mano, tocó la carne y los panes sin levadura; subió fuego de la peña, que consumió la carne y los panes, y el ángel de Jehová desapareció de su vista.
22
וַיַּרְא גִּדְעוֹן, כִּי-מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה הוּא; וַיֹּאמֶר גִּדְעוֹן, אֲהָהּ אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה--כִּי-עַל-כֵּן רָאִיתִי מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה, פָּנִים אֶל-פָּנִים.
Gideon saw that he was the angel of Jehovah, and Gideon said: "Alas, Lord Jehovah! For I have seen the angel of Jehovah face to face."note
Gedeón vio que era el ángel de Jehová, y dijo: «¡Ay, Señor Jehová! Porque he visto al ángel de Jehová cara a cara.»
23
וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ יְהוָה שָׁלוֹם לְךָ, אַל-תִּירָא: לֹא, תָּמוּת.
Jehovah said to him: "Peace to you; do not be afraid; you shall not die."note
Jehová le dijo: «La paz sea contigo; no temas, no morirás.»
24
וַיִּבֶן שָׁם גִּדְעוֹן מִזְבֵּחַ לַיהוָה, וַיִּקְרָא-לוֹ יְהוָה שָׁלוֹם; עַד, הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה--עוֹדֶנּוּ, בְּעָפְרַת אֲבִי הָעֶזְרִי.
Gideon built an altar there to Jehovah and called it Jehovah-shalom; to this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.note
Gedeón edificó allí un altar a Jehová y lo llamó Jehová-salom; hasta hoy está todavía en Ofra de los abiezeritas.
25
וַיְהִי, בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא, וַיֹּאמֶר לוֹ יְהוָה קַח אֶת-פַּר-הַשּׁוֹר אֲשֶׁר לְאָבִיךָ, וּפַר הַשֵּׁנִי שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים; וְהָרַסְתָּ, אֶת-מִזְבַּח הַבַּעַל אֲשֶׁר לְאָבִיךָ, וְאֶת-הָאֲשֵׁרָה אֲשֶׁר-עָלָיו, תִּכְרֹת.
That same night Jehovah said to him: "Take your father's bull, the second bull of seven years, and tear down your father's altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah that is beside it.note
Aquella misma noche Jehová le dijo: «Toma el novillo de tu padre, el segundo novillo de siete años, y derriba el altar de Baal que tu padre tiene, y corta la Asera que está junto a él.
26
וּבָנִיתָ מִזְבֵּחַ לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ, עַל רֹאשׁ הַמָּעוֹז הַזֶּה--בַּמַּעֲרָכָה; וְלָקַחְתָּ, אֶת-הַפָּר הַשֵּׁנִי, וְהַעֲלִיתָ עוֹלָה, בַּעֲצֵי הָאֲשֵׁרָה אֲשֶׁר תִּכְרֹת.
Build an altar to Jehovah your God on the top of this stronghold, in the proper order; take the second bull and offer a burnt offering with the wood of the Asherah you will cut down."note
Y edifica un altar a Jehová tu Dios en la cumbre de esta fortaleza, en el lugar ordenado; toma el segundo novillo y ofrécelo en holocausto con la leña de la Asera que cortarás.»
27
וַיִּקַּח גִּדְעוֹן עֲשָׂרָה אֲנָשִׁים, מֵעֲבָדָיו, וַיַּעַשׂ, כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר אֵלָיו יְהוָה; וַיְהִי כַּאֲשֶׁר יָרֵא אֶת-בֵּית אָבִיו וְאֶת-אַנְשֵׁי הָעִיר, מֵעֲשׂוֹת יוֹמָם--וַיַּעַשׂ לָיְלָה.
Gideon took ten of his servants and did as Jehovah had said to him. But because he feared his father's household and the men of the city, he could not do it by day, so he did it by night.note
Gedeón tomó diez hombres de sus siervos e hizo como Jehová le había dicho; pero, temiendo a la casa de su padre y a los hombres de la ciudad, no lo hizo de día, sino de noche.
28

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–6 · seven years of Midian, and a country in holes

The cycle has its usual opening, with one change. Verse 1 gives the sin in the familiar words of Judges 3:7 and Judges 4:1 (both now on these pages), but the oppressor is Midian, not a king, and the sentence is seven years. The book's oppressors have so far been named kings. Midian is a people, and the next chapters will show what that means.

Dens, caves and strongholds. Verse 2 says Israel made for themselves the dens, caves and strongholds in the hills, "because of Midian". The Hebrew mitnharot and mitsadot are the words for hiding-places and fortified refuges, and the translation keeps both: the people were living in the holes of the hills.

Locusts and camels. Verse 5 compares the raiders to locusts, a simile for a force that cannot be counted, and adds the camels, which are the detail that makes this an invasion from the desert. The translation keeps the locust simile rather than softening it to "a great number."

Verses 7–10 · a prophet with a speech

The prophet's speech recites the Exodus. Verse 8 introduces "a man, a prophet" sent by Jehovah. The speech recites the Exodus, the promise of the land, and a charge not to fear the Amorites' gods. The translation keeps the speech in quotation marks, as the Hebrew's own speech markers have it.

"You have not listened to my voice." The verdict of verse 10 is the same verdict as Judges 2:2 (now on these pages). Jehovah is making the same complaint a second time, which is why the translation says "my voice" in both places.

Verses 11–18 · the angel under the terebinth, and a man who wants a sign

"The terebinth in Ophrah." Ha-elah is the terebinth or large oak, the tree under which a holy site could stand. The shelf is divided: the ASV reads "the oak", the NWT 1984 "the big tree", and the TNM 2019 "el árbol grande". The translation says "terebinth", the word the Hebrew names, and the note keeps the reading of the others.

Gideon threshes in the winepress. Hobet chittim bagat means he is beating out wheat in the winepress. The verse gives the reason, to hide it from the Midianites, and the translation keeps the hiding.

"Mighty man of valour." The greeting in verse 12 calls the man in the winepress gibbor ha-chayil, a warrior. Gideon's reply in verse 13 is a complaint, and the translation keeps the complaint, because the chapter depends on the gap between the title and the man.

"My clan is the poorest in Manasseh." Verse 15 says his elef is the poorest. The Hebrew word elef is "thousand" and also "clan" or "military unit," and the shelf reads it both ways: the NWT 1984 "My thousand is the least in", and the NWT 2013 "My clan is the least in". The translation says "clan," because the sentence is about a family's standing, not a count.

Verses 15–16 · the least, and the promise

"The least in my father's house." Gideon's self-description is a refusal, and the Hebrew ha-tsa'ir means the youngest or least. The translation keeps "least," which is the nearest English to the same kind of humility, not a claim about age.

"As one man." Jehovah's answer in verse 16 is ke-ish echad, as one man, The translation keeps the phrase as the Hebrew gives it.

Verses 17–18 · the sign that Gideon asks for

"Give me a sign." Ot, a sign, is what Gideon asks for in verse 17, and the translation keeps the request as he makes it: he wants to know that the speaker is Jehovah.

"Do not leave from here." Verse 18 has Gideon ask the visitor to wait while he fetches his offering. The Hebrew al-na tamush, do not depart, is the polite form, and the translation keeps "please" in front of it.

Verses 19–24 · the fire from the rock, and Jehovah-shalom

The offering and the fire. Gideon's offering is a young goat and an ephah of unleavened cakes, and the angel touches it with the tip of the staff so that fire comes out of the rock. Verse 21 says the angel then went out of sight. The translation keeps the sequence exactly, because it is the sign.

"Jehovah-shalom." The Hebrew of verse 24 is YHVH shalom, and the altar is named with the name of Jehovah, not with 'Adonai'. The English edition used in the source prints "Adonai-shalom", which the Hebrew does not say; the translation prints "Jehovah-shalom." The shelf agrees: the ASV has "Jehovah-shalom", and the NWT 1984 "Je·hoʹvah-shaʹlom".

"Peace to you; do not be afraid." Verse 23 is the angel's reassurance, shalom lekha, and the name of the altar comes from it. The translation keeps "peace" in both places, so the reader hears the name come from the word.

Verses 25–27 · the father's altar, and the night work

"Take your father's bull." Verse 25 says the second bull is "of seven years", and the translation keeps "of seven years" as the Hebrew says it, rather than "seven years old" in the modern sense.

"Cut down the Asherah." Verse 25 names the Asherah beside the altar of Baal, and the translation keeps "Asherah" as a name for the cult object (Judges 3:7, now on these pages). The verse says the altar of Baal belongs to Gideon's father.

"Because he feared." Verse 27 gives the reason for the night work: he feared his father's household and the men of the city. The translation says so plainly, and does not turn the fear into faith.

Verses 28–30 · the city wakes, and the father is asked to give up his son

"Who did this thing?" Verses 28 and 29 are the city's search, and the answer is the son of Joash. The translation keeps the inquiry as the Hebrew gives it.

"Bring out your son, that he may die." Verse 30 has the men of the city demand Joash's son. The translation keeps the demand as it stands.

Verses 31–32 · Joash's answer, and the name Jerubbaal

"If he is a god, let him contend for himself." Joash's reply in verse 31 is the book's sharpest irony. The Hebrew yarev means to contend or plead, the verb of a legal case. The translation keeps "contend," so the reader hears the court in it.

"Jerubbaal." Verse 32 gives Gideon a second name, Jerubbaal, from the verb in Joash's speech: "let Baal contend with him." The name is a sentence. The NWT 1984 reads it as a legal defence, "make a legal defense", and the ASV as "Let Baal contend against him", and the translation keeps "Let Baal contend with him."

Verses 33–35 · the valley of Jezreel, and the spirit

"The spirit of Jehovah clothed Gideon." The verb lavshah, clothed, is the one the Hebrew uses for putting on a garment. The translation keeps the image, because the spirit is put on him like a robe.

The valley of Jezreel. The enemy camps in the valley of Jezreel in verse 33, and the call to arms goes out through Manasseh, Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali. The translation keeps the names and the order of the messengers.

Verses 36–40 · the fleece, the dew, and a test asked twice

"Let me test once more." In verse 39 Gideon asks for a second sign, and the verb anasseh, let me test, is the same verb Judges 2:22 uses for Jehovah's test of Israel (now on these pages). The translation keeps "test," so the reader sees that Gideon now tests God.

"A bowlful of water." Verse 38 says Gideon wrung dew out of the fleece, a bowlful of water. The Hebrew yimets is to squeeze, and the translation keeps the squeezing.

The chapter ends with the sign, not the war. Verse 40 says God did so that night. Judges 7 (now on these pages) is where the army is reduced and the battle begins.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: "terebinth" for elah, not "oak"; "clan" for elef in verse 15, where the sentence is about a family's standing; "Jehovah-shalom" for the altar name, kept from the Hebrew's YHVH rather than the source's Adonai; "Let Baal contend with him" for the name Jerubbaal; and "test" for nasah, the same verb Judges 2:22 used of Jehovah's test of Israel.

Echoes paid. The verdict of verse 10 repeats Judges 2:2. The "did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah" of verse 1 is the formula of Judges 3:7 and 4:1. The "test" of Judges 2:22 returns in Gideon's own test in verse 39. The "one man" of verse 16 is the phrase the chapter uses for Gideon's army.

Echoes planted. The fleece and the dew are the sign that Gideon's army will depend on, and the reduction of the army is the next chapter's business. The name Jerubbaal will be the one the book uses for him from here.
