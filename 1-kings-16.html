Jehu son of Hanani gives Baasha the sentence of the house of Jeroboam. Baasha dies at Tirzah; Elah reigns two years and is killed there by Zimri, who destroys the house of Baasha and reigns seven days. The army makes Omri king at Gibbethon, Zimri burns the citadel over himself, and Israel divides between Tibni and Omri until Tibni dies. Omri buys the hill of Samaria. Ahab succeeds him, marries Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal, and builds a house of Baal. Hiel rebuilds Jericho.
From the end of Baasha to the thirty-eighth year of Asa for Ahab (1 Kings 16:29). Omri’s twelve years do not add up from the thirty-first year of Asa (16:23), and the usual proposal counts them from the twenty-seventh.

Y el pueblo que iba tras Omri pudo más que el pueblo que iba tras Tibni hijo de Ginat; y Tibni murió, y Omri reinó.

Y compró el monte Samaria a Semer por dos talentos de plata, y edificó el monte, y llamó el nombre de la ciudad que edificó según el nombre de Semer, señor del monte: Samaria.
25

And Omri did what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, and he did worse than all who were before him.note
Y Omri hizo lo malo a los ojos de Jehová, e hizo peor que todos los que hubo antes de él.

וְאַחְאָב בֶּן-עָמְרִי, מָלַךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, בִּשְׁנַת שְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁמֹנֶה שָׁנָה, לְאָסָא מֶלֶךְ יְהוּדָה; וַיִּמְלֹךְ אַחְאָב בֶּן-עָמְרִי עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, בְּשֹׁמְרוֹן, עֶשְׂרִים וּשְׁתַּיִם, שָׁנָה.
And Ahab son of Omri reigned over Israel in the thirty-eighth year of Asa king of Judah; and Ahab son of Omri reigned over Israel in Samaria twenty-two years.note
Y Acab hijo de Omri reinó sobre Israel en el año treinta y ocho de Asa rey de Judá; y Acab hijo de Omri reinó sobre Israel en Samaria veintidós años.

Y Acab hijo de Omri hizo lo malo a los ojos de Jehová más que todos los que hubo antes de él.

And as if it were a light thing for him to walk in the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, he took as wife Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians, and he went and served Baal and bowed down to him.note
Y como si fuera cosa ligera andar en los pecados de Jeroboam hijo de Nabat, tomó por mujer a Jezabel hija de Et-baal rey de los sidonios, y fue y sirvió a Baal y se inclinó ante él.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–7 · 1 Kings 16:1–7 Jehu son of Hanani and Baasha — “from the dust”, the sentence handed on, and why a king is condemned for what the prophet foretold

The other Jehu. “The word of Jehovah came to Jehu son of Hanani”: this Jehu is a prophet, and not the Jehu who takes the throne in 2 Kings 9. Chronicles has a seer named Hanani rebuking Asa for leaning on the king of Aram (2 Chronicles 16:7) and a Jehu son of Hanani rebuking Jehoshaphat (19:2), and says his words were taken up into the book of the kings of Israel (20:34). The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has the word come “by the hand of” Jehu here, where the Hebrew has “to”.

“From the dust”. This is the sentence on Jeroboam with one phrase changed. Jeroboam heard “I lifted you up from among the people, and made you leader over my people Israel” (1 Kings 14:7, now on these pages). Baasha hears “I lifted you up from the dust”, the same verb, and the word is afar. Hannah’s song has the dust and the same verb in one line: “He raises the poor from the dust; from the ash heap he lifts up the needy” (1 Samuel 2:8, now on these pages). The kingdom here is a thing given, and the giver can remember where he found the man. “Leader” is nagid. The NWT 1984 has “leader”, which this page follows, the ASV and the KJV “prince”, the Geneva “captain”, the NIV “ruler”, and the TLB “to make you king”, the one word the verse avoids. Verse 2 ends on “their sins”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “their vanities”.

“I am burning out after Baasha”. The verb is the one Jeroboam’s house heard, ba’ar, “as one burns out dung” (1 Kings 14:10, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “making a clean sweep after”, the ASV “utterly sweep away”, the KJV “take away the posterity of”, the Douay “cut down the posterity of”, and the NIV “wipe out”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “I raise up”, which is another sense of the same letters. ⚠ The sentence changes person in the middle. It speaks to Baasha about Baasha, then says “your house”. The ASV and the KJV keep “thy house”, and the NIV “your house”. The NWT 1984 smooths it to “his house”, where the Hebrew has beitkha. Verse 4 is the dogs and the birds of 1 Kings 14:11 (now on these pages), with the name changed. As the note there promised, the man who carried out that sentence now has it passed on himself.

⚠ “And because he struck him”. Baasha’s death has already been told, and verse 7 goes back to give the sentence a second time, with two grounds. One is that he was like the house of Jeroboam. The other is ve’al asher-hikkah oto, “and because he struck him”, and “him” has no name. If it means Nadab, or the house of Jeroboam, then Baasha is condemned for the act that 1 Kings 15:29 (now on these pages) calls the word of Jehovah carried out. The ASV has “because he smote him”, the NWT 1984 “because he struck him down”, the KJV and the Geneva “because he killed him”, and the NIV “because he destroyed it”. The Douay, following the Latin, prints “for this cause he slew him, that is to say, Jehu the son of Hanani, the prophet”, which makes the prophet the victim. The Hebrew gives no name here, and Chronicles has a Jehu son of Hanani alive in the reign of Jehoshaphat (2 Chronicles 19:2). The TLB turns the clause into “despite the fact that”, gives the destroying of Jeroboam’s descendants to God, and drops the charge. The same thing happens on a larger scale with Jehu the king. Jehovah praises him for what he did to the house of Ahab (2 Kings 10:30), and Hosea says “I will bring the blood of Jezreel to account on the house of Jehu” (Hosea 1:4, now on these pages). Neither text explains how the two go together, and this verse leaves them side by side.

Verses 8–14 · 1 Kings 16:8–14 Elah and Zimri — “drinking himself drunk”, anyone who urinates against the wall, and “with their vapors”

The twenty-sixth year. Baasha began in the third year of Asa and reigned twenty-four (1 Kings 15:33, now on these pages), so parts of years are counted as years again, and Elah’s two run from the twenty-sixth to the twenty-seventh. Chronicles has Baasha marching on Judah in the thirty-sixth year of Asa (2 Chronicles 16:1), ten years after this verse has buried him. The note at 1 Kings 15:17 (now on these pages) sets out the problem. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has no date in verses 8, 10 or 15.

“Drinking himself drunk”. Two Hebrew participles side by side, shoteh shikkor, “drinking, drunk”. The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and of Ben-hadad in his booths (1 Kings 20:16, now on these pages), and both men are drinking when the blow comes. The king’s army is away at Gibbethon (verse 15), and the king is in a steward’s house in his own capital. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 have “drinking himself drunk”, and the NIV “getting drunk”. The TLB has “half drunk”. The Hebrew has no half there; the half in this verse belongs to the chariots, of which Zimri commands half. Arza “was over the household”, the office Ahishar held under Solomon (1 Kings 4:6, now on these pages). The ASV and the NWT 1984 have “who was over the household”, the NIV “the palace administrator”, and the Douay “the governor of Thersa”.

“Anyone who urinates against the wall”. This is the soldier’s word for a male, mashtin be-qir, kept as it was at 1 Kings 14:10 (now on these pages). The KJV has “one that pisseth against a wall”, the Geneva “one to piss against a wall”, and the NWT 1984 “urinates against a wall”. The ASV has “a single man-child”, and the NIV “a single male”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has none of this and goes straight from the striking to the word of Jehovah. “His redeemers” is go’alav, the kin whose duty it was to answer a killing, “the redeemer of blood” of Numbers 35:19 (now on these pages). Zimri removes the men who would have come after him. The NWT 1984 has “his avengers of blood”, which says the job, and the ASV and the KJV “his kinsfolks”, which does not. “His friend” is singular in the Hebrew. It may be a collective, or the court title of “the king’s friend” in 1 Kings 4:5 (now on these pages), and the verse does not say which. The ASV and the KJV have “his friends”.

“With their vapors”. The word is hevel, the breath of Ecclesiastes. “Their vapors” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 32:21 (now on these pages), here, and verse 26. The word says what they are worth, and it does not say what they are. The ASV and the KJV have “vanities”. The NIV has “worthless idols” and the NWT 1984 “vain idols”, both of which supply a noun the Hebrew leaves out.

Verses 15–20 · 1 Kings 16:15–20 Zimri’s seven days — the shortest reign in Kings, Gibbethon again, the citadel at Tirzah, and “his conspiracy that he conspired”

“Seven days”. This is the shortest reign Kings records. The next is Shallum’s month (2 Kings 15:13). The name is the same as that of the man at Peor (Numbers 25:14, now on these pages), and the text draws no line between them. It will have a future of its own: when Jehu the king rides into Jezreel, Jezebel calls down to him, “Is it peace, Zimri, murderer of his master?” (2 Kings 9:31). A week on the throne, and the name becomes a word for what he did.

Gibbethon again. “The people were camped against Gibbethon, which belonged to the Philistines”: Nadab was killed besieging this town in the third year of Asa (1 Kings 15:27–28, now on these pages), and this is the twenty-seventh. Whether the army ever left, the text does not say, but two kings have now lost their thrones while Israel was busy not taking Gibbethon. “The people” is ha-am, the men under arms. The ASV and the KJV have “the people”, the NIV “the Israelites”, and the TLB “the army of Israel”. “All Israel” then makes a king in one day in a camp. Omri is “the commander of the army”, Joab’s title (1 Kings 1:19, now on these pages). Kings gives him no father and no tribe, and it gives Zimri none either, where Baasha had both (1 Kings 15:27, now on these pages).

“The citadel of the king’s house”. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The word is armon, the fortified keep. The NIV has “the citadel”, the ASV “the castle”, the NWT 1984 “the dwelling tower”, and the KJV and the Geneva “the palace”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has “the cave”. He “burned the king’s house over him”: the ASV and the KJV have “burnt the king’s house over him”, and the Douay “burnt himself with the king’s house”. Omri reigns six years at Tirzah and then builds somewhere else (verses 23–24), and the text does not connect the two facts.

A full verdict for a short reign. “For his sins which he sinned, doing what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, walking in the way of Jeroboam”: Zimri had seven days, spent on a purge and a siege, and Kings gives him the verdict it gave a king of twenty-two years, down to making Israel sin. The formula is a form, and it does not bend for the calendar. “His conspiracy that he conspired” keeps the Hebrew’s repetition, kishro asher qashar. The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and of Shallum (2 Kings 15:15), the two shortest reigns in Kings. It is the one deed the formula names for either man. The NWT 1984 has “his conspiracy with which he conspired”, the ASV, the KJV and the Geneva “his treason that he wrought”, and the NIV “the rebellion he carried out”.

Verses 21–22 · 1 Kings 16:21–22 Tibni and Omri — Israel divided in half, and the four-word end of a civil war

“Divided in half”. The Hebrew says “half” three times in one verse, la-chetsi … chatsi … ve-ha-chatsi, and this page keeps it. The kingdom that split from Judah in 1 Kings 12 (now on these pages) now splits again, and the man who began this round commanded half the chariots. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 have “two parts”, and the NIV “two factions”. Only Tibni has “to make him king” attached to his half, since the other half already had a king. The name stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, these two, and nothing else is known of him or of Ginath.

“And Tibni died, and Omri reigned”. Four Hebrew words. By the dates in verses 15 and 23 this may have taken four years, and the text does not say how it ended. The ASV and the KJV have “so Tibni died”, and the NWT 1984 “Tibni met death”. The TLB has “Tibni was killed”, which the Hebrew does not say. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition adds a brother, “and Joram his brother at that time”, and has Omri reign “after” Tibni. The readings differ.

Verses 23–28 · 1 Kings 16:23–28 Omri buys the hill of Samaria — two talents of silver, Shomron from Shemer, and what Kings leaves out

Twelve years that do not add up. Omri is made king in the twenty-seventh year of Asa (verses 15–16). Here he reigns from the thirty-first, for twelve years, and yet Ahab succeeds him in the thirty-eighth (verse 29). The usual proposal is that the twelve are counted from the camp at Gibbethon, the twenty-seventh through the thirty-eighth, and that the thirty-first marks the end of Tibni. That is a proposal, and the verse does not say so.

⭐ The price of a capital. “He bought the hill Samaria from Shemer for two talents of silver”. The census silver of Exodus gives the size of a talent: 603,550 men at half a shekel is 301,775 shekels, and the text calls that a hundred talents and 1,775 shekels (Exodus 38:25–26, now on these pages), so a talent is 3,000 shekels and Omri paid 6,000. Abraham paid 400 for Machpelah (Genesis 23:16, now on these pages) and David 50 for the threshing floor (2 Samuel 24:24, now on these pages). A kikkar is an ingot, and this is a capital bought outright, owned by a dynasty and by no tribe. The TLB prints “for $4,000”, a sum that is not in the Hebrew. His son will try the same thing with a vineyard and be told no (1 Kings 21:2–3, now on these pages).

Shomron, from Shemer. The owner is Shemer and the city is Shomron, and the verse makes the pun itself: “after the name of Shemer, the lord of the hill: Samaria — Shomron”. English loses the link, and the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition keeps it, Semer and Semeron. The letters are those of shamar, to keep, and Micah will use that verb on this very king: “the statutes of Omri are kept” (Micah 6:16, now on these pages). The verse calls the hill Samaria before it explains the name. 1 Kings 13:32 (now on these pages) has already spoken of “the cities of Samaria”, and this is the verse that note was waiting for. The Hebrew is har, the usual word for a mountain. The ASV, the KJV and the NIV have “the hill”, and the NWT 1984 and the Geneva “the mountain”. “He built the hill” is plainer than any of them, with no “on”. The NWT 1984 prints “build [on] the mountain”, and marks what it supplied. Shemer is “lord of the hill”. The Geneva has “lord of the mountain”, the NWT 1984 “the master of the mountain”, and the ASV and the KJV “owner of the hill”. The city he named stands until Assyria takes it (2 Kings 18:10).

What Kings leaves out. “He did worse than all who were before him”: Jeroboam heard that at 1 Kings 14:9 (now on these pages), Omri earns it here, and Ahab will earn it five verses on. “And in his sin”: the written Hebrew has “his sins” and the reading tradition “his sin” (see ketiv / qere), and the translation follows the reading. The KJV and the NWT 1984 have “his sin”, and the ASV, the Douay and the Geneva “his sins”. For “his might that he showed” Kings sends the reader to the lost book. Others remembered more. The Mesha inscription in the Louvre names “Omri king of Israel” as an oppressor of Moab, and Assyrian records go on calling the kingdom the house of Omri after his line is gone. Those two are reported from published translations, and neither is part of this text. In Kings the founder of the dynasty is a land purchase and a verdict.

Verses 29–33 · 1 Kings 16:29–33 Ahab and Jezebel — “as if it were a light thing”, the house of Baal in Samaria, and the Asherah

Ahab son of Omri. The full name comes three times in two verses. A son is named by his father here because there is now a dynasty to name him by. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition dates him to “the second year of Jehoshaphat” where the Hebrew has the thirty-eighth of Asa, and just before this it inserts a whole account of Jehoshaphat’s reign, which the Hebrew keeps for chapter 22 (1 Kings 22:41, now on these pages). The two chronologies differ. The Assyrian account of the battle of Qarqar lists “2,000 chariots, 10,000 soldiers of Ahab, the Israelite”, as reported in the published translation, and Kings never mentions it.

“As if it were a light thing”. The Hebrew is a question dropped into a statement, ha-naqel, “was it a light thing?” The question stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and at Ezekiel 8:17, of the house of Judah. Jeroboam’s sins were the standing measure of evil for every king of Israel, and for Ahab they are only where he starts. The ASV and the KJV have “as if it had been a light thing”, and the NWT 1984 “[as if it were] the most trivial thing”, with its brackets. The Geneva keeps the question: “For was it a light thing”. The Douay has “Nor was it enough”, the TLB “as though that were not enough”, and the NIV “considered it trivial”.

Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal. She enters in a subordinate clause. Solomon loved Sidonian women and went after Ashtoreth of the Sidonians (1 Kings 11:1, 5, now on these pages), and now the word is back. “King of the Sidonians” is the loose use of the name that the entry on Sidon explains. Josephus, quoting Menander, has a certain Ithobalus, “the priest of Astarte”, kill the king of Tyre and reign thirty-two years (Against Apion 1.18), and he is usually taken for this Ethbaal, though nothing in the verse confirms it. The meaning of her name is uncertain. Revelation will use it as a code name (Revelation 2:20, now on these pages). “He went and served Baal and bowed down to him”: that is the warning of 1 Kings 9:6 (now on these pages), “you go and serve other gods and bow down to them”, carried out word for word. The Hebrew has the article, ha-ba’al, “the Baal”, and all the shelf versions drop it, as this page does at Judges 2:13 (now on these pages).

The house of Baal and the Asherah. “In the house of Baal which he built in Samaria”: the verb is the one used for Solomon’s house, and Israel’s capital now has a temple, for the wrong god. Jehu the king will tear it down (2 Kings 10:27). The ASV, the KJV and the Geneva have “the house of Baal”, and the NIV and the Douay “the temple of Baal”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition does not print the name there, and has “the house of his abominations”. “Ahab made the Asherah” has the article, a known object: see asherah. The ASV has “the Asherah”, the NWT 1984 “the sacred pole”, and the NIV “an Asherah pole”. The KJV and the Geneva have “a grove”, the Douay “planted a grove”, and the TLB “other idols”. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition adds a clause about his own life being destroyed, which is not in the Hebrew.

Verse 34 · 1 Kings 16:34 Hiel rebuilds Jericho — Joshua’s curse paid, and what “at the cost of” does not tell us

⭐ An oath comes due. “At the cost of his firstborn he will lay its foundation, and at the cost of his youngest son he will set up its gates”: that was Joshua at the ruins of Jericho (Joshua 6:26, now on these pages), and this is the same pair of verbs, in the same order, after the long wait that note promised. There is a detail in the closing line. Joshua 6:26 says Joshua swore the oath, and it does not say Jehovah told him to. Kings calls it “the word of Jehovah which he spoke by the hand of Joshua son of Nun”. “Built” is the plain verb. The NIV has “rebuilt”, and the ASV and the KJV “build”. People were already at Jericho in David’s day (2 Samuel 10:5, now on these pages), so what Hiel raises is a foundation and gates, a walled city. He is “the Bethelite”, from the town of the calf (1 Kings 12:29, now on these pages).

⚠ “At the cost of”. The Hebrew is one letter, be-, on each son: “in Abiram”, “in Segub”. This page keeps the rendering of Joshua 6:26, but the phrase carries more than the letter does. The KJV and the Geneva have “in Abiram”, as the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition does. The ASV has “with the loss of”, the NIV “at the cost of”, and the NWT 1984 “At the forfeit of”. The TLB says each “died”. The verse does not say how they died. One reading has the curse falling on him, a son at each stage of the work. Another, proposed in older scholarship, sees a foundation sacrifice, a father giving his sons to the walls. The verse gives neither, and this page does not choose. “Segub” is the reading; the written Hebrew has Segib (see ketiv / qere), and the translation follows the reading.

Three words kept. “According to the word of Jehovah which he spoke by the hand of” has now closed three accounts in a chapter and a half: Ahijah’s (1 Kings 15:29, now on these pages), Jehu’s (verse 12) and Joshua’s, the last one centuries old. The chapter ends in the reign of the worst king so far, on the observation that the word still works. The next sentence in the book has a man nobody has met telling Ahab that there will be no rain “except by my word” (1 Kings 17:1, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “burning out” for ba’ar, as in 1 Kings 14:10; “leader” for nagid; “anyone who urinates against the wall” for the soldier’s word; “his redeemers” for go’alav; “with their vapors” for hevel, as in Deuteronomy 32:21; “the citadel” for armon; “his conspiracy that he conspired”, with the Hebrew’s repetition; “in half” three times over; “built the hill”, with no “on”; “a light thing” for naqel; “the Asherah”, with the article; and “at the cost of”, as in Joshua 6:26. The written and read Hebrew are recorded at verses 26 and 34, and the Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition is named wherever it goes its own way, which in this chapter is often.

Echoes paid. The sentence on Jeroboam’s house passed on to the man who carried it out (1 Kings 14:7–11; 15:29); Gibbethon (1 Kings 15:27); “the cities of Samaria” (1 Kings 13:32); the statutes of Omri (Micah 6:16); the warning to go and serve other gods and bow down to them (1 Kings 9:6); the Sidonians (1 Kings 11:1, 5); the Asherahs (1 Kings 14:15, 23); Joshua’s oath over Jericho (Joshua 6:26).

Echoes planted. Elijah, and “except by my word” (1 Kings 17:1, now on these pages); Ben-hadad drinking himself drunk (1 Kings 20:16, now on these pages); Naboth’s vineyard, which a king could not buy (1 Kings 21:2–3, now on these pages); the dogs and the birds on Ahab’s house (1 Kings 21:24, now on these pages); Jehoshaphat in his own place (1 Kings 22:41, now on these pages); Jehu the king, and “Is it peace, Zimri?” (2 Kings 9:31); the house of Baal torn down (2 Kings 10:27); Shallum’s month (2 Kings 15:13, 15); the fall of Samaria (2 Kings 18:10); Jehu son of Hanani and Jehoshaphat (2 Chronicles 19:2; 20:34). The rest are yet to come in this translation.
