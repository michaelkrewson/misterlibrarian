The same letter, still from Ephesus. Paul answers the questions the Corinthians wrote him about marriage, and the chapter is the longest answer in the letter. Acts 18 has Paul in Corinth before he wrote, and Acts 19 has him in Ephesus, the city the letter is written from.
c. AD 54–55, the same letter and date as chapters 1 to 6, written from Ephesus. Nothing in Acts dates the questions about marriage; the chapter gives no date of its own.

Verses 1–7 · a slogan about touching, and the gift of each one

“Now concerning the things you wrote.” Peri de hōn egrapsate, now concerning what you wrote, is the first of several sections of the letter that answer the Corinthians’ own questions. Nothing else of their letter survives. Kalon anthrōpō gynaikos mē haptesthai, it is good for a man not to touch a woman: the sentence may be the Corinthians’ own position, which Paul quotes, or Paul’s starting point before his qualification in verse 2. The Greek does not mark a quotation, and the translation does not mark one. The shelf: the KJV “It is good for a man not to touch a woman,” the ASV the same, and the NWT 1984 “it is well for a man not to touch a woman.”

“Because of the prevalence of sexual immorality.” Dia de tas porneias, because of the immoralities, is plural, and the sentence gives the reason for marriage: each has his own wife or husband. Porneia is the general word of 6:18. The KJV has “to avoid fornication,” a purpose clause where the Greek gives a cause, and the NWT 2013 keeps the cause: “because of the prevalence of sexual immorality.”

“Due,” and “authority over the body.” Tēn opheilēn, the due, is what one owes, and in verse 4 exousiazei, has authority, is the verb of exousia, power over. The sentence gives each spouse a claim on the other’s body. It is the reciprocal form of the argument of 6:13 and 6:20, where the body belongs to the Lord and to God. The NWT 1984 has “The wife does not exercise authority over her own body, but her husband does,” and the KJV “hath not power of her own body.” The translation keeps “authority,” the word of the Greek.

“Except by mutual consent.” Ek symphōnou pros kairon, by mutual agreement for a set time, is the one condition on the withholding of marital relations, and its purpose is prayer. The KJV has “with consent for a time, that ye may give yourselves to fasting and prayer,” with “fasting” added. The word is not in the Greek of the critical text this translation reads, and the note records it. The reason for the limit is the danger that Satanas will tempt them through akrasia, the lack of self-control (akrasia). The ASV has “Defraud ye not one the other, except it be by consent for a season, that ye may give yourselves unto prayer,” and the NWT 2013 “Do not be depriving each other except by mutual consent for an appointed time.”

“As a concession, not as a command.” Kata syngnōmēn, by concession, is set against kat’ epitagēn, by command (epitagē). Paul makes a distinction between what he allows and what he orders, and the next verses use the same distinction (verse 10). The NWT 1984 has “by way of concession, not in the way of a command,” and the KJV “by permission, and not of commandment.”

“Each has his own gift from God.” Hekastos idion echei charisma ek theou, each has his own gift from God, where charisma is the gift of charisma, the word of 1:7. Paul wishes all people were as he is, and then says the gift differs: one in this way, another in that. Commentators divide over whether the gift is the state of being unmarried or the capacity for it. The note records the question and does not settle it.

In verse 1 the RV60 reads “it is good for a man not to touch a woman”, the RV 1909 “it is good for a man not to touch a woman”, and the NVI “It is better not to have sexual relations.” In verse 2 the RV60 reads “because of fornications” and the NVI “given how much immorality there is.” In verse 3 the RV60 reads “let the husband render to the wife the marital duty”, the RV 1909 “let the husband pay the wife the due benevolence”, and the NVI “fulfil his marital duty.” In verse 4 the RV60 reads “has no authority over his own body”, and the NVI “no longer has rights over his own body.” In verse 5 the RV60 reads “do not deny one another”, the RV 1909 “do not defraud one another”, and the NVI “Do not deprive each other”, and then “because of lack of self-control.” In verse 6 the RV60 reads “by way of concession, not of command”, and the NVI “as a concession, not as a command.” In verse 7 the RV 1909 reads “I would that all men were as I myself am”, and the NVI “I wish that all were as I am.”

Verses 8–9 · the unmarried, the widows, and the burning

“To the unmarried and the widows.” Tois agamois kai tais chērais, to the unmarried and to the widows, is a group of people without spouses. The advice is that they remain as Paul is, and the translation keeps the comparison with Paul. The KJV has “I say therefore to the unmarried and widows, It is good for them if they abide even as I,” and the NWT 1984 “to the unmarried persons and the widows, it is well for them that they remain even as I am.”

“It is better to marry than to burn.” Kreitton gar estin gamēsai ē pyroūsthai, it is better to marry than to be inflamed. Pyroūsthai is the verb for burning with fire, and the metaphor is passion. The translation says “to burn with passion,” because the Greek word makes the passion the fire. The TNM 1987 has “estar encendidos [de pasión],” in brackets for the words it adds, and the NWT 2013 “to be inflamed with passion.”

In verse 8 the RV60 reads “it were good for them if they remained as I am”, and the NVI “it would be better for them to stay unmarried, as I am.” In verse 9 the RV60 reads “to burn”, the RV 1909 “that it is better to marry than to burn”, and the NVI “to burn with passion.”

Verses 10–16 · the Lord’s command, Paul’s word, and the holy children

“Not I but the Lord.” Ouk egō alla ho kyrios, not I but the Lord, is the first of two statements that separate a command from a word of Paul. For the married, the command is that a wife should not separate from her husband. The saying is the Lord’s own, and the gospels record it: Mark 10:11–12 and the teaching on Deuteronomy 24:1 in Mark 10:4. Matthew has an exception clause, Matthew 5:32 and 19:9, which Paul does not repeat here. The shelf: the KJV “yet not I, but the Lord,” the NWT 1984 “not I but the Lord.”

“Let her remain unmarried or be reconciled.” Katallagētō, be reconciled, is the verb of restored peace. If the wife has separated, she has two options in the sentence. The husband is not to put her away. The TNM 1987 keeps the two options as they are: “que permanezca sin casarse, o, si no, que se reconcilie con su esposo.”

“I, and not the Lord.” Tois de loipois legō egō, ouch ho kyrios, to the rest I say, I, not the Lord. Here Paul speaks of cases the Lord did not address: a believer married to an unbelieving spouse. The translation keeps the difference between the two authorities, and it does not say whether Paul claims the authority of revelation in the second case. The NWT 2013 has “But to the others I say, yes, I, not the Lord,” and the TNM 1987 “sí, yo, no el Señor.”

“Sanctified.” Hēgiastai, is made holy, is the verb of hagios, holy. The sentence says the unbelieving spouse is made holy in the believing one, and the children are holy because of it. The preposition is en, in, and the sentence does not say whether the holiness is a status or a relation. The KJV has “sanctified by the wife,” and the NWT 1984 “sanctified in relation to [his] wife.”

“God has called us to peace.” If the unbelieving partner leaves, ou dedoulōtai, the believer is not enslaved, and God has called to eirēnē, peace. The KJV has “a brother or a sister is not under bondage in such cases,” and the ASV “is not under bondage in such cases.” The verse does not say whether a believer may remarry. The note records that the question is open in the text and that the commentaries differ.

“How do you know whether you will save.” Ti gar oidas, gynai, ei ton andra sōseis, for what do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband. The verb sōzō, save, is the verb of 1:18, and here it is the salvation of a spouse. The question is open: the believer cannot know the outcome. The KJV “For what knowest thou, O wife, whether thou shalt save thy husband?” and the NWT 2013 “how do you know whether you will save your husband?”

In verse 10 the RV60 reads “not I, but the Lord: let not the wife depart”; the RV 1909 “I command, yet not I but the Lord”; the NVI “I give this command (not I, but the Lord).” In verse 11 the RV60 reads “let her remain unmarried” and the NVI “she must not marry again.” In verse 12 the RV60 reads “a woman who has an unbelieving husband”, the RV 1909 “an unfaithful wife”, and the NVI “(not the Lord).” In verse 14 the RV60 reads “the unbelieving husband is sanctified by the wife”, and the NVI “has been made holy through marriage to a believing wife.” In verse 15 the RV60 reads “a brother or sister is not under bondage”, the NVI “the believing spouse is not bound”, and “let them not hinder.” In verse 16 the RV60 reads “for what do you know, O wife, whether you shall save your husband”, and the NVI “How do you know, wife, whether you will save your husband?”

Verses 17–24 · remaining in the calling: circumcision, slavery, and bought with a price

“As Jehovah has apportioned to each.” Ei mē hekastō hōs emerisen ho kyrios, only, as the Lord has apportioned to each. The sentence has two parallel clauses, the Lord has apportioned, and God has called, so the Lord is the one who distributes, and God is the one who calls. The referent of kyrios is the God of Israel here, not Jesus: the sentence has God in the second clause and the same work in the first, so the translation reads “Jehovah,” as the rule for these uses requires. The NWT 1984 has “as Jehovah has given each one a portion,” and the TNM 1987 “según Jehová haya dado a cada uno una porción”; the ASV keeps “the Lord,” and the KJV reads “as God hath distributed.”

“Was anyone called circumcised?” Peritetmēmenos tis eklēthē, the circumcised person called, and en akrobystia, in uncircumcision. Paul says the state of the call does not change: the circumcised should not undo it, and the uncircumcised should not get circumcised. The verses say that tērēsis entolōn theou, keeping God’s commandments, is what counts. The shelf: the KJV “Was any man called being circumcised? let him not become uncircumcised,” and the NWT 2013 “Was any man already circumcised when he was called? Let him not undo his circumcision.”

“Were you called as a slave?” Doulos eklēthēs, were you called a slave. Paul tells the slave not to be troubled. Then the sentence turns on a word with two readings: alla ei kai dynasai eleutheros genesthai, mallon chrēsai, but if you can become free, rather make use of it, or rather take the chance. The Greek does not say what it is, the slavery or the freedom, and scholars divide. The shelf: the NWT 1984 “rather seize the opportunity,” the KJV “use it rather.” The translation keeps “make use of it,” which is the wider of the two readings.

“The Lord’s freedman.” Apeleutheros kyriou, the Lord’s freedman, is the paradox of verse 22: the slave who was called in the Lord is free in the Lord, and the free who was called is a slave of Christ. The word for freedman is the word a slave receives on manumission. The KJV has “is the Lord’s freeman,” the ASV “is the Lord’s freedman.”

“You were bought with a price.” Timēs ēgorasthēte, you were bought with a price, the same words as 6:20 (agorazō). The purchase is the reason for the instruction: do not become slaves of men. The translation keeps the echo. The KJV “Ye are bought with a price; be not ye the servants of men,” and the TNM 2019 “Se pagó un precio por ustedes; dejen de hacerse esclavos de los hombres.”

In verse 17 the RV60 and the RV 1909 read “as the Lord has distributed to each”, and the NVI “according to the condition the Lord has assigned to him.” In verse 18 the RV60 reads “was anyone called circumcised? let him remain circumcised”, and the NVI “let him not undo his circumcision.” In verse 20 the RV60 reads “in the state in which he was called”, and the NVI “in the condition he was in when God called him.” In verse 21 the RV60 reads “but if you may be made free, use it rather”, and the NVI “make use of your freedom.” In verse 23 the RV60 reads “do not become slaves of men”, and the NVI “do not become slaves of anyone.”

Verses 25–28 · virgins, an opinion, and the present distress

“I have no command from the Lord.” Epitagēn kyriou ouk echō, gnōmēn de didōmi, I have no command from the Lord, but I give an opinion. Gnōmē is the word for a judgment or opinion (gnōmē), and it is set against the command of the Lord. The opinion is given hōs ēleēmenos hypo kyriou pistos einai, as one who has been shown mercy by the Lord to be trustworthy. The KJV “I have no commandment of the Lord: yet I give my judgment, as one that hath obtained mercy of the Lord to be faithful,” and the NWT 1984 “I have no command from the Lord, but I give my opinion.”

“Because of the present distress.” Dia tēn enestōsan anagkēn, because of the present necessity. The phrase is anankē, necessity or distress, and the verse calls it present. The sentence does not say what the distress is, and the commentaries offer several readings. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “in view of the present difficulties,” the KJV “for the present distress.”

“Bound to a wife.” Dedesai gynaikí? mē zētei lysin, are you bound to a wife? Do not seek release. The question is about the state a man is in; the pattern of the verse is mē zētei, do not seek, both for the married and the loosed. The verb deō, bind, is the verb of the marriage bond in verse 39. The TNM 2019 “¿Estás atado a una esposa? No intentes quedar libre.”

“You would spare you.” Thlipsin de tē sarki hexousin, egō de hymōn pheidomai, such will have distress in the flesh, but I am sparing you. The word pheidomai, spare, is Paul’s own: he wants to spare them the distress. The ASV has “I would spare you,” and the KJV “but I spare you.” The note records the distress as Paul names it and does not say what it is.

In verse 25 the RV60 reads “but I give my judgment”, and the NVI “I give my opinion.” In verse 26 the RV60 reads “because of the present distress”, and the NVI “because of the present crisis.” In verse 27 the RV60 reads “do not seek to be loosed”, and the NVI “do not seek divorce.” In verse 28 the RV60 reads “and I would spare you this”, and the NVI “I want to spare you this.”

Verses 29–35 · the time shortened, the form of the world, and the divided heart

“The time is shortened.” Ho kairos synestalmenos estin, the time is shortened, or reduced, is a word about compressing the span of time. To loipon, from now on, is the word that opens the list of how to live in the present. The NWT 1984 has “the time left is reduced,” the KJV “the time is short,” and the ASV “the time is shortened.”

“As though they had none.” Hōs mē echontes, as not having, is the form of a list of five contrasts: wives, weeping, rejoicing, buying, using the world. Hōs mē, as not, is the pattern of each. The list is about holding things without being held by them, and the verb katachraomai, use fully, in the last item is the word for using up. The TNM 1987 has “sean como si no tuvieran,” and the TNM 2019 “sean como si no las tuvieran.”

“The form of this world is passing away.” Paragei gar to schēma tou kosmou toutou, for the form of this world is passing away. Schēma is the outward form or scene, the way a thing appears on a stage (schēma). The NWT 2013 has “the scene of this world is changing,” the KJV “the fashion of this world passeth away.” The translation keeps “form,” because the Greek word is the form, not the fashion.

“Free from anxiety.” Amerimnous einai, to be without anxiety, is the word for a mind not divided (amerimnos). Paul then sets the unmarried man, anxious about the Lord’s things, against the married man, anxious about the world’s things and about pleasing his wife, and says he is memeristai, divided. The KJV has “careful for the things that belong to the Lord,” and the NWT 1984 “anxious for the things of the Lord.” The translation keeps “anxious,” the word of the Greek merimnaō, and records that the shelf moves between “careful” and “anxious.”

“Holy in body and in spirit.” The unmarried woman and the virgin are anxious for the things of the Lord, hina ē hagia kai tō sōmati kai tō pneumati, so that she may be holy in body and spirit. The verse matches the body of 6:13 and 6:19, where the body is the Lord’s temple. The sentence has two groups in one: hē agamos kai hē parthenos, the unmarried woman and the virgin, and the text does not say whether they are one or two. The KJV has “that she may be holy both in body and in spirit,” and the TNM 1987 “para ser santa tanto en su cuerpo como en su espíritu.”

“Not a noose.” Ouch hina brochon hymin epiballō, not to put a noose on you. Paul says he is not restricting them. The word eupharedron, attendance, describes a servant who sits beside the master. The sentence is a disclaimer, and it is put as a benefit: pros to hymōn autōn symphoron, for your own advantage. The TNM 1987 “no para echarles un lazo” and the KJV “not that I may cast a snare upon you.”

In verse 29 the RV60 reads “let those who have wives be as though they had none”, and the NVI “those who have wives should live as if they do not.” In verse 31 the RV60 reads “and those who use this world as not abusing it”, the RV 1909 “the fashion of this world passes away”, and the NVI “for this world in its present form is passing away.” In verse 32 the NVI reads “free from anxieties” and the RV60 “without care.” In verse 34 the RV60 reads “that she may be holy both in body and in spirit”, and the NVI “is concerned about the things of the Lord, and how she may be devoted to him in both body and spirit.” In verse 35 the RV60 reads “not to cast a snare upon you”, and the NVI “not to put any restraint on you.”

Verses 36–40 · the virgin, the one who stands firm, and the widow

“His virgin.” Tēn parthenon autou, his virgin, is the phrase of verses 36 to 38, and the Greek does not say who his is or who the virgin is. Readings split three ways: a father and his daughter who is past the age of marriage; a man and the woman he is betrothed to; and a man and a woman who have chosen to live together without marriage. The chapter has no sentence that settles it, and this translation states the phrase and leaves the reading open. The ASV has “his virgin daughter” in verse 36 and 37, and the KJV “his virgin,” without the word for daughter.

“He does not sin. Let them marry.” Ouch hamartanei; gameitōsan, he does not sin; let them marry. The sentence says marriage is permitted, and the decision is the man’s. Hyperakmos, past the bloom, describes an age. The NWT 1984 “he does not sin; let them marry,” and the TNM 2019 “no peca. Que se casen.”

“Stands firm, under no necessity.” Hestēken en tē kardia autou hedraios, stands firm in his heart, is the picture of a settled mind. The verse makes three conditions: no necessity, authority over his will, and a decision in his heart. The KJV “that standeth stedfast in his heart, having no necessity, but hath power over his own will,” and the NWT 2013 “stands settled in his heart.”

“Bound as long as her husband lives.” Gynē dedetai, a wife is bound, is the verb of the marriage bond, repeated from verse 27. The verse says that when the husband dies she is free to marry, monon en kyriō, only in the Lord. The phrase in the Lord is the one used of the believing marriage in verse 39 and of the freedman in verse 22. The KJV “a wife is bound by the law as long as her husband liveth,” and the TNM 2019 “La esposa está atada a su esposo mientras él esté vivo.” The verb for the husband’s death is koimēthē, fell asleep, a common word for death in the Greek of the period.

“I think I also have God’s spirit.” Dokō de kagō pneuma theou echein, I think I also have God’s spirit. Paul closes with a claim about his own authority, and the sentence is put as an opinion, dokō, I think. The sentence has the same modesty as verse 25. The KJV “and I think also that I have the Spirit of God,” and the NWT 1984 “and I certainly think I also have God’s spirit.” The translation keeps “I think,” because the verb is an opinion in the Greek.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Here the shelf divides the reading. The RV60 and the RV 1909 read “his virgin daughter” in verses 36 and 37 (“to keep his virgin daughter”), the reading of father and daughter. The NVI reads “betrothed” in all three verses (“he is not treating his betrothed properly”, “has decided not to marry his betrothed”, “marries his betrothed”); it is the reading of a man and his fiancée. In verse 39 the RV60 reads “she is free to be married to whom she wishes”, and the NVI “she is free to marry.” In verse 40 the RV60 reads “in my judgment she is happier if she so remains”, and the NVI “in my judgment she is happier that way.”

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “due” for opheilē at 7:3; “authority” for exousiazō at 7:4, the word of exousia; “concession” and “command” for syngnōmē and epitagē at 7:6 and 7:25; “form” for schēma at 7:31; “bought with a price” for ēgorasthēte at 7:23; “calling” for the family of kaleō from 7:17 to 7:24.

Echoes paid. The body for the Lord of 6:13 and the temple of 6:19, now turned into the spouse’s authority over the body (7:4); the price of 6:20, now the reason for not becoming slaves of men (7:23); the Satan of 5:5, who tempts the unrestrained (7:5); the gift of 1:7, now a gift from God that differs by person (7:7); and the anxious question of 4:3 about human judgment, now a question of pleasing a spouse (7:33).

Echoes planted. The things of the Lord and the things of the world divide the chapter, and chapter 8 will take the question of food with the things offered to idols. The form of this world is passing away (7:31), a sentence chapter 15 takes up as the change of the body. Paul’s own word as against the command of the Lord (7:10, 7:12, 7:25) returns in chapter 11, where Paul says he received the Lord’s supper from the Lord.

Next in this book: 1 Corinthians 8, already on these pages. Paul turns from marriage to meat that has been offered to idols, and to the saying that knowledge puffs up and love builds up.
