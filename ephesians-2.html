Ephesians 2 — The Mister Translation

1
Καὶ ὑμᾶς ὄντας νεκροὺς τοῖς παραπτώμασιν καὶ ταῖς ἁμαρτίαις ὑμῶν
And you — dead in your trespasses and your sins,note
Y a vosotros —muertos en vuestros delitos y vuestros pecados,
2
ἐν αἷς ποτε περιεπατήσατε κατὰ τὸν αἰῶνα τοῦ κόσμου τούτου, κατὰ τὸν ἄρχοντα τῆς ἐξουσίας τοῦ ἀέρος, τοῦ πνεύματος τοῦ νῦν ἐνεργοῦντος ἐν τοῖς υἱοῖς τῆς ἀπειθείας·
in which you once walked, according to the age of this world, according to the ruler of the authority of the air, the spirit now at work in the sons of disobedience —note
en los cuales anduvisteis en otro tiempo, según el curso de este mundo, según el príncipe de la potestad del aire, el espíritu que ahora obra en los hijos de la desobediencia—
3
ἐν οἷς καὶ ἡμεῖς πάντες ἀνεστράφημέν ποτε ἐν ταῖς ἐπιθυμίαις τῆς σαρκὸς ἡμῶν, ποιοῦντες τὰ θελήματα τῆς σαρκὸς καὶ τῶν διανοιῶν, καὶ ἤμεθα τέκνα φύσει ὀργῆς ὡς καὶ οἱ λοιποί·
among whom we too all once conducted ourselves, in the desires of our flesh, doing the will of the flesh and of the thoughts, and we were by nature children of wrath, as also the rest.note
entre los cuales también todos nosotros vivimos en otro tiempo en los deseos de nuestra carne, haciendo la voluntad de la carne y de los pensamientos, y éramos por naturaleza hijos de ira, lo mismo que los demás.
4
ὁ δὲ θεὸς πλούσιος ὢν ἐν ἐλέει, διὰ τὴν πολλὴν ἀγάπην αὐτοῦ ἣν ἠγάπησεν ἡμᾶς,
But God, being rich in mercy, because of his great love with which he loved us,note
Pero Dios, que es rico en misericordia, por su gran amor con que nos amó,
5
καὶ ὄντας ἡμᾶς νεκροὺς τοῖς παραπτώμασιν συνεζωοποίησεν τῷ Χριστῷ— χάριτί ἐστε σεσῳσμένοι—
and we being dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ — by grace you have been saved —note
aun estando nosotros muertos en pecados, nos dio vida juntamente con Cristo —por gracia sois salvos—
cross-refs⤷ 1:7
6
καὶ συνήγειρεν καὶ συνεκάθισεν ἐν τοῖς ἐπουρανίοις ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ,
and raised us up together, and seated us together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus,note
y juntamente con él nos resucitó, y asimismo nos hizo sentar en los lugares celestiales con Cristo Jesús,
7
ἵνα ἐνδείξηται ἐν τοῖς αἰῶσιν τοῖς ἐπερχομένοις τὸ ὑπερβάλλον πλοῦτος τῆς χάριτος αὐτοῦ ἐν χρηστότητι ἐφʼ ἡμᾶς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ.
so that he might display, in the ages to come, the surpassing riches of his grace, in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus.note
para mostrar en los siglos venideros las abundantes riquezas de su gracia, en su bondad para con nosotros en Cristo Jesús.
8
τῇ γὰρ χάριτί ἐστε σεσῳσμένοι διὰ πίστεως· καὶ τοῦτο οὐκ ἐξ ὑμῶν, θεοῦ τὸ δῶρον·
For by grace you have been saved, through faith, and this not of yourselves — it is the gift of God —note
Porque por gracia sois salvos, por medio de la fe, y esto no de vosotros: es don de Dios;
9
οὐκ ἐξ ἔργων, ἵνα μή τις καυχήσηται.
not of works, so that no one should boast.note
no por obras, para que nadie se gloríe.
10
αὐτοῦ γάρ ἐσμεν ποίημα, κτισθέντες ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ ἐπὶ ἔργοις ἀγαθοῖς οἷς προητοίμασεν ὁ θεὸς ἵνα ἐν αὐτοῖς περιπατήσωμεν.
For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, so that we should walk in them.note
Porque somos hechura suya, creados en Cristo Jesús para buenas obras, las cuales Dios preparó de antemano para que anduviésemos en ellas.
cross-refs⤷ Rom 1:20
11
Διὸ μνημονεύετε ὅτι ποτὲ ὑμεῖς τὰ ἔθνη ἐν σαρκί, οἱ λεγόμενοι ἀκροβυστία ὑπὸ τῆς λεγομένης περιτομῆς ἐν σαρκὶ χειροποιήτου,
Therefore remember that you were once the nations in the flesh — called “uncircumcision” by the so-called “circumcision” made in the flesh by hands —note
Por tanto, acordaos de que en otro tiempo vosotros, los gentiles en la carne, llamados «incircuncisión» por los que se llaman «circuncisión» en la carne, hecha por manos,
12
ὅτι ἦτε τῷ καιρῷ ἐκείνῳ χωρὶς Χριστοῦ, ἀπηλλοτριωμένοι τῆς πολιτείας τοῦ Ἰσραὴλ καὶ ξένοι τῶν διαθηκῶν τῆς ἐπαγγελίας, ἐλπίδα μὴ ἔχοντες καὶ ἄθεοι ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ.
that you were, at that time, apart from Christ, alienated from the citizenship of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world.note
en aquel tiempo estabais sin Cristo, alejados de la ciudadanía de Israel y ajenos a los pactos de la promesa, sin esperanza y sin Dios en el mundo.
13
νυνὶ δὲ ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ ὑμεῖς οἵ ποτε ὄντες μακρὰν ἐγενήθητε ἐγγὺς ἐν τῷ αἵματι τοῦ Χριστοῦ.
But now, in Christ Jesus, you who were once far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.note
Pero ahora, en Cristo Jesús, vosotros, que en otro tiempo estabais lejos, habéis sido acercados por la sangre de Cristo.
14
αὐτὸς γάρ ἐστιν ἡ εἰρήνη ἡμῶν, ὁ ποιήσας τὰ ἀμφότερα ἓν καὶ τὸ μεσότοιχον τοῦ φραγμοῦ λύσας, τὴν ἔχθραν ἐν τῇ σαρκὶ αὐτοῦ,
For he himself is our peace, who made the two one, and broke down the dividing wall of the fence — the hostility —note
Porque él mismo es nuestra paz, que de ambos hizo uno, y derribó el muro divisorio de la valla —la enemistad—,
15
τὸν νόμον τῶν ἐντολῶν ἐν δόγμασιν καταργήσας, ἵνα τοὺς δύο κτίσῃ ἐν αὑτῷ εἰς ἕνα καινὸν ἄνθρωπον ποιῶν εἰρήνην,
in his flesh, abolishing the law of commandments in decrees, so that he might create the two, in himself, into one new man, making peace,note
en su carne, aboliendo la ley de los mandamientos expresados en decretos, para crear en sí mismo de los dos un solo hombre nuevo, haciendo la paz,
16
καὶ ἀποκαταλλάξῃ τοὺς ἀμφοτέρους ἐν ἑνὶ σώματι τῷ θεῷ διὰ τοῦ σταυροῦ ἀποκτείνας τὴν ἔχθραν ἐν αὐτῷ·
and might reconcile both, in one body, to God through the cross, having killed the hostility in himself.note
y reconciliar con Dios a ambos en un solo cuerpo, por medio de la cruz, dando muerte en sí mismo a la enemistad.
17
καὶ ἐλθὼν εὐηγγελίσατο εἰρήνην ὑμῖν τοῖς μακρὰν καὶ εἰρήνην τοῖς ἐγγύς·
And coming, he announced the good news of peace to you who were far off, and peace to those who were near,note
Y vino y anunció las buenas nuevas de paz a vosotros que estabais lejos, y paz a los que estaban cerca,
18
ὅτι διʼ αὐτοῦ ἔχομεν τὴν προσαγωγὴν οἱ ἀμφότεροι ἐν ἑνὶ πνεύματι πρὸς τὸν πατέρα.
because through him we both have access, in one Spirit, to the Father.note
porque por medio de él los unos y los otros tenemos acceso, en un solo Espíritu, al Padre.
19
ἄρα οὖν οὐκέτι ἐστὲ ξένοι καὶ πάροικοι, ἀλλὰ ἐστὲ συμπολῖται τῶν ἁγίων καὶ οἰκεῖοι τοῦ θεοῦ,
So then you are no longer strangers and foreigners, but you are fellow citizens with the holy ones and members of the household of God,note
Así que ya no sois extranjeros ni forasteros, sino conciudadanos de los santos y miembros de la casa de Dios,
20
ἐποικοδομηθέντες ἐπὶ τῷ θεμελίῳ τῶν ἀποστόλων καὶ προφητῶν, ὄντος ἀκρογωνιαίου αὐτοῦ Χριστοῦ Ἰησοῦ,
built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the cornerstone,note
edificados sobre el fundamento de los apóstoles y profetas, siendo la principal piedra del ángulo Jesucristo mismo,
21
ἐν ᾧ πᾶσα οἰκοδομὴ συναρμολογουμένη αὔξει εἰς ναὸν ἅγιον ἐν κυρίῳ,
in whom the whole building, fitted together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord,note
en quien todo el edificio, bien coordinado, va creciendo para ser un templo santo en el Señor;
22
ἐν ᾧ καὶ ὑμεῖς συνοικοδομεῖσθε εἰς κατοικητήριον τοῦ θεοῦ ἐν πνεύματι.
in whom you also are being built together into a dwelling place of God in the Spirit.note
en quien también vosotros sois juntamente edificados para morada de Dios en el Espíritu.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–3 · dead, not sick

⚠ “Dead,” and the word is not softened anywhere in the " "sentence that follows. Nekrous — the ordinary word for a corpse. " "Paul does not say the readers were weak, or sick, or lost; the diagnosis is death, and death " "is not a condition a corpse can improve from from the inside. The grammar of the whole " "passage depends on this: verse 4 does not open with “and so you tried harder” but " "with “but God” — the hinge of the chapter, four verses of " "diagnosis answered by two words.

" "

“The ruler of the authority of the air.” A title for the " "devil found nowhere else in the New Testament in this exact form. KJV " "“the prince of the power of the air”; NIV " "“the ruler of the kingdom of the air.” Ancient cosmology commonly placed the " "lower atmosphere, between earth and moon, under hostile spiritual powers — a physical " "picture, not only a moral one, of exactly where the “heavenly places” of 1:3 are contested territory.

" "

⚠ “By nature children of wrath.” Physei, by " "nature — not by choice, not by accumulated bad acts, but as a condition prior to any " "of them. This single word has carried an enormous weight of later theology (it is one of the " "proof-texts for original sin, argued over by Augustine and Pelagius, by the Reformers and " "their opponents), and this library reports the weight rather than settling under it: the " "verse states a condition Paul says is universal (“as also the rest”) without " "explaining its mechanism.

Verses 4–7 · the hinge of the letter, in two words

⚠ “But God” — two words carrying the whole turn of the " "chapter. Ho de theos, positioned exactly where the syntax of vv1-3 seemed " "to be heading toward a verb and a subject dying, unanswered, under its own weight. Some " "preachers have built entire sermons on this phrase alone; the Greek earns it grammatically, " "not just rhetorically — verses 1-3 never complete their own sentence, and God's action " "in verse 5 is what finally supplies the missing main verb.

" "

“Made us alive together with Christ” … “raised us up " "together” … “seated us together.” Three verbs, each built " "on syn-, “with” — synezōopoiēsen, synēgeiren, " "synekathisen — and each describing something that has, grammatically, already " "happened (aorist tense) to people the chapter has just called corpses. The resurrection and " "enthronement are not future hopes here; they are reported as already accomplished, with " "Christ, in the same three stages his own story took: death, resurrection, session at the " "right hand.

" "

“By grace you have been saved” appears twice in this chapter " "(here and v8) in the identical Greek construction — a perfect passive, an action " "completed in the past with continuing effect, not an ongoing project. It interrupts its own " "sentence both times, set off, almost muttered, as if Paul cannot get three clauses into this " "argument without saying it again.

Verses 8–10 · not of works, for good works

The most quoted sentence in the letter turns on a preposition most " "translations agree on and a pronoun they do not. “This” (touto) is " "neuter; “grace” and “faith” are both feminine in Greek, so “this” " "grammatically cannot refer narrowly to either word alone — it points to the whole " "preceding act of being saved by grace through faith, not to faith considered as a private " "achievement that might itself be a kind of work. The library flags this because a great deal " "of later argument has turned on whether faith itself is the gift being named.

" "

⚠ “Workmanship.” Poiēma — a thing made, " "a work of craftsmanship, and yes, the direct ancestor of English poem: a made thing, " "composed rather than assembled. It is used in the New Testament only here and at Romans " "1:20, of the created world itself. The same word for what God made in six days is used here " "of what God makes of a person.

" "

Not of works … for good works. Verse 9 shuts one door and verse " "10 opens another with the identical word, erga, and the juxtaposition is deliberate: " "salvation is emphatically not earned by good works, and just as emphatically it is for " "them — a purpose stated, not a payment required. KJV " "and ASV keep “workmanship”; NIV “handiwork”; NWT " "“work.”

Verses 11–13 · a nickname, in quotation marks

“Uncircumcision” … “circumcision.” Both " "words in the Greek carry the marker “the so-called” (legomenoi / " "legomenēs) — Paul is naming a slur and its mirror-slur, not endorsing either " "as the real basis of the distinction he is about to erase. This translation keeps the quotation " "marks the Greek's own vocabulary supplies, where several versions print the labels as if they " "were neutral descriptions.

" "

⚠ Four losses, named in a row. Apart from Christ; alienated from " "Israel's citizenship; strangers to the covenants; without hope and without God. The list is " "not rhetorical padding — each phrase names a specific, real exclusion a Gentile in " "Ephesus would have felt exactly, in a city where the distinction between citizen and " "foreigner was a matter of hard civic law, not sentiment.

“Brought near by the blood of Christ.” “Far” and " "“near” are technical rabbinic terms for Gentile and Jew, and Paul will requote " "both in v17, echoing Isaiah 57:19's “peace, peace, to the far and to the near.” " "The distance being closed is not primarily emotional; it is the same citizenship-and-covenant " "distance just listed in v12, closed by the same blood named at 1:7.

Verses 14–18 · a wall you could once actually touch

⚠ “The dividing wall of the fence” is very likely not a " "metaphor pulled from nowhere. The Second Temple's inner courts were fenced off from " "the outer Court of the Gentiles by a stone balustrade called the soreg, posted with " "inscribed warning stones in Greek and Latin: no foreigner may enter within the " "balustrade… whoever is caught will have himself to blame for the death that follows. " "Two of these actual stones have been excavated — one in 1871, now in the Istanbul " "Archaeology Museum; one in 1936, now in the Israel Museum, Jerusalem. Paul, who nearly lost " "his life in that same temple over an accusation of bringing a Gentile past that exact " "barrier (Acts 21:28-29), writes to a Gentile-majority church that Christ has broken down a " "wall his own readers may have seen with their own eyes and read the warning on.

" "

“One new man.” Kainon " "— new in kind, not new in time (neos), the same distinction this library has " "tracked since Revelation. Not a merger of Jew and Gentile into a bigger version of either, " "but a third thing neither was before.

" "

“Having killed the hostility in himself” (v16) and “he himself " "is our peace” (v14) frame the whole unit: hostility named at the start, killed " "at the center, replaced by a peace preached in person (v17) to two audiences who could not " "previously stand in the same court of the same building.

Verses 19–22 · citizens, family, and a building still going up

“Fellow citizens… members of the household.” Two " "images in one verse, civic and domestic, both correcting v12's “alienated… " "strangers” point for point: no longer excluded from the citizenship of Israel, but " "citizens; no longer strangers to the covenants, but family.

" "

⚠ “Cornerstone” may not mean what a modern reader pictures. " "Akrogōniaios — literally “highest-corner-stone” — is read two " "ways by scholars of ancient architecture: the foundation stone set first, at the corner, that " "fixes the alignment of two walls; or the capstone that completes an arch or gable at the top, " "the last stone placed rather than the first. Both readings connect to the same Old Testament " "text lying behind this word, “the stone the builders rejected has become the head of " "the corner” (Psalm 118:22, quoted again at 1 Peter 2:6-7). KJV/ASV “chief corner stone”; " "NIV “chief cornerstone” — the English shelf " "does not disagree on the word, only architectural historians disagree on what it built.

" "

The building is not finished. “Grows” (v21) and “are " "being built together” (v22) are both present tense, ongoing action, not a completed " "structure being described after the fact. The temple this chapter ends on is still under " "construction, and the readers are not spectators of it but material being added to it.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “dead” for nekrous (v1), the corpse-word kept unsoftened, since the chapter's whole argument depends on the diagnosis being total; “workmanship” for poiēma (v10), the word behind English “poem”; the quotation marks kept around “uncircumcision” and “circumcision” (v11), since the Greek itself flags both as labels rather than neutral descriptions; “dividing wall of the fence” (v14), kept concrete rather than abstracted into a general metaphor for prejudice; “one new man” (v15), kainon rather than neos, new in kind and not merely in time; and “cornerstone” for akrogōniaios (v20), flagged rather than resolved between the foundation-stone and capstone readings.

Echoes paid. “By grace you have been saved” (v5), the interrupting parenthesis chapter 1 was already building toward across its two-hundred-word opening sentence; the redemption through his blood from 1:7, now the blood that brings the far near (v13); and power measured by resurrection, the yardstick 1:19-20 set by Christ's own raising, now applied to the readers themselves — “you were dead… God made you alive together with Christ.”

Echoes planted. The household of God and the temple still under construction (vv19-22), an image this letter will return to for marriage and the armor of God; the one new man (v15), which chapter 3 will call the mystery now fully disclosed; and the peace preached to far and near (v17), which reaches back to Isaiah's own words for the same two audiences.

Next in this letter: Ephesians 3 — Paul, prisoner for the Gentiles, and the mystery named but not yet explained in chapter 2 finally given its content; the reason he can pray boldly despite his chains; and a prayer for power to grasp a love that, he says, surpasses knowledge.
