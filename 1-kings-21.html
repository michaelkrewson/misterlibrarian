Naboth of Jezreel will not give Ahab the inheritance of his fathers. Jezebel writes letters in the king’s name, and two sons of worthlessness bear witness at a fast; Naboth is stoned, and Ahab goes down to take possession. Elijah meets him in the vineyard with the sentence on Jeroboam’s house, and when Ahab puts on sackcloth the evil is put off to his son’s days.
‘After these things’ is the only date, and the chapter comes after the two Aramean campaigns. The three years without war of 1 Kings 22:1 follow it in the Hebrew.

1
וַיְהִי, אַחַר הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, כֶּרֶם הָיָה לְנָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי, אֲשֶׁר בְּיִזְרְעֶאל--אֵצֶל הֵיכַל אַחְאָב, מֶלֶךְ שֹׁמְרוֹן.
And it happened after these things: Naboth the Jezreelite had a vineyard, which was in Jezreel, beside the palace of Ahab king of Samaria.note
Y sucedió después de estas cosas: Nabot el jezreelita tenía una viña, que estaba en Jezreel, junto al palacio de Acab rey de Samaria.
2
וַיְדַבֵּר אַחְאָב אֶל-נָבוֹת לֵאמֹר תְּנָה-לִּי אֶת-כַּרְמְךָ וִיהִי-לִי לְגַן-יָרָק, כִּי הוּא קָרוֹב אֵצֶל בֵּיתִי, וְאֶתְּנָה לְךָ תַּחְתָּיו, כֶּרֶם טוֹב מִמֶּנּוּ; אִם טוֹב בְּעֵינֶיךָ, אֶתְּנָה-לְךָ כֶסֶף מְחִיר זֶה.
And Ahab spoke to Naboth, saying: “Give me your vineyard, and let it be a garden of greens for me, for it is near beside my house; and let me give you in its place a vineyard better than it. If it is good in your eyes, let me give you silver, the price of this.”note
Y Acab habló a Nabot, diciendo: «Dame tu viña, y será para mí huerto de hortalizas, porque está cerca, junto a mi casa; y déjame darte en su lugar una viña mejor que ella. Si es bueno a tus ojos, déjame darte plata, el precio de esta».
3
וַיֹּאמֶר נָבוֹת, אֶל-אַחְאָב:  חָלִילָה לִּי מֵיְהוָה, מִתִּתִּי אֶת-נַחֲלַת אֲבֹתַי לָךְ.
And Naboth said to Ahab: “Far be it from me, by Jehovah, that I should give you the inheritance of my fathers.”note
Y Nabot dijo a Acab: «Lejos esté de mí, por Jehová, que yo te dé la heredad de mis padres».
4
וַיָּבֹא אַחְאָב אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ סַר וְזָעֵף, עַל-הַדָּבָר אֲשֶׁר-דִּבֶּר אֵלָיו נָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי, וַיֹּאמֶר, לֹא-אֶתֵּן לְךָ אֶת-נַחֲלַת אֲבוֹתָי; וַיִּשְׁכַּב, עַל-מִטָּתוֹ, וַיַּסֵּב אֶת-פָּנָיו, וְלֹא-אָכַל לָחֶם.
And Ahab came to his house sullen and raging over the word that Naboth the Jezreelite had spoken to him, for he had said: “I will not give you the inheritance of my fathers.” And he lay down on his bed and turned his face away, and he did not eat bread.note
Y vino Acab a su casa huraño y airado por la palabra que le había hablado Nabot el jezreelita, porque había dicho: «No te daré la heredad de mis padres». Y se acostó en su cama y volvió su rostro, y no comió pan.
5
וַתָּבֹא אֵלָיו, אִיזֶבֶל אִשְׁתּוֹ; וַתְּדַבֵּר אֵלָיו, מַה-זֶּה רוּחֲךָ סָרָה, וְאֵינְךָ, אֹכֵל לָחֶם.
And Jezebel his wife came to him and spoke to him: “Why is your spirit sullen, and you are not eating bread?”note
Y vino a él Jezabel su mujer y le habló: «¿Por qué está huraño tu espíritu, y no comes pan?».
6
וַיְדַבֵּר אֵלֶיהָ, כִּי-אֲדַבֵּר אֶל-נָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי וָאֹמַר לוֹ תְּנָה-לִּי אֶת-כַּרְמְךָ בְּכֶסֶף, אוֹ אִם-חָפֵץ אַתָּה, אֶתְּנָה-לְךָ כֶרֶם תַּחְתָּיו; וַיֹּאמֶר, לֹא-אֶתֵּן לְךָ אֶת-כַּרְמִי.
And he spoke to her: “Because I spoke to Naboth the Jezreelite and said to him, ‘Give me your vineyard for silver; or if you wish, let me give you a vineyard in its place.’ And he said, ‘I will not give you my vineyard.’”note
Y él le habló: «Porque hablé a Nabot el jezreelita y le dije: “Dame tu viña por plata; o si lo deseas, déjame darte una viña en su lugar”. Y él dijo: “No te daré mi viña”».
7
וַתֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו, אִיזֶבֶל אִשְׁתּוֹ, אַתָּה, עַתָּה תַּעֲשֶׂה מְלוּכָה עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל; קוּם אֱכָל-לֶחֶם, וְיִטַב לִבֶּךָ--אֲנִי אֶתֵּן לְךָ, אֶת-כֶּרֶם נָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי.
And Jezebel his wife said to him: “You — do you now exercise kingship over Israel? Rise, eat bread, and let your heart be glad. I will give you the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite.”note
Y Jezabel su mujer le dijo: «Tú, ¿ejerces tú ahora el reinado sobre Israel? Levántate, come pan, y alégrese tu corazón. Yo te daré la viña de Nabot el jezreelita».
8
וַתִּכְתֹּב סְפָרִים בְּשֵׁם אַחְאָב, וַתַּחְתֹּם בְּחֹתָמוֹ; וַתִּשְׁלַח הספרים סְפָרִים, אֶל-הַזְּקֵנִים וְאֶל-הַחֹרִים אֲשֶׁר בְּעִירוֹ, הַיֹּשְׁבִים, אֶת-נָבוֹת.
And she wrote letters in Ahab’s name and sealed them with his seal, and she sent letters to the elders and to the nobles who were in his city, who lived with Naboth.note
Y ella escribió cartas en nombre de Acab y las selló con su sello, y envió cartas a los ancianos y a los nobles que había en su ciudad, que vivían con Nabot.
9
וַתִּכְתֹּב בַּסְּפָרִים, לֵאמֹר:  קִרְאוּ-צוֹם, וְהֹשִׁיבוּ אֶת-נָבוֹת בְּרֹאשׁ הָעָם.
And she wrote in the letters, saying: “Proclaim a fast, and seat Naboth at the head of the people;note
Y escribió en las cartas, diciendo: «Proclamen un ayuno, y sienten a Nabot a la cabeza del pueblo;
10
וְהוֹשִׁיבוּ שְׁנַיִם אֲנָשִׁים בְּנֵי-בְלִיַּעַל, נֶגְדּוֹ, וִיעִדֻהוּ לֵאמֹר, בֵּרַכְתָּ אֱלֹהִים וָמֶלֶךְ; וְהוֹצִיאֻהוּ וְסִקְלֻהוּ, וְיָמֹת.
and seat two men, sons of worthlessness, opposite him, and let them testify against him, saying, ‘You have blessed God and the king.’ And bring him out and stone him, that he may die.”note
y sienten a dos hombres, hijos de la nada, frente a él, y que testifiquen contra él, diciendo: “Has bendecido a Dios y al rey”. Y sáquenlo y apedréenlo, para que muera».
11
וַיַּעֲשׂוּ אַנְשֵׁי עִירוֹ הַזְּקֵנִים וְהַחֹרִים, אֲשֶׁר הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּעִירוֹ, כַּאֲשֶׁר שָׁלְחָה אֲלֵיהֶם, אִיזָבֶל--כַּאֲשֶׁר כָּתוּב בַּסְּפָרִים, אֲשֶׁר שָׁלְחָה אֲלֵיהֶם.
And the men of his city, the elders and the nobles who lived in his city, did as Jezebel had sent to them, as it was written in the letters that she had sent to them.note
Y los hombres de su ciudad, los ancianos y los nobles que vivían en su ciudad, hicieron como Jezabel les había enviado, como estaba escrito en las cartas que ella les había enviado.
12
קָרְאוּ, צוֹם; וְהֹשִׁיבוּ אֶת-נָבוֹת, בְּרֹאשׁ הָעָם.
They proclaimed a fast and seated Naboth at the head of the people.note
Proclamaron un ayuno y sentaron a Nabot a la cabeza del pueblo.
13
וַיָּבֹאוּ שְׁנֵי הָאֲנָשִׁים בְּנֵי-בְלִיַּעַל, וַיֵּשְׁבוּ נֶגְדּוֹ, וַיְעִדֻהוּ אַנְשֵׁי הַבְּלִיַּעַל אֶת-נָבוֹת נֶגֶד הָעָם לֵאמֹר, בֵּרַךְ נָבוֹת אֱלֹהִים וָמֶלֶךְ; וַיֹּצִאֻהוּ מִחוּץ לָעִיר, וַיִּסְקְלֻהוּ בָאֲבָנִים וַיָּמֹת.
And the two men, the sons of worthlessness, came and sat opposite him; and the men of worthlessness testified against him, against Naboth, before the people, saying: “Naboth has blessed God and the king.” And they brought him outside the city and stoned him with stones, and he died.note
Y los dos hombres, los hijos de la nada, vinieron y se sentaron frente a él; y los hombres de la nada testificaron contra él, contra Nabot, delante del pueblo, diciendo: «Nabot ha bendecido a Dios y al rey». Y lo sacaron fuera de la ciudad y lo apedrearon con piedras, y murió.
14
וַיִּשְׁלְחוּ, אֶל-אִיזֶבֶל לֵאמֹר:  סֻקַּל נָבוֹת, וַיָּמֹת.
And they sent to Jezebel, saying: “Naboth has been stoned, and he is dead.”note
Y enviaron a Jezabel, diciendo: «Nabot ha sido apedreado, y ha muerto».
15
וַיְהִי כִּשְׁמֹעַ אִיזֶבֶל, כִּי-סֻקַּל נָבוֹת וַיָּמֹת; וַתֹּאמֶר אִיזֶבֶל אֶל-אַחְאָב, קוּם רֵשׁ אֶת-כֶּרֶם נָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי אֲשֶׁר מֵאֵן לָתֶת-לְךָ בְכֶסֶף--כִּי אֵין נָבוֹת חַי, כִּי-מֵת.
And when Jezebel heard that Naboth had been stoned and was dead, Jezebel said to Ahab: “Rise, take possession of the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, which he refused to give you for silver; for Naboth is not alive, for he is dead.”note
Y cuando Jezabel oyó que Nabot había sido apedreado y había muerto, Jezabel dijo a Acab: «Levántate, toma posesión de la viña de Nabot el jezreelita, que rehusó darte por plata; porque Nabot no está vivo, porque ha muerto».
16
וַיְהִי כִּשְׁמֹעַ אַחְאָב, כִּי מֵת נָבוֹת; וַיָּקָם אַחְאָב, לָרֶדֶת אֶל-כֶּרֶם נָבוֹת הַיִּזְרְעֵאלִי--לְרִשְׁתּוֹ.  {פ}
And when Ahab heard that Naboth was dead, Ahab rose to go down to the vineyard of Naboth the Jezreelite, to take possession of it.note
Y cuando Acab oyó que Nabot había muerto, se levantó Acab para bajar a la viña de Nabot el jezreelita, para tomar posesión de ella.
17
וַיְהִי, דְּבַר-יְהוָה, אֶל-אֵלִיָּהוּ הַתִּשְׁבִּי, לֵאמֹר.
And the word of Jehovah came to Elijah the Tishbite, saying:note
Y vino la palabra de Jehová a Elías el tisbita, diciendo:
18
קוּם רֵד, לִקְרַאת אַחְאָב מֶלֶךְ-יִשְׂרָאֵל--אֲשֶׁר בְּשֹׁמְרוֹן; הִנֵּה בְּכֶרֶם נָבוֹת, אֲשֶׁר-יָרַד שָׁם לְרִשְׁתּוֹ.
“Rise, go down to meet Ahab king of Israel, who is in Samaria. Look — he is in the vineyard of Naboth, where he has gone down to take possession of it.note
«Levántate, baja al encuentro de Acab rey de Israel, que está en Samaria. Mira, está en la viña de Nabot, adonde ha bajado para tomar posesión de ella.
19
וְדִבַּרְתָּ אֵלָיו לֵאמֹר, כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה, הֲרָצַחְתָּ, וְגַם-יָרָשְׁתָּ; וְדִבַּרְתָּ אֵלָיו לֵאמֹר, כֹּה אָמַר יְהוָה, בִּמְקוֹם אֲשֶׁר לָקְקוּ הַכְּלָבִים אֶת-דַּם נָבוֹת, יָלֹקּוּ הַכְּלָבִים אֶת-דָּמְךָ גַּם-אָתָּה.
And you shall speak to him, saying: ‘Thus says Jehovah: Have you murdered, and also taken possession?’ And you shall speak to him, saying: ‘Thus says Jehovah: In the place where the dogs licked the blood of Naboth, the dogs will lick your blood, yours too.’”note
Y le hablarás, diciendo: “Así dice Jehová: ¿Has asesinado, y también has tomado posesión?”. Y le hablarás, diciendo: “Así dice Jehová: En el lugar donde los perros lamieron la sangre de Nabot, los perros lamerán tu sangre, la tuya también”».
20
וַיֹּאמֶר אַחְאָב אֶל-אֵלִיָּהוּ, הַמְצָאתַנִי אֹיְבִי; וַיֹּאמֶר מָצָאתִי--יַעַן הִתְמַכֶּרְךָ, לַעֲשׂוֹת הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה.
And Ahab said to Elijah: “Have you found me, my enemy?” And he said: “I have found you, because you have sold yourself to do what is evil in the eyes of Jehovah.note
Y Acab dijo a Elías: «¿Me has hallado, enemigo mío?». Y él dijo: «Te he hallado, porque te has vendido para hacer lo malo a los ojos de Jehová.
21
הִנְנִי מֵבִי אֵלֶיךָ רָעָה, וּבִעַרְתִּי אַחֲרֶיךָ; וְהִכְרַתִּי לְאַחְאָב מַשְׁתִּין בְּקִיר, וְעָצוּר וְעָזוּב בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל.
Look — I am bringing evil on you, and I will burn out after you, and I will cut off from Ahab anyone who urinates against the wall, whether shut up or set free in Israel.note
Mira, yo traigo el mal sobre ti, y quemaré tras de ti, y cortaré de Acab a todo el que orine contra la pared, sea encerrado o libre en Israel.
22
וְנָתַתִּי אֶת-בֵּיתְךָ, כְּבֵית יָרָבְעָם בֶּן-נְבָט, וּכְבֵית, בַּעְשָׁא בֶן-אֲחִיָּה:  אֶל-הַכַּעַס אֲשֶׁר הִכְעַסְתָּ, וַתַּחֲטִא אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and like the house of Baasha son of Ahijah, for the provocation with which you have provoked to anger, and have made Israel sin.note
Y pondré tu casa como la casa de Jeroboam hijo de Nabat y como la casa de Baasa hijo de Ahías, por la provocación con que has provocado a ira, y has hecho pecar a Israel.
23
וְגַם-לְאִיזֶבֶל--דִּבֶּר יְהוָה, לֵאמֹר:  הַכְּלָבִים יֹאכְלוּ אֶת-אִיזֶבֶל, בְּחֵל יִזְרְעֶאל.
And also of Jezebel Jehovah spoke, saying: ‘The dogs shall eat Jezebel in the rampart of Jezreel.’note
Y también de Jezabel habló Jehová, diciendo: “Los perros comerán a Jezabel en el antemuro de Jezreel”.
24
הַמֵּת לְאַחְאָב בָּעִיר, יֹאכְלוּ הַכְּלָבִים; וְהַמֵּת, בַּשָּׂדֶה, יֹאכְלוּ, עוֹף הַשָּׁמָיִם.
Whoever of Ahab’s dies in the city the dogs shall eat, and whoever dies in the field the birds of the heavens shall eat.”note
Al de Acab que muera en la ciudad, se lo comerán los perros, y al que muera en el campo, se lo comerán las aves de los cielos».
25
רַק, לֹא-הָיָה כְאַחְאָב, אֲשֶׁר הִתְמַכֵּר, לַעֲשׂוֹת הָרַע בְּעֵינֵי יְהוָה--אֲשֶׁר-הֵסַתָּה אֹתוֹ, אִיזֶבֶל אִשְׁתּוֹ.
There was indeed no one like Ahab, who sold himself to do what was evil in the eyes of Jehovah, whom Jezebel his wife incited.note
A la verdad no hubo nadie como Acab, que se vendió para hacer lo malo a los ojos de Jehová, a quien Jezabel su mujer incitó.
26
וַיַּתְעֵב מְאֹד, לָלֶכֶת אַחֲרֵי הַגִּלֻּלִים--כְּכֹל, אֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ הָאֱמֹרִי, אֲשֶׁר הוֹרִישׁ יְהוָה, מִפְּנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.  {פ}
And he acted very abominably in going after the idols, according to all that the Amorite did, whom Jehovah dispossessed before the sons of Israel.note
Y actuó de manera muy abominable al ir tras los ídolos, conforme a todo lo que hizo el amorreo, a quien Jehová desalojó delante de los hijos de Israel.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · 1 Kings 21:1–4 Naboth’s vineyard — “a garden of greens”, “Far be it from me, by Jehovah”, and “the inheritance of my fathers”

“After these things”. The last chapter ended with Ahab going home “sullen and raging”, and this one sends him home with the same two words at verse 4. The Greek Bible in the Vaticanus tradition has this chapter straight after the call of Elisha, as its chapter 20, so that join is not there. It also has no “sullen and raging” at verse 4: there only “the spirit of Achaab was troubled”.

“Ahab king of Samaria”. His usual title is “king of Israel”. “King of Samaria” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible as a title of an Israelite king, here and at 2 Kings 1:3 (now on these pages); the king of Shimron in Joshua is a different word. Naboth’s vineyard is in Jezreel, where Ahab rode in the rain (1 Kings 18:45, now on these pages), so the king from the capital is next door. The Greek has the vineyard near his “threshingfloor”.

⭐ “A garden of greens”. The words stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible. The other is Deuteronomy 11:10 (now on these pages), where Moses says the land is not like Egypt, “where you would sow your seed and water it with your foot, like a garden of greens”. The verse here makes nothing of that. It is the reader who hears a king asking to turn an Israelite vineyard into what Egypt grew. The ASV and the KJV have “a garden of herbs”, the NWT 1984 “a garden of vegetables”, the NIV “a vegetable garden”, and the TLB “a garden”.

“The price of this”. As an offer it is fair: a better vineyard, or silver. Ahab’s father bought the hill of Samaria (1 Kings 16:24, now on these pages), and David would not take a threshing floor except “for a price” (2 Samuel 24:24, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “money as the price of this”, and the NIV “whatever it is worth”. The Hebrew word is kesef, silver.

⭐ “Far be it from me, by Jehovah, that I should give you the inheritance of my fathers”. The opening words stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible. The other two are David sparing Saul (1 Samuel 24:6; 26:11, both now on these pages). The ASV has “Jehovah forbid it me”, the NWT 1984 “It is unthinkable on my part, from Jehovah’s standpoint”, and the TLB “Not on your life!”, which drops Jehovah from the only sentence in which Naboth names him. Nachalah is land that stays with a family. “The land is mine”, Jehovah says, and it is not to be sold for good (Leviticus 25:23), and each is to hold to the inheritance of his fathers (Numbers 36:7, both now on these pages). Naboth cites neither. He gives a reason, and it is Jehovah.

“And he turned his face away”. The king goes to bed and will not eat, over a vegetable patch. The ASV has “turned away his face”, and the NWT 1984 “kept his face turned”. The Douay and the TLB have “to the wall”, and the Hebrew has no wall here. The NIV has “sulking”.

Verses 5–7 · 1 Kings 21:5–7 Jezebel takes over — what Ahab leaves out, “do you now exercise kingship?”, and “Rise, eat bread”

“Why is your spirit sullen”. She uses the first of the two words the narrator has just used of him, sar. The NIV has “so sullen”; the ASV and the NWT 1984 have “sad”, and so lose it.

⭐ What Ahab leaves out. The Hebrew tells Naboth’s answer three times, and it shrinks. At verse 3 it is “Far be it from me, by Jehovah, that I should give you the inheritance of my fathers”. At verse 4 the narrator keeps the inheritance and drops Jehovah. At verse 6 Ahab tells it as “I will not give you my vineyard”, and the fathers have gone too. His own offer changes as well: silver now comes first, and the better vineyard is only a vineyard. What reaches Jezebel is a man who will not sell. The ASV keeps all this. The NIV has “Sell me your vineyard”, where the Hebrew has “Give me your vineyard for silver”. The Greek puts “the inheritance of my fathers” back into Ahab’s mouth.

⚠ “You — do you now exercise kingship over Israel?” The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, and the Hebrew puts “you” first. It has no question word, so it can be a question or a jeer. The ASV has “Dost thou now govern the kingdom of Israel?”, the NWT 1984 “Is it you that now exercise the kingship over Israel?”, the NIV “Is this how you act as king over Israel?”, and the TLB “Are you the king of Israel or not?”. The Douay takes it as irony, “Thou art of great authority indeed”. Samuel had said what a king would do: “your fields and your vineyards and your olive groves, the best of them, he will take” (1 Samuel 8:14, now on these pages).

“Rise, eat bread … I will give you”. “Rise, eat” is what the angel said to Elijah under the broom tree (1 Kings 19:5, now on these pages). The sentence opened with “you” and it closes with “I”, and “give” is Naboth’s verb: he said “I will not give”, and she says “I will give”.

Verses 8–14 · 1 Kings 21:8–14 The letters — Ahab’s seal, a fast, two sons of worthlessness, and “You have blessed God and the king” (cursed God)

“In Ahab’s name, and sealed them with his seal”. Chotam is a signet. The Hebrew writes “the letters” with the article and reads it without (ketiv and qere), and this translation follows the reading. Whether Ahab knew is not said, and Jehovah’s question at verse 19 is put to him all the same. David wrote such a letter to Joab, by Uriah’s hand (2 Samuel 11:14, now on these pages), and Jehu will write letters too (2 Kings 10:1). The ASV and the NWT 1984 have “sealed them with his seal”, and the Douay “with his ring”.

⭐ “As Jezebel had sent to them”. The letters carried Ahab’s name, but the narrator says they did as Jezebel sent, and verse 14 has them report to her. They knew whose letters these were. They are “the men of his city”, and verse 8 says they “lived with Naboth”. The TLB has “The city fathers followed the queen’s instructions”, which is shorter. The Hebrew repeats the whole order in the carrying out, nearly word for word, and this page keeps the repetition.

“Proclaim a fast”. The two words stand in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: these two, Jeremiah 36:9, and Nineveh at Jonah 3:5. What the fast was for is not said; a public fast says that something is wrong, and an accusation can then follow. The NWT 1984 has “at the head of the people”, with the Hebrew, as at Deuteronomy 20:9 (now on these pages). The ASV and the KJV have “on high among the people”, and the NIV “in a prominent place”. Whether this is an honour or the dock, the verse does not say.

“Two men, sons of worthlessness”. Two is the law: “At the mouth of two witnesses” (Deuteronomy 17:6, now on these pages). Her letters keep it. “Sons of worthlessness” stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible. The first is Deuteronomy 13:14 (now on these pages), men who lead a city astray. The KJV has “sons of Belial”, the ASV “base fellows”, the NWT 1984 “good-for-nothing fellows”, the NIV “scoundrels”, the Douay “sons of the devil” at verse 13, and the TLB “two men who had no conscience”.

⚠ “You have blessed God and the king” — the Hebrew says “blessed” and means “cursed”. The verb is berakh, “bless”, in both verses. It is the same substitution as in Job (Job 1:5, 11, now on these pages; 2:5, 9), and these pages print the word there and do the same here. The sense is the charge the law forbids, “You shall not revile God, nor curse a ruler of your people” (Exodus 22:28, now on these pages). The ASV, the NIV and the NWT 1984 have “curse” or “cursed”, and the KJV, the Geneva and the Douay “blaspheme”. The Greek keeps “blessed”. The one thing Naboth is recorded as saying about God is “Far be it from me, by Jehovah”.

“Outside the city … stoned him with stones”. That is the law’s phrase (Deuteronomy 17:5, now on these pages), and the whole form of a trial is kept. Stephen dies the same way: false witnesses, “thrown out of the city”, and stones (Acts 6:13; 7:58, both now on these pages). And two false witnesses come forward at Matthew 26:60 (now on these pages). How the vineyard came to the king is not explained. Naboth’s sons are not mentioned here, and 2 Kings 9:26 says their blood was shed too.

Verses 15–16 · 1 Kings 21:15–16 “Rise, take possession” — the verb of the conquest, and a word that is dropped

⭐ “Rise, take possession”. The verb is yarash, the word for Israel’s taking of the land: “Go up, take possession”, said Moses (Deuteronomy 1:21, now on these pages). It stands four times here, at verses 15, 16, 18 and 19, and verse 26 will use the same root of the Amorites. A king of Israel is conquering a vineyard in Israel. The ASV and the NWT 1984 have “take possession”.

“For Naboth is not alive, for he is dead”. The men said “stoned” to Jezebel; she heard “stoned”; and to Ahab she says only “dead”. Ahab “heard that Naboth was dead”, and he asks nothing. She also says “refused to give you for silver”, which is his version and not Naboth’s. The NIV has “refused to sell you”, and the ASV “not alive, but dead”. The TLB has “He’s dead!”.

“Ahab rose”. The Greek has him tear his garments and put on sackcloth here, on hearing of the death, before he goes down. The Hebrew keeps that for verse 27, after Elijah.

Verses 17–19 · 1 Kings 21:17–19 Elijah the Tishbite — “Have you murdered, and also taken possession?”, and the dogs

“Rise, go down”. Elijah has not been seen since he threw his mantle on Elisha, and “the Tishbite” is his title from 1 Kings 17:1 (now on these pages). “Rise” is what Jezebel said to Ahab. “Who is in Samaria” sits oddly beside a vineyard in Jezreel; the ASV has “who dwelleth in Samaria”, and the NIV “who rules in Samaria”, which read it as his seat and not his whereabouts.

⭐ “Have you murdered, and also taken possession?” The Hebrew is two verbs and a joining word, ha-ratsachta ve-gam yarashta. The first is the verb of “You shall not murder” (Exodus 20, now on these pages): see ratsach. The NWT 1984 has “Have you murdered”, and the NIV “Have you not murdered a man”; the ASV and the KJV have “killed”, which is a different word. Ahab threw no stone, the text does not show him writing a word, and the question is put to him. The TLB has “Isn’t killing Naboth bad enough? Must you rob him too?”.

⚠ “In the place where the dogs licked”. The verb is laqaq, the lapping of Gideon’s men (Judges 7:5, now on these pages). The text has not said until now that dogs were there. Ahab’s blood will be licked at the pool of Samaria (1 Kings 22:38), and Jehu will throw the body of Ahab’s son on Naboth’s plot and quote this word (2 Kings 9:25–26), so “the place” is not simple. Verse 29 moves the evil to the son’s days, and readers differ on how far that covers it. The Greek has “In every place where the swine and the dogs have licked”, and adds “and the harlots shall wash themselves in thy blood”, which it draws from 1 Kings 22:38. The text does not show Elijah saying verse 19 at all: it cuts straight to Ahab’s greeting.

Verses 20–26 · 1 Kings 21:20–26 “Have you found me, my enemy?” — sold, burned out, the rampart of Jezreel, and no one like Ahab

“Have you found me, my enemy?” Last time it was “Is this you, troubler of Israel?” (1 Kings 18:17, now on these pages). The ASV has “Hast thou found me, O mine enemy?”, and the NIV makes it a statement, “So you have found me, my enemy!”. The answer is one word in Hebrew, matsati, “I have found”.

⭐ “Because you have sold yourself”. The king offered silver for a vineyard, and the man would not sell. Now the thing sold is the king. See hitmakker. The ASV, the KJV and the NWT 1984 have “sold thyself” or “sold yourself”. The TLB has “sold yourself to the devil”, and there is no devil in the verse.

“Look — I am bringing evil on you”. Elijah is speaking, and the “I” is Jehovah’s, with no “Thus says Jehovah” to mark it. The NIV adds “He says”. The NWT 1984 opens a quotation inside Elijah’s words at verse 20. The Hebrew spells “bringing” here without its last letter, and “bring” at verse 29 the same way.

The old sentence. Most of this has been said twice before, and it is copied: Jeroboam’s house (1 Kings 14:10–11) and Baasha’s (16:3–4, both now on these pages). “Anyone who urinates against the wall”, mashtin be-qir, is kept by the KJV and the NWT 1984; the ASV has “every man-child”, and the NIV “every last male”. “Burn out” is bi’er; the ASV has “utterly sweep thee away”, and the NWT 1984 “make a clean sweep”. The sentence names the provoking and the sin of Israel. It does not name Naboth.

⚠ “In the rampart of Jezreel”. The Hebrew here has chel, “rampart”, and the telling at 2 Kings 9:10, 36 has cheleq, “portion, plot”. They differ by one letter. This page follows the Hebrew of this verse. The ASV has “by the rampart”, and the KJV and the NIV “by the wall”, and the Greek has “within the fortification”. The NWT 1984 has “in the plot of land”, and the Douay “in the field”, which agree with 2 Kings.

⚠ “Whom Jezebel his wife incited”. The narrator speaks here. The ASV and the NIV put these two verses in parentheses, and the NWT 1984 leaves them inside Elijah’s speech. “Incited” is sut, as at 1 Samuel 26:19 (now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “egged on”, and the ASV “stirred up”. The verse holds both things together: he sold himself, and she incited him.

“According to all that the Amorite did, whom Jehovah dispossessed”. The Hebrew has the singular, “the Amorite”. “Dispossessed” is yarash again, as at 1 Kings 14:24 (now on these pages): the man who took possession of a vineyard behaves like the people who lost the land. Gillulim are “idols”; the NWT 1984 has “dungy idols”, which takes the word from a root for dung, as some readers do.

Verses 27–29 · 1 Kings 21:27–29 Ahab in sackcloth — “and went about gently”, “Have you seen…?”, and the evil put off

⭐ “And put sackcloth on his flesh and fasted”. The chapter has had one fast, Jezebel’s, and this is the other. The sackcloth is what Ben-hadad’s servants wore to Ahab (1 Kings 20:31–32, now on these pages), and it gets a hearing here as it did there. Ahab says nothing. The vineyard is not mentioned: nothing is given back. The Douay has “haircloth upon his flesh”, and the TLB “put on rags”. The Greek has a longer verse, “pierced with sorrow”, and has him put on sackcloth “in the day that he smote Nabuthai”, which is said nowhere in the Hebrew.

“And went about gently”. The word is at, the “Gently” of David’s order about Absalom (2 Samuel 18:5, now on these pages). The ASV and the KJV have “went softly”, the NIV “went around meekly”, the NWT 1984 “walking despondently”, and the Douay “walked with his head cast down”. The Hebrew says how he walked, and not what he felt.

“Have you seen how Ahab has humbled himself?” “Have you seen” is what a prophet asked Ahab about the army of Aram (1 Kings 20:13, now on these pages). Now Jehovah asks Elijah about Ahab, and Elijah’s answer is not given. In these letters, “humbled himself” stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the other four are in 2 Chronicles. The NWT 1984 has “on my account”, and the ASV “before me”, which is nearer to the Hebrew.

⚠ “In the days of his son”. The evil is put off and not cancelled, and it falls on someone who has done none of this. The law says “sons shall not be put to death for fathers” (Deuteronomy 24:16, now on these pages), and the verse does not explain itself. Readers differ. Some take it as mercy to Ahab at his son’s cost, and some point out that Ahab dies in the next chapter by the word of a prophet all the same. The ASV has “in his son’s day”, and the NIV “on his house in the days of his son”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a garden of greens”, as in Deuteronomy 11:10; “silver” for kesef; “Far be it from me, by Jehovah”; “the inheritance of my fathers”; “sullen and raging”, as at 20:43, and “sullen” again in Jezebel’s mouth; “exercise kingship”; “sons of worthlessness”; “at the head of the people”; “blessed”, where the Hebrew writes it and means the opposite, as in Job; “take possession” and “dispossessed”, one root; “murdered”, the commandment’s verb; “licked”; “sold yourself”; “anyone who urinates against the wall” and “whether shut up or set free”, as at 14:10; “burn out”; “rampart”, with the Hebrew of this verse; “incited”; “the Amorite”, in the singular; “gently”, as at 2 Samuel 18:5; and “humbled himself”. The written and read Hebrew are recorded at verse 8.

Echoes paid. Sullen and raging (1 Kings 20:43); the garden of greens (Deuteronomy 11:10); the price (1 Kings 16:24; 2 Samuel 24:24); “Far be it from me, by Jehovah” (1 Samuel 24:6; 26:11); the land that is Jehovah’s (Leviticus 25:23; Numbers 36:7); what a king will take (1 Samuel 8:14); “Rise, eat” (1 Kings 19:5); the letter (2 Samuel 11:14); two witnesses and the stones (Deuteronomy 17:5–6); sons of worthlessness (Deuteronomy 13:14); “blessed” for cursed (Job 1:5, 11); reviling God and the ruler (Exodus 22:28); “Go up, take possession” (Deuteronomy 1:21); “You shall not murder” (Exodus 20); lapping (Judges 7:5); “troubler of Israel” (1 Kings 18:17); the sentence on Jeroboam and on Baasha (1 Kings 14:10–11; 16:3–4); “incited” (1 Samuel 26:19); the nations dispossessed (1 Kings 14:24); the sackcloth of Ben-hadad’s servants (1 Kings 20:31–32); “Gently” (2 Samuel 18:5); and “Have you seen” (1 Kings 20:13).

Echoes planted. The dogs and the pool of Samaria (1 Kings 22:38); Naboth’s plot, his sons’ blood, and the son of Ahab (2 Kings 9:25–26); the portion of Jezreel (2 Kings 9:10, 36); and Jehu’s letters (2 Kings 10:1). None of these is translated here yet.
