A father's teaching to his sons: what his own father told him when he was a tender only child, acquire wisdom and with all you have acquired acquire understanding, the path of the wicked to be avoided, the path of the righteous like dawn growing to full day, guard your heart for from it are the goings-out of life, and a level track that turns neither right nor left.
Attributed to Solomon, son of David (reigned c. 970–931 BC), like the rest of the book's opening collection; wisdom literature keeps no calendar of its own.

Porque comen el pan de la maldad,

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · “Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father” — a father’s discipline, learning, and a son who was once the only one

A father’s discipline. “Hear, sons, a father’s discipline, and pay attention so as to know understanding”: chapter 1 opened with “Hear, my son, your father’s discipline” (Proverbs 1:8); here the father speaks to sons in the plural. Musar is “discipline”, and binah “understanding”. The NWT 1984 has “Listen, O sons, to the discipline of a father”; the ASV “Hear, my sons, the instruction of a father”; the KJV “Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father”.

Good learning. “For good learning I give you; do not forsake my teaching”: leqach, “learning”, is from laqach, to take, the thing a hearer takes in, as in “let the wise hear, and add to their learning” (Proverbs 1:5), and torati, “my teaching”, is the word of “do not forget my teaching” (Proverbs 3:1). The ASV has “For I give you good doctrine; Forsake ye not my law”; the NWT 1984 “For good instruction is what I certainly shall give to YOU”; the NIV “I give you sound learning, so do not forsake my teaching”.

The only one. “For I was a son to my father, tender, and the only one in the sight of my mother”: the teacher remembers being taught. Yachid, “the only one”, is the word of “your son, your only one, whom you love” (Genesis 22:2, now on these pages): not a count of siblings so much as the child who was everything to his mother. The NWT 1984 keeps it bare, “tender and the only one before my mother”; the KJV and ASV have “only beloved”; the NIV “cherished by my mother”. The Greek makes it “obedient and beloved”.

Keep and live. “Let your heart hold fast my words; keep my commandments and live”: yitmakh, “hold fast”, is to grip and not let slip. The line “keep my commandments and live” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and in the warning against the strange woman (Proverbs 7:2). The KJV has “Let thine heart retain my words: keep my commandments, and live”; the NWT 1984 “Keep my commandments and continue living”.

Verses 5–9 · “Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom” — acquire, acquire, a verse the Greek does not have, and a crown delivered

Acquire wisdom. “Acquire wisdom, acquire understanding”: qeneh is from qanah, the ordinary verb for buying, so wisdom is something you go out and get, and pay for. “Do not forsake her, and she will keep you; love her, and she will guard you”: wisdom is a woman again, as in chapters 1 and 3. The NWT 1984 has “Acquire wisdom, acquire understanding”; the KJV “Get wisdom, get understanding”.

The beginning of wisdom. “The beginning of wisdom: acquire wisdom; and with all you have acquired, acquire understanding”: reshit chokhmah is the opening of the line, and readings differ on how it joins the rest. It may be a definition, “the beginning of wisdom is this: acquire wisdom”, the first step being to want it; or reshit may mean the first in rank, “wisdom is the principal thing”. The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible; the other says “the beginning of wisdom is the fear of Jehovah” (Psalm 111:10), close to “The fear of Jehovah is the beginning of knowledge” (Proverbs 1:7). The second half spends everything: “with all you have acquired”, be-khol qinyankha, the noun of the same verb. The NIV takes the first reading, “The beginning of wisdom is this: Get wisdom”; the ASV the second, “Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom”; the NWT 1984 “Wisdom is the prime thing. Acquire wisdom; and with all that you acquire, acquire understanding”. The Greek has no verse 7 at all.

Prize her. “Prize her highly, and she will lift you up; she will honor you when you embrace her”: salseleha is a rare verb whose sense is a guess from its partner: its letters stand in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the other is a noun for vine branches (Jeremiah 6:9). The NWT 1984 has “Highly esteem it, and it will exalt you”; the ASV “Exalt her, and she will promote thee”; the NIV “Cherish her, and she will exalt you”.

A wreath and a crown. “She will give your head a wreath of grace; a crown of beauty she will deliver to you”: livyat chen, “a wreath of grace”, is the father’s teaching in “a wreath of grace for your head” (Proverbs 1:9); the phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, these two. Temaggenekka, “she will deliver to you”, is the verb of Melchizedek’s blessing, “God Most High, who has delivered your foes into your hand” (Genesis 14:20, now on these pages). The ASV has “She will give to thy head a chaplet of grace; A crown of beauty will she deliver to thee”; the NWT 1984 “To your head it will give a wreath of charm; a crown of beauty it will bestow upon you”.

Verses 10–13 · “I have led thee in paths of uprightness” — tracks of uprightness, a step not hemmed in, and discipline you do not let go

Tracks of uprightness. “Hear, my son, and receive my sayings, and the years of your life will be many. In the way of wisdom I have taught you; I have led you in tracks of uprightness”: ma’gelei-yosher, the ma’gal being a track, the rut a wheel cuts in a road, the word of “level the track of the righteous” (Isaiah 26:7). The Greek has trochiais orthais, “straight tracks”, the same wheel-word Hebrews uses (see trochia). The NWT 1984 has “I will cause you to tread in the tracks of uprightness”; the ASV “I have led thee in paths of uprightness”; the KJV “I have led thee in right paths”.

A step not hemmed in. “When you walk, your step will not be hemmed in; and if you run, you will not stumble”: lo-yetsar, “will not be narrow”, is the verb of a tight place, the opposite of open ground. The NWT 1984 has “When you walk, your pace will not be cramped”; the ASV “When thou goest, thy steps shall not be straitened”.

Do not let go. “Hold fast to discipline, do not let go; guard her, for she is your life”: al teref, “do not let go”, is the plea of the Gibeonites to Joshua, “Do not let your hands fall slack from your servants” (Joshua 10:6, now on these pages). The two words stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible. Musar is a masculine noun, but “guard her” is feminine: the pronoun reaches back to wisdom, the “her” of vv6–8. The NWT 1984 has “Take hold on discipline; do not let go”; the ASV “Take fast hold of instruction; let her not go”.

Verses 14–17 · “Enter not into the path of the wicked” — a path to avoid, sleep robbed, and the bread of wickedness

Avoid it. “Do not enter the path of the wicked, and do not walk straight on in the way of evil men. Avoid it, do not pass through it; turn aside from it, and pass on”: four short orders, as if the father were steering a boy by the shoulders. Te’asher, “walk straight on”, is a verb of going forward. The NWT 1984 has “and do not walk straight on into the way of the bad ones”; the ASV “Enter not into the path of the wicked, And walk not in the way of evil men”; the ASV goes on, “Avoid it, pass not by it; Turn from it, and pass on”.

Sleep robbed. “For they do not sleep unless they have done evil, and their sleep is robbed unless they make someone stumble”: the wicked lose sleep the way honest people do, but over the harm they have not yet done. The written text has yikhshulu, “they stumble”, the text as read yakhshilu, “they make someone stumble”. Chapter 1 had the same gang, “their feet run to evil” (Proverbs 1:16). The NWT 1984 has “and their sleep has been snatched away unless they cause someone to stumble”; the ASV “And their sleep is taken away, unless they cause some to fall”.

The bread of wickedness. “For they eat the bread of wickedness, and drink the wine of violence”: chamasim, “violence”, is plural in the Hebrew, acts of violence. Their food and drink are paid for with harm, or are the harm itself. The ASV has “For they eat the bread of wickedness, And drink the wine of violence”; the NWT 1984 “and the wine of acts of violence is what they drink”.

Verses 18–19 · “The path of the just is as the shining light, that shineth more and more unto the perfect day” — dawn, and deep darkness

The light of dawn. “But the path of the righteous is like the light of dawn, growing brighter and brighter until the day is full”: or nogah, the light of brightness, the first gleam; holekh va-or, “going and shining”, keeps getting lighter; and ad nekhon ha-yom, “until the day is firm”, is the height of noon. The phrase stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The ASV has “But the path of the righteous is as the dawning light, That shineth more and more unto the perfect day”; the NWT 1984 “like the bright light that is getting lighter and lighter until the day is firmly established”.

Deep darkness. “The way of the wicked is like deep darkness; they do not know over what they stumble”: afelah is the thick dark of the plague in Egypt, “a darkness of thick gloom in all the land of Egypt, three days” (Exodus 10:22, now on these pages), and of the covenant curse, “groping at noon as the blind man gropes in the dark” (Deuteronomy 28:29). The righteous walk toward noon; the wicked grope in the dark even at noon. The ASV has “The way of the wicked is as darkness: They know not at what they stumble”; the NWT 1984 “The way of the wicked ones is like the gloom”.

Verses 20–23 · “Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life” — words kept in sight, healing, and the goings-out of life

Kept in sight. “My son, attend to my words; incline your ear to my sayings. Let them not slip from your eyes; keep them in the middle of your heart”: al yalizu, “let them not slip”, is the verb of “let them not slip from your eyes” in Proverbs 3:21. The NWT 1984 has “May they not get away from your eyes”; the ASV “Let them not depart from thine eyes”.

Life and healing. “For they are life to those who find them, and healing to all their flesh”: marpe, “healing”, is from rafa, to heal; the body is included in what wisdom does. The ASV has “For they are life unto those that find them, And health to all their flesh”.

The goings-out of life. “Above all that you guard, guard your heart, for from it are the goings-out of life”: mi-kol mishmar, “above all guarding”, more than any other thing kept under watch. Totse’ot, “goings-out”, is the word Numbers uses for where a border runs out and ends, “and its goings-out shall be at the Sea” (Numbers 34:5, now on these pages): the heart is where life’s roads start and where they reach. The KJV has “Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life”; the NWT 1984 “More than all else that is to be guarded, safeguard your heart, for out of it are the sources of life”; the NIV “for everything you do flows from it”.

Verses 24–27 · “Ponder the path of thy feet” — a crooked mouth, eyes ahead, a level track, and neither right nor left

Mouth, eyes, feet. “Put away from you crookedness of mouth, and put deviousness of lips far from you”: iqqeshut, “crookedness”, is from the root of “the crooked” in “Thorns and traps are in the way of the crooked” (Proverbs 22:5). “Let your eyes look straight ahead, and your eyelids look right in front of you”: from the heart of v23 the father goes outward, mouth, eyes, feet. The NWT 1984 has “Remove from yourself the crookedness of speech”; the ASV “Put away from thee a wayward mouth”; the ASV again, “Let thine eyes look right on”.

Level the track. “Level the track of your foot, and all your ways will be established”: palles, “level”, is the verb of “you, Upright One, level the track of the righteous” (Isaiah 26:7); here the son is told to do it himself. The Greek has “make straight tracks for your feet”, and that is the line Hebrews 12:13 quotes, “and make straight tracks for your feet”. The ASV has “Make level the path of thy feet”; the NWT 1984 “Smooth out the course of your foot”; the KJV “Ponder the path of thy feet”.

Neither right nor left. “Do not turn to the right or to the left; turn your foot away from evil”: the idiom of the law, the king who must not “turn aside from the commandment, right or left” (Deuteronomy 17:20, now on these pages). The Greek adds two lines the Hebrew does not have: “for God knows the ways on the right, but those on the left are crooked; and he will make your tracks straight, and lead your journeys forward in peace”. The Douay keeps them: “For the Lord knoweth the ways that are on the right hand: but those are perverse which are on the left hand”. The ASV has “Turn not to the right hand nor to the left: Remove thy foot from evil”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “discipline” for musar; “learning” for leqach, as in 1:5, and “teaching” for torah, as in 3:1; “the only one” for yachid, as in Genesis 22:2; “acquire” for every qanah; “a wreath of grace”, as in 1:9; “deliver” for miggen, as in Genesis 14:20; “tracks” for ma’gal, as in Isaiah 26:7, “path” for orach and “way” for derekh; “do not let go” for al teref; “keep” for shamar and “guard” for natsar; “deep darkness” for afelah; “goings-out” for totse’ot, as in Numbers 34; and “level” for palles.

Echoes paid. The father’s discipline of 1:8 and the learning of 1:5; the teaching of 3:1 and the words that must not slip of 3:21; the wreath of grace of 1:9; the beginning of knowledge of 1:7; the gang whose feet run to evil in 1:16; the only son of Genesis 22:2; Melchizedek’s “delivered” in Genesis 14:20; the Gibeonites’ “do not let your hands fall slack” in Joshua 10:6; the darkness of Exodus 10:22 and Deuteronomy 28:29; the border’s goings-out in Numbers 34:5; the crooked of 22:5; the level track of Isaiah 26:7; the right or left of Deuteronomy 17:20; and the straight tracks of Hebrews 12:13.

Echoes planted. “Keep my commandments and live” said again before the strange woman (Proverbs 7:2); the beginning of wisdom as the fear of Jehovah (Psalm 111:10); and the vine branches of Jeremiah 6:9. None is translated here yet.
