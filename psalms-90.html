A prayer headed “of Moses, the man of God”: Jehovah is the dwelling of every generation, older than the mountains; man is turned back to crushing and the days are grass that passes by evening; our hidden things stand in the light of his face; seventy years, or eighty; a plea to number our days, and for Jehovah to return and relent, to satisfy us in the morning, and to establish the work of our hands.
Undated. The heading names Moses and no occasion, and the psalm names none; it is filed here, beside the other Moses texts, as a psalm the heading attributes to the wilderness years, not one the text dates. ⚠ Whether Moses wrote it is a question the Hebrew does not answer, only that the prayer is “of” him.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Psalm 90:1–2 “Lord, you have been a dwelling place for us” — a prayer of Moses, and a God older than the mountains

⭐ “Of Moses the man of God”. The heading is tefillah lemoshe ish ha’elohim, “a prayer, of Moses the man of God”. “Moses the man of God” stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 33:1, where it opens Moses’ last blessing (Deuteronomy 33:1, now on these pages), then Joshua 14:6, 1 Chronicles 23:14, 2 Chronicles 30:16, this verse and Ezra 3:2. The Hebrew does not say that Moses wrote it, only that it is of him, with the same lamed that stands in front of David’s name in his headings. The TLB “A prayer of Moses, the man of God”, and the Douay “A prayer of Moses the man of God”. The heading and the first line are one verse in the Hebrew, as at Psalm 130:1, so the verse numbers here are the shelf’s.

⭐ “A dwelling place”. Ma’on is a dwelling, a home (dictionary), and “a dwelling place are you” stands in this one verse. It is the word Moses uses in his last blessing, “a dwelling place is the God of old” (Deuteronomy 33:27, now on these pages), and the one the next psalm has, “you have made the Most High your dwelling” (Psalm 91:9, now on these pages). The first line sets people in a home that is God, in the generations that follow, before the psalm turns to what the generations are. The KJV has “our dwelling place”, the ASV “our dwelling-place”, the NIV “our dwelling place”, the NWT 1984 “a real dwelling for us”, the TLB “our home” and the Douay “our refuge”. The name of God in the line is Adonai, “the Lord”; the name Jehovah does not come until verse 13.

⭐ Born, and in labor. “Before the mountains were born”: the clause stands in this one verse, with the mountains as something that has a birth, and the next clause continues the picture, vatecholel, from chul, to writhe, to be in labor, with “the earth and the world” (tevel). Who is in labor is not settled by the grammar, God or the earth, and the shelf splits on it: the KJV has “or ever thou hadst formed the earth and the world”, the NIV “or you brought forth the whole world”, the NWT 1984 “you proceeded to bring forth as with labor pains the earth and the productive land” and the TLB “before the earth was formed”. The end of the verse is a phrase that stands once: “from everlasting to everlasting” in this form stands in this one verse, me’olam ad olam; the neighbors have a longer form, “from everlasting and to everlasting” with a vav (Psalm 103:17, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “from time indefinite to time indefinite you are God” and the Douay “from eternity and to eternity thou art God”.

Verses 3–6 · Psalm 90:3–6 “You turn man back to crushing” — a thousand years like yesterday, and grass that passes

⭐ “Return, sons of man”. Tashev enosh ad dakka: you turn man back to dakka, crushed matter, crushing (dictionary); “to crushing” stands in this one verse. The verb, shuvu, “return”, is the verb of Eden: “for dust you are, and to dust you will return” (Genesis 3:19, now on these pages), and “for dust you are” stands in that one verse. The KJV has “Thou turnest man to destruction”, the Geneva “Thou turnest man to destruction”, the NIV “You turn people back to dust”, the NWT 1984 “You make mortal man go back to crushed matter” and the TLB “You speak, and man turns back to dust”. The Douay, from the Latin, reverses it: “Turn not man away to be brought low”. “Return, sons of man” stands in this one verse.

⭐ A thousand years, and a watch. “A thousand years in your eyes are like yesterday when it passes, and a watch in the night”: the word ashmurah, “and a watch”, stands in this one verse (dictionary), a turn of the guard, the same keeping as the watchmen of Psalm 130:6 (now on these pages). The sum is turned around in the letter of Peter, “with the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day” (2 Peter 3:8, now on these pages). The KJV has “as a watch in the night”, the NIV “like a watch in the night”, the NWT 1984 “as a watch during the night” and the Douay “as a watch in the night”. The TLB has “They are like a single hour!”.

⭐ Swept away, and grass. Zeramtam, “you sweep them away”, is a flood word, and the form stands in two verses, here and Ezekiel 23:20, where it is used of a very different flow; the KJV has “Thou carriest them away as with a flood”, the NWT 1984 “You have swept them away”, the Geneva “Thou hast overflowed them” and the NIV “you sweep people away in the sleep of death”. The sleep and the morning grass: “like grass that passes on” stands in this one verse, and the same grass of the morning is in Isaiah 40:6–8 and Psalm 103:15 (both now on these pages). Verse 6 repeats the day in miniature, “in the morning it blossoms … at evening it is cut down”. The KJV has “in the morning it flourisheth, and groweth up; in the evening it is cut down, and withereth”, the NWT 1984 “At evening it withers and certainly dries up” and the Douay “in the evening he shall fall, grow dry, and wither”.

Verses 7–9 · Psalm 90:7–9 “consumed by your anger” — hidden things in the light of your face, and years like a sigh

⭐ Consumed, and finished. Verse 7 and verse 9 have the same verb in two forms: kalinu, “we are consumed”, and killinu, “we finish”; the consonants are the same, and the psalm sets the people’s end beside the anger in verse 7 and the people’s own finishing in verse 9. The KJV has “we are consumed by thine anger”, the NWT 1984 “we have come to an end in your anger”, the TLB “We die beneath your anger” and the Douay “in thy wrath we have fainted away”.

⭐ “Our hidden things”. Alumenu: the word stands in this one verse (dictionary), from alam, to hide, and it is the secret things, set “in the light of your face”, limeor panekha (dictionary). The first line has a written and a read pair: the Hebrew prints shat and then shattah, “you set”, the written form without its final letter; this is a ketiv/qere pair, kept in the Hebrew column. The KJV has “our secret sins in the light of thy countenance”, the NIV “our secret sins in the light of your presence”, the NWT 1984 “Our hidden things before your bright face” and the TLB “You spread out our sins before you—our secret sins—and see them all”. The Douay has “our life in the light of thy countenance”.

⭐ “Like a sigh”. Kemo hegeh: “like a sigh” stands in this one verse (dictionary), the sound of breath, a murmur, a moan. Six renderings, none alike: the KJV “as a tale that is told”, the Geneva “as a thought”, the NIV “with a moan”, the NWT 1984 “just like a whisper”, the TLB “All our days are filled with sighing” and the Douay “as a spider”.

Verse 10 · Psalm 90:10 “seventy years, and if by strength, eighty”

⭐ Seventy. “The days of our years in them are seventy years”: “the days of our years” stands in this one verse. “Seventy years” stands in fifteen verses of the Hebrew Bible. Besides this one, they are ages in Genesis (5:12, 5:31, 11:26, 12:4 and 25:7), a sentence on Tyre (Isaiah 23:15 and 23:17), and the seventy years of the exile (Jeremiah 25:11–12, now on these pages, Jeremiah 29:10, Zechariah 1:12, Zechariah 7:5, 2 Chronicles 36:21 and Daniel 9:2). So the figure a person lives and the figure a nation waits are the same word.

⭐ “Their pride is toil and wickedness”. The second half is a puzzle line: verahbam amal va’aven; “and their pride” stands in this one verse, and the sum is “toil and wickedness”, or trouble and sorrow, and the verse closes, “for it is cut off quickly” stands in this one verse, gaz chish, and “we fly away”. The KJV has “threescore years and ten”, “yet is their strength labour and sorrow” and “it is soon cut off, and we fly away”; the NIV has “yet the best of them are but trouble and sorrow”; the NWT 1984 “Yet their insistence is on trouble and hurtful things”; the Geneva “yet their strength is but labor and sorrow”; the TLB “But even the best of these years are often empty and filled with pain”; and the Douay “and what is more of them is labour and sorrow”.

Verses 11–12 · Psalm 90:11–12 “To number our days, so make known” — a heart of wisdom

⭐ “Who knows?” Verse 11 asks, mi yode’a oz apekha, “who knows the strength of your anger”, and the second half measures it by fear, “and your wrath according to the fear of you”. The KJV has “Who knoweth the power of thine anger?”, the NIV “If only we knew the power of your anger!”, the NWT 1984 “Who is there knowing the strength of your anger” and the TLB “Who can realize the terrors of your anger?”.

⭐ “To number our days”. “to number our days” stands in this one verse, limnot yamenu, and “a heart of wisdom” stands in this one verse, levav chokhmah. The verb that asks God to teach is hoda, “make known”, and the Hebrew puts the counting first and the asking after it. The KJV has “So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom”, the NIV “Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom”, the NWT 1984 “Show us just how to count our days in such a way that we may bring a heart of wisdom in”, the Geneva “Teach us so to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom” and the TLB “Teach us to number our days and recognize how few they are”. The Douay, which carries the Latin over verses 11 and 12, has “Can number thy wrath? So make thy right hand known: and men learned in heart, in wisdom”.

Verses 13–15 · Psalm 90:13–15 “Return, Jehovah! How long?” — relent, and satisfy us in the morning

⭐ The name, once. The name Jehovah is in this psalm once, here. The line is “return, Jehovah” in four verses of the Hebrew Bible, Numbers 10:36, Psalm 6:5, this verse and Psalm 126:4. The plea that follows, “and relent concerning” stands in two verses, this one and Moses’ own plea at the golden calf, “Turn from your burning anger and relent concerning the evil against your people” (Exodus 32:12, now on these pages). The prayer of Moses uses the words of Moses. The KJV has “Return, O Lord, how long? and let it repent thee concerning thy servants”, the NIV “Relent, Lord! How long will it be? Have compassion on your servants”, the NWT 1984 “Do return, O Jehovah! How long will it be? And feel regret over your servants”, the Geneva “and be pacified toward thy servants” and the TLB “come and bless us! How long will you delay?”.

⭐ “In the morning”. The grass was green “in the morning” (verses 5 and 6); now the prayer asks for the morning back: “satisfy us in the morning with your steadfast love” stands in this one verse, with chesed (dictionary), and then the gladness of verse 15 is measured against the affliction, “make us glad as the days” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “O satisfy us early with thy mercy”, the NIV “Satisfy us in the morning with your unfailing love”, the NWT 1984 “Satisfy us in the morning with your loving-kindness”, the Geneva “Fill us with thy mercy in the morning” and the TLB “Satisfy us in our earliest youth with your loving-kindness”. For the second, the KJV has “Make us glad according to the days wherein thou hast afflicted us” and the TLB “Give us gladness in proportion to our former misery!”.

Verses 16–17 · Psalm 90:16–17 “Let your work be seen” — and the work of our hands, establish it

⭐ Work, and the work of our hands. God’s po’olekha, “your work”, and his hadarkha, “your splendor”, are to be seen by the servants and upon their children; then the psalm turns to the work of the people. “The favor of the Lord” stands in this one verse, no’am, pleasantness, favor (dictionary), and the last two lines say the same thing twice: “and the work of our hands establish” stands in this one verse, and then again, “and the work of our hands, establish it”. The KJV has “let the beauty of the Lord our God be upon us”, the NIV “May the favor of the Lord our God rest on us”, the NWT 1984 “let the pleasantness of Jehovah our God prove to be upon us”, the Geneva “let the beauty of the Lord our God be upon us” and the Douay “let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us”. The TLB has “let the Lord our God favor us and give us success”. For the work, the KJV has “establish thou the work of our hands upon us”, the Geneva “direct thou the work of our hands”, the NWT 1984 “do you firmly establish” and the Douay “direct thou the works of our hands over us”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a dwelling place” for ma’on; “crushing” for dakka; “iniquities” for avonot, as at Psalm 130:3; “steadfast love” for chesed; “relent” for nacham, as at Exodus 32:12; “the Lord” for Adonai and “Jehovah” for the name. The Hebrew has one written and read pair in this psalm, shat and shattah in verse 8, kept as it is printed.

Echoes paid. The blessing of Moses the man of God (Deuteronomy 33:1 and 33:27); the dust of Genesis 3:19; the dwelling of Psalm 91:9 and the everlasting of Psalm 103:17; the grass of Isaiah 40:6–8 and Psalm 103:15; the watchmen of Psalm 130:6; the thousand years of 2 Peter 3:8; the seventy years of Jeremiah 25:11–12; and the relenting of Exodus 32:12.

Echoes planted. The other “Moses the man of God” verses (Joshua 14:6, 1 Chronicles 23:14, 2 Chronicles 30:16, Ezra 3:2); the flood of Ezekiel 23:20; the other “return, Jehovah” pleas (Numbers 10:36, Psalm 6:5, Psalm 126:4); and the other seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10, Zechariah 1:12, Zechariah 7:5, 2 Chronicles 36:21, Daniel 9:2). None of these is translated here yet.
