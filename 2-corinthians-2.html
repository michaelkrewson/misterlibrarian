Paul writes from Macedonia after a visit to Corinth that went badly, and after the trip to Troas where he could not rest without Titus. He explains why he did not come in sorrow, asks the congregation to forgive the one who caused grief, and speaks of God leading the apostles in triumph.
Undated by the text: the chapter names no year. Its time-markers are the planned visit (v1), the letter written with tears (v4), and the arrival at Troas followed by the move into Macedonia (vv12–13).

2 Corinthians 2 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
Ἔκρινα γὰρ ἐμαυτῷ τοῦτο, τὸ μὴ πάλιν ἐν λύπῃ πρὸς ὑμᾶς ἐλθεῖν·
For I decided this for myself, not to come to you again in sorrow.note
Porque decidí para mí mismo no volver a visitarlos con tristeza.
2
εἰ γὰρ ἐγὼ λυπῶ ὑμᾶς, καὶ τίς ὁ εὐφραίνων με εἰ μὴ ὁ λυπούμενος ἐξ ἐμοῦ;
For if I cause you sorrow, who is there to make me glad except the one I have grieved?note
Porque si yo los entristezco, ¿quién me alegrará, sino el que yo entristecí?
3
καὶ ἔγραψα τοῦτο αὐτὸ ἵνα μὴ ἐλθὼν λύπην σχῶ ἀφʼ ὧν ἔδει με χαίρειν, πεποιθὼς ἐπὶ πάντας ὑμᾶς ὅτι ἡ ἐμὴ χαρὰ πάντων ὑμῶν ἐστιν.
I wrote this very thing so that when I came I would not have sorrow from those who ought to bring me joy, since I am confident about all of you, that my joy is the joy of all of you.note
Esto mismo les escribí para que, al llegar, no tenga tristeza por los que debían darme gozo, confiado en todos ustedes, de que mi gozo es el gozo de todos ustedes.
4
ἐκ γὰρ πολλῆς θλίψεως καὶ συνοχῆς καρδίας ἔγραψα ὑμῖν διὰ πολλῶν δακρύων, οὐχ ἵνα λυπηθῆτε, ἀλλὰ τὴν ἀγάπην ἵνα γνῶτε ἣν ἔχω περισσοτέρως εἰς ὑμᾶς.
For out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you with many tears, not so that you would be grieved, but so that you would know the love I have for you in abundance.note
Porque, con mucha aflicción y angustia de corazón, les escribí con muchas lágrimas; no para que se entristecieran, sino para que conocieran el amor que tengo por ustedes, que es mayor.
5
Εἰ δέ τις λελύπηκεν, οὐκ ἐμὲ λελύπηκεν, ἀλλὰ ἀπὸ μέρους ἵνα μὴ ἐπιβαρῶ πάντας ὑμᾶς.
But if anyone has caused sorrow, he has caused it not to me, but in some measure—so that I do not overburden all of you.note
Y si alguien ha causado tristeza, no me ha entristecido a mí, sino en parte, para no abrumar a todos ustedes.
6
ἱκανὸν τῷ τοιούτῳ ἡ ἐπιτιμία αὕτη ἡ ὑπὸ τῶν πλειόνων,
The punishment inflicted by the majority is enough for such a person.note
Para tal persona basta esta reprensión dada por la mayoría.
7
ὥστε τοὐναντίον μᾶλλον ὑμᾶς χαρίσασθαι καὶ παρακαλέσαι, μή πως τῇ περισσοτέρᾳ λύπῃ καταποθῇ ὁ τοιοῦτος.
So instead you should rather forgive him and comfort him, so that such a person is not swallowed up by excessive sorrow.note
Más bien, al contrario, deben perdonarlo y consolarlo, para que no sea tragado por una tristeza excesiva.
8
διὸ παρακαλῶ ὑμᾶς κυρῶσαι εἰς αὐτὸν ἀγάπην·
So I urge you to confirm your love for him.note
Por eso les ruego que confirmen su amor hacia él.
9
εἰς τοῦτο γὰρ καὶ ἔγραψα ἵνα γνῶ τὴν δοκιμὴν ὑμῶν, εἰ εἰς πάντα ὑπήκοοί ἐστε.
For this is why I also wrote: to know whether you are proved and obedient in everything.note
Pues para esto también les escribí: para conocer si demuestran ser obedientes en todo.
10
ᾧ δέ τι χαρίζεσθε, κἀγώ· καὶ γὰρ ἐγὼ ὃ κεχάρισμαι, εἴ τι κεχάρισμαι, διʼ ὑμᾶς ἐν προσώπῳ Χριστοῦ,
Whomever you forgive anything, I also forgive. For what I have forgiven, if I have forgiven anything, I did for your sake in the presence of Christ,note
A quien ustedes perdonen algo, yo también lo perdono. Porque lo que yo haya perdonado, si es que he perdonado algo, lo hice por ustedes en presencia de Cristo,
11
ἵνα μὴ πλεονεκτηθῶμεν ὑπὸ τοῦ Σατανᾶ, οὐ γὰρ αὐτοῦ τὰ νοήματα ἀγνοοῦμεν.
so that we may not be taken advantage of by Satan, for we are not ignorant of his schemes.note
para que Satanás no nos saque ventaja, pues no ignoramos sus propósitos.
12
Ἐλθὼν δὲ εἰς τὴν Τρῳάδα εἰς τὸ εὐαγγέλιον τοῦ Χριστοῦ καὶ θύρας μοι ἀνεῳγμένης ἐν κυρίῳ,
When I came to Troas to preach the gospel of Christ, and a door was opened to me in the Lord,note
Cuando llegué a Troas para anunciar las buenas noticias de Cristo, y se me abrió una puerta en el Señor,
13
οὐκ ἔσχηκα ἄνεσιν τῷ πνεύματί μου τῷ μὴ εὑρεῖν με Τίτον τὸν ἀδελφόν μου, ἀλλὰ ἀποταξάμενος αὐτοῖς ἐξῆλθον εἰς Μακεδονίαν.
my spirit had no rest, because I did not find Titus my brother there. So I said goodbye to them and went on to Macedonia.note
mi espíritu no tuvo descanso, porque no encontré a mi hermano Tito. Así que me despedí de ellos y partí hacia Macedonia.
14
Τῷ δὲ θεῷ χάρις τῷ πάντοτε θριαμβεύοντι ἡμᾶς ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ καὶ τὴν ὀσμὴν τῆς γνώσεως αὐτοῦ φανεροῦντι διʼ ἡμῶν ἐν παντὶ τόπῳ·
But thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumph in Christ and through us spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of him in every place.note
Pero gracias a Dios, que siempre nos lleva en triunfo en Cristo y por medio de nosotros esparce en todo lugar el aroma del conocimiento de él.
15
ὅτι Χριστοῦ εὐωδία ἐσμὲν τῷ θεῷ ἐν τοῖς σῳζομένοις καὶ ἐν τοῖς ἀπολλυμένοις,
For we are the fragrance of Christ to God, among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing.note
Porque para Dios somos el aroma de Cristo, entre los que se salvan y entre los que se pierden.
16
οἷς μὲν ὀσμὴ ἐκ θανάτου εἰς θάνατον, οἷς δὲ ὀσμὴ ἐκ ζωῆς εἰς ζωήν. καὶ πρὸς ταῦτα τίς ἱκανός;
To the one group an aroma from death to death; to the other an aroma from life to life. And who is sufficient for these things?note
Para unos, olor de muerte que lleva a la muerte; para otros, aroma de vida que lleva a la vida. ¿Y quién es suficiente para estas cosas?
17
οὐ γάρ ἐσμεν ὡς οἱ πολλοὶ καπηλεύοντες τὸν λόγον τοῦ θεοῦ, ἀλλʼ ὡς ἐξ εἰλικρινείας, ἀλλʼ ὡς ἐκ θεοῦ κατέναντι θεοῦ ἐν Χριστῷ λαλοῦμεν.
For we are not like the many who peddle the word of God, but as those who speak in sincerity, as from God, before God, in Christ.note
Porque no somos como muchos, que trafican con la palabra de Dios, sino que hablamos con sinceridad, como enviados de Dios, delante de Dios y en Cristo.
Verses 1–4 · the visit not made in sorrow, and the letter written with tears

“I decided this for myself.” ekrina gar emautō touto, I decided this for myself. The verb krinō is the verb of judging a matter, and Paul says he has judged that he will not come again in sorrow. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For I have made up my mind not to come to you again in sadness.” The KJV “But I determined this with myself, that I would not come again to you in heaviness.”

“I wrote with many tears.” ek gar pollēs thlipseōs kai synochēs kardias egrapsa hymin dia pollōn dakrywn, out of much affliction and anguish of heart I wrote to you with many tears. The sentence refers to an earlier letter. Commentators debate which letter this is, and whether it is part of 1 Corinthians or a lost letter; that question is reported here, not settled, and the translation keeps Paul’s words. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For out of much tribulation and anguish of heart I wrote you with many tears, not to sadden you, but to let you know the depth of love I have for you.”

“To know the love I have.” tēn agapēn hina gnōte, the love so that you may know. The noun agapē is the word of 1 Corinthians 13, and Paul says the letter was written so the Corinthians would know it. The shelf: the KJV “not that ye should be grieved, but that ye might know the love which I have more abundantly unto you.”

Verses 5–11 · the one who caused sorrow, the rebuke of the majority, forgiveness and comfort, and Satan’s advantage

“If anyone has caused sorrow.” ei de tis lelypēken, if anyone has caused sorrow. The sentence does not name the person, and the note keeps him unnamed. The grief is said to fall on the whole congregation, not on Paul, and the sentence says so to avoid overburdening them. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now if anyone has caused sadness, he has saddened, not me, but all of you to an extent—not to be too harsh in what I say.”

“Forgive and comfort him.” charisasthai kai parakalesai, to forgive and to comfort. The verbs are the verbs of grace and of encouragement, and the sentence says the rebuke has been enough and the reversal is now needed. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “Ahora deben perdonarlo bondadosamente y consolarlo para que no se sienta abrumado por estar demasiado triste.”

“Satan.” hina mē pleonekthōmen hypo tou Satana, so that we are not taken advantage of by Satan. The word is a transliteration of the Hebrew for adversary, used here of the adversary of the congregation. The shelf: the KJV “Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.”

Verses 12–13 · Troas, the open door, and the missing Titus

“A door was opened to me.” thyras moi aneōgmenēs en kyriō, a door was opened to me in the Lord. The door is the same image as 1 Corinthians 16:9, and the sentence says it was open in Troas. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now when I arrived in Troʹas to declare the good news about the Christ and a door was opened to me in the Lord.”

“My spirit had no rest.” ouk eschēka anesin tō pneumati mou, my spirit had no rest. The noun anesis is relief, and the sentence says Paul could not rest because Titus was not there. The shelf: the KJV “I had no rest in my spirit, because I found not Titus my brother.”

Verses 14–17 · the triumph in Christ, the fragrance to some for death and to others for life, and the peddlers of the word

“Leads us in triumph.” thriambeuonti, leads in triumph. The verb is the word of a Roman general’s parade through the city, and Paul uses it for God leading the apostles. The sentence then names the aroma: the knowledge of Christ spread through them in every place. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “who always leads us in a triumphal procession in company with the Christ and through us spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of him in every place!” The KJV “which always causeth us to triumph in Christ, and maketh manifest the savour of his knowledge by us in every place.”

“An aroma from death to death.” osmē ek thanatou eis thanaton, an aroma from death to death, and to the others from life to life. The same fragrance has opposite effects on those who are saved and those who perish. The shelf: the TNM 2019 “Para estos últimos es un olor de muerte que lleva a la muerte, pero para los primeros es un aroma de vida que lleva a la vida.”

“Peddle the word of God.” kapēleuontes ton logon tou theou, peddling the word of God. The verb is the verb of a shopkeeper who sells for profit, and Paul sets his own sincerity against it. The shelf: the KJV “For we are not as many, which corrupt the word of God: but as of sincerity, but as of God, in the sight of God speak we in Christ.” The NWT 2013 “we are not peddlers of the word of God as many men are” keeps the verb.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “sorrow” for lypē at 2:1–7; “forgive” for charizomai at 2:7 and 2:10; “aroma” for osmē at 2:14–16; “peddle” for kapēleuō at 2:17, the word of the trader.

Echoes paid. The door of 1 Corinthians 16:9 returns in Troas (2:12); the love of 1 Corinthians 13 returns as the love the Corinthians are asked to confirm (2:8); the judgment of 1 Corinthians 5 returns as the rebuke of the majority (2:6); and the Satan of 1 Corinthians 5:5 returns in 2:11.

Echoes planted. Who is sufficient (2:16) returns in chapter 3, where Paul says that sufficiency is from God (3:5).

2 Corinthians 3, already on these pages. Paul turns from the triumph to the letter of Christ written on hearts.
