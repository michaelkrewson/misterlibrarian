Ai falls a second time, by ambush rather than by miracle: thirty thousand men sent by night, a decoy force that lets itself be beaten and flees toward the wilderness, the whole garrison drawn out and the city taken and burned behind it. The king is hanged until evening and buried under a heap of stones at his own gate; the city itself is made a permanent ruin. Joshua then leads the nation north to Shechem and builds the altar Deuteronomy 27 legislated on Mount Ebal, writes a copy of the law on its stones, and has half of Israel stand facing Gerizim and half facing Ebal while every word of the law -- blessing and curse -- is read aloud to the whole assembly, women, children and resident aliens included.
Directly continuous with chapter 7: the campaign against Ai resumes once Achan is removed, still in the early weeks after Jericho's fall and the Gilgal Passover (5:10). The march to Shechem for the altar ceremony is a real journey (Ebal and Gerizim sit roughly 30 miles/45 km north of Gilgal) that the text does not date separately from the Ai campaign it follows; some harmonizers (following the Talmud, Sotah 36a) compress it into the same day, which this page's own notes report without adopting. Same contested early/late-date question as chapters 1-7 (c. 1406 BC on the early date, c. 1230-1210 BC on the late).

Joshua 8 — The Mister Translation

1
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, אַל-תִּירָא וְאַל-תֵּחָת--קַח עִמְּךָ אֵת כָּל-עַם הַמִּלְחָמָה, וְקוּם עֲלֵה הָעָי; רְאֵה נָתַתִּי בְיָדְךָ, אֶת-מֶלֶךְ הָעַי וְאֶת-עַמּוֹ, וְאֶת-עִירוֹ, וְאֶת-אַרְצוֹ.
And Jehovah said to Joshua: ‘Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed. Take all the people of war with you, and rise, go up to Ai. See — I have given into your hand the king of Ai, and his people, and his city, and his land.note
Y Jehová dijo a Josué: «No tengas miedo ni te acobardes. Toma contigo a toda la gente de guerra, levántate y sube a Hai. Mira — he entregado en tu mano al rey de Hai, y a su pueblo, y a su ciudad, y a su tierra.
2
וְעָשִׂיתָ לָעַי וּלְמַלְכָּהּ, כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשִׂיתָ לִירִיחוֹ וּלְמַלְכָּהּ--רַק-שְׁלָלָהּ וּבְהֶמְתָּהּ, תָּבֹזּוּ לָכֶם; שִׂים-לְךָ אֹרֵב לָעִיר, מֵאַחֲרֶיהָ.
And you shall do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king — only its spoil and its livestock you may take as plunder for yourselves. Set an ambush for the city, behind it.’note
Y harás a Hai y a su rey como hiciste a Jericó y a su rey — solo que su botín y su ganado tomaréis para vosotros como presa. Pon una emboscada a la ciudad, por detrás de ella».
3
וַיָּקָם יְהוֹשֻׁעַ וְכָל-עַם הַמִּלְחָמָה, לַעֲלוֹת הָעָי; וַיִּבְחַר יְהוֹשֻׁעַ שְׁלֹשִׁים אֶלֶף אִישׁ, גִּבּוֹרֵי הַחַיִל, וַיִּשְׁלָחֵם, לָיְלָה.
So Joshua rose, and all the people of war, to go up to Ai; and Joshua chose thirty thousand men, valiant warriors, and sent them out by night.note
Y se levantó Josué, y toda la gente de guerra, para subir a Hai; y escogió Josué a treinta mil hombres, guerreros valientes, y los envió de noche.
4
וַיְצַו אֹתָם לֵאמֹר, רְאוּ אַתֶּם אֹרְבִים לָעִיר מֵאַחֲרֵי הָעִיר--אַל-תַּרְחִיקוּ מִן-הָעִיר, מְאֹד; וִהְיִיתֶם כֻּלְּכֶם, נְכֹנִים.
And he commanded them, saying: ‘Look — you are to lie in ambush against the city, behind the city. Do not go very far from the city, and all of you be ready.note
Y les mandó, diciendo: «Mirad — vosotros vais a poneros en emboscada contra la ciudad, por detrás de la ciudad. No os alejéis mucho de la ciudad, y estad todos preparados.
5
וַאֲנִי, וְכָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אִתִּי, נִקְרַב, אֶל-הָעִיר; וְהָיָה, כִּי-יֵצְאוּ לִקְרָאתֵנוּ כַּאֲשֶׁר בָּרִאשֹׁנָה, וְנַסְנוּ, לִפְנֵיהֶם.
And I, and all the people who are with me, will approach the city; and when they come out against us, as at first, we will flee before them.note
Y yo, y todo el pueblo que está conmigo, nos acercaremos a la ciudad; y cuando salgan a nuestro encuentro, como la primera vez, huiremos delante de ellos.
6
וְיָצְאוּ אַחֲרֵינוּ, עַד הַתִּיקֵנוּ אוֹתָם מִן-הָעִיר--כִּי יֹאמְרוּ, נָסִים לְפָנֵינוּ כַּאֲשֶׁר בָּרִאשֹׁנָה; וְנַסְנוּ, לִפְנֵיהֶם.
And they will come out after us, until we have drawn them away from the city — for they will say, “They are fleeing before us, as at first” — and we will flee before them.note
Y saldrán tras nosotros, hasta que los hayamos alejado de la ciudad — porque dirán: “Huyen delante de nosotros, como la primera vez” — y huiremos delante de ellos.
7
וְאַתֶּם, תָּקֻמוּ מֵהָאוֹרֵב, וְהוֹרַשְׁתֶּם, אֶת-הָעִיר; וּנְתָנָהּ יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם, בְּיֶדְכֶם.
Then you shall rise up from the ambush, and take possession of the city; and Jehovah your God will give it into your hand.note
Entonces os levantaréis de la emboscada, y tomaréis posesión de la ciudad; y Jehová vuestro Dios la entregará en vuestra mano.
8
וְהָיָה כְּתָפְשְׂכֶם אֶת-הָעִיר, תַּצִּיתוּ אֶת-הָעִיר בָּאֵשׁ--כִּדְבַר יְהוָה, תַּעֲשׂוּ; רְאוּ, צִוִּיתִי אֶתְכֶם.
And when you have seized the city, you shall set the city on fire. According to the word of Jehovah you shall do. See — I have commanded you.’note
Y cuando hayáis tomado la ciudad, le prenderéis fuego. Conforme a la palabra de Jehová haréis. Mirad — os lo he mandado».
9
וַיִּשְׁלָחֵם יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, וַיֵּלְכוּ אֶל-הַמַּאְרָב, וַיֵּשְׁבוּ בֵּין בֵּית-אֵל וּבֵין הָעַי, מִיָּם לָעָי; וַיָּלֶן יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא, בְּתוֹךְ הָעָם.
So Joshua sent them out, and they went to the ambush, and stayed between Bethel and Ai, west of Ai; but Joshua lodged that night among the people.note
Y Josué los envió, y fueron a la emboscada, y se quedaron entre Betel y Hai, al occidente de Hai; pero Josué pasó aquella noche en medio del pueblo.
10
וַיַּשְׁכֵּם יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בַּבֹּקֶר, וַיִּפְקֹד אֶת-הָעָם; וַיַּעַל הוּא וְזִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, לִפְנֵי הָעָם--הָעָי.
And Joshua rose early in the morning, and mustered the people, and went up, he and the elders of Israel, before the people, to Ai.note
Y Josué se levantó de mañana, y pasó revista al pueblo, y subió, él y los ancianos de Israel, delante del pueblo, contra Hai.
11
וְכָל-הָעָם הַמִּלְחָמָה אֲשֶׁר אִתּוֹ, עָלוּ וַיִּגְּשׁוּ, וַיָּבֹאוּ, נֶגֶד הָעִיר; וַיַּחֲנוּ מִצְּפוֹן לָעַי, וְהַגַּי בֵּינָו וּבֵין-הָעָי.
And all the people of war who were with him went up, and drew near, and came in front of the city, and camped north of Ai, with the valley between him and Ai.note
Y todo el pueblo de guerra que estaba con él subió, y se acercaron, y llegaron delante de la ciudad, y acamparon al norte de Hai, con el valle entre él y Hai.
12
וַיִּקַּח, כַּחֲמֵשֶׁת אֲלָפִים אִישׁ; וַיָּשֶׂם אוֹתָם אֹרֵב, בֵּין בֵּית-אֵל וּבֵין הָעַי--מִיָּם לָעִיר.
And he took about five thousand men, and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai, west of the city.note
Y tomó unos cinco mil hombres, y los puso en emboscada entre Betel y Hai, al occidente de la ciudad.
13
וַיָּשִׂימוּ הָעָם אֶת-כָּל-הַמַּחֲנֶה, אֲשֶׁר מִצְּפוֹן לָעִיר, וְאֶת-עֲקֵבוֹ, מִיָּם לָעִיר; וַיֵּלֶךְ יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא, בְּתוֹךְ הָעֵמֶק.
So the people set the whole camp that was north of the city, and its rear guard west of the city; and Joshua went that night into the middle of the valley.note
Así puso el pueblo todo el campamento que estaba al norte de la ciudad, y su retaguardia al occidente de la ciudad; y Josué fue aquella noche al medio del valle.
14
וַיְהִי כִּרְאוֹת מֶלֶךְ-הָעַי, וַיְמַהֲרוּ וַיַּשְׁכִּימוּ וַיֵּצְאוּ אַנְשֵׁי-הָעִיר לִקְרַאת-יִשְׂרָאֵל לַמִּלְחָמָה הוּא וְכָל-עַמּוֹ לַמּוֹעֵד--לִפְנֵי הָעֲרָבָה; וְהוּא לֹא יָדַע, כִּי-אֹרֵב לוֹ מֵאַחֲרֵי הָעִיר.
And when the king of Ai saw it, the men of the city hurried and rose early, and went out to meet Israel in battle, he and all his people, at the appointed place, before the Arabah; but he did not know that there was an ambush against him, behind the city.note
Y cuando lo vio el rey de Hai, los hombres de la ciudad se apresuraron y se levantaron de mañana, y salieron al encuentro de Israel para la batalla, él y todo su pueblo, en el lugar señalado, delante del Arabá; pero él no sabía que había una emboscada contra él por detrás de la ciudad.
15
וַיִּנָּגְעוּ יְהוֹשֻׁעַ וְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, לִפְנֵיהֶם; וַיָּנֻסוּ, דֶּרֶךְ הַמִּדְבָּר.
And Joshua and all Israel let themselves be struck before them, and fled by way of the wilderness.note
Y Josué y todo Israel se dejaron herir delante de ellos, y huyeron camino del desierto.
16
וַיִּזָּעֲקוּ, כָּל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר בעיר בָּעַי, לִרְדֹּף, אַחֲרֵיהֶם; וַיִּרְדְּפוּ אַחֲרֵי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, וַיִּנָּתְקוּ מִן-הָעִיר.
And all the people who were in the city were called out to pursue them; and they pursued Joshua, and were drawn away from the city.note
Y fue convocado todo el pueblo que estaba en la ciudad para perseguirlos; y persiguieron a Josué, y se alejaron de la ciudad.
17
וְלֹא-נִשְׁאַר אִישׁ, בָּעַי וּבֵית אֵל, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-יָצְאוּ, אַחֲרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; וַיַּעַזְבוּ אֶת-הָעִיר פְּתוּחָה, וַיִּרְדְּפוּ אַחֲרֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. {פ}
And there was not a man left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel; and they left the city open, and pursued Israel. {פ}note
Y no quedó hombre en Hai ni en Betel que no saliera tras Israel; y dejaron la ciudad abierta, y persiguieron a Israel. {פ}
18
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ, נְטֵה בַּכִּידוֹן אֲשֶׁר-בְּיָדְךָ אֶל-הָעַי--כִּי בְיָדְךָ, אֶתְּנֶנָּה; וַיֵּט יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בַּכִּידוֹן אֲשֶׁר-בְּיָדוֹ, אֶל-הָעִיר.
And Jehovah said to Joshua: ‘Stretch out the javelin that is in your hand toward Ai, for I will give it into your hand.’ And Joshua stretched out the javelin that was in his hand toward the city.note
Y Jehová dijo a Josué: «Extiende hacia Hai la jabalina que está en tu mano, porque yo la entregaré en tu mano». Y Josué extendió hacia la ciudad la jabalina que tenía en su mano.
19
וְהָאוֹרֵב קָם מְהֵרָה מִמְּקוֹמוֹ וַיָּרוּצוּ, כִּנְטוֹת יָדוֹ, וַיָּבֹאוּ הָעִיר, וַיִּלְכְּדוּהָ; וַיְמַהֲרוּ, וַיַּצִּיתוּ אֶת-הָעִיר בָּאֵשׁ.
And the ambush rose quickly from its place, and they ran as soon as he stretched out his hand, and entered the city and captured it; and they hurried and set the city on fire.note
Y los de la emboscada se levantaron con prontitud de su lugar, y corrieron en cuanto él extendió su mano, y entraron en la ciudad y la tomaron; y se apresuraron, y prendieron fuego a la ciudad.
20
וַיִּפְנוּ אַנְשֵׁי הָעַי אַחֲרֵיהֶם וַיִּרְאוּ, וְהִנֵּה עָלָה עֲשַׁן הָעִיר הַשָּׁמַיְמָה, וְלֹא-הָיָה בָהֶם יָדַיִם לָנוּס, הֵנָּה וָהֵנָּה; וְהָעָם הַנָּס הַמִּדְבָּר, נֶהְפַּךְ אֶל-הָרוֹדֵף.
And when the men of Ai turned and looked, look — the smoke of the city was going up to the sky, and they had no strength left to flee this way or that; and the people who were fleeing toward the wilderness turned back on their pursuers.note
Y cuando los hombres de Hai se volvieron y miraron, mira — el humo de la ciudad subía hacia el cielo, y no les quedaban fuerzas para huir ni hacia aquí ni hacia allá; y el pueblo que huía hacia el desierto se volvió contra sus perseguidores.
21
וִיהוֹשֻׁעַ וְכָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל רָאוּ, כִּי-לָכַד הָאֹרֵב אֶת-הָעִיר, וְכִי עָלָה, עֲשַׁן הָעִיר; וַיָּשֻׁבוּ, וַיַּכּוּ אֶת-אַנְשֵׁי הָעָי.
And when Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had captured the city, and that the smoke of the city was going up, they turned back and struck down the men of Ai.note
Y cuando Josué y todo Israel vieron que la emboscada había tomado la ciudad, y que subía el humo de la ciudad, se volvieron y mataron a los hombres de Hai.
22
וְאֵלֶּה יָצְאוּ מִן-הָעִיר, לִקְרָאתָם, וַיִּהְיוּ לְיִשְׂרָאֵל בַּתָּוֶךְ, אֵלֶּה מִזֶּה וְאֵלֶּה מִזֶּה; וַיַּכּוּ אוֹתָם, עַד-בִּלְתִּי הִשְׁאִיר-לוֹ שָׂרִיד וּפָלִיט.
And the others came out of the city against them, so that Israel had them in the middle, these on this side and those on that; and they struck them down until no one was left remaining or escaping.note
Y los otros salieron de la ciudad contra ellos, de modo que quedaron en medio de Israel, estos de un lado y aquellos del otro; y los mataron, hasta no dejarles superviviente ni fugitivo.
23
וְאֶת-מֶלֶךְ הָעַי, תָּפְשׂוּ חָי; וַיַּקְרִבוּ אֹתוֹ, אֶל-יְהוֹשֻׁעַ.
And the king of Ai they took alive, and brought him to Joshua.note
Y al rey de Hai tomaron vivo, y lo trajeron a Josué.
24
וַיְהִי כְּכַלּוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל לַהֲרֹג אֶת-כָּל-יֹשְׁבֵי הָעַי בַּשָּׂדֶה, בַּמִּדְבָּר אֲשֶׁר רְדָפוּם בּוֹ, וַיִּפְּלוּ כֻלָּם לְפִי-חֶרֶב, עַד-תֻּמָּם; וַיָּשֻׁבוּ כָל-יִשְׂרָאֵל הָעַי, וַיַּכּוּ אֹתָהּ לְפִי-חָרֶב.
And when Israel had finished killing all the inhabitants of Ai in the open field, in the wilderness where they had pursued them, and all of them had fallen by the edge of the sword until they were finished, all Israel returned to Ai and struck it with the edge of the sword.note
Y cuando Israel acabó de matar a todos los habitantes de Hai en el campo abierto, en el desierto adonde los habían perseguido, y todos ellos habían caído a filo de espada hasta acabarse, todo Israel volvió a Hai, y la hirió a filo de espada.
25
וַיְהִי כָל-הַנֹּפְלִים בַּיּוֹם הַהוּא, מֵאִישׁ וְעַד-אִשָּׁה--שְׁנֵים עָשָׂר, אָלֶף: כֹּל, אַנְשֵׁי הָעָי.
And all who fell that day, men and women together, were twelve thousand — all the people of Ai.note
Y todos los que cayeron aquel día, hombres y mujeres juntos, fueron doce mil — todo el pueblo de Hai.
26
וִיהוֹשֻׁעַ לֹא-הֵשִׁיב יָדוֹ, אֲשֶׁר נָטָה בַּכִּידוֹן, עַד אֲשֶׁר הֶחֱרִים, אֵת כָּל-יֹשְׁבֵי הָעָי.
And Joshua did not draw back his hand, with which he had stretched out the javelin, until he had devoted to destruction all the inhabitants of Ai.note
Y Josué no retiró su mano, con la que había extendido la jabalina, hasta que hubo consagrado a la destrucción a todos los habitantes de Hai.
27

as Moses the servant of Jehovah had commanded the sons of Israel, as it is written in the book of the law of Moses: an altar of whole stones, on which no iron had been lifted up; and they offered on it burnt offerings to Jehovah, and sacrificed peace offerings.note
como Moisés, siervo de Jehová, había mandado a los hijos de Israel, como está escrito en el libro de la ley de Moisés: un altar de piedras enteras, sobre las cuales no se había alzado hierro; y ofrecieron sobre él holocaustos a Jehová, y sacrificaron ofrendas de paz.

There was not a word of all that Moses had commanded that Joshua did not read before all the assembly of Israel, and the women, and the little ones, and the resident alien who walked among them.note
No hubo palabra de todo lo que Moisés había mandado que Josué no leyera delante de toda la asamblea de Israel, y las mujeres, y los niños, y el extranjero residente que andaba entre ellos.
Verses 1–2 · The reassurance Moses once gave the whole nation, given now to one man alone — and the concession Jericho never got

★★★ Jehovah answers the disaster at Ai with the exact two verbs Moses used on the edge of the land the first time, before the spies were ever sent. Al-tira ve’al-techat, ‘do not be afraid and do not be dismayed,’ stands in exactly four verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 1:21 (already on these pages), where Moses tells the whole nation to go up and take the land — the very command whose hearing led, eleven verses later, to the spies and the forty years; this verse, where the same two verbs answer the one campaign that has already gone wrong; and 1 Chronicles 22:13 and 28:20 (neither yet on these pages), David to Solomon, twice, about building the house of God. Every use answers a beginning or a setback with the identical pair, never a new one. See chatat for the fourth-verse count.

★★ And v2 hands Ai something Jericho was never offered. Only its spoil and its livestock you may take as plunder for yourselves reads, almost word for word, the law Deuteronomy 20:14 (already on these pages) sets for a city ‘very far off,’ outside the seven nations under the ban — not the law for a city of these nations, which Deuteronomy 20:16–17 requires to be left no breathing thing. Ai is not far off; it is the second Canaanite town Israel meets. The chapter does not explain the moderation, and neither does anything that comes before it — it simply widens the permission the moment the account of theft and its cost is behind it.

Verses 3–9, 11–17, 20–23 · The ambush, and a headcount the chapter never squares

★★ Joshua chooses thirty thousand men for the operation (v3), then sets ‘about five thousand’ in the ambush itself (v12) — and the text never states what the other twenty-five thousand are doing. The most natural reading folds the five thousand inside the thirty, as a specialized detachment within a larger call-up that also mans the decoy force of v5–15; the numbers are not flatly contradictory, but they are also never reconciled in so many words, and this translation reports the two figures as the chapter gives them rather than supplying the missing sentence.

★ The plan itself is a decoy, staged twice over. Joshua’s main body will ‘let itself be struck’ (v15) and run ‘as at first’ — the same collapse Israel actually suffered at Joshua 7:4–5 (now on these pages), now performed on purpose. Ai’s whole garrison takes the bait: there was not a man left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel (v17), and the king twice does not know what stands behind his own city (v14, and again by implication at v19–20). See arav for how densely the chapter’s own vocabulary of lying-in-wait is worked, and for a second undercount this same chapter’s spelling hides.

Verse 10 · Joshua rises early a fourth and final time — and the elders go up with him

★★★ ‘And Joshua rose early in the morning’ stands in exactly four verses of the Hebrew Bible, and this is the last of them. The form vayashkem Yehoshua stands in four verses: Joshua 3:1, the morning the camp breaks for the Jordan; 6:12, the second of seven mornings circling Jericho; 7:16 (all now on these pages), the morning the sifting that finds Achan begins; and this verse. The phrase never recurs in the book again — four crises, the same answer each time, and then it is retired. ★★ This is also the first of the four where anyone rises with him. The elders of Israel go up before the people, at the front, restored to public leadership after the chapter where they were last seen — falling on their faces with Joshua until evening, dust on their heads, after Ai first beat them back (Joshua 7:6, now on these pages). The campaign that follows Achan’s exposure is not Joshua’s alone.

Verses 18–19, 26 · The javelin, and a shelf that cannot agree what Joshua is holding

★★ The kidon Jehovah has Joshua point at Ai is a signal, never a throw — Joshua’s hand stays extended from v18 until v26, the whole length of the battle, the one gesture holding the whole outcome the way Moses’s raised hand did against Amalek (Exodus 17:11–13, already on these pages) — a shared GESTURE, not a shared word; the two accounts use none of the same vocabulary for it. ⚠ The shelf cannot settle what is in his hand. NWT and NIV read ‘javelin’, Spanish NVI ‘jabalina’; ASV, KJV, Geneva, RV 1909 and RV60 read ‘spear’ / «lanza» — the same noun survives even where the two editions part ways on the verb («levanta» in 1909, «extiende» in the 1960 revision); the Living Bible reads ‘spear’ too. The Douay-Rheims alone reads ‘shield’ — following the Vulgate’s clipeus, a Latin-tradition divergence rather than a live alternative reading of the Hebrew, the same class this shelf’s doctrine flags whenever that version goes its own way.

Verses 24–26 · ‘Until they were finished’ — the sentence that closed the writing of the Torah, now closing a city

★★★ Israel ‘finishes’ killing the inhabitants of Ai in the identical grammar Deuteronomy used for Moses finishing the Torah. Ad-tumam, ‘until they were finished,’ stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible, and the frame it sits in, vayehi kekhalot X … ad tumam, belongs first to Deuteronomy 31:24 (now on these pages): when Moses had finished writing the words of this law in a book … until they were finished. This verse repeats the whole frame word for word, subject and object both reversed — the finishing is now a slaughter, not a scroll — and Joshua 10:20 (not yet on these pages) uses it a third time, of five kings’ armies. See tamam, which this book has already made the verb of the crossing and the circumcising; this chapter turns it toward killing, and turns it back toward writing eight verses later, at v32.

★ And Joshua’s own hand does not come down until the same finishing is reached (v26) — the javelin gesture and the ad-tumam formula closing on the same moment, one visual and one grammatical.

Verse 27 · The word Achan died for, now taken in the open

★★ Ai’s shalal is Jericho’s shalal, with the one thing changed that changes everything: permission. Achan took shalal from Jericho when the whole city and everything in it was cherem, forbidden to any hand (Joshua 7:21, now on these pages) — and it cost him and his household their lives. Here the identical noun is authorized in advance, at v2, and the chapter closes the loop at v27: Israel took as plunder for themselves, according to the word of Jehovah that he had commanded Joshua. Same object, same verb of taking, opposite verdict — decided entirely by whether Jehovah had already said yes.

Verses 28–29 · A mound forever, and a heap of stones — a law and a grave repeating themselves

★★★ Ai becomes the one thing a law already on these pages was written for. See tel: tel olam, ‘a mound forever,’ stands in exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible — Deuteronomy 13:17, the sentence decreed for an apostate ISRAELITE town, and this verse, its one execution, on a Canaanite one. The law and its only enactment use the same two words, on the opposite kind of city from the one the law names.

★★ And the king of Ai is buried under the exact phrase Achan was, one chapter earlier. See gal: gal-avanim gadol, ‘a great heap of stones,’ stands in three verses of the whole Hebrew Bible — Achan’s cairn (Joshua 7:26, now on these pages), this one, and Absalom’s in the forest (2 Samuel 18:17, not yet on these pages). A thief, an enemy king, and a king’s own rebel son, each marked the identical way.

★ The hanging itself performs a law already on these pages. See talah: Deuteronomy 21:22–23 (already on these pages) makes hanging a display, never a method — kill first, hang the corpse after, take it down by dark. This verse keeps every term of it: the king dies in the battle, is hung only afterward, comes down at sunset, and is thrown at the gate rather than left up. ⚠ NWT reads ‘stake’ here, its fixed choice for the word across both Testaments; the rest of the shelf keeps ‘tree’; the Douay-Rheims, following the Vulgate’s patibulum, reads ‘gibbet’ — a scaffold, not a tree, the same Latin-tradition shift its rendering of kidon makes two verses earlier.

Verses 30–32 · The altar Deuteronomy promised, out of order in the oldest Greek copies — and a copy of the law whose own name is a mistranslation

★★★ Joshua performs, almost word for word, a ceremony this book has been carrying since Deuteronomy 27 (now on these pages): the altar of unhewn stones no iron has touched, the burnt offerings and peace offerings, the half-and-half stand on Gerizim and Ebal. See the Gerizim and Ebal entries, both updated by this chapter. ⚠ Readings differ on where this scene even belongs. In the Masoretic Text it closes the Ai campaign, here, before the Gibeonites arrive. The Septuagint — and, in the same order, a Dead Sea Scroll fragment of Joshua — instead places it after 9:2 (not yet on these pages), between the coalition of kings forming against Israel and Gibeon’s delegation. This translation follows the Masoretic order and reports the variant rather than resolving it. The disagreement is between two ANCIENT witnesses over where the scene sits in the book, not over whether it happened — and it is not a question the modern shelf can settle either way: this project’s whole shelf is built from Masoretic and Vulgate translations (see the doctrine section), neither of which preserves the Septuagint’s order, so no comparison of renderings could show it either.

★★★ And the copy Joshua writes carries, by accident, the name of a book that has not been written yet. See mishneh: joined directly to torah, the word stands in exactly two verses of the whole Hebrew Bible — Deuteronomy 17:18, commanding a future king to write himself ‘a copy of this law,’ mishneh ha-torah ha-zot, and this verse, where it actually happens, on stones, with no king yet in Israel. The Greek Old Testament took Deuteronomy’s phrase the other way — not ‘a copy of the law’ but ‘this SECOND law,’ to deuteronomion touto — and that mistranslation is where the fifth book of the Torah gets its English and Latin name. The Douay-Rheims, translating the Vulgate’s inheritance of the Greek, prints it at this very verse: Joshua wrote ‘the Deuteronomy of the law of Moses’ — naming, nine books early, the book this project has not reached.

Verses 33–35 · Gerizim and Ebal, and the exact household Deuteronomy named for reading the law aloud

★ The resident alien stands beside the native-born at the ark, as the two words are almost always paired on these pages. Neither is singled out from the ceremony; both halves of the nation cross to whichever mountain, together.

★★ V34 reads ‘the blessing and the curse’ with the general noun, qelalah — not the performative word Deuteronomy 27 actually used twelve times over. See berakhah and qalal, the fourth of this dictionary’s four curse-words kept apart: Deuteronomy 27:15–26 pronounces each of its twelve curses with arur, ‘cursed is…,’ from a different root entirely (arar). Joshua reads all twelve aloud here, but the chapter’s own summary noun for the whole category is the other word — the specific performative curse and the general term that names it, kept exactly as distinct here as everywhere else in this dictionary.

★★★ And v35’s audience is, almost word for word, the household Deuteronomy 31:12 (now on these pages) named for the septennial public reading of the whole law. Assemble the people, the men and the women and the little ones, and your ger who is in your gates, Moses commands there; Joshua reads before all the assembly of Israel, and the women, and the little ones, and the resident alien who walked among them. The triad ‘the women and the little ones’ stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible, in several different kinds of company — twice marking a cherem’s exceptions at Deuteronomy 2:34 and 3:6, once marking a distant city’s permitted captives at 20:14 (all already on these pages), here, at Deuteronomy 31:12, and once more at Judges 21:10 (not yet on these pages). This is not the calendared seventh-year reading itself — Joshua does it once, on entry, not once in seven years — but the exact personnel the law specified for it stands assembled here for the first time there is land to stand on.

Echoes planted. The kings west of the river gather ‘with one accord’ to fight Israel at 9:1–2; Gibeon hears specifically ‘what Joshua had done to Jericho and to Ai’ at 9:3, and its delegation claims to come ‘from a far country’ at 9:6, reaching for the very distant-city exemption this chapter already lent Ai itself. Ad-tumam returns a third time at 10:20, of five kings’ armies; those same five kings are hanged on five trees until evening and taken down at sunset at 10:26–27 — the law of Deuteronomy 21 kept a second time, this time into a cave rather than under a heap of stones. And the elders of Israel go up with Joshua one more time, to the same Shechem, when he gathers all the tribes a last time at 24:1.

Decisions. Hebrew and English verse numbers align through this whole chapter; the Masoretic text carries two open breaks, {פ} after v17 and {פ} after v29, sealing the ambush narrative off from the day that follows it and the altar scene off from what comes after. Al-tira ve’al-techat is ‘do not be afraid and do not be dismayed,’ matching the Deuteronomy 1 page exactly; ha-Ai is printed as a name without its article, as at Genesis 12:8; gibborei ha-chayil is ‘valiant warriors’; orev and its plene twin ha-orev are both ‘the ambush’, with the spelling difference kept in the dictionary rather than the verse; vayashkem is ‘rose early’, as on the Joshua 3, 6 and 7 pages; zqenei Yisrael is ‘the elders of Israel’; ha-Aravah is kept as a name, the Arabah; kidon is ‘javelin’, with the shelf’s split recorded in the note; tumam and ad-tumam are ‘finished’, joining the tamam thread this book has carried since ch. 3; hecherim is ‘devoted to destruction’, as on the Joshua 6 and 7 pages; shalal is ‘spoil’ and bazezu ‘took as plunder’; tel-olam shemamah is ‘a mound forever, a desolation’; talah is ‘hanged’; nivlato is ‘his corpse’, the carcass-sense homonym of ch. 7’s nevalah, outrage, kept apart in the dictionary; gal-avanim gadol is ‘a great heap of stones’, matching ch. 7’s own phrase exactly; even shelemot is ‘whole stones’; mishneh torat Mosheh is ‘a copy of the law of Moses’; ger and ezrach are ‘resident alien’ and ‘native-born’, the paired legal terms of the Deuteronomy and Numbers pages; berakhah is ‘the blessing’ and qelalah ‘the curse’, kept apart from Deuteronomy 27’s arur; qahal is ‘the assembly’, as on the Numbers 16 and Deuteronomy pages. ‘Jehovah’ for the tetragrammaton continues this project’s own convention; God speaks twice in this chapter (vv1–2, 18), and every other voice — the whole ambush briefing of vv4–8 included — is Joshua’s own command to his own men.

Library. One new dictionary entry in both languages: kidon, the javelin a whole shelf cannot agree on. One entry gains a Spanish twin for the first time, chatat, alongside the four-verse reassurance formula it now carries. Seven existing entries extended in both languages: mishneh torah, gaining its one other occurrence in the whole Hebrew Bible and a matching Douay-Rheims quote nine books early; tamam, gaining the Deuteronomy 31 echo; arav, corrected from six verses to eight once the plene spelling is counted with the defective one; qahal, gaining the Deuteronomy 31:12 audience; gal and tel, both upgraded from bare citations to real links now that this chapter exists; and talah, gaining the law’s own real enactment. No new encyclopedia entries — this chapter’s places all already existed — but three updated: Gerizim and Ebal, whose own forward claims from the Deuteronomy 27 page now resolve, and Ai, whose account of its own destruction is complete.

⚠ A note on order: Joshua stands on these pages at chapters 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Joshua 9–24 remain the open sequence; next in this book is Joshua 9, the kings gathering against Israel and Gibeon’s delegation in worn sacks and dry bread.
