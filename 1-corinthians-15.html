The same letter, still from Ephesus. Paul argues from the Corinthians’ denial of the resurrection: if the dead are not raised, Christ is not raised, and the preaching and the faith are empty. He gives the order of the rising, and describes the change at the last trumpet.

Así que, sea yo o sean ellos, así predicamos, y así creyeron ustedes.

Now if Christ is preached as raised from the dead, how do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?note
Ahora bien, si se está predicando que Cristo fue levantado de entre los muertos, ¿cómo es que algunos de ustedes dicen que no hay resurrección de los muertos?

13
εἰ δὲ ἀνάστασις νεκρῶν οὐκ ἔστιν, οὐδὲ Χριστὸς ἐγήγερται·
But if there is no resurrection of the dead, neither has Christ been raised.note
Pero si no hay resurrección de los muertos, tampoco Cristo ha sido levantado.
14

And if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is empty and your faith is empty too.note
Y si Cristo no ha sido levantado, entonces nuestra predicación es vana, y vana también la fe de ustedes.

We are even found to be false witnesses about God, because we testified against God that he raised Christ, whom he did not raise, if indeed the dead are not raised.note
Además, resultamos ser falsos testigos de Dios, porque hemos dado testimonio contra Dios de que él levantó al Cristo, a quien no levantó, si es que los muertos no son levantados.
16
εἰ γὰρ νεκροὶ οὐκ ἐγείρονται, οὐδὲ Χριστὸς ἐγήγερται·
For if the dead are not raised, neither has Christ been raised.note
Porque si los muertos no son levantados, tampoco Cristo ha sido levantado.
17
εἰ δὲ Χριστὸς οὐκ ἐγήγερται, ματαία ἡ πίστις ὑμῶν· ἔτι ἐστὲ ἐν ταῖς ἁμαρτίαις ὑμῶν.
And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile; you are still in your sins.note
Y si Cristo no ha sido levantado, su fe es inútil; todavía están en sus pecados.
18
ἄρα καὶ οἱ κοιμηθέντες ἐν Χριστῷ ἀπώλοντο.
Then also those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished.note
Entonces también los que durmieron en Cristo han perecido.

19
εἰ ἐν τῇ ζωῇ ταύτῃ ἐν Χριστῷ ἠλπικότες ἐσμὲν μόνον, ἐλεεινότεροι πάντων ἀνθρώπων ἐσμέν.
If in this life only we have hoped in Christ, we are more pitiable than all people.note
Si solo en esta vida hemos puesto nuestra esperanza en Cristo, somos más dignos de lástima que todas las personas.
20
Νυνὶ δὲ Χριστὸς ἐγήγερται ἐκ νεκρῶν, ἀπαρχὴ τῶν κεκοιμημένων.
But now Christ has been raised from the dead, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep.note
Pero ahora Cristo ha sido levantado de entre los muertos, como primicias de los que durmieron.
cross-refs⤷ 2 Thessalonians 2:13
21
ἐπειδὴ γὰρ δι᾽ ἀνθρώπου θάνατος καὶ δι᾽ ἀνθρώπου ἀνάστασις νεκρῶν·
For since death came through a man, resurrection of the dead comes through a man.note
Porque, puesto que la muerte vino por un hombre, la resurrección de los muertos también viene por un hombre.
22
ὥσπερ γὰρ ἐν τῷ Ἀδὰμ πάντες ἀποθνῄσκουσιν, οὕτως καὶ ἐν τῷ Χριστῷ πάντες ζῳοποιηθήσονται.
For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive.note
Porque así como en Adán todos mueren, así también en Cristo todos serán vivificados.
23
ἕκαστος δὲ ἐν τῷ ἰδίῳ τάγματι· ἀπαρχὴ Χριστός, ἔπειτα οἱ τοῦ Χριστοῦ ἐν τῇ παρουσίᾳ αὐτοῦ·
But each in his own order: Christ the firstfruits, then those who belong to Christ at his coming.note
Pero cada uno en su propio orden: Cristo, las primicias; luego los que pertenecen a Cristo, en su presencia.
24
εἶτα τὸ τέλος, ὅταν παραδιδῷ τὴν βασιλείαν τῷ θεῷ καὶ πατρί, ὅταν καταργήσῃ πᾶσαν ἀρχὴν καὶ πᾶσαν ἐξουσίαν καὶ δύναμιν,
Then the end, when he hands the kingdom over to God the Father, when he has abolished every rule and every authority and power.note
Luego viene el fin, cuando entregue el reino a Dios y Padre, cuando haya abolido todo gobierno, toda autoridad y todo poder.
25
δεῖ γὰρ αὐτὸν βασιλεύειν ἄχρι οὗ θῇ πάντας τοὺς ἐχθροὺς ὑπὸ τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ.
For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet.note
Porque él debe reinar hasta que haya puesto a todos sus enemigos debajo de sus pies.
26
ἔσχατος ἐχθρὸς καταργεῖται ὁ θάνατος,
The last enemy to be abolished is death.note
El último enemigo que será abolido es la muerte.
cross-refs⤷ Hebrews 2:14
27
πάντα γὰρ ὑπέταξεν ὑπὸ τοὺς πόδας αὐτοῦ. ὅταν δὲ εἴπῃ ὅτι πάντα ὑποτέτακται, δῆλον ὅτι ἐκτὸς τοῦ ὑποτάξαντος αὐτῷ τὰ πάντα.
For “he has put all things under his feet.” But when it says that all things have been put under him, it is clear that this excludes the one who put all things under him.note
Porque «todo lo sometió bajo sus pies». Pero cuando dice que todo ha sido sometido, es claro que se excluye al que sometió a él todas las cosas.
cross-refs⤷ Philippians 3:21⤷ Hebrews 2:8
28
ὅταν δὲ ὑποταγῇ αὐτῷ τὰ πάντα, τότε αὐτὸς ὁ υἱὸς ὑποταγήσεται τῷ ὑποτάξαντι αὐτῷ τὰ πάντα, ἵνα ᾖ ὁ θεὸς πάντα ἐν πᾶσιν.
And when all things have been put under him, then the Son himself will also be put under the one who put all things under him, so that God may be all in all.note
Y cuando todas las cosas le hayan sido sometidas, entonces el Hijo mismo también se someterá al que le sometió todas las cosas, para que Dios sea todo en todos.
29
Ἐπεὶ τί ποιήσουσιν οἱ βαπτιζόμενοι ὑπὲρ τῶν νεκρῶν; εἰ ὅλως νεκροὶ οὐκ ἐγείρονται, τί καὶ βαπτίζονται ὑπὲρ αὐτῶν;
Otherwise, what will those do who are baptized for the dead? If the dead are not raised at all, why then are they baptized for them?note
De otro modo, ¿qué harán los que se bautizan por los muertos? Si los muertos no son levantados en absoluto, ¿por qué se bautizan por ellos?
30
τί καὶ ἡμεῖς κινδυνεύομεν πᾶσαν ὥραν;
And why are we in danger every hour?note
¿Y por qué nosotros también estamos en peligro a toda hora?
31
καθʼ ἡμέραν ἀποθνῄσκω, νὴ τὴν ὑμετέραν καύχησιν ἣν ἔχω ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ τῷ κυρίῳ ἡμῶν.
I die every day; I swear by the boast about you that I have in Christ Jesus our Lord.note
Cada día muero; lo juro por el orgullo que siento por ustedes en Cristo Jesús nuestro Señor.
32
εἰ κατὰ ἄνθρωπον ἐθηριομάχησα ἐν Ἐφέσῳ, τί μοι τὸ ὄφελος; εἰ νεκροὶ οὐκ ἐγείρονται, Φάγωμεν καὶ πίωμεν, αὔριον γὰρ ἀποθνῄσκομεν.
If in the manner of men I fought with beasts at Ephesus, what does it profit me? If the dead are not raised, “let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.”note
Si, como hombres comunes, luché con fieras en Éfeso, ¿de qué me sirve? Si los muertos no son levantados, «comamos y bebamos, porque mañana moriremos».
33
μὴ πλανᾶσθε· φθείρουσιν ἤθη χρηστὰ ὁμιλίαι κακαί.
Do not be deceived: bad company corrupts good morals.note
No se dejen engañar: las malas compañías echan a perder las buenas costumbres.
34
ἐκνήψατε δικαίως καὶ μὴ ἁμαρτάνετε, ἀγνωσίαν γὰρ θεοῦ τινες ἔχουσιν· πρὸς ἐντροπὴν ὑμῖν λαλῶ.
Wake up to righteousness, and do not sin; for some have no knowledge of God. I say this to your shame.note
Vuelvan en sí con justicia, y no pequen; porque algunos no tienen conocimiento de Dios. Lo digo para vergüenza de ustedes.
35
Ἀλλὰ ἐρεῖ τις· Πῶς ἐγείρονται οἱ νεκροί, ποίῳ δὲ σώματι ἔρχονται;
But someone will say, “How are the dead raised? With what kind of body do they come?”note
Pero alguien dirá: «¿Cómo son levantados los muertos? ¿Con qué clase de cuerpo vienen?».
36
ἄφρων σύ· ὃ σπείρεις, οὐ ζῳοποιεῖται ἐὰν μὴ ἀποθάνῃ·
Fool! What you sow does not come to life unless it dies.note
¡Insensato! Lo que siembras no vuelve a la vida si antes no muere.
37
καὶ ὃ σπείρεις, οὐ τὸ σῶμα τὸ γενησόμενον σπείρεις ἀλλὰ γυμνὸν κόκκον εἰ τύχοι σίτου ἤ τινος τῶν λοιπῶν·
And what you sow is not the body that will be, but a bare seed, perhaps of wheat or of another kind.note
Y lo que siembras no es el cuerpo que va a nacer, sino un grano desnudo, quizá de trigo o de otra clase.
38
ὁ δὲ θεὸς δίδωσιν αὐτῷ σῶμα καθὼς ἠθέλησεν, καὶ ἑκάστῳ τῶν σπερμάτων ἴδιον σῶμα.
But God gives it a body as he has willed, and to each seed its own body.note
Pero Dios le da un cuerpo como quiso, y a cada semilla su propio cuerpo.
39
οὐ πᾶσα σὰρξ ἡ αὐτὴ σάρξ, ἀλλὰ ἄλλη μὲν ἀνθρώπων, ἄλλη δὲ σὰρξ κτηνῶν, ἄλλη δὲ σὰρξ πτηνῶν, ἄλλη δὲ ἰχθύων.
Not all flesh is the same flesh: there is one flesh of humans, another of animals, another of birds, and another of fish.note
No toda carne es la misma carne: una es la de los hombres, otra la de los animales, otra la de las aves y otra la de los peces.
40
καὶ σώματα ἐπουράνια, καὶ σώματα ἐπίγεια· ἀλλὰ ἑτέρα μὲν ἡ τῶν ἐπουρανίων δόξα, ἑτέρα δὲ ἡ τῶν ἐπιγείων.
And there are heavenly bodies and earthly bodies; but the glory of the heavenly is one, and that of the earthly is another.note
Hay cuerpos celestes y cuerpos terrestres; pero una es la gloria de los celestes, y otra la de los terrestres.
41
ἄλλη δόξα ἡλίου, καὶ ἄλλη δόξα σελήνης, καὶ ἄλλη δόξα ἀστέρων· ἀστὴρ γὰρ ἀστέρος διαφέρει ἐν δόξῃ.
One glory of the sun, another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars; for one star differs from another in glory.note
Una es la gloria del sol, otra la de la luna y otra la de las estrellas; porque una estrella difiere de otra en gloria.
42
Οὕτως καὶ ἡ ἀνάστασις τῶν νεκρῶν. σπείρεται ἐν φθορᾷ, ἐγείρεται ἐν ἀφθαρσίᾳ·
So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption, it is raised in incorruption;note
Así también es la resurrección de los muertos: se siembra en corrupción, se levanta en incorrupción;
cross-refs⤷ Galatians 6:8⤷ Ephesians 6:24
43
σπείρεται ἐν ἀτιμίᾳ, ἐγείρεται ἐν δόξῃ· σπείρεται ἐν ἀσθενείᾳ, ἐγείρεται ἐν δυνάμει·
it is sown in dishonor, it is raised in glory; it is sown in weakness, it is raised in power.note
se siembra en deshonra, se levanta en gloria; se siembra en debilidad, se levanta en poder.
44
σπείρεται σῶμα ψυχικόν, ἐγείρεται σῶμα πνευματικόν. Εἰ ἔστιν σῶμα ψυχικόν, ἔστιν καὶ πνευματικόν.
It is sown a natural body, it is raised a spiritual body. If there is a natural body, there is also a spiritual one.note
Se siembra un cuerpo natural, se levanta un cuerpo espiritual. Si hay un cuerpo natural, también hay uno espiritual.
45
οὕτως καὶ γέγραπται· Ἐγένετο ὁ πρῶτος ἄνθρωπος Ἀδὰμ εἰς ψυχὴν ζῶσαν· ὁ ἔσχατος Ἀδὰμ εἰς πνεῦμα ζῳοποιοῦν.
So also it is written: “The first man Adam became a living soul”; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit.note
Así también está escrito: «El primer hombre, Adán, fue hecho un ser vivo»; el último Adán, un espíritu que da vida.
46
ἀλλʼ οὐ πρῶτον τὸ πνευματικὸν ἀλλὰ τὸ ψυχικόν, ἔπειτα τὸ πνευματικόν.
But the spiritual is not first; the natural is first, and then the spiritual.note
Pero lo espiritual no es lo primero, sino lo natural; luego lo espiritual.
47
ὁ πρῶτος ἄνθρωπος ἐκ γῆς χοϊκός, ὁ δεύτερος ἄνθρωπος ἐξ οὐρανοῦ.
The first man is from the earth, made of dust; the second man is from heaven.note
El primer hombre es de la tierra, hecho de polvo; el segundo hombre es del cielo.
48
οἷος ὁ χοϊκός, τοιοῦτοι καὶ οἱ χοϊκοί, καὶ οἷος ὁ ἐπουράνιος, τοιοῦτοι καὶ οἱ ἐπουράνιοι·
As the one made of dust, so also those made of dust; and as the heavenly one, so also those who are heavenly.note
Como el hecho de polvo, así son también los hechos de polvo; y como el celestial, así son también los celestiales.
49
καὶ καθὼς ἐφορέσαμεν τὴν εἰκόνα τοῦ χοϊκοῦ, φορέσομεν καὶ τὴν εἰκόνα τοῦ ἐπουρανίου.
And just as we have borne the image of the one made of dust, we shall also bear the image of the heavenly one.note
Y así como hemos llevado la imagen del hecho de polvo, llevaremos también la imagen del celestial.
50
Τοῦτο δέ φημι, ἀδελφοί, ὅτι σὰρξ καὶ αἷμα βασιλείαν θεοῦ κληρονομῆσαι οὐ δύναται οὐδὲ ἡ φθορὰ τὴν ἀφθαρσίαν κληρονομεῖ.
This I say, brothers: flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does corruption inherit incorruption.note
Esto les digo, hermanos: carne y sangre no pueden heredar el reino de Dios, ni la corrupción hereda la incorrupción.
51
ἰδοὺ μυστήριον ὑμῖν λέγω· πάντες οὐ κοιμηθησόμεθα, πάντες δὲ ἀλλαγησόμεθα,
Behold, I tell you a mystery: we will not all fall asleep, but we will all be changed,note
Miren, les digo un misterio: no todos dormiremos, pero todos seremos transformados,
cross-refs⤷ 1 Thessalonians 4:15
52
ἐν ἀτόμῳ, ἐν ῥιπῇ ὀφθαλμοῦ, ἐν τῇ ἐσχάτῃ σάλπιγγι· σαλπίσει γάρ, καὶ οἱ νεκροὶ ἐγερθήσονται ἄφθαρτοι, καὶ ἡμεῖς ἀλλαγησόμεθα.
in a moment, in the blink of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we will be changed.note
en un momento, en un abrir y cerrar de ojos, al sonar la última trompeta. Porque sonará la trompeta, y los muertos serán levantados incorruptibles, y nosotros seremos transformados.
cross-refs⤷ 1 Thessalonians 4:16
53
δεῖ γὰρ τὸ φθαρτὸν τοῦτο ἐνδύσασθαι ἀφθαρσίαν καὶ τὸ θνητὸν τοῦτο ἐνδύσασθαι ἀθανασίαν.
For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.note
Porque esto corruptible tiene que vestirse de incorrupción, y esto mortal tiene que vestirse de inmortalidad.
cross-refs⤷ 1 Timothy 6:16
54
ὅταν δὲ τὸ φθαρτὸν τοῦτο ἐνδύσηται ἀφθαρσίαν καὶ τὸ θνητὸν τοῦτο ἐνδύσηται ἀθανασίαν τότε γενήσεται ὁ λόγος ὁ γεγραμμένος· Κατεπόθη ὁ θάνατος εἰς νῖκος.
And when this corruptible puts on incorruption, and this mortal puts on immortality, then the word that is written will come about: “Death is swallowed up in victory.”note
Y cuando esto corruptible se vista de incorrupción y esto mortal se vista de inmortalidad, entonces se cumplirá la palabra escrita: «La muerte fue devorada en victoria».
55
ποῦ σου, θάνατε, τὸ νῖκος; ποῦ σου, θάνατε, τὸ κέντρον;
“Death, where is your victory? Death, where is your sting?”note
«Muerte, ¿dónde está tu victoria? Muerte, ¿dónde está tu aguijón?».
56
τὸ δὲ κέντρον τοῦ θανάτου ἡ ἁμαρτία, ἡ δὲ δύναμις τῆς ἁμαρτίας ὁ νόμος·
The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law.note
El aguijón de la muerte es el pecado, y el poder del pecado es la Ley.
57
τῷ δὲ θεῷ χάρις τῷ διδόντι ἡμῖν τὸ νῖκος διὰ τοῦ κυρίου ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ.
But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.note
Pero gracias a Dios, que nos da la victoria por medio de nuestro Señor Jesucristo.
58
Ὥστε, ἀδελφοί μου ἀγαπητοί, ἑδραῖοι γίνεσθε, ἀμετακίνητοι, περισσεύοντες ἐν τῷ ἔργῳ τοῦ κυρίου πάντοτε, εἰδότες ὅτι ὁ κόπος ὑμῶν οὐκ ἔστιν κενὸς ἐν κυρίῳ.
So then, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not empty in the Lord.note
Así que, mis queridos hermanos, manténganse firmes, inconmovibles, siempre abundando en la obra del Señor, sabiendo que su trabajo en el Señor no es vano.
Verses 1–2 · the gospel received, the stand taken, and the holding fast

“The gospel I announced.” to euangelion ho euēngelisamēn hymin, the good news that I announced to you. The verb euangelizō is to bring good news, and the noun is the word of the gospel (euangelion). The chapter opens by naming what the Corinthians received and stand in. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now I remind you, brothers, of the good news that I declared to you, which you also accepted, and for which you have taken your stand.”

“If you hold fast.” ei katechete, if you hold fast. The verb katechō, to hold fast, is the verb of 11:2 for the traditions. The condition is put in the sentence without accusation: the saving depends on keeping the word, and believing in vain is the danger. The shelf: the KJV “By which also ye are saved, if ye keep in memory what I preached unto you, unless ye have believed in vain.”

Verses 3–8 · the tradition handed on: the death, the burial, the third day, and the appearances

“I handed on to you what I also received.” parelabon and paredōka, the verbs of receiving and handing on that the chapter uses for tradition (paradosis), the same as 11:23. The sentence gives a list that Paul says he received, and it ties the death and the third day to the Scriptures. The note does not say which Scriptures are meant; the sentence gives only the phrase. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For among the first things I handed on to you was what I also received, that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures;” and the KJV “For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures.”

“He was seen.” ōphthē, he was seen, the same verb in each appearance: Cephas, the Twelve, more than five hundred, James, all the apostles, and Paul. The verb is the passive of horaō, to see, and it names an appearance rather than a vision of something inward. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “After that he appeared to more than 500 brothers at one time, most of whom are still with us, though some have fallen asleep in death.”

“As to one born out of due time.” hōsperei tō ektrōmati, as to one born prematurely. The word is an image of a birth out of its time, and Paul applies it to himself as the last. The shelf: the KJV “And last of all he was seen of me also, as of one born out of due time.”

Verses 9–11 · least of the apostles, the persecutor, and the grace that labored

“The least of the apostles.” ho elachistos tōn apostolōn, the least of the apostles (apostolos). Paul names his persecution of the congregation of God, the word ekklēsia rendered “congregation” in every chapter of this translation, and then the grace of God that made him what he is. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “For I am the least of the apostles, and I am not worthy of being called an apostle, because I persecuted the congregation of God.”

“By the grace of God I am what I am.” charti de theou eimi ho eimi, the grace of God. The word charis is the same as in 1:4. The shelf: the KJV “But by the grace of God I am what I am: and his grace which was bestowed upon me was not in vain; but I laboured more abundantly than they all.”

Verses 12–19 · if there is no resurrection, then Christ has not been raised, and the preaching and the faith are empty

“No resurrection of the dead.” anastasis nekrōn, the rising of the dead. The argument is conditional throughout: if there is no resurrection, then Christ is not raised, and then the preaching is empty and the faith is empty too. The sentence is an argument from the implication, and the translation keeps each “if” as the Greek has it. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Now if it is being preached that Christ has been raised from the dead, how is it that some among you say there is no resurrection of the dead?”

“False witnesses.” pseudomartyres tou theou, false witnesses of God. The word is the word of a witness in court, and the sentence makes the preachers the witnesses who would be wrong. The shelf: the KJV “Yea, and we are found false witnesses of God; because we have testified of God that he raised up Christ.”

“Fallen asleep.” koimēthentes, those who fell asleep. The verb for the dead is the same as in verse 6, and the sentence says that without a resurrection they have perished. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Then also those who have fallen asleep in death in union with Christ have perished.” The TNM 2019 “también los que se han dormido en la muerte en unión con Cristo han muerto para siempre” keeps the verb “perished” as “died for good.”

Verses 20–28 · the firstfruits, Adam and Christ, the order, the end, and the handing over

“Firstfruits.” aparchē, the first portion of a harvest. Christ is the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep, and verse 23 gives the order: each in his own rank. The word tagma, rank or order, is the military word for a company in its place.

“As in Adam all die.” The contrast of Adam and Christ puts one man against another, and death through the first against life through the last. The sentence does not give the details of the Genesis account; it assumes them. The shelf: the KJV “For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”

“Then the end.” eita to telos, then the end, when he hands over the kingdom to God the Father, and abolishes every rule, authority, and power. The sentence describes a sequence, and the translation keeps the sequence as the Greek gives it. The readings of verses 24–28 divide over who the subject of the handing over is and what the subjection of the Son means. The note records these readings and does not choose one; the translation keeps “the Son himself will also be put under” in the passive, as the Greek has it. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “then the Son himself will also subject himself to the One who subjected all things to him, that God may be all things to everyone.” The KJV “then shall the Son also himself be subject unto him that put all things under him, that God may be all in all.”

“The last enemy.” The verse quotes the psalm in verse 27: he put all things under his feet, and the sentence notes that the one who puts all under him is excluded. The shelf: the KJV “The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death.” The NWT 2013 “And the last enemy, death, is to be brought to nothing.”

Verses 29–34 · baptism for the dead, daily danger, the beasts at Ephesus, and the bad company

“Baptized for the dead.” hyper tōn nekrōn, for the dead. The sentence refers to a practice Paul does not describe or endorse, and the Greek gives no explanation. The note records that the reading is open: the phrase could mean baptism on behalf of the dead, or for the dead in the sense of for the sake of the hope of their rising. Paul does not explain it, and the translation does not supply the explanation. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Otherwise, what will they do who are being baptized for the purpose of being dead ones?”

“I die every day.” kath’ hēmeran apothnēskō. The verb is the verb of dying, and the statement is the daily danger of verse 30. The oath is by the boast Paul has in the Corinthians. The shelf: the KJV “I protest by your rejoicing which I have in Christ Jesus our Lord, I die daily.”

“Fought with beasts at Ephesus.” ethēriomachēsa. The phrase is literal in form and can be read as a figure for the opposition Paul faced. The sentence says it is “in the manner of men,” and the note records the two readings rather than choosing. The shelf: the KJV “If after the manner of men I have fought with beasts at Ephesus, what advantageth it me, if the dead rise not?”

“Bad company corrupts good morals.” homiliai kakai. The sentence quotes a saying, and the word ēthē is character or habits. The shelf: the KJV “Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.”

Verses 35–38 · how are the dead raised, and with what body; the seed that dies and the body God gives

“With what kind of body.” poiō sōmati, with what kind of body. The question is the objection of a listener, and the answer is the image of the seed. Paul calls the questioner a fool, and the sentence gives the seed as the picture: what is sown does not come to life unless it dies. The word sōma, body (sōma), is the body the seed will receive. The shelf: the KJV “But some man will say, How are the dead raised up? and with what body do they come?” The NWT 2013 “Nevertheless, someone will say: “How are the dead to be raised up? Yes, with what sort of body are they coming?””

“God gives it a body.” kathōs ēthelēsen, as he has willed. The sentence says God gives each seed its own body. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “but God gives it a body just as it has pleased him, and gives to each of the seeds its own body.”

Verses 39–41 · flesh, bodies heavenly and earthly, and the glory of each

“Not all flesh is the same flesh.” ou pasa sarx hē autē sarx, not every flesh is the same flesh. The sentence lists four kinds of flesh and then names two kinds of body: heavenly and earthly. The glory is a different kind for each. The shelf: the KJV “All flesh is not the same flesh: but there is one kind of flesh of men, another flesh of beasts, another of fishes, and another of birds.”

“One glory of the sun.” The three heavenly lights are the image of the difference in glory, and the sentence ends with the star differing from star. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “The glory of the sun is one sort, and the glory of the moon is another, and the glory of the stars is another; in fact, one star differs from another star in glory.”

Verses 42–49 · sown physical, raised spiritual; the first man and the second; the image of the heavenly

“Sown in corruption, raised in incorruption.” speiretai en phthora, egeiretai en aphthariai. The four pairs are sown and raised: corruption and incorruption, dishonor and glory, weakness and power. The verb speirō is to sow, the picture of the seed in verses 36–38. The word aphtharsia, incorruption, is the word of the body that does not decay. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “It is sown in dishonor; it is raised up in glory. It is sown in weakness; it is raised up in power.”

“Physical and spiritual.” sōma psychikon and sōma pneumatikon. The adjective psychikos is the one for natural, soulish life; pneumatikos is the one for the Spirit. The sentence does not say the spiritual body is not a body; it says there is a body of each kind. The shelf: the KJV “It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body. There is a natural body, and there is a spiritual body.”

“The first man, the second.” ho prōtos anthrōpos Adam eis psychēn zōsan; ho eschatos Adam eis pneuma zōopoioun, the first man Adam became a living soul; the last Adam became a life-giving spirit. The quotation is Genesis 2:7 in the Greek. The sentence names Christ as the last Adam and the second man from heaven. The shelf: the KJV “The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit.” In KJV verse 47 “the Lord from heaven” adds the word “the Lord,” which the Greek does not print; the NWT 2013 “the second man is from heaven” follows the Greek.

“The image of the heavenly.” eikona tou epouraniou, the image of the heavenly one. The verb phoreō, to wear or bear, is the verb of clothing, and the sentence puts the image as a garment. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “And just as we have borne the image of the one made of dust, we will bear also the image of the heavenly one.”

Verses 50–53 · flesh and blood cannot inherit; the mystery; the change at the last trumpet

“Flesh and blood cannot inherit.” sarx kai haima basileian theou klēronomēsai ou dynatai. The sentence is that flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom, and corruption cannot inherit incorruption. The statement sets the body of verse 44 against the kingdom of verse 24. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “But I tell you this, brothers, that flesh and blood cannot inherit God’s Kingdom, nor does corruption inherit incorruption.”

“A mystery.” mystērion, a sacred secret (mystērion). The word is the one of 14:2, and here it names the change of the living. The note records that the sentence distinguishes those who fall asleep from those who are changed, and the translation keeps the distinction. The shelf: the KJV “Behold, I shew you a mystery; We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed.”

“The last trumpet.” en tē eschatē salpigki, at the last trumpet. The sentence says the trumpet sounds and the dead are raised incorruptible, and those alive are changed. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “in a moment, in the blink of an eye, during the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised up incorruptible, and we will be changed.”

Verses 54–58 · death swallowed up in victory, the sting of sin and the law, and the steadfast work

“Death is swallowed up in victory.” Katepothē ho thanatos eis nikos. The first quotation is usually traced to Isaiah 25:8, and the second line to Hosea 13:14, which has the word kentron, sting. The sentence quotes the two prophets and says the victory is God’s. The shelf: the KJV “So when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.”

“The sting of death is sin.” to kentron tou thanatou hē hamartia, the sting of death is sin. The sentence names the power of sin as the law. The word nomos is the law, and the translation keeps “law” for it. The shelf: the KJV “The sting of death is sin; and the strength of sin is the law.”

“Steadfast, immovable.” hedraioi ginesthe, ametakinētoi, be steadfast, immovable. The chapter ends with the instruction, and the work is the work of the Lord. The shelf: the NWT 2013 “Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always having plenty to do in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in connection with the Lord.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “congregation” for ekklēsia at 15:9; “firstfruits” for aparchē at 15:20 and 15:23; “incorruption” for aphtharsia at 15:42, 15:50, 15:52–54; “natural” for psychikos at 15:44–46, which the older English prints as “natural” or “soulish”; “mystery” for mystērion at 15:51, the word of 14:2; “law” for nomos at 15:56.

Echoes paid. The resurrection that the Corinthians deny (15:12) answers the question of the body in 6:13–20 and 12:27, now the body that is raised; the firstfruits of 15:20 return the harvest of the seed in 15:36–38; the sleep of those who died (11:30) returns in 15:6, 15:18, 15:20 and 15:51; the mystery of 14:2 returns at 15:51; the law of 14:21 returns at 15:56; and the grace of 1:4 returns at 15:10.

Echoes planted. The subjection of the Son (15:28) is the last echo of the Trinitarian readings of this letter; the note records the readings there. The image of the heavenly (15:49) has no other place in the letter. The victory of 15:57 is the last word of the chapter; the letter continues in chapter 16.
