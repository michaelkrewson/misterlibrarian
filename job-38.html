Jehovah answers Job out of the storm: who darkens counsel with words without knowledge; where was Job when the earth was founded, its cornerstone laid, and the morning stars sang; who shut the sea with doors and said thus far; has Job commanded the morning, entered the springs of the sea and the gates of death, seen the storehouses of snow and hail; has the rain a father, can he bind the Pleiades and loosen Orion, send the lightnings, tip the water-skins of heaven; and who hunts for the lioness and prepares food for the raven.
Undated. The book gives no date and names no king; the answer from the storm is the book’s own turning point, after the three friends and Elihu have spoken, and it takes the questions of the earlier chapters and answers none of them directly. ⚠ The speech is addressed to Job alone, and Job’s reply is in 40:3–5 and 42:1–6, not on this page.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · Job 38:1–3 Jehovah answers “out of the storm” — “Who is this that darkens counsel?”

⭐ The storm, and a written and read word. “And Jehovah answered Job” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and at the second round (Job 40:1 and 40:6). The answer comes min hase’arah, “out of the storm”, and the Hebrew prints the phrase twice, written as one word and read as two, a ketiv/qere pair (the written minhas’arah, the read min hase’arah), kept in the Hebrew column. The storm is also the wind that takes up Elijah (2 Kings 2:1, now on these pages), and Ezekiel’s vision begins in a storm wind (Ezekiel 1:4, now on these pages). The KJV has “answered Job out of the whirlwind”, the NIV “spoke to Job out of the storm”, the NWT 1984 “answer Job out of the windstorm”, the TLB “answered Job from the whirlwind” and the Douay “answered Job out of a whirlwind”.

⭐ “Who is this?” “Who is this that darkens counsel” stands in this one verse, machshikh etsah, with etsah, counsel, plan; and the words are bemillin bli da’at, “with words without knowledge”. The phrase bli da’at is the book’s own, and Job says it back to God at the end (Job 42:3, now on these pages). The KJV has “Who is this that darkeneth counsel by words without knowledge?”, the NIV “Who is this that obscures my plans with words without knowledge?”, the NWT 1984 “Who is this that is obscuring counsel By words without knowledge?”, the Douay “Who is this that wrappeth up sentences in unskillful words?” and the TLB “Why are you using your ignorance to deny my providence?”.

⭐ “Gird up your loins”. “Gird up your loins now like a man” stands in two verses, here and 40:7, where it is said again before the second speech; the loins are girded for a fight or a journey. The KJV has “Gird up now thy loins like a man”, the NIV “Brace yourself like a man”, the NWT 1984 “Gird up your loins, please, like an able-bodied man”, the Douay “Gird up thy loins like a man” and the TLB “Now get ready to fight”.

Verses 4–7 · Job 38:4–7 “Where were you when I founded the earth?” — the cornerstone, and the morning stars

⭐ The question. “Where were you when I founded the earth” stands in this one verse, and the challenge that goes with it is “declare, if you know understanding”, which stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? declare, if thou hast understanding”, the NIV “Where were you when I laid the earth’s foundation? Tell me, if you understand”, the NWT 1984 “Where did you happen to be when I founded the earth?” and the TLB “Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Tell me, if you know so much”. The building picture runs through the next verses like a house: “who set its measures” stands in this one verse, “stretched the line upon it” stands in two verses, here and Isaiah 34:11, then the sockets, adaneha, and the cornerstone.

⭐ The cornerstone. “Its cornerstone”, even pinnatah, stands in this one verse; pinnah is the corner of Psalm 118, “the stone the builders rejected has become the head of the corner” (Psalm 118:22, now on these pages; dictionary). The KJV has “who laid the corner stone thereof”, the NIV “who laid its cornerstone”, the NWT 1984 “who laid its cornerstone” and the Douay “who laid the corner stone thereof”.

⭐ The morning stars, and the sons of God. “The morning stars sang together” stands in this one verse, and the second half answers it: “sons of God”, in this spelling, stands in this one verse; with the article, “the sons of God” come before Jehovah in the heavenly court at the book’s opening (Job 1:6, now on these pages) and are in Genesis 6:2 (now on these pages). The KJV has “the morning stars sang together, and all the sons of God shouted for joy”, the NWT 1984 “the morning stars joyfully cried out together, And all the sons of God began shouting in applause”, the NIV “all the angels shouted for joy”, the TLB “all the angels shouted for joy” and the Douay “the morning stars praised me together, and all the sons of God made a joyful melody”.

Verses 8–11 · Job 38:8–11 the sea shut up with doors — “Thus far you will come, and no farther”

⭐ The sea, born and swaddled. “And who shut up the sea with doors” stands in this one verse, and the sea is a child: “when it burst forth, issuing from the womb” stands in this one verse, with a cloud for its garment, “the cloud its garment”, which stands in this one verse, and “thick darkness its swaddling band”, chatullato, which stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Or who shut up the sea with doors, when it brake forth, as if it had issued out of the womb?” and “a swaddlingband for it”, the NIV “Who shut up the sea behind doors when it burst forth from the womb”, the NWT 1984 “thick gloom as its swaddling band” and the Douay “wrapped it in a mist as in swaddling bands”. The first gathering of the waters is in Genesis 1:9 (now on these pages).

⭐ “Thus far, and no farther”. The limit is set in speech: “and broke my limit upon it” stands in this one verse, with choq, a decree, a boundary, and “thus far you will come, and no farther” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Hitherto shalt thou come, but no further: and here shall thy proud waves be stayed”, the NIV “This far you may come and no farther; here is where your proud waves halt”, the NWT 1984 “This far you may come, and no farther”, the TLB “Thus far and no farther shall you come” and the Douay “Hitherto thou shalt come, and shalt go no further”. “The pride of your waves” stands in this one verse.

Verses 12–15 · Job 38:12–15 “Have you commanded the morning?” — the wicked shaken out of the earth

⭐ Commanding the dawn. “Have you made the dawn know its place” stands in this one verse, shachar, the dawn (dictionary), the word of Psalm 63:2 (now on these pages); and the Hebrew has a second written and read pair here, yeda’ta written yeda’tah with a final letter, “you made it know”. The KJV has “Hast thou commanded the morning since thy days; and caused the dayspring to know his place”, the NIV “Have you ever given orders to the morning, or shown the dawn its place”, the NWT 1984 “Did you cause the dawn to know its place”, the TLB “Have you ever once commanded the morning to appear” and the Douay “Didst thou since thy birth command the morning”.

⭐ Taking the earth by its edges. The dawn takes hold “of the edges of the earth” (kanfot ha’arets, the wings or skirts of the earth), which stands in this one verse, and shakes the wicked out of it, “that the wicked be shaken out of it”, which stands in this one verse, as one shakes a cloth (dictionary). The KJV has “That it might take hold of the ends of the earth, that the wicked might be shaken out of it”, the NIV “that it might take the earth by the edges and shake the wicked out of it”, the NWT 1984 “To take hold on the extremities of the earth, That the wicked ones might be shaken out from it”, the Douay “didst thou hold the extremities of the earth shaking them” and the TLB “to end the night’s wickedness”.

⭐ Clay under a seal. “Clay under a seal” stands in this one verse: at dawn the earth takes shape like clay that is pressed, and its features stand out like folds of a garment; and “from the wicked their light is withheld” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “It is turned as clay to the seal; and they stand as a garment”, the NIV “The earth takes shape like clay under a seal”, the NWT 1984 “It transforms itself like clay under a seal” and the Douay “The seal shall be restored as clay”.

Verses 16–21 · Job 38:16–21 the springs of the sea, the gates of death — “where is the way where light dwells?”

⭐ The sea’s springs, and the gates. “The search of the deep” stands in this one verse, cheqer tehom, and the gates of death are “the gates of death”, which stands in three verses: Psalm 9:14, Psalm 107:18 and this one, none of the others translated here yet; and then “the gates of deep darkness” stands in this one verse, with tsalmavet, the word of the valley of Psalm 23 (Psalm 23:4, now on these pages). The KJV has “Hast thou entered into the springs of the sea? or hast thou walked in the search of the depth?”, the NIV “Have you journeyed to the springs of the sea or walked in the recesses of the deep?”, the NWT 1984 “Have you come to the sources of the sea”, the Douay “Hast thou entered into the depths of the sea” and the TLB “Have you explored the springs from which the seas come”. For the gates, the KJV has “the doors of the shadow of death”, the NIV “the gates of the deepest darkness”, the NWT 1984 “the gates of deep shadow” and the Douay “the darksome doors”.

⭐ Where light dwells. “Where is the way where light dwells?”, with darkness asked after beside it, and the sarcasm of verse 21: “you know, for then you were born” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Knowest thou it, because thou wast then born? or because the number of thy days is great?”, the NIV “Surely you know, for you were already born! You have lived so many years!”, the NWT 1984 “Have you come to know because at that time you were being born”, the Douay “Didst thou know then that thou shouldst be born?” and the TLB “But of course you know all this! For you were born before it was all created”.

Verses 22–27 · Job 38:22–27 the storehouses of snow and hail — a rain on land where no man is

⭐ The storehouses. “The storehouses of snow, and the storehouses of hail”: the word is otsar, a treasury (dictionary), and “the storehouses of hail” stands in this one verse; the hail is “held back for a time of distress, for a day of battle and war”, which stands in this one verse, the hail of the plague (Exodus 9:23, now on these pages) and the hail that fought for Israel at Gibeon (Joshua 10:11, now on these pages). The KJV has “Hast thou entered into the treasures of the snow?”, the NIV “Have you entered the storehouses of the snow”, the NWT 1984 “Have you entered into the storehouses of the snow” and the TLB “Have you visited the treasuries of the snow”; for the hail’s purpose, the KJV has “against the time of trouble, against the day of battle and war” and the Douay “for the time of the enemy, against the day of battle and war”.

⭐ Rain where no man is. “Who has split a channel for the flood” stands in this one verse, and the rain falls “on a land where no man is” in this form in this one verse, to satisfy sho’ah umesho’ah, “ruin and waste”; Zephaniah has the same pair, spelled with a vav, for the day of Jehovah (Zephaniah 1:15, now on these pages). The KJV has “To cause it to rain on the earth, where no man is; on the wilderness, wherein there is no man”, the NIV “an uninhabited desert”, the NWT 1984 “To make it rain upon the land where there is no man” and the Douay “That it should rain on the earth without man in the wilderness”.

Verses 28–33 · Job 38:28–33 “Does the rain have a father?” — ice, the Pleiades and Orion, the statutes of heaven

⭐ A father for the rain. “Does the rain have a father?”, and “the drops of dew”, eglei tal, stands in this one verse; the ice has a womb and the frost of heaven a mother, and the waters hide themselves “like stone” in winter. The KJV has “Hath the rain a father? or who hath begotten the drops of dew?”, the NIV “Does the rain have a father? Who fathers the drops of dew?”, the NWT 1984 “Does there exist a father for the rain, Or who gave birth to the dewdrops?”, the TLB “Has the rain a father? Where does dew come from?” and the Douay “Who is the father of rain?”.

⭐ The Pleiades, Orion, the Bear. “The bonds of the Pleiades” stands in this one verse, with ma’adannot, the word of Agag’s fate (1 Samuel 15:32, now on these pages), read here as bonds; “the cords of Orion” stands in this one verse, “the Mazzaroth” stands in this one verse, and “and the Bear, with her sons” stands in this one verse. The star names are old and the versions disagree: the KJV has “Canst thou bind the sweet influences of Pleiades, or loose the bands of Orion?” and “guide Arcturus with his sons”, the NIV “Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades? Can you loosen Orion’s belt?” and “lead out the Bear with its cubs”, the NWT 1984 “the Kiʹmah constellation” and “the Keʹsil constellation”, the TLB “Can you restrain Orion or Pleiades?” and the Douay “canst thou stop the turning about of Arcturus?”.

⭐ The statutes of heaven. “The statutes of heaven” stands in two verses, this one and Jeremiah 33:25 (now on these pages), and the neighbor is the covenant of day and night, “the statutes of the moon and the stars” (Jeremiah 31:35–36, now on these pages); “its rule on the earth” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “Knowest thou the ordinances of heaven? canst thou set the dominion thereof in the earth?”, the NIV “Do you know the laws of the heavens?”, the NWT 1984 “Have you come to know the statutes of the heavens”, the TLB “Do you know the laws of the universe” and the Douay “Dost thou know the order of heaven”.

Verses 34–38 · Job 38:34–38 the cloud, the lightnings — “Here we are” — and two rare words for the inward parts

⭐ Lightnings that answer. “Can you lift your voice to the cloud”, and “Here we are”, hinenu, stands in eight verses: Genesis 44:16 and 50:18, Numbers 14:40, Joshua 9:25, 2 Samuel 5:1, Jeremiah 3:22, this one and Ezra 9:15, in which it is a people answering; here it is the lightnings that would answer. The KJV has “Canst thou send lightnings, that they may go and say unto thee, Here we are?”, the NIV “Do they report to you, ‘Here we are’?”, the NWT 1984 “say to you, ‘Here we are!’?”, the TLB “Can you make lightning appear and cause it to strike as you direct it?” and the Douay “will they return and say to thee: Here we are?”.

⚠ Two words the versions cannot place. “Who put wisdom in the tuchot? Or who gave understanding to the sekhvi?” The word sekhvi stands in this one verse, and tuchot is a word the book of Psalms has once, “you desire truth in the tuchot” (Psalm 51:8, now on these pages), where it is the inward parts. The versions diverge in four directions: the KJV has “wisdom in the inward parts” and “understanding to the heart”, the NIV “Who gives the ibis wisdom or gives the rooster understanding?”, the NWT 1984 “Who put wisdom in the cloud layers”, the Douay “who gave the cock understanding?” and the TLB “Who gives intuition and instinct?”. This translation prints “the inward parts” for the first, which Psalm 51:8 supports, and “the mind” for the second, which has no parallel to check it against.

⭐ The water-skins of heaven. “The water-skins of heaven” stands in this one verse, nivlei shamayim, the jars or skins that are tipped over to make rain, and the last picture is of the dust running into a mass and the clods sticking together, the parched earth at the first rain. The KJV has “who can stay the bottles of heaven”, the NIV “Who can tip over the water jars of the heavens”, the NWT 1984 “the water jars of heaven—who can tip [them] over”, the TLB “Who can tilt the water jars of heaven” and the Douay “who can make the harmony of heaven to sleep?”.

Verses 39–41 · Job 38:39–41 the lioness and the raven — “Who prepares for the raven its food?”

⭐ The lioness. The speech turns from the sky to animals and begins with the hunter: “prey for the lioness” stands in this one verse, with lavi, the lioness, and her young lions in the covert. The KJV has “Wilt thou hunt the prey for the lion?”, the NIV “Do you hunt the prey for the lioness and satisfy the hunger of the lions”, the NWT 1984 “Can you hunt prey for a lion itself”, the TLB “Can you stalk prey like a lioness” and the Douay “Wilt thou take the prey for the lioness”.

⭐ The raven. “Who prepares for the raven its food” stands in this one verse, and the young ravens “cry out to God”, which stands in this one verse, wandering for lack of food. The raven is the first bird of the flood (Genesis 8:7, now on these pages), the bird that fed Elijah at the brook (1 Kings 17:6, now on these pages), and the one Jesus names, “consider the ravens” (Luke 12:24, now on these pages). The KJV has “Who provideth for the raven his food? when his young ones cry unto God, they wander for lack of meat”, the NIV “Who provides food for the raven when its young cry out to God and wander about for lack of food?”, the NWT 1984 “Who prepares for the raven its food When its own young ones cry to God for help”, the TLB “Who provides for the ravens when their young cry out to God” and the Douay “Who provideth food for the raven, when her young ones cry to God”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “the storm” for se’arah; “counsel” for etsah; “founded” for yasad; “cornerstone” for pinnah; “the sons of God” for benei Elohim; “storehouses” for otsarot; “statutes” for chuqqot; “Jehovah” for the name, which speaks in verse 1 and is not named again in the chapter. The Hebrew has two written and read pairs in this chapter, at verse 1 (min hase’arah) and verse 12 (yada’ta), kept as it is printed.

Echoes paid. The storm that takes Elijah (2 Kings 2:1) and Ezekiel’s storm (Ezekiel 1:4); the cornerstone of Psalm 118:22; the sons of God of Job 1:6 and Genesis 6:2; the waters of Genesis 1:9; the dawn of Psalm 63:2; the valley of Psalm 23:4; the hail of Exodus 9:23 and Joshua 10:11; Zephaniah 1:15; Agag’s word in 1 Samuel 15:32; the statutes of Jeremiah 31:35–36; Psalm 51:8; the ravens of Genesis 8:7, 1 Kings 17:6 and Luke 12:24; and Job’s own saying back in Job 42:3.

Echoes planted. The second round of the answer (Job 40:1, 40:6, 40:7); the other gates of death (Psalm 9:14, 107:18); the line stretched in Isaiah 34:11; the other statutes of heaven (Jeremiah 33:25); and the other “here we are” verses (Genesis 44:16, 50:18, Numbers 14:40, Joshua 9:25, 2 Samuel 5:1, Jeremiah 3:22, Ezra 9:15). None of these is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Job stands on these pages at chapters 1, 38 and 42, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Job 2.
