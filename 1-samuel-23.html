“Will the lords of Keilah hand me over?”: David inquires of Jehovah and saves Keilah from a Philistine raid on its threshing floors; Abiathar comes to him with the ephod, and when Saul calls out the people to besiege the walled town, the ephod answers that Saul will come and Keilah will hand David over. David leaves with about six hundred men for the strongholds of the wilderness of Ziph. Jonathan finds him at Horesh, tells him he will be king, and the two cut a covenant before Jehovah. The Ziphites betray him to Saul; in the wilderness of Maon Saul is closing in round the mountain when a Philistine raid calls him away, and the place is named the Crag of the Divisions. David goes up to the strongholds of En-gedi.
Undated: in Saul’s reign, soon after the massacre at Nob in chapter 22; Saul seeks David “all the days”, and the stay in the wilderness of Ziph is of unstated length.

וַיַּגִּדוּ לְדָוִד, לֵאמֹר: הִנֵּה פְלִשְׁתִּים נִלְחָמִים בִּקְעִילָה, וְהֵמָּה שֹׁסִים אֶת-הַגֳּרָנוֹת.
And they told David, saying: ‘Look — the Philistines are fighting against Keilah, and they are plundering the threshing floors.’note
Y dieron aviso a David, diciendo: «Mira, los filisteos están peleando contra Keila, y están saqueando las eras».
2
וַיִּשְׁאַל דָּוִד בַּיהוָה, לֵאמֹר, הַאֵלֵךְ וְהִכֵּיתִי, בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים הָאֵלֶּה; {ס} וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל-דָּוִד, לֵךְ וְהִכִּיתָ בַפְּלִשְׁתִּים, וְהוֹשַׁעְתָּ, אֶת-קְעִילָה.
And David inquired of Jehovah, saying: ‘Shall I go and strike these Philistines?’ And Jehovah said to David: ‘Go, and strike the Philistines, and save Keilah.’note
Y David consultó a Jehová, diciendo: «¿Iré y heriré a estos filisteos?». Y Jehová dijo a David: «Ve, y hiere a los filisteos, y salva a Keila».
3
וַיֹּאמְרוּ אַנְשֵׁי דָוִד, אֵלָיו, הִנֵּה אֲנַחְנוּ פֹה בִּיהוּדָה, יְרֵאִים; וְאַף כִּי-נֵלֵךְ קְעִלָה, אֶל-מַעַרְכוֹת פְּלִשְׁתִּים. {ס}
And David’s men said to him: ‘Look — we are afraid here in Judah; how much more if we go to Keilah, against the battle lines of the Philistines!’note
Y los hombres de David le dijeron: «Mira, nosotros tenemos miedo aquí en Judá; ¡cuánto más si vamos a Keila, contra las líneas de batalla de los filisteos!».
4
וַיּוֹסֶף עוֹד דָּוִד לִשְׁאוֹל בַּיהוָה, וַיַּעֲנֵהוּ יְהוָה; וַיֹּאמֶר, קוּם רֵד קְעִילָה--כִּי-אֲנִי נֹתֵן אֶת-פְּלִשְׁתִּים, בְּיָדֶךָ.
And David inquired of Jehovah yet again; and Jehovah answered him, and said: ‘Arise, go down to Keilah, for I am giving the Philistines into your hand.’note
Y David volvió a consultar a Jehová; y Jehová le respondió, y dijo: «Levántate, baja a Keila, porque yo doy a los filisteos en tu mano».
5
וַיֵּלֶךְ דָּוִד וַאֲנָשָׁו קְעִילָה וַיִּלָּחֶם בַּפְּלִשְׁתִּים, וַיִּנְהַג אֶת-מִקְנֵיהֶם, וַיַּךְ בָּהֶם, מַכָּה גְדוֹלָה; וַיֹּשַׁע דָּוִד, אֵת יֹשְׁבֵי קְעִילָה. {ס}
And David and his men went to Keilah, and fought with the Philistines, and drove away their livestock, and struck them with a great slaughter; and David saved the inhabitants of Keilah.note
Y David y sus hombres fueron a Keila, y pelearon contra los filisteos, y se llevaron su ganado, y los hirieron con una gran matanza; y David salvó a los habitantes de Keila.
6
וַיְהִי, בִּבְרֹחַ אֶבְיָתָר בֶּן-אֲחִימֶלֶךְ אֶל-דָּוִד--קְעִילָה; אֵפוֹד, יָרַד בְּיָדוֹ.
And it happened, when Abiathar son of Ahimelech fled to David, to Keilah, [that] an ephod came down in his hand.note
Y sucedió que, cuando Abiatar hijo de Ahimelec huyó a David, a Keila, un efod bajó en su mano.
7
וַיֻּגַּד לְשָׁאוּל, כִּי-בָא דָוִד קְעִילָה; וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל, נִכַּר אֹתוֹ אֱלֹהִים בְּיָדִי--כִּי נִסְגַּר לָבוֹא, בְּעִיר דְּלָתַיִם וּבְרִיחַ.
And it was told Saul that David had come to Keilah. And Saul said: ‘God has disowned him into my hand; for he has shut himself in by entering a city with gates and bars.’note
Y se le dio aviso a Saúl de que David había venido a Keila. Y Saúl dijo: «Dios lo ha repudiado en mi mano; porque se ha encerrado al entrar en una ciudad con puertas y barras».
8
וַיְשַׁמַּע שָׁאוּל אֶת-כָּל-הָעָם, לַמִּלְחָמָה, לָרֶדֶת קְעִילָה, לָצוּר אֶל-דָּוִד וְאֶל-אֲנָשָׁיו.
And Saul summoned all the people to war, to go down to Keilah, to besiege David and his men.note
Y Saúl convocó a todo el pueblo a la guerra, para bajar a Keila, para sitiar a David y a sus hombres.
9
וַיֵּדַע דָּוִד--כִּי עָלָיו, שָׁאוּל מַחֲרִישׁ הָרָעָה; וַיֹּאמֶר אֶל-אֶבְיָתָר הַכֹּהֵן, הַגִּישָׁה הָאֵפוֹד. {ס}
And David knew that Saul was devising the evil against him; and he said to Abiathar the priest: ‘Bring the ephod here.’note
Y David supo que Saúl tramaba el mal contra él; y dijo a Abiatar el sacerdote: «Trae aquí el efod».
10
וַיֹּאמֶר, דָּוִד, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, שָׁמֹעַ שָׁמַע עַבְדְּךָ כִּי-מְבַקֵּשׁ שָׁאוּל לָבוֹא אֶל-קְעִילָה--לְשַׁחֵת לָעִיר, בַּעֲבוּרִי.
And David said: ‘Jehovah, God of Israel, your servant has surely heard that Saul is seeking to come to Keilah, to destroy the city on my account.note
Y David dijo: «Jehová, Dios de Israel, tu siervo ha oído de cierto que Saúl procura venir a Keila, para destruir la ciudad por causa mía.
11
הֲיַסְגִּרֻנִי בַעֲלֵי קְעִילָה בְיָדוֹ הֲיֵרֵד שָׁאוּל, כַּאֲשֶׁר שָׁמַע עַבְדֶּךָ--יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, הַגֶּד-נָא לְעַבְדֶּךָ; {ס} וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, יֵרֵד. {ס}
Will the lords of Keilah hand me over into his hand? Will Saul come down, as your servant has heard? Jehovah, God of Israel, tell your servant, please.’ And Jehovah said: ‘He will come down.’note
¿Me entregarán los señores de Keila en su mano? ¿Bajará Saúl, como ha oído tu siervo? Jehová, Dios de Israel, te ruego que se lo digas a tu siervo». Y Jehová dijo: «Bajará».
12
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד--הֲיַסְגִּרוּ בַּעֲלֵי קְעִילָה אֹתִי וְאֶת-אֲנָשַׁי, בְּיַד-שָׁאוּל; וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, יַסְגִּירוּ. {ס}
And David said: ‘Will the lords of Keilah hand me and my men over into the hand of Saul?’ And Jehovah said: ‘They will hand [you] over.’note
Y David dijo: «¿Entregarán los señores de Keila a mí y a mis hombres en la mano de Saúl?». Y Jehová dijo: «[Te] entregarán».
13
וַיָּקָם דָּוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו כְּשֵׁשׁ-מֵאוֹת אִישׁ, וַיֵּצְאוּ מִקְּעִלָה, וַיִּתְהַלְּכוּ, בַּאֲשֶׁר יִתְהַלָּכוּ; וּלְשָׁאוּל הֻגַּד, כִּי-נִמְלַט דָּוִד מִקְּעִילָה, וַיֶּחְדַּל, לָצֵאת.
And David and his men arose, about six hundred men, and went out of Keilah, and went about wherever they went about. And it was told Saul that David had escaped from Keilah; and he ceased to go out.note
Y David y sus hombres se levantaron, unos seiscientos hombres, y salieron de Keila, y anduvieron por donde anduvieron. Y a Saúl se le dio aviso de que David había escapado de Keila; y dejó de salir.
14
וַיֵּשֶׁב דָּוִד בַּמִּדְבָּר בַּמְּצָדוֹת, וַיֵּשֶׁב בָּהָר בְּמִדְבַּר-זִיף; וַיְבַקְשֵׁהוּ שָׁאוּל כָּל-הַיָּמִים, וְלֹא-נְתָנוֹ אֱלֹהִים בְּיָדוֹ.
And David stayed in the wilderness, in the strongholds, and stayed in the hill country, in the wilderness of Ziph. And Saul sought him all the days, and God did not give him into his hand.note
Y David se quedó en el desierto, en las fortalezas, y se quedó en la región montañosa, en el desierto de Zif. Y Saúl lo buscaba todos los días, y Dios no lo dio en su mano.
15
וַיַּרְא דָּוִד, כִּי-יָצָא שָׁאוּל לְבַקֵּשׁ אֶת-נַפְשׁוֹ; וְדָוִד בְּמִדְבַּר-זִיף, בַּחֹרְשָׁה. {ס}
And David saw that Saul had come out to seek his life; and David was in the wilderness of Ziph, at Horesh.note
Y David vio que Saúl había salido a buscar su vida; y David estaba en el desierto de Zif, en Hores.
16
וַיָּקָם יְהוֹנָתָן בֶּן-שָׁאוּל, וַיֵּלֶךְ אֶל-דָּוִד חֹרְשָׁה; וַיְחַזֵּק אֶת-יָדוֹ, בֵּאלֹהִים.
And Jonathan son of Saul arose, and went to David at Horesh, and strengthened his hand in God.note
Y Jonatán hijo de Saúl se levantó, y fue a David a Hores, y fortaleció su mano en Dios.
17
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלָיו אַל-תִּירָא, כִּי לֹא תִמְצָאֲךָ יַד שָׁאוּל אָבִי, וְאַתָּה תִּמְלֹךְ עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל, וְאָנֹכִי אֶהְיֶה-לְּךָ לְמִשְׁנֶה; וְגַם-שָׁאוּל אָבִי, יֹדֵעַ כֵּן.
And he said to him: ‘Do not be afraid, for the hand of Saul my father will not find you; and you will be king over Israel, and I will be second to you; and Saul my father also knows it is so.’note
Y le dijo: «No temas, porque la mano de Saúl mi padre no te hallará; y tú serás rey sobre Israel, y yo seré segundo a ti; y también Saúl mi padre sabe que es así».
18
וַיִּכְרְתוּ שְׁנֵיהֶם בְּרִית, לִפְנֵי יְהוָה; וַיֵּשֶׁב דָּוִד בַּחֹרְשָׁה, וִיהוֹנָתָן הָלַךְ לְבֵיתוֹ. {ס}
And the two of them cut a covenant before Jehovah; and David stayed at Horesh, and Jonathan went to his house.note
Y los dos cortaron un pacto delante de Jehová; y David se quedó en Hores, y Jonatán se fue a su casa.
19
וַיַּעֲלוּ זִפִים אֶל-שָׁאוּל, הַגִּבְעָתָה לֵאמֹר: הֲלוֹא דָוִד מִסְתַּתֵּר עִמָּנוּ בַמְּצָדוֹת, בַּחֹרְשָׁה--בְּגִבְעַת הַחֲכִילָה, אֲשֶׁר מִימִין הַיְשִׁימוֹן.
And Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah, saying: ‘Is not David hiding himself with us in the strongholds at Horesh, on the hill of Hachilah, which is south of the Jeshimon?note
Y subieron unos zifeos a Saúl, a Guibeá, diciendo: «¿No está David escondiéndose con nosotros en las fortalezas, en Hores, en el collado de Haquilá, que está al sur del Yesimón?
20
וְעַתָּה לְכָל-אַוַּת נַפְשְׁךָ הַמֶּלֶךְ, לָרֶדֶת--רֵד; וְלָנוּ הַסְגִּירוֹ, בְּיַד הַמֶּלֶךְ.
And now, according to all the craving of your soul to come down, O king, come down; and ours [is] to hand him over into the king’s hand.’note
Y ahora, conforme a toda el ansia de tu alma de bajar, oh rey, baja; y a nosotros [nos toca] entregarlo en la mano del rey».
21
וַיֹּאמֶר שָׁאוּל, בְּרוּכִים אַתֶּם לַיהוָה: כִּי חֲמַלְתֶּם, עָלָי.
And Saul said: ‘Blessed are you of Jehovah, for you have had pity on me.note
Y Saúl dijo: «Benditos sean ustedes de Jehová, porque se han compadecido de mí.
22
לְכוּ-נָא הָכִינוּ עוֹד, וּדְעוּ וּרְאוּ אֶת-מְקוֹמוֹ אֲשֶׁר תִּהְיֶה רַגְלוֹ--מִי רָאָהוּ, שָׁם: כִּי אָמַר אֵלַי, עָרוֹם יַעְרִם הוּא.
Go, please, make sure yet again, and know and see his place, where his foot will be, [and] who has seen him there; for it has been said to me, “He is surely cunning.”note
Vayan, les ruego, asegúrense todavía más, y sepan y vean su lugar, dónde estará su pie, [y] quién lo ha visto allí; porque se me ha dicho: “De cierto es astuto”.
23
וּרְאוּ וּדְעוּ, מִכֹּל הַמַּחֲבֹאִים אֲשֶׁר יִתְחַבֵּא שָׁם, וְשַׁבְתֶּם אֵלַי אֶל-נָכוֹן, וְהָלַכְתִּי אִתְּכֶם; וְהָיָה, אִם-יֶשְׁנוֹ בָאָרֶץ--וְחִפַּשְׂתִּי אֹתוֹ, בְּכֹל אַלְפֵי יְהוּדָה.
And see, and know about all the hiding places where he hides himself, and come back to me with certainty, and I will go with you; and it shall be, if he is in the land, that I will search him out among all the thousands of Judah.’note
Y vean, y sepan de todos los escondites donde se esconde, y vuelvan a mí con certeza, y yo iré con ustedes; y sucederá que, si está en la tierra, lo rastrearé entre todos los millares de Judá».
24
וַיָּקוּמוּ וַיֵּלְכוּ זִיפָה, לִפְנֵי שָׁאוּל; וְדָוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו בְּמִדְבַּר מָעוֹן, בָּעֲרָבָה--אֶל, יְמִין הַיְשִׁימוֹן.
And they arose, and went to Ziph ahead of Saul. And David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the Arabah, south of the Jeshimon.note
Y se levantaron, y se fueron a Zif delante de Saúl. Y David y sus hombres estaban en el desierto de Maón, en el Arabá, al sur del Yesimón.
25
וַיֵּלֶךְ שָׁאוּל וַאֲנָשָׁיו, לְבַקֵּשׁ, וַיַּגִּדוּ לְדָוִד, וַיֵּרֶד הַסֶּלַע וַיֵּשֶׁב בְּמִדְבַּר מָעוֹן; וַיִּשְׁמַע שָׁאוּל, וַיִּרְדֹּף אַחֲרֵי-דָוִד מִדְבַּר מָעוֹן.
And Saul and his men went to seek [him]; and they told David, and he went down to the crag, and stayed in the wilderness of Maon. And Saul heard, and pursued after David [into] the wilderness of Maon.note
Y Saúl y sus hombres fueron a buscar[lo]; y dieron aviso a David, y bajó al peñasco, y se quedó en el desierto de Maón. Y Saúl lo oyó, y persiguió a David [por] el desierto de Maón.
26
וַיֵּלֶךְ שָׁאוּל מִצַּד הָהָר, מִזֶּה, וְדָוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו מִצַּד הָהָר, מִזֶּה; וַיְהִי דָוִד נֶחְפָּז לָלֶכֶת, מִפְּנֵי שָׁאוּל, וְשָׁאוּל וַאֲנָשָׁיו עֹטְרִים אֶל-דָּוִד וְאֶל-אֲנָשָׁיו, לְתָפְשָׂם.
And Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side of the mountain. And David was hurrying to get away from before Saul, and Saul and his men were closing in around David and his men, to seize them.note
Y Saúl iba por este lado del monte, y David y sus hombres por aquel lado del monte. Y David se apresuraba a irse de delante de Saúl, y Saúl y sus hombres iban cercando a David y a sus hombres, para apresarlos.
27
וּמַלְאָךְ בָּא, אֶל-שָׁאוּל לֵאמֹר: מַהֲרָה וְלֵכָה, כִּי-פָשְׁטוּ פְלִשְׁתִּים עַל-הָאָרֶץ.
But a messenger came to Saul, saying: ‘Hurry, and come, for the Philistines have made a raid on the land!’note
Pero un mensajero vino a Saúl, diciendo: «¡Date prisa, y ven, porque los filisteos han hecho una incursión en la tierra!».

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · “Shall I go and strike these Philistines?” — Keilah’s threshing floors, and a question that is answered

Keilah and its threshing floors. “Look — the Philistines are fighting against Keilah, and they are plundering the threshing floors”: ve-hemmah shosim et-ha-goranot. Keilah is a town of Judah’s lowland, listed with Achzib and Mareshah (Joshua 15:44, already on these pages) (Keilah). The name stands in sixteen verses of the Hebrew Bible, most of them in this chapter. The threshing floor is where the harvested grain lay to be beaten out, so the raiders have come at harvest, for the year’s bread. The ASV has “and are robbing the threshing-floors”; the NWT 1984 “and they are pillaging the threshing floors”; the Geneva “and spoil the barns”. The Greek has the Philistines plundering and trampling the threshing floors.

David asks. “And David inquired of Jehovah, saying: ‘Shall I go and strike these Philistines?’”: va-yish’al David ba-YHVH (sha’al). In the last chapter Doeg told Saul that the priest of Nob had inquired of Jehovah for David (22:10, already on these pages); now David inquires himself. When Saul asked, “Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will you give them into the hand of Israel?”, “he did not answer him that day” (14:37, already on these pages). David asks the same kind of question twice, in verses 2 and 4, and is answered both times. How he asked, the chapter tells only in verse 6. The ASV has “Therefore David inquired of Jehovah, saying, Shall I go and smite these Philistines?”; the NWT 2013 “Should I go and strike down these Phi·lisʹtines?”; the TLB “Shall I go and attack them?”

Afraid here in Judah. “Look — we are afraid here in Judah; how much more if we go to Keilah, against the battle lines of the Philistines!”: el-ma’arkhot Pelishtim, the word of Goliath’s “I taunt the battle lines of Israel this day” (17:10, already on these pages). These are the men of the cave of Adullam (22:2), and they are afraid even here, in Judah. The second inquiry is for them, and the answer is firmer: “Arise, go down to Keilah, for I am giving the Philistines into your hand”, ki-ani noten, a participle, the thing already under way. The NWT 1984 has “because I am giving the Phi·lisʹtines into your hand”; the ASV “for I will deliver the Philistines into thy hand”; the Douay has the men go “against the hands of the Philistines”. The Greek has them ask how it will be if they go to Keilah, into the spoils of the Philistines.

David saved Keilah. “And drove away their livestock, and struck them with a great slaughter”: makkah gedolah, as when David “struck them with a great slaughter” in 19:8 (already on these pages) (makkah). “And David saved the inhabitants of Keilah”: va-yosha David (yasha). Jehovah had told Samuel that the man he would send “will save my people from the hand of the Philistines” (9:16), and the sons of worthlessness asked of Saul, “How will this one save us?” (10:27, both already on these pages). Here the verb has David for its subject, and the Philistines for the enemy. The NWT 1984 has “and David came to be the savior of the inhabitants of Keiʹlah”; the KJV “So David saved the inhabitants of Keilah”; the TLB leaves David out of the sentence: “and so the people of Keilah were saved”.

Verse 6 · “An ephod came down in his hand” — Abiathar reaches David, and the instrument he carried

The ephod in his hand. “And it happened, when Abiathar son of Ahimelech fled to David, to Keilah, [that] an ephod came down in his hand”: efod yarad be-yado (ephod). The last chapter left Abiathar escaping from Nob and fleeing “after David” (22:20, already on these pages), and David telling him, “Stay with me” (22:23) (Zadok and Abiathar). This verse says what he brought: the ephod, which a priest of his house carried in Saul’s camp (14:3, already on these pages). In the Hebrew the ephod is the subject: it “came down in his hand”. The NWT 1984 keeps it so: “there was an ephʹod that went down in his hand”; the ASV has “that he came down with an ephod in his hand”. The NIV, the TLB and the Geneva put the verse in brackets. The TLB also adds what the ephod was for: “to get answers for David”.

When did it come? The verse looks back from after the battle, and it raises a question: if Abiathar came to David “to Keilah”, how did David inquire before going there, in verses 2 and 4? Readings differ. Abiathar, who had joined David before (22:20–23), may have come down with him to Keilah; the Greek says so: “and he went down with David to Keilah, having an ephod in his hand”. Or David asked by another means before the ephod came, perhaps through Gad the prophet (22:5, already on these pages) (reported, not decided). What the verse makes plain is that from now on David has the ephod, and in verse 9 he sends for it.

Verses 7–8 · “God has disowned him into my hand” — Saul and the walled town

Disowned. “God has disowned him into my hand”: nikkar oto Elohim be-yadi. The verb is from the root of nakar, to recognize (nakar), and in this form it means to treat as a stranger, to disown: Jeremiah uses it of the people who “made this place foreign, and burned incense in it to other gods” (Jeremiah 19:4, already on these pages). Saul says that God has cast David off and given him over. The Greek has “God has sold him into my hands”, reading makar, to sell, one letter different; the NWT 1984 follows the Greek: “God has sold him into my hand”. The ASV has “God hath delivered him into my hand”, and the KJV “into mine hand”; the NWT 2013 “God has handed him over to me”. Verse 14 answers him: “and God did not give him into his hand.”

Shut in. “For he has shut himself in by entering a city with gates and bars”: ki nisgar lavo be-ir delatayim u-veriach. “Gates and bars” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: the fortified cities of Bashan (Deuteronomy 3:5, already on these pages), Keilah here, Solomon’s Beth-horon (2 Chronicles 8:5), and the towns Asa fortified (2 Chronicles 14:6 in the Hebrew numbering). The verb is sagar, to shut (sagar). The rest of the chapter uses its causative, hisgir, to shut someone up into another’s hand, to hand him over: the lords of Keilah would do it (verses 11–12), and the Ziphites offer to (verse 20). A wall keeps an enemy out only while the town inside wants it to. The NWT 2013 has “for he has trapped himself by entering a city with gates and bars”; the TLB “for he has trapped himself in a walled city!”

Summoned. “And Saul summoned all the people to war”: va-yeshamma Sha’ul et-kol-ha-am, from shama, to hear: he made them hear. Saul summons the people with this verb in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, and before the war on Amalek, “And Saul summoned the people, and mustered them at Telaim” (15:4, already on these pages). Then it was Amalek; now it is his son-in-law, in a town of Judah. The ASV has “And Saul summoned all the people to war”; the KJV “And Saul called all the people together to war”.

Verses 9–12 · “Will the lords of Keilah hand me over?” — the ephod, two questions, and two one-word answers

Devising, or keeping silent. “And David knew that Saul was devising the evil against him”: ki alav Sha’ul machrish ha-ra’ah. Charash is to plough, to cut into, and so to devise: “Do not devise evil against your neighbour” (Proverbs 3:29, already on these pages). A second charash means to be silent, and the participle machrish stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; of Abraham’s servant at the well, who watched Rebekah and “kept silent” (Genesis 24:21); of Saul himself on the day he was chosen king, when the sons of worthlessness despised him, “But he was like one keeping silent” (10:27, both already on these pages); and of the fool who “keeps silent” and is counted wise (Proverbs 17:28). The Greek read it so here: “Saul does not keep silent about the evil concerning him”. The ASV has “that Saul was devising mischief against him”; the KJV “that Saul secretly practised mischief against him”; the NWT 1984 “that Saul was fabricating mischief against him”.

Bring the ephod here. “Bring the ephod here”: haggishah ha-efod. Saul gave the same order to Ahijah, “Bring the ark of God here” (14:18, already on these pages), where the Greek reads “the ephod”, and then broke off the inquiry when the noise in the Philistine camp grew: “Withdraw your hand” (14:19). David waits for the answer. He will give the order to Abiathar again at Ziklag (30:7, now on these pages). The ASV has “Bring hither the ephod”; the NWT 1984 “Do bring the ephʹod near.”

On my account. “Jehovah, God of Israel, your servant has surely heard that Saul is seeking to come to Keilah, to destroy the city on my account”: shamoa shama, the verb doubled. The city of the priests had just been struck with the edge of the sword because of him (22:19, 22, already on these pages); the fear is not idle. The ASV has “O Jehovah, the God of Israel, thy servant hath surely heard”; the TLB “I have heard that Saul is planning to come and destroy Keilah because I am here.”

The lords of Keilah. “Will the lords of Keilah hand me over into his hand?”: ha-yasgiruni ba’alei Qe’ilah. A ba’al is an owner, a master (ba’al), and the ba’alei of a town are its men of property and standing: “the lords of Jericho fought against you” (Joshua 24:11, already on these pages). The ba’alei of Shechem stand in eleven verses of the Hebrew Bible, all of them in Judges 9: the men who made Abimelech king and then dealt treacherously with him (Judges 9:6, 23, already on these pages). The KJV has “Will the men of Keilah deliver me up into his hand?”; the Geneva “Will the lords of Keilah deliver me up into his hand?”; the NWT 1984 “Will the landowners of Keiʹlah surrender me into his hand?”; the NWT 2013 “Will the leaders of Keiʹlah surrender me into his hand?”; the NIV “Will the citizens of Keilah surrender me to him?”

What the law forbids. To hand over a fugitive is what the law tells a town not to do. “You shall not hand over to his master a slave who has escaped to you from his master” (Deuteronomy 23:16), and of the city of refuge, “they shall not deliver up the manslayer into his hand” (Joshua 20:5, both already on these pages): lo tasgir, lo yasgiru, the verb of this verse. David asks whether the town he has just saved will do the thing that is forbidden.

Two answers, one word each. Jehovah answers the second question first: “He will come down”, yered; and when David asks the first again, “They will hand [you] over”, yasgiru. Each answer is a single word in the Hebrew. The NWT 1984 has “They will do the surrendering”; the ASV “They will deliver thee up”; the NIV “They will.” The Greek has no verse 12; in verse 11 it asks instead whether he will be shut in, and the answer is that he will.

An answer about what would happen. Neither answer comes to pass: David leaves, and Saul “ceased to go out” (verse 13). What Jehovah tells him is what Saul and the men of Keilah would do if he stayed. For that reason the passage became a standard proof text in the sixteenth-century debate over God’s knowledge of what would happen under conditions that never come about, the “middle knowledge” argued by the Jesuit Luis de Molina, read beside the saying of Jesus that Tyre and Sidon “would have repented long ago” (Matthew 11:21, already on these pages) (reported, not verified). Other readers take the answers simply as a warning of what was about to happen. The chapter itself draws no lesson from it.

Verses 13–14 · “About six hundred men” — wherever they went, the strongholds of the wilderness, and the God who did not give him up

Six hundred. “And David and his men arose, about six hundred men”: ke-shesh-me’ot ish. Four hundred had gathered to him at Adullam (22:2, already on these pages); now there are six hundred, the number he will take to Gath (27:2, now on these pages) and to the brook Besor (30:9, now on these pages). The Greek keeps the earlier number, “about four hundred”. The KJV has “which were about six hundred”; the NWT 2013 “about 600 in number”.

Wherever they went. “And went about wherever they went about”: va-yithallekhu ba-asher yithallakhu. A verb repeated with asher between is the Hebrew way of leaving a thing open: David tells Ittai in Absalom’s revolt, “I go where I go” (2 Samuel 15:20), and God answers Moses, “I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE” (Exodus 3:14, already on these pages). The KJV has “and went whithersoever they could go”; the NWT 1984 “and continued walking about wherever they could walk about”; the Douay “wandered up and down uncertain where they should stay”; the TLB “and began roaming the countryside”. “And he ceased to go out”: va-yechdal; the Geneva has “and he left off his journey”.

The strongholds. “And David stayed in the wilderness, in the strongholds”: ba-metsadot, the plural of metsad, from the root of metsudah, the stronghold of the last chapter (metsudah). The plural stands in nine verses of the Hebrew Bible, three of them in this chapter (verses 14, 19 and 29). Israel hid in them once before, from Midian: “the dens that are in the mountains, and the caves, and the strongholds” (Judges 6:2, already on these pages). “In the hill country, in the wilderness of Ziph”: Ziph is a town of the hill country of Judah, listed with Maon and Carmel (Joshua 15:55, already on these pages) (Ziph). The NWT 1984 has “in places difficult to approach”; the KJV “in strong holds”; the TLB “in the wilderness caves”.

God did not give him. “And Saul sought him all the days, and God did not give him into his hand”: ve-lo-netano Elohim be-yado. Saul said in verse 7 that God had disowned David into his hand; the narrator says that God did not give him. The NWT 1984 has “and God did not give him into his hand”; its revision, the NWT 2013, “but Jehovah did not give him into his hand”, where the Hebrew has Elohim, God.

Verses 15–18 · “He strengthened his hand in God” — Jonathan at Horesh, and the last time the two meet

Saw, or feared. “And David saw that Saul had come out to seek his life”: va-yar David. The same consonants can be read va-yira, “and he feared”, and the NWT 1984 reads them so: “And David continued in fear because Saul had gone out to look for his soul”. The vowels of the Hebrew text say “saw”; Jonathan’s first words, “Do not be afraid”, answer either reading. The ASV has “And David saw that Saul was come out to seek his life”; the NWT 2013 “David was aware that Saul had gone out to seek his life”.

Horesh, or the wood. “And David was in the wilderness of Ziph, at Horesh”: ba-Choreshah. A choresh is a thicket or wooded height, as in Ezekiel’s cedar “with forest shade” (Ezekiel 31:3), and with the article the word may mean “in the thicket”; the KJV has “in a wood”, and the ASV and the Geneva “in the wood”. The NWT 1984 and the NIV make it a name, Horesh, and so does this translation (Horesh). The Greek puts him in New Ziph, reading chadashah, new, for choreshah: dalet for resh, the two letters it confused in “Doeg the Syrian” (21:8, already on these pages).

Jonathan comes. “And Jonathan son of Saul arose, and went to David at Horesh, and strengthened his hand in God”: va-yechazzek et-yado be-Elohim (chazaq). To strengthen a man’s hands is to put heart into him, for good or ill: the men of Shechem “strengthened his hands to kill his brothers” (Judges 9:24, already on these pages). Saul’s son comes to the man Saul is hunting, and strengthens him “in God” (Jonathan). The NWT 1984 has “that he might strengthen his hand in regard to God”; the NIV “and helped him find strength in God”; the NWT 2013 “and he helped him find strength in Jehovah”; the Geneva “and comforted him in God”.

Second to you. “And you will be king over Israel, and I will be second to you”: ve-anokhi ehyeh-lekha le-mishneh. The mishneh is the second in rank: Joseph rode in “the second chariot” of Pharaoh, mirkevet ha-mishneh (Genesis 41:43, already on these pages), and Mordecai was “second to King Ahasuerus” (Esther 10:3). The king’s heir says it plainly to the man who will take the throne. “And Saul my father also knows it is so”: Saul had told Jonathan that as long as the son of Jesse lives “you shall not be established, you or your kingdom” (20:31, already on these pages), and he will say it to David himself, “I know that you will surely be king” (24:20, now on these pages). The ASV has “and I shall be next unto thee”; the NWT 1984 “and I myself shall become second to you”; the TLB “and I will be next to you, as my father is well aware.”

The last meeting. “And the two of them cut a covenant before Jehovah”: va-yikhretu shneihem brit (brit; karat). Jonathan cut a covenant with David when they met (18:3) and with his house when they parted (20:16, both already on these pages); here the two of them cut it together. “And David stayed at Horesh, and Jonathan went to his house.” The book never brings them together again: the next time Jonathan’s name is written, he dies beside his father on Gilboa (31:2, now on these pages), and David’s lament says, “I am distressed for you, my brother Jonathan” (2 Samuel 1:26, already on these pages). The TLB has “So the two of them renewed their pact of friendship”; the NIV “Then Jonathan went home, but David remained at Horesh.”

Verses 19–23 · “Is not David hiding himself with us?” — the Ziphites, a king who is pitied, and a man who is surely cunning

The Ziphites. “And Ziphites went up to Saul at Gibeah, saying: ‘Is not David hiding himself with us?’”: ha-lo David mistatter immanu. Ziph is a town of David’s own tribe; its men go to the Benjamite king at Gibeah (22:6–7, already on these pages). The words “David hiding himself” stand in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; when the Ziphites come to Saul a second time (26:1, now on these pages); and in the heading of Psalm 54, “when the Ziphites came and said to Saul, Is not David hiding himself with us?” (Psalm 54:2 in the Hebrew numbering).

South of the Jeshimon. “On the hill of Hachilah, which is south of the Jeshimon”: be-giv’at ha-Chakhilah asher mimin ha-yeshimon, literally “on the right of”: to someone facing east, the right hand is the south. Hachilah has not been identified (Hachilah). Ha-yeshimon is “the wasteland”, the view from Pisgah and from Peor (Numbers 21:20; 23:28, both already on these pages) (Jeshimon); here the same word names another waste, the wilderness of Judah west of the Dead Sea. The KJV has “which is on the south of Jeshimon”; the ASV “which is on the south of the desert”; the Geneva “which is on the right side of Jeshimon”; the NWT 1984 “which is to the right side of Je·shiʹmon”.

All the craving of your soul. “And now, according to all the craving of your soul to come down, O king, come down; and ours [is] to hand him over into the king’s hand”: le-khol-avvat nafshekha (avvah), the phrase of the law that lets Israel eat meat “as much as your soul desires” (Deuteronomy 12:15, already on these pages). What the king craves is David. “To hand him over”: hasgiro, the verb of verses 11 and 12. The NWT 1984 has “And now in harmony with all the craving of your soul, O king, to come down, come down”; the ASV “according to all the desire of thy soul to come down”; the TLB “and your fondest wish will be fulfilled!”

You have had pity on me. “Blessed are you of Jehovah, for you have had pity on me”: ki chamaltem alai (chamal). It is the verb of the war on Amalek: “do not spare him” (15:3), and “Saul and the people spared Agag” (15:9, both already on these pages). And it is the blessing Saul gave Samuel at Gilgal, with Agag still alive: “Blessed are you of Jehovah! I have carried out the word of Jehovah” (15:13). The ASV has “for ye have had compassion on me”; the Douay “for you have pitied my case”; the TLB “At last someone has had pity on me!”

He is surely cunning. “For it has been said to me, ‘He is surely cunning’”: arom ya’rim hu, the verb doubled. The root is the serpent’s, “more crafty than any wild animal” (Genesis 3:1) (arum), and the Gibeonites’, who “acted with cunning” (Joshua 9:4, both already on these pages). The Hebrew does not say who said it to him. The last thing David did before the cave of Adullam was to play the madman at Gath (21:14, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “for it has been said to me that he himself is surely cunning”; the KJV “for it is told me that he dealeth very subtilly”; the Douay, from the Latin, turns it round and makes Saul the crafty one: “for he thinketh of me, that I lie craftily in wait for him”.

The hiding places. “All the hiding places where he hides himself”: ha-machabo’im asher yitchabbe sham. When the people looked for their new king, Jehovah said, “he has hidden himself among the baggage” (10:22); and the Philistines at Michmash said, “Hebrews coming out of the holes where they have hidden themselves”, asher hitchabe’u-sham (14:11, both already on these pages). The man who once hid himself is now searching every hiding place for another. “And come back to me with certainty”: el-nakhon. The phrase stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here, and when David’s spies learn that Saul “had come for certain” (26:4, now on these pages).

The thousands of Judah. “I will search him out among all the thousands of Judah”: ve-chippasti oto be-khol alfei Yehudah. Chippes is to search a thing out (chafas): Laban “searched but did not find the teraphim” (Genesis 31:35), and Joseph’s steward “searched” the sacks (Genesis 44:12, both already on these pages). “The thousands of Judah” stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; in Micah, where Bethlehem is “small to be among the thousands of Judah” (Micah 5:1 in the Hebrew numbering); and twice in Zechariah 12:5–6 (none of them translated here yet). The ASV has “that I will search him out among all the thousands of Judah”; the NIV “I will track him down among all the clans of Judah”; the TLB “I’ll find him if I have to search every inch of the entire land!”

Verses 24–28 · “Sela-hammahlekoth” — the wilderness of Maon, the two sides of the mountain, and the Crag of the Divisions

The wilderness of Maon. “And they arose, and went to Ziph ahead of Saul. And David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the Arabah, south of the Jeshimon”: ba-aravah (aravah). Maon is a town of the hill country of Judah, listed with Carmel, Ziph and Juttah (Joshua 15:55, already on these pages) (Maon), and it is the town of Nabal (25:2, now on these pages). The Greek read ba-aravah as ba-erev, “toward evening”. The KJV has “in the plain on the south of Jeshimon”; the NWT 1984 “in the Arʹa·bah to the south of Je·shiʹmon”; the Geneva “in the plain on the right hand of Jeshimon”.

The crag. “And he went down to the crag”: va-yered ha-sela (sela). This translation renders sela “crag” throughout, as the crag Moses was told to speak to. The KJV has “wherefore he came down into a rock”; the NWT 1984 “he at once went down to the crag”.

This side and that side. “And Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side of the mountain”: mi-tsad ha-har mi-zeh … mi-tsad ha-har mi-zeh. The Hebrew says mi-zeh, “from this”, for both sides, as it does for the two crags at Michmash, “a tooth of rock on this side and a tooth of rock on that side” (14:4, already on these pages). “And David was hurrying to get away from before Saul”: nechpaz. The Douay has “and David despaired of being able to escape from the face of Saul”, and the Greek has David “sheltering” as he went.

Closing in. “And Saul and his men were closing in around David and his men, to seize them”: otrim, from atar, to surround (atar). Elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible the verb is mostly the verb of crowning, as in “who crowns you with steadfast love and compassion” (Psalm 103:4, already on these pages), and its noun atarah is a crown, like the crown of the king of Rabbah that will be set on David’s head (2 Samuel 12:30, now on these pages). The Latin keeps the picture: Saul and his men ringed David in modum coronae, “in the manner of a crown”. The ASV has “for Saul and his men compassed David and his men round about to take them”; the NWT 1984 “all the while Saul and his men were closing in on David and his men to grab hold of them”.

A raid on the land. “But a messenger came to Saul, saying: ‘Hurry, and come, for the Philistines have made a raid on the land!’”: ki-fashtu Pelishtim al-ha-arets. Pashat is to strip off, and of raiders to spread out over a country: the Amalekites will raid the Negev and Ziklag (30:1, now on these pages), and the Philistines the towns of the lowland in Ahaz’s day (2 Chronicles 28:18). The chapter began with Philistines raiding Keilah and ends with them raiding the land; David answered the first raid, and the second draws Saul away from him. The KJV has “for the Philistines have invaded the land”; the ASV “for the Philistines have made a raid upon the land”; the Douay “for the Philistines have poured in themselves upon the land”.

The Crag of the Divisions. “Therefore they called that place Sela-hammahlekoth — the Crag of the Divisions”: sela ha-machleqot (Sela-hammahlekoth). Machleqot is from chalaq, to divide (chalaq), and the place is where Saul and David were parted; elsewhere the noun names the divisions of the priests and Levites in Chronicles (1 Chronicles 23:6). A second chalaq means smooth, as Jacob is “a smooth man” (Genesis 27:11, already on these pages), and the name has also been read as the crag of slipping away (reported, not decided). The NWT 1984 has “the Crag of the Divisions”; the Douay “the Rock of division”; the TLB “The Rock of Escape”; the ASV keeps the name, “Sela-hammahlekoth”. The Greek has “the rock that was divided”.

Verse 29 · “The strongholds of En-gedi” — the spring of the kid, and where the chapter ends

En-gedi. “And David went up from there, and stayed in the strongholds of En-gedi”: bi-metsadot Ein-gedi, “the spring of the kid”, the young goat (En-gedi). It is a spring in the cliffs above the western shore of the Dead Sea, one of the towns of the wilderness of Judah (Joshua 15:62), and the place of the Song’s “vineyards of En-gedi” (Song of Solomon 1:14, both already on these pages). In the next chapter Saul goes to look for David “on the face of the rocks of the wild goats” (24:2, now on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “in the places difficult to approach at En-geʹdi”; the NIV “and lived in the strongholds of En Gedi”; the TLB “David then went to live in the caves of Engedi.”

Chapter 23, or chapter 24. Where this verse belongs is counted two ways. The Hebrew text on these pages ends chapter 23 with it, as the English shelf does; the Greek, the Latin, and the Douay and Geneva on the shelf open chapter 24 with it, as many printed Hebrew Bibles also do (reported). Either way it is a hinge: the strongholds of En-gedi are where the next chapter’s cave is.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact, distinct words: “plundering” for shosim; “inquired” for sha’al, as in 22:10; “the battle lines” for ma’arkhot, as in 17:10; “I am giving” for the participle noten; “a great slaughter” for makkah gedolah, as in 19:8; “saved” for yasha, as in 9:16; “an ephod came down in his hand”, with the ephod as the subject; “disowned” for nikkar; “shut himself in” for nisgar, and “hand over” for hisgir, in verses 11, 12 and 20, as in Deuteronomy 23:16 and Joshua 20:5; “gates and bars”, as in Deuteronomy 3:5; “summoned” for va-yeshamma, as in 15:4; “devising” for machrish, as in Proverbs 3:29; “Bring the ephod here” for haggishah, as in 14:18; “the lords of Keilah” for ba’alei Qe’ilah, as in Joshua 24:11; “went about wherever they went about” for va-yithallekhu ba-asher yithallakhu; “ceased” for chadal; “stayed” for yashav, as in chapter 22; “the strongholds” for metsadot, as in Judges 6:2; “Horesh” for Choreshah, as a name; “saw” for va-yar, with the vowels; “strengthened his hand” for chizzeq yad, as in Judges 9:24; “second to you” for le-mishneh, as in Genesis 41:43; “cut a covenant”, as in 18:3 and 20:16; “south of” for mimin, “on the right of”; “the Jeshimon”, as a name, as in Numbers 23:28; “all the craving of your soul” for avvat nefesh; “had pity” for chamal, the verb of 15:3, 9; “Blessed are you of Jehovah”, as in 15:13; “surely cunning” for arom ya’rim; “hides himself” for hitchabbe, as in 10:22 and 14:11; “with certainty” for el-nakhon; “search out” for chippes, as in Genesis 31:35 and 44:12; “the thousands of Judah”; “the Arabah” for aravah; “the crag” for sela, as in Numbers 20:8; “on this side … on that side” for mi-zeh … mi-zeh, as in 14:4; “closing in around” for otrim; “made a raid” for pashat; and “Sela-hammahlekoth — the Crag of the Divisions”.
