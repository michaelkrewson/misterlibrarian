At Gilgal a man of worthlessness, Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite, blows the horn and cries ‘We have no portion in David’; every man of Israel goes up after him, and Judah clings to the king from the Jordan to Jerusalem. David puts the ten concubines he left in a house of ward, sustains them, and does not go in to them. Amasa is told to summon Judah in three days and delays beyond the time; David sends Abishai after Sheba, and Joab’s men, the Cherethites and the Pelethites and the mighty men go out. At the great stone in Gibeon Joab asks Amasa ‘Is it peace with you, my brother?’, takes his beard with the right hand, and strikes him in the belly; a young man of Joab’s calls the people after Joab, and the body is moved off the highway. Joab besieges Sheba in Abel of Beth-maacah, a wise woman speaks to him from the wall, and the people cut off Sheba’s head and throw it to Joab, who blows the horn and sends the army home. The chapter ends with the list of David’s officers.
After the crossing of the Jordan and the quarrel at Gilgal: Sheba’s revolt, the pursuit north to Abel of Beth-maacah, and Joab’s return to Jerusalem.

1
וְשָׁם נִקְרָא אִישׁ בְּלִיַּעַל, וּשְׁמוֹ שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי--אִישׁ יְמִינִי; וַיִּתְקַע בַּשֹּׁפָר, וַיֹּאמֶר אֵין-לָנוּ חֵלֶק בְּדָוִד וְלֹא נַחֲלָה-לָנוּ בְּבֶן-יִשַׁי--אִישׁ לְאֹהָלָיו, יִשְׂרָאֵל.
And there happened to be there a man of worthlessness, whose name was Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite; and he blew the horn, and said: ‘We have no portion in David, and no inheritance for us in the son of Jesse; every man to his tent, O Israel!’note
Y había allí por casualidad un hombre de la nada, cuyo nombre era Seba hijo de Bicri, benjaminita; y tocó el cuerno, y dijo: «No tenemos parte en David, ni herencia para nosotros en el hijo de Isaí; ¡cada uno a su tienda, Israel!»
2
וַיַּעַל כָּל-אִישׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל, מֵאַחֲרֵי דָוִד, אַחֲרֵי, שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי; וְאִישׁ יְהוּדָה דָּבְקוּ בְמַלְכָּם, מִן-הַיַּרְדֵּן וְעַד-יְרוּשָׁלִָם.
And every man of Israel went up from after David, after Sheba son of Bichri; but the men of Judah clung to their king, from the Jordan to Jerusalem.note
Y todo hombre de Israel subió de en pos de David, en pos de Seba hijo de Bicri; pero los hombres de Judá se mantuvieron unidos a su rey, desde el Jordán hasta Jerusalén.
3
וַיָּבֹא דָוִד אֶל-בֵּיתוֹ, יְרוּשָׁלִַם, וַיִּקַּח הַמֶּלֶךְ אֵת עֶשֶׂר-נָשִׁים פִּלַגְשִׁים אֲשֶׁר הִנִּיחַ לִשְׁמֹר הַבַּיִת וַיִּתְּנֵם בֵּית-מִשְׁמֶרֶת וַיְכַלְכְּלֵם, וַאֲלֵיהֶם לֹא-בָא; וַתִּהְיֶינָה צְרֻרוֹת עַד-יוֹם מֻתָן, אַלְמְנוּת חַיּוּת.  {ס}
And David came to his house, to Jerusalem; and the king took the ten women, concubines, whom he had left to keep the house, and put them in a house of ward, and sustained them; but he did not go in to them. And they were shut up until the day of their death, in a living widowhood.note
Y David llegó a su casa, a Jerusalén; y el rey tomó a las diez mujeres, concubinas, que había dejado para guardar la casa, y las puso en una casa de custodia, y las sustentó; pero no entró a ellas. Y quedaron encerradas hasta el día de su muerte, en una viudez en vida.
4
וַיֹּאמֶר הַמֶּלֶךְ אֶל-עֲמָשָׂא, הַזְעֶק-לִי אֶת-אִישׁ-יְהוּדָה שְׁלֹשֶׁת יָמִים; וְאַתָּה, פֹּה עֲמֹד.
And the king said to Amasa: ‘Summon to me the men of Judah in three days; and you, stand here.’note
Y el rey dijo a Amasa: «Convócame a los hombres de Judá dentro de tres días; y tú, quédate aquí».
5
וַיֵּלֶךְ עֲמָשָׂא, לְהַזְעִיק אֶת-יְהוּדָה; וייחר וַיּוֹחֶר, מִן-הַמּוֹעֵד אֲשֶׁר יְעָדוֹ.  {ס}
And Amasa went to summon Judah; but he delayed beyond the appointed time that he had set him.note
Y Amasa fue a convocar a Judá; pero se demoró más allá del tiempo señalado que el rey le había fijado.
6
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד, אֶל-אֲבִישַׁי, עַתָּה יֵרַע לָנוּ שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי, מִן-אַבְשָׁלוֹם; אַתָּה קַח אֶת-עַבְדֵי אֲדֹנֶיךָ, וּרְדֹף אַחֲרָיו--פֶּן-מָצָא לוֹ עָרִים בְּצֻרוֹת, וְהִצִּיל עֵינֵנוּ.
And David said to Abishai: ‘Now Sheba son of Bichri will be worse for us than Absalom. You, take your lord’s servants and pursue after him, lest he find for himself fortified cities, and escape from our eye.’note
Y David dijo a Abisai: «Ahora Seba hijo de Bicri será peor para nosotros que Absalón. Tú, toma a los siervos de tu señor y persíguelo, no sea que halle para sí ciudades fortificadas, y escape de nuestro ojo».
7
וַיֵּצְאוּ אַחֲרָיו אַנְשֵׁי יוֹאָב, וְהַכְּרֵתִי וְהַפְּלֵתִי וְכָל-הַגִּבֹּרִים; וַיֵּצְאוּ, מִירוּשָׁלִַם, לִרְדֹּף, אַחֲרֵי שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי.
And Joab’s men went out after him, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites and all the mighty men; and they went out from Jerusalem to pursue after Sheba son of Bichri.note
Y salieron tras él los hombres de Joab, y los cereteos y los peleteos y todos los valientes; y salieron de Jerusalén para perseguir a Seba hijo de Bicri.
8
הֵם, עִם-הָאֶבֶן הַגְּדוֹלָה אֲשֶׁר בְּגִבְעוֹן, וַעֲמָשָׂא, בָּא לִפְנֵיהֶם; וְיוֹאָב חָגוּר מִדּוֹ לְבֻשׁוֹ, וְעָלָו חֲגוֹר חֶרֶב מְצֻמֶּדֶת עַל-מָתְנָיו בְּתַעְרָהּ, וְהוּא יָצָא, וַתִּפֹּל.  {ס}
They were by the great stone that is in Gibeon, and Amasa came before them. And Joab was girded with his garment, his clothing, and over it a belt with a sword fastened at his loins in its sheath; and he went out, and it fell.note
Estaban ellos junto a la gran piedra que está en Gabaón, y Amasa vino delante de ellos. Y Joab estaba ceñido con su ropa, su vestido, y sobre ella un cinto con una espada sujeta a sus lomos en su vaina; y él salió, y cayó.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר יוֹאָב לַעֲמָשָׂא, הֲשָׁלוֹם אַתָּה אָחִי; וַתֹּחֶז יַד-יְמִין יוֹאָב, בִּזְקַן עֲמָשָׂא--לִנְשָׁק-לוֹ.
And Joab said to Amasa: ‘Is it peace with you, my brother?’ And the right hand of Joab took hold of Amasa’s beard to kiss him.note
Y Joab dijo a Amasa: «¿Hay paz contigo, hermano mío?». Y la mano derecha de Joab tomó la barba de Amasa para besarlo.
10
וַעֲמָשָׂא לֹא-נִשְׁמַר בַּחֶרֶב אֲשֶׁר בְּיַד-יוֹאָב, וַיַּכֵּהוּ בָהּ אֶל-הַחֹמֶשׁ וַיִּשְׁפֹּךְ מֵעָיו אַרְצָה וְלֹא-שָׁנָה לוֹ--וַיָּמֹת; וְיוֹאָב, וַאֲבִישַׁי אָחִיו, רָדַף, אַחֲרֵי שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי.
But Amasa was not on guard against the sword that was in Joab’s hand; and he struck him with it in the belly, and poured out his bowels to the ground, and did not do it to him a second time — and he died. And Joab, and Abishai his brother, pursued after Sheba son of Bichri.note
Pero Amasa no se guardó de la espada que estaba en la mano de Joab; y este lo hirió con ella en el vientre, y derramó sus entrañas por tierra, y no se lo hizo una segunda vez — y murió. Y Joab, y Abisai su hermano, persiguieron a Seba hijo de Bicri.
11
וְאִישׁ עָמַד עָלָיו, מִנַּעֲרֵי יוֹאָב; וַיֹּאמֶר, מִי אֲשֶׁר חָפֵץ בְּיוֹאָב וּמִי אֲשֶׁר-לְדָוִד--אַחֲרֵי יוֹאָב.
And a man of Joab’s young men stood over him, and said: ‘Whoever delights in Joab, and whoever is David’s — after Joab!’note
Y un hombre de los jóvenes de Joab se paró junto a él, y dijo: «El que se complace en Joab, y el que es de David — ¡en pos de Joab!»
12
וַעֲמָשָׂא מִתְגֹּלֵל בַּדָּם, בְּתוֹךְ הַמְסִלָּה; וַיַּרְא הָאִישׁ כִּי-עָמַד כָּל-הָעָם, וַיַּסֵּב אֶת-עֲמָשָׂא מִן-הַמְסִלָּה הַשָּׂדֶה וַיַּשְׁלֵךְ עָלָיו בֶּגֶד, כַּאֲשֶׁר רָאָה, כָּל-הַבָּא עָלָיו וְעָמָד.
And Amasa was rolling in the blood in the middle of the highway; and the man saw that all the people stood still, and he turned Amasa from the highway into the field, and threw a garment over him, because he saw that everyone who came to him stood still.note
Y Amasa se revolcaba en la sangre en medio del camino real; y el hombre vio que todo el pueblo se detenía, y apartó a Amasa del camino hacia el campo, y echó sobre él un manto, porque vio que todo el que llegaba a él se detenía.
13
כַּאֲשֶׁר הֹגָה, מִן-הַמְסִלָּה--עָבַר כָּל-אִישׁ, אַחֲרֵי יוֹאָב, לִרְדֹּף, אַחֲרֵי שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי.
When he was removed from the highway, every man passed on after Joab to pursue after Sheba son of Bichri.note
Cuando fue apartado del camino, todo hombre pasó en pos de Joab para perseguir a Seba hijo de Bicri.
14
וַיַּעֲבֹר בְּכָל-שִׁבְטֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, אָבֵלָה וּבֵית מַעֲכָה--וְכָל-הַבֵּרִים;  {ס}  ויקלהו וַיִּקָּהֲלוּ, וַיָּבֹאוּ אַף-אַחֲרָיו.
And he passed through all the tribes of Israel to Abel and Beth-maacah, and all the Berites; and they were gathered, and also went in after him.note
Y él pasó por todas las tribus de Israel hasta Abel y Bet-maaca, y todos los beritas; y se juntaron, y entraron también en pos de él.
15
וַיָּבֹאוּ וַיָּצֻרוּ עָלָיו, בְּאָבֵלָה בֵּית הַמַּעֲכָה, וַיִּשְׁפְּכוּ סֹלְלָה אֶל-הָעִיר, וַתַּעֲמֹד בַּחֵל; וְכָל-הָעָם אֲשֶׁר אֶת-יוֹאָב, מַשְׁחִיתִם לְהַפִּיל הַחוֹמָה.
And they came and besieged him in Abel of Beth-maacah, and cast up a mound against the city, and it stood at the rampart; and all the people who were with Joab were working ruin, to make the wall fall.note
Y vinieron y lo sitiaron en Abel de Bet-maaca, y levantaron una rampa de asedio contra la ciudad, y quedó junto al antemuro; y todo el pueblo que estaba con Joab obraba ruina, para hacer caer el muro.
16
וַתִּקְרָא אִשָּׁה חֲכָמָה, מִן-הָעִיר; שִׁמְעוּ שִׁמְעוּ, אִמְרוּ-נָא אֶל-יוֹאָב, קְרַב עַד-הֵנָּה, וַאֲדַבְּרָה אֵלֶיךָ.
And a wise woman called from the city: ‘Hear, hear! Say, please, to Joab: Come near here, and let me speak to you.’note
Y una mujer sabia gritó desde la ciudad: «¡Oigan, oigan! Digan, por favor, a Joab: Acércate hasta aquí, y hablaré contigo».
17
וַיִּקְרַב אֵלֶיהָ, וַתֹּאמֶר הָאִשָּׁה הַאַתָּה יוֹאָב וַיֹּאמֶר אָנִי; וַתֹּאמֶר לוֹ, שְׁמַע דִּבְרֵי אֲמָתֶךָ, וַיֹּאמֶר, שֹׁמֵעַ אָנֹכִי.
And he came near to her, and the woman said: ‘Are you Joab?’ And he said: ‘I am.’ And she said to him: ‘Hear the words of your handmaid.’ And he said: ‘I am hearing.’note
Y él se acercó a ella, y la mujer dijo: «¿Eres tú Joab?». Y él dijo: «Yo soy». Y ella le dijo: «Oye las palabras de tu sierva». Y él dijo: «Estoy oyendo».
18
וַתֹּאמֶר, לֵאמֹר:  דַּבֵּר יְדַבְּרוּ בָרִאשֹׁנָה לֵאמֹר, שָׁאוֹל יְשָׁאֲלוּ בְּאָבֵל וְכֵן הֵתַמּוּ.
And she said, saying: ‘They used to speak, at the first, saying: “Let them surely ask at Abel”; and so they finished.note
Y ella dijo: «Solían decir antiguamente: “Que pregunten sin falta en Abel”; y así concluían.
19
אָנֹכִי, שְׁלֻמֵי אֱמוּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל; אַתָּה מְבַקֵּשׁ, לְהָמִית עִיר וְאֵם בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל--לָמָּה תְבַלַּע, נַחֲלַת יְהוָה.  {פ}
I am of the peaceable, the faithful of Israel; you are seeking to put to death a city and a mother in Israel. Why will you swallow up the inheritance of Jehovah?’note
Yo soy de los pacíficos, de los fieles de Israel; tú buscas dar muerte a una ciudad y a una madre en Israel. ¿Por qué has de tragarte la herencia de Jehová?»
20
וַיַּעַן יוֹאָב, וַיֹּאמַר:  חָלִילָה חָלִילָה לִי, אִם-אֲבַלַּע וְאִם-אַשְׁחִית.
And Joab answered, and said: ‘Far be it, far be it from me, that I should swallow up or ruin.note
Y Joab respondió y dijo: «Lejos, lejos esté de mí tragar o arruinar.
21
לֹא-כֵן הַדָּבָר, כִּי אִישׁ מֵהַר אֶפְרַיִם שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי שְׁמוֹ נָשָׂא יָדוֹ בַּמֶּלֶךְ בְּדָוִד--תְּנוּ-אֹתוֹ לְבַדּוֹ, וְאֵלְכָה מֵעַל הָעִיר; וַתֹּאמֶר הָאִשָּׁה אֶל-יוֹאָב, הִנֵּה רֹאשׁוֹ מֻשְׁלָךְ אֵלֶיךָ בְּעַד הַחוֹמָה.
The matter is not so; but a man of the hill country of Ephraim, Sheba son of Bichri is his name, has lifted up his hand against the king, against David. Give him alone, and I will go from the city.’ And the woman said to Joab: ‘Look — his head will be thrown to you over the wall.’note
No es así el asunto; sino que un hombre del monte de Efraín, Seba hijo de Bicri es su nombre, ha alzado su mano contra el rey, contra David. Entréguenlo a él solo, y me iré de la ciudad». Y la mujer dijo a Joab: «Mira, su cabeza te será arrojada por encima del muro».
22
וַתָּבוֹא הָאִשָּׁה אֶל-כָּל-הָעָם בְּחָכְמָתָהּ, וַיִּכְרְתוּ אֶת-רֹאשׁ שֶׁבַע בֶּן-בִּכְרִי וַיַּשְׁלִכוּ אֶל-יוֹאָב, וַיִּתְקַע בַּשֹּׁפָר, וַיָּפֻצוּ מֵעַל-הָעִיר אִישׁ לְאֹהָלָיו; וְיוֹאָב שָׁב יְרוּשָׁלִַם, אֶל-הַמֶּלֶךְ.  {ס}
And the woman went to all the people in her wisdom; and they cut off the head of Sheba son of Bichri, and threw it to Joab. And he blew the horn, and they were scattered from the city, every man to his tent; and Joab returned to Jerusalem, to the king.note
Y la mujer fue a todo el pueblo con su sabiduría; y cortaron la cabeza de Seba hijo de Bicri, y la arrojaron a Joab. Y él tocó el cuerno, y se dispersaron de la ciudad, cada uno a su tienda; y Joab volvió a Jerusalén, al rey.
23
וְיוֹאָב, אֶל כָּל-הַצָּבָא יִשְׂרָאֵל; וּבְנָיָה, בֶּן-יְהוֹיָדָע, עַל-הכרי הַכְּרֵתִי, וְעַל-הַפְּלֵתִי.
And Joab was over all the army of Israel; and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites;note
Y Joab estaba sobre todo el ejército de Israel; y Benaía hijo de Joiada sobre los cereteos y los peleteos;
24
וַאֲדֹרָם, עַל-הַמַּס; וִיהוֹשָׁפָט בֶּן-אֲחִילוּד, הַמַּזְכִּיר.
and Adoram was over the forced labour; and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder;note
y Adoram sobre los trabajos forzados; y Josafat hijo de Ahilud era cronista;
25
ושיא וּשְׁוָא, סֹפֵר; וְצָדוֹק וְאֶבְיָתָר, כֹּהֲנִים.
and Sheva was scribe; and Zadok and Abiathar were priests;note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · 2 Samuel 20 Sheba son of Bichri — a man of worthlessness, “We have no portion in David”, and a horn

A man of worthlessness. “And there happened to be there a man of worthlessness, whose name was Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjaminite”: the Hebrew is ish beliyaal (see beliyaal), and the last place the previous chapter named was Gilgal (2 Samuel 19:41, now on these pages), where Israel and Judah were quarrelling over the king. The ASV has “a base fellow”. The NIV has “a troublemaker”. The NWT 1984 has “a good-for-nothing man”. The TLB has “a hothead”. The Douay has “a man of Belial”. Rahlfs’ Greek has huios paranomos, “a lawless son”. He is “a Benjaminite”, ish yemini, with the same word, yemini, that was said of Shimei (2 Samuel 19:17), and Joab will call him “a man of the hill country of Ephraim” (verse 21); the page does not reconcile the two.

The horn and the cry. “And he blew the horn, and said: ‘We have no portion in David, and no inheritance for us in the son of Jesse; every man to his tent, O Israel!’”: the horn is the shofar, and “and he blew the horn”, with the horn written in this text without the letter vav, stands in two verses: this one and verse 22, where Joab’s horn sends the same people home. The cry is two nouns, chelek and nachalah, “portion” and “inheritance”, and the second half is Nabal’s sneer: “Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse?” (1 Samuel 25:10, now on these pages). The ASV has “We have no portion in David”. The NIV has “We have no share in David, no part in Jesse’s son!”. The NWT 1984 has “We have no share in David”. The Douay has “We have no part in David, nor inheritance in the son of Isai”. The TLB has “We want nothing to do with David”. Rahlfs’ Greek has ouk estin hēmin meris en Dauid, and ends the cry “to your tents”, in the second person. “Every man to his tent” stands in seven verses: Gideon’s dismissal of the rest of Israel (Judges 7:8), Israel’s flight when the Philistines beat it (1 Samuel 4:10), Saul’s sending the people home (13:2), Israel’s flight to its tents after Absalom’s defeat (2 Samuel 19:9, all now on these pages), here, verse 22, and 2 Chronicles 25:22.

His tent, or his gods. The last words of the cry are the point on which the shelf splits. The NWT 1984 has “Every one to his gods, O Israel!”. The Hebrew printed here reads le’ohalav, “to his tents”, and so do the ASV and NIV; the “gods” reading is the one an old Jewish list of scribal changes is reported to give for this verse, and that report is not checked here. The same cry is used again, as a question and with “your tents”, when Israel answers Rehoboam (1 Kings 12:16; 2 Chronicles 10:16).

Went up, and clung. “And every man of Israel went up from after David, after Sheba son of Bichri; but the men of Judah clung to their king, from the Jordan to Jerusalem”: the Hebrew sets two prepositions side by side, me’acharei David … acharei Sheva, “from after David … after Sheba”, so that following is the thing that moves. The ASV has “the men of Judah clave unto their king”. The NIV has “stayed by their king”. The NWT 1984 has “they stuck to their king”. The Douay has “stuck to their king”. Rahlfs’ Greek has ekollēthē, “was glued”. The verb is davaq, the one that says a man “clings to his wife” (Genesis 2:24) and that “Ruth clung to her” (Ruth 1:14, both now on these pages). “From the Jordan to Jerusalem” is the road the king has just been brought up. Judah had said that the king was near to it (2 Samuel 19:43), and the man of Israel had claimed “ten hands” in him (19:44); verse 3 has ten women, eser nashim, and says nothing about the number.

Verse 3 · 2 Samuel 20 The ten women — a house of ward, “sustained”, and a living widowhood

Ten women, concubines. “And the king took the ten women, concubines, whom he had left to keep the house”: “ten women, concubines” stands in two verses, 2 Samuel 15:16, where he leaves them, and this one. In between they were the wives Nathan said would be taken before David’s eyes and given to his neighbour (2 Samuel 12:11–12) and the women Absalom “went in to” on the roof (2 Samuel 16:22, all now on these pages; see pilegesh). “And put them in a house of ward”: the Hebrew is beit mishmeret. The ASV has “put them in ward”. The NIV has “put them in a house under guard”. The NWT 1984 has “put them in a house of confinement”. The TLB has “placed in seclusion”. The Douay has “put them in ward”.

Sustained. “And sustained them; but he did not go in to them”: the verb is vaykhalkhelem, from kilkal, the verb for what Barzillai did for the king at Mahanaim (2 Samuel 19:33) and for what the king promises him in Jerusalem (19:34); here it is what David does for the women he will not see. The ASV has “provided them with sustenance”. The NIV has “He provided for them”. The NWT 1984 has “he kept on supplying food to them”. The TLB has “Their needs were to be cared for”. The Douay has “allowing them provisions”. “Did not go in to them” uses the verb of Absalom’s act on the roof: the same bo, “go in”, that was said of him.

A living widowhood. “And they were shut up until the day of their death, in a living widowhood”: the Hebrew is almenut chayyut, “a widowhood of life”, and the two words together stand only here. The first is the noun of almanah, a widow; the second is “life”, set after it as the word that cancels it, since the husband is alive. The ASV has “living in widowhood”. The NIV has “living as widows”. The NWT 1984 has “in a widowhood with a living [husband]”. The TLB has “in virtual widowhood”. The Douay has “living in widowhood”. Rahlfs’ Greek has chērai zōsai, “living widows”. “Shut up” is tserurot, “bound up”.

What the verse does and does not say. Four verbs have David for their subject and the women for their object: he took them, put them in ward, sustained them, and did not go in to them. The women have no speech in the verse, and the verse passes no judgment on the king. It says what was done, what was withheld, and for how long, and then the narrative turns to Amasa.

Verses 4–7 · 2 Samuel 20 Amasa told to summon Judah in three days and late — “worse for us than Absalom”, and Abishai sent after Sheba

Summon. “Summon to me the men of Judah in three days; and you, stand here”: the verb is haz’eq, “cry out”, used here of calling men together, and it is Amasa’s first task since the king promised him the army “in place of Joab” (2 Samuel 19:14, now on these pages). The ASV has “Call me the men of Judah together within three days”. The NIV has “Summon the men of Judah to come to me within three days”. The NWT 1984 has “Call the men of Judah together to me within three days”. The TLB has “mobilize the army of Judah within three days”. The Douay has “Assemble to me all the men of Juda”. Rahlfs’ Greek has boēson, “shout”.

Delayed. “And Amasa went to summon Judah; but he delayed beyond the appointed time that he had set him”: at this word the text is written one way and read another, written vayyiher and read vayyoher, “and he delayed”. The Hebrew says “from the appointed time that he appointed him”, min-hamo’ed asher ye’ado, with the one root twice. The ASV has “he tarried longer than the set time”. The NIV has “he took longer than the time the king had set for him”. The NWT 1984 has “he came later than the fixed time”. The TLB has “it took him longer than the three days he had been given”. The Douay has “tarried beyond the set time”. Rahlfs’ Greek has echronisen, “took time”. The text does not say why he was late, and does not report what the king said of it.

Worse for us than Absalom. “Now Sheba son of Bichri will be worse for us than Absalom. You, take your lord’s servants and pursue after him, lest he find for himself fortified cities, and escape from our eye”: David speaks to Abishai, not to Joab, and calls himself “your lord”. The ASV has “Now will Sheba the son of Bichri do us more harm than did Absalom”. The NIV has “Now Sheba son of Bikri will do us more harm than Absalom did”. The NWT 1984 has “Now Sheʹba the son of Bichʹri will be worse for us than Abʹsa·lom”. The TLB has “That fellow Sheba is going to hurt us more than Absalom did”. The last clause is vehitsil eineinu, a form of natsal, “deliver”, with “our eye” for its object. The ASV has “escape out of our sight”. The NIV has “escape from us”. The NWT 1984 has “escape before our eyes”. Rahlfs’ Greek has skiasei tous ophthalmous hēmōn, “will shade our eyes”.

Joab’s men. “And Joab’s men went out after him, and the Cherethites and the Pelethites and all the mighty men”: the text says “Joab’s men”, though the order was given to Abishai, and it does not say who commands them. The NIV has “went out under the command of Abishai”, which is added. “The Cherethites and the Pelethites” stands in five verses: 2 Samuel 8:18, this one, and 1 Kings 1:38, 44 (both now on these pages), and 1 Chronicles 18:17. The guard that crossed before the king when he fled Absalom (2 Samuel 15:18) goes out now after Sheba.

Verses 8–13 · 2 Samuel 20 Joab and Amasa at the great stone in Gibeon — “Is it peace with you, my brother?”, the belly, and the people who stood still

The sword that fell. “They were by the great stone that is in Gibeon, and Amasa came before them. And Joab was girded with his garment, his clothing, and over it a belt with a sword fastened at his loins in its sheath; and he went out, and it fell”: Gibeon is where Joab’s men and Abner’s sat on either side of the pool (2 Samuel 2:13, now on these pages). The last clause is vehu yatsa vattippol, “and he went out, and it fell”, and the verb “fell” is feminine, as the word for sword is. The Hebrew does not say whether the sword fell by chance or by his hand. The ASV has “as he went forth it fell out”. The NIV has “As he stepped forward, it dropped out of its sheath”. The NWT 1984 has “And he himself came forth, and so it fell out”. The TLB has “he stealthily slipped the dagger from its sheath”. The Douay has “made in such manner as to come out with the least motion and strike”. Rahlfs’ Greek has kai hē machaira exēlthen kai epesen, “and the sword went out and fell”.

Is it peace with you, my brother? “Is it peace with you, my brother?” is hashalom attah achi (see shalom), the question asked of the young man Absalom (2 Samuel 18:29, now on these pages). Amasa is Joab’s cousin, the son of Abigal, sister of Zeruiah (2 Samuel 17:25), and the word for “brother” is the one the next verses use of Abishai. “And the right hand of Joab took hold of Amasa’s beard to kiss him”: the verb is vattochez, feminine, and its subject is the hand. The ASV has “Joab took Amasa by the beard with his right hand”. The NWT 1984 has “Joʹab’s right hand took hold of A·maʹsa’s beard”. The Douay has “he took Amasa by the chin with his right hand”. Rahlfs’ Greek has ekratēsen hē cheir hē dexia Iōab, “the right hand of Joab held”. The kiss is one of this book’s kisses (2 Samuel 14:33; 19:40).

In the belly. “But Amasa was not on guard against the sword that was in Joab’s hand; and he struck him with it in the belly, and poured out his bowels to the ground, and did not do it to him a second time — and he died”: el-hachomesh is the place of the spear in 2 Samuel 2:23 (Abner and Asahel), of the blow at the gate in 3:27 (Joab and Abner) and of the house in 4:6 (Rechab and Baanah), all now on these pages (see chomesh). The ASV has “in the body”. The NIV has “into his belly”. The NWT 1984 has “in the abdomen”. The Douay has “in the side”. The TLB has “stabbed him in the stomach”. “And did not do it to him a second time” stands only here; Abishai had said of Saul “I will not do it to him twice” (1 Samuel 26:8, now on these pages), in the first person, and not as the report of a blow. The TLB also has Amasa not noticing “the dagger in his left hand”; the Hebrew names no hand but the right one of verse 9. “And poured out his bowels” stands only here, and vayyishpokh, “poured”, is the verb of the mound in verse 15. “And Joab, and Abishai his brother, pursued”: the verb is singular.

Whoever delights in Joab. “And a man of Joab’s young men stood over him, and said: ‘Whoever delights in Joab, and whoever is David’s — after Joab!’”: the verb is chafets, the one Saul’s servants used when they told David that “the king delights in you” (1 Samuel 18:22, now on these pages). The ASV has “He that favoreth Joab, and he that is for David, let him follow Joab”. The NIV has “Whoever favors Joab, and whoever is for David, let him follow Joab!”. The NWT 1984 has “Whoever has found delight in Joʹab and whoever belongs to David, let him follow Joʹab!”. The TLB has “If you are for David, come and follow Joab”. The Douay has “Behold he that would have been in Joab’s stead the companion of David”, from the Latin. The call puts Joab’s name first and last and David’s between. “And Amasa was rolling in the blood in the middle of the highway”: the participle mitgolel with “in the blood” stands only here. “All the people stood still” repeats the verb of 2 Samuel 2:23, where everyone who came to the place where Asahel had fallen stood still, and of 2:28, where they stood still at Joab’s horn. The man “turned Amasa from the highway into the field, and threw a garment over him”, and “every man passed on after Joab”. The Douay has “that they who passed might not stop on his account”, which gives the reason the Hebrew leaves to the man’s seeing.

Verses 14–15 · 2 Samuel 20 Abel of Beth-maacah — the Berites, a mound at the rampart, and “working ruin”

He passed through. “And he passed through all the tribes of Israel to Abel and Beth-maacah, and all the Berites; and they were gathered, and also went in after him”: the verb has no named subject, and the translation lets “he” stand; the NIV and NWT name Sheba, and the ASV leaves it “he”, so that “after him” would be Sheba’s. The two names, Abelah and Beit Ma’akhah, are one town in the next verse, “Abel of Beth-maacah”, and it is a town in the north, where Ben-hadad strikes it in 1 Kings 15:20 and Tiglath-pileser takes it in 2 Kings 15:29. At the end the text is again written one way and read another, written vayyiqlehu and read vayyikkahalu, “and they were gathered”.

All the Berites. “The Berites”, haBerim, is a word that stands only here, and the shelf reads it three ways. The ASV has “all the Berites”. The NIV has “the entire region of the Bikrites”. The NWT 1984 has “all the Bichʹrites”. The TLB has “his own clan of Bichri”. The Douay has “all the chosen men”. Rahlfs’ Greek has pantes en Charri, which keeps the word as a name. The “Bikrites” reading takes the word for a form of Sheba’s father’s name; it is a conjecture, and the Hebrew as printed has Berim.

A mound at the rampart. “And they came and besieged him in Abel of Beth-maacah, and cast up a mound against the city, and it stood at the rampart”: vayyishpekhu solelah, “they poured a mound”, with the verb of the poured bowels in verse 10, and vattaʿamod bachel, “and it stood at the rampart”. The ASV has “it stood against the rampart”. The NIV has “it stood against the outer fortifications”. The NWT 1984 has “as it was standing within a rampart”. The TLB has “built a mound to the top of the city wall”. The Douay has “the city was besieged”. The KJV has “it stood in the trench”. The word chel is a defence outside the wall, and the shelf cannot agree whether it is a ditch or a bank.

Working ruin. “And all the people who were with Joab were working ruin, to make the wall fall”: the participle is mashchitim, from shachat, “ruin”, the verb of “they ruined the sons of Ammon” (2 Samuel 11:1, now on these pages), and it has no object in the Hebrew. The ASV has “battered the wall”. The NIV has “battering the wall”. The NWT 1984 has “undermining the wall”. The TLB has “began battering it down”. The Douay has “laboured to throw down the walls”. Rahlfs’ Greek has enooun katabalein, “were planning to throw down”. It is the verb Joab will refuse in verse 20.

Verses 16–22 · 2 Samuel 20 The wise woman of Abel — “a mother in Israel”, “Let them surely ask at Abel”, and a head over the wall

A wise woman. “And a wise woman called from the city: ‘Hear, hear! Say, please, to Joab: Come near here, and let me speak to you’”: “a wise woman”, ishah chakhamah, stands in two verses, the woman of Tekoa whom Joab sent for (2 Samuel 14:2) and this one (see chokhmah); neither is named, and this one sends for Joab. “Hear, hear” stands only here. When he comes she asks “Are you Joab?”, and says “Hear the words of your handmaid”; “the words of your handmaid” stands in two verses, here and in Abigail’s mouth to David (1 Samuel 25:24), and the woman of Tekoa’s “Let your maidservant speak” (14:12) is a third way of asking (all now on these pages). He answers “I am hearing”. The ASV has “I do hear”. The NIV has “I’m listening”. The NWT 1984 has “I am listening”.

A mother in Israel. “I am of the peaceable, the faithful of Israel; you are seeking to put to death a city and a mother in Israel. Why will you swallow up the inheritance of Jehovah?”: “peaceable” and “faithful” are two plurals, shelumim and emunim. “A mother in Israel”, em beYisra’el, stands in two verses: this one and Deborah’s “I arose, a mother in Israel” (Judges 5:7, now on these pages); the third verse that spells the same letters, 2 Kings 5:15, has “if” and not “mother”. The ASV has “thou seekest to destroy a city and a mother in Israel”. The NIV has “You are trying to destroy a city that is a mother in Israel”. The NWT 1984 has “You are seeking to put to death a city and a mother in Israel”. The TLB has “You are destroying an ancient, peace-loving city”. Rahlfs’ Greek has polin kai mētropolin, “a city, even a mother-city”. “Swallow up” is bala, the verb of Hushai’s warning that the king might be swallowed up (2 Samuel 17:16). “The inheritance of Jehovah” stands in four verses: David’s complaint that he is driven from it (1 Samuel 26:19, now on these pages), this one, 2 Samuel 21:3 (now on these pages) and Psalm 127:3. It is the noun of the cry in verse 1: Sheba says Israel has no nachalah in David, and the woman says that Joab is swallowing Jehovah’s.

Far be it, far be it. “Far be it, far be it from me, that I should swallow up or ruin”: the doubled chalilah stands only here (see chalilah). The ASV has “Far be it, far be it from me, that I should swallow up or destroy”. The NIV has “Far be it from me to swallow up or destroy!”. The NWT 1984 has “It is altogether unthinkable on my part that I should swallow up and that I should bring to ruin”. The TLB has “That isn’t it at all”. Rahlfs’ Greek has hileōs moi hileōs moi, “be gracious to me”. His second verb, “ruin”, is the one his own people are doing in verse 15, and the Hebrew has no word to say how the two fit. “A man of the hill country of Ephraim, Sheba son of Bichri is his name, has lifted up his hand against the king, against David”: the words “his hand against the king” stand only here, and the men who “lifted their hand against my lord the king” are Ahimaaz’s phrase about Absalom’s army (2 Samuel 18:28, now on these pages).

Let them surely ask at Abel. “They used to speak, at the first, saying: ‘Let them surely ask at Abel’; and so they finished”: dabber yedabberu barishonah leimor sha’ol yish’alu be’Abel vekhen hetammu, and the sense is uncertain. The ASV has “They shall surely ask counsel at Abel: and so they ended the matter”. The NIV has “‘Get your answer at Abel,’ and that settled it”. The NWT 1984 has “Let them but inquire in Abel, and thus they will certainly end the matter”. The TLB has “If you want to settle an argument, ask advice at Abel”. Rahlfs’ Greek has en Abel kai en Dan, “in Abel and in Dan”, two cities where the Hebrew has one. The infinitive sha’ol, “surely ask”, is spelled with the same letters as Saul’s name, Sha’ul, “asked for”, and as Sheol; the words are not the same. “Look — his head will be thrown to you over the wall”: Absalom was thrown into the pit (2 Samuel 18:17) and a garment over Amasa (verse 12). “And the woman went to all the people in her wisdom”, bechokhmatah, and the text gives no word of hers; the people cut off the head. “And he blew the horn, and they were scattered from the city, every man to his tent”: the cry of verse 1 is the order that closes it, and “Joab returned to Jerusalem, to the king” without a word from the king.

Verses 23–26 · 2 Samuel 20 David’s officers again — Joab over the army, Adoram over the forced labour, Sheva the scribe, and Ira the Jairite

The list again. “And Joab was over all the army of Israel; and Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites”: this is the list of 2 Samuel 8:16–18 (now on these pages) with four changes. Joab is over “all the army of Israel” where the first list said only “the army”; the forced labour has a man over it; the scribe is Sheva where he was Seraiah; and Ira the Jairite is a priest where David’s sons were. The ASV has “Now Joab was over all the host of Israel”. The NIV has “Joab was over Israel’s entire army”. The NWT 1984 has “Joʹab was over all the army of Israel”. The TLB has “Joab was commander-in-chief of the army”. Between verse 22 and verse 23 the text says nothing of how the king and Joab settled what Joab did to Amasa, whom the king had named in his place (2 Samuel 19:14).

Adoram. “And Adoram was over the forced labour”: al-hamas (see mas). The ASV has “Adoram was over the men subject to taskwork”. The NIV has “Adoniram was in charge of forced labor”. The NWT 1984 has “A·doʹram was over those conscripted for forced labor”. The TLB has “Adoram was in charge of the forced labor battalions”. The Douay has “Aduram over the tributes”. Rahlfs’ Greek has Adōniram. The Hebrew here is Adoram, and the same man or the same office stands in 1 Kings 4:6 as “Adoniram the son of Abda … over the levy” and in 1 Kings 12:18, where Rehoboam sends “Adoram, who was over the levy” and Israel stones him. “And Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud was the recorder”, as in 8:16.

Written and read. “And Benaiah … was over the Cherethites and the Pelethites”: at this verse the text is written hakkari, “the Carites”, and read hakkerethi, “the Cherethites”. “And Sheva was scribe”: here it is written shiʼa and read shewa. The first list had Seraiah (2 Samuel 8:17), and 1 Chronicles 18:16 has Shavsha. The ASV has “Sheva was scribe”. The NIV has “Sheva was secretary”. The NWT 1984 has “Sheʹva was secretary”. The TLB has “Sheva was the secretary”. “And Zadok and Abiathar were priests” is the pair of 2 Samuel 15:29, who returned the ark of God to Jerusalem and stayed there.

A priest to David. “And also Ira the Jairite was a priest to David”: the Hebrew is kohen ledavid, the word that 8:18 used of David’s sons. The ASV has “chief minister unto David”. The KJV has “a chief ruler about David”. The Geneva has “chief about David”. The NIV has “David’s priest”. The NWT 1984 has “a priest of David”. The Douay has “the priest of David”. The TLB has “David’s personal chaplain”. Rahlfs’ Greek has hiereus. “Jairite” may mean of Jair, whose villages are the Havvoth-jair (Numbers 32:41), and the text does not call him a Levite; the translations that say “chief minister” or “chief ruler” are choosing a sense because of who he is, and there is no other Hebrew word for them to be translating. The chapter that began with a horn ends with a list, and the list begins with Joab.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “a man of worthlessness”; “we have no portion in David”; “every man to his tent”; “clung to their king”; “the ten women, concubines”; “a house of ward”; “sustained”; “a living widowhood”; “summon”; “worse for us than Absalom”; “escape from our eye”; “Is it peace with you, my brother?”; “the right hand of Joab took hold”; “in the belly”; “poured out his bowels”; “did not do it to him a second time”; “whoever delights in Joab”; “stood still”; “the Berites”; “a mound”; “working ruin”; “a wise woman”; “the words of your handmaid”; “a city and a mother in Israel”; “swallow up”; “the inheritance of Jehovah”; “far be it, far be it”; “lifted up his hand against the king”; “in her wisdom”; “the forced labour”; and “a priest to David”; with the written forms in the notes.

Echoes paid. The ten concubines left, taken and shut away (2 Samuel 15:16; 12:11–12; 16:22); the kilkal of Barzillai (2 Samuel 19:33–34); Nabal’s “son of Jesse” (1 Samuel 25:10); Gideon’s, Israel’s and Saul’s tents (Judges 7:8; 1 Samuel 4:10; 13:2; 2 Samuel 19:9); Adam’s and Ruth’s clinging (Genesis 2:24; Ruth 1:14); the blows “in the belly” that killed Asahel, Abner and Ish-bosheth (2 Samuel 2:23; 3:27; 4:6); the people who stood still at Asahel’s body and at Joab’s horn (2 Samuel 2:23, 28); Abishai’s “I will not do it to him twice” (1 Samuel 26:8); Saul’s servants’ “delights in you” (1 Samuel 18:22); the Cherethites and Pelethites (2 Samuel 8:18; 1 Kings 1:38, 44); the guard that crossed before the king (2 Samuel 15:18); the ruining of Ammon (2 Samuel 11:1); the wise woman of Tekoa, Abigail’s handmaid and Deborah the mother in Israel (2 Samuel 14:2, 12; 1 Samuel 25:24; Judges 5:7); Hushai’s “swallowed up” (2 Samuel 17:16); David’s “inheritance of Jehovah” (1 Samuel 26:19); the lifted hand of Absalom’s men and Absalom thrown into the pit (2 Samuel 18:28, 17); Amasa the cousin set over the army and promised it (2 Samuel 17:25; 19:14); and the first list of David’s officers (2 Samuel 8:16–18).

Echoes planted. Israel’s answer to Rehoboam, with “portion”, “inheritance” and “to your tents” (1 Kings 12:16; 2 Chronicles 10:16); David’s charge to Solomon about what Joab did to Abner and Amasa, and Joab at the altar, struck down by Benaiah (1 Kings 2:5, 28–34); Ben-hadad’s and Tiglath-pileser’s capture of Abel-beth-maacah (1 Kings 15:20; 2 Kings 15:29); Adoniram and Adoram over the levy (1 Kings 4:6; 12:18); the scribe Shavsha (1 Chronicles 18:16); the “inheritance of Jehovah” again (2 Samuel 21:3, now on these pages; Psalm 127:3); the famine and the Gibeonites (2 Samuel 21:1–2, now on these pages); and Ira the Jairite’s namesakes (2 Samuel 23:26, 38). None of the others is translated here yet.
