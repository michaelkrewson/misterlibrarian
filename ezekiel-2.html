The commission: the voice tells Ezekiel to stand, spirit sets him on his feet, and he is sent to a rebellious house that will know a prophet has been among them whether it listens or not. He is told not to fear them, and a hand holds out a scroll written front and back with dirges, moaning and woe.
Follows the vision of chapter 1 directly, which dates itself to the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's exile (1:2). Chapter 2 gives no date of its own.

Ezekiel 2 — The Mister Translation

💬 Discuss this chapter
1
וַיֹּאמֶר, אֵלָי:  בֶּן-אָדָם עֲמֹד עַל-רַגְלֶיךָ, וַאֲדַבֵּר אֹתָךְ.
And he said to me, “Son of man, stand on your feet, and I will speak with you.”note
Y me dijo: «Hijo de hombre, ponte en pie, y hablaré contigo».
2
וַתָּבֹא בִי רוּחַ, כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר אֵלַי, וַתַּעֲמִדֵנִי, עַל-רַגְלָי; וָאֶשְׁמַע, אֵת מִדַּבֵּר אֵלָי. {פ}
And spirit entered into me as he spoke to me, and set me on my feet; and I heard the one speaking to me.note
Y entró espíritu en mí mientras me hablaba, y me puso en pie; y oí al que me hablaba.
3
וַיֹּאמֶר אֵלַי, בֶּן-אָדָם שׁוֹלֵחַ אֲנִי אוֹתְךָ אֶל-בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל--אֶל-גּוֹיִם הַמּוֹרְדִים, אֲשֶׁר מָרְדוּ-בִי:  הֵמָּה וַאֲבוֹתָם פָּשְׁעוּ בִי, עַד-עֶצֶם הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
And he said to me, “Son of man, I am sending you to the sons of Israel, to rebellious nations that have rebelled against me; they and their fathers have transgressed against me to this very day.note
Y me dijo: «Hijo de hombre, te envío a los hijos de Israel, a naciones rebeldes que se han rebelado contra mí; ellos y sus padres han transgredido contra mí hasta este mismo día.
4
וְהַבָּנִים, קְשֵׁי פָנִים וְחִזְקֵי-לֵב--אֲנִי שׁוֹלֵחַ אוֹתְךָ, אֲלֵיהֶם; וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵיהֶם, כֹּה אָמַר אֲדֹנָי יְהוִה.
And the sons are hard of face and stiff of heart; I am sending you to them, and you shall say to them, ‘Thus says the Lord Jehovah.’note
Y los hijos son duros de rostro y tercos de corazón; te envío a ellos, y les dirás: “Así dice el Señor Jehová”.
5
וְהֵמָּה אִם-יִשְׁמְעוּ וְאִם-יֶחְדָּלוּ, כִּי בֵּית מְרִי הֵמָּה--וְיָדְעוּ, כִּי נָבִיא הָיָה בְתוֹכָם. {פ}
And they, whether they hear or whether they refuse—for they are a rebellious house—will know that a prophet was among them.note
Y ellos, escuchen o se nieguen —pues son una casa rebelde—, sabrán que hubo un profeta en medio de ellos.
6
וְאַתָּה בֶן-אָדָם אַל-תִּירָא מֵהֶם וּמִדִּבְרֵיהֶם אַל-תִּירָא, כִּי סָרָבִים וְסַלּוֹנִים אוֹתָךְ, וְאֶל-עַקְרַבִּים, אַתָּה יוֹשֵׁב:  מִדִּבְרֵיהֶם אַל-תִּירָא וּמִפְּנֵיהֶם אַל-תֵּחָת, כִּי בֵּית מְרִי הֵמָּה.
And you, son of man, do not be afraid of them, and do not be afraid of their words, though briers and thorns are with you and you dwell among scorpions; do not be afraid of their words, and do not be dismayed at their faces, for they are a rebellious house.note
Y tú, hijo de hombre, no les tengas miedo, ni tengas miedo de sus palabras, aunque haya zarzas y espinos contigo y habites entre escorpiones; no tengas miedo de sus palabras, ni te desanimes ante sus rostros, porque son una casa rebelde.
7
וְדִבַּרְתָּ אֶת-דְּבָרַי אֲלֵיהֶם, אִם-יִשְׁמְעוּ וְאִם-יֶחְדָּלוּ:  כִּי מְרִי, הֵמָּה. {פ}
And you shall speak my words to them, whether they hear or whether they refuse; for rebellion is what they are.note
Y les dirás mis palabras, escuchen o se nieguen; porque rebeldía es lo que son.
8
וְאַתָּה בֶן-אָדָם, שְׁמַע אֵת אֲשֶׁר-אֲנִי מְדַבֵּר אֵלֶיךָ--אַל-תְּהִי-מֶרִי, כְּבֵית הַמֶּרִי:  פְּצֵה פִיךָ--וֶאֱכֹל, אֵת אֲשֶׁר-אֲנִי נֹתֵן אֵלֶיךָ.
And you, son of man, hear what I am speaking to you. Do not be rebellious like the rebellious house. Open your mouth and eat what I am giving you.”note
Y tú, hijo de hombre, oye lo que te hablo. No seas rebelde como la casa rebelde. Abre tu boca y come lo que te doy».
9
וָאֶרְאֶה, וְהִנֵּה-יָד שְׁלוּחָה אֵלָי; וְהִנֵּה-בוֹ, מְגִלַּת-סֵפֶר.
And I looked, and there was a hand stretched out to me, and in it a scroll of a book.note
Y miré, y había una mano extendida hacia mí, y en ella un rollo de libro.
10
וַיִּפְרֹשׂ אוֹתָהּ לְפָנַי, וְהִיא כְתוּבָה פָּנִים וְאָחוֹר; וְכָתוּב אֵלֶיהָ, קִנִים וָהֶגֶה וָהִי. {ס}
And he spread it before me, and it was written on front and back; and written on it were dirges and moaning and woe.note
Y lo extendió delante de mí, y estaba escrito por delante y por detrás; y había escritas en él endechas, gemido y ayes.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–2 · Ezekiel 2:1–2 “son of man, stand on your feet” — spirit enters him and sets him on them

⭐ “Son of man.” The chapter opens with the title the voice will keep using for him, ben adam (ben adam). “Son of man” stands in 107 verses of the Hebrew Bible, and it stands in ninety-three verses of Ezekiel. The other fourteen include Numbers 23:19, Psalm 8:5 (now on these pages), Job 35:8 and Daniel 8:17 (not yet on these pages), where an angel says to Daniel the same two words that the voice says here. In Ezekiel it is the name he is called by, and these are the first words of the voice that chapter 1 ended on: “I heard a voice speaking” (Ezekiel 1:28, now on these pages). The TLB renders it “son of dust”, hearing adam in adamah, the ground.

⭐ On his feet. He had fallen on his face (1:28), and the voice does not lift him; it tells him to stand, and then it is spirit that does it. “And it set me on my feet” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here and Ezekiel 3:24 (now on these pages). The KJV has “stand upon thy feet, and I will speak unto thee” and “the spirit entered into me when he spake unto me, and set me upon my feet”; the NIV has “stand up on your feet and I will speak to you” and “the Spirit came into me and raised me to my feet”; the NWT 2013 “spirit came into me and made me stand up on my feet”. ⚠ The Hebrew word is ruach, which is wind, breath or spirit (ruach), and the shelf splits on the capital letter: this translation prints it without one.

Verses 3–5 · Ezekiel 2:3–5 “I am sending you” — rebellious nations — hard of face, stiff of heart — a rebellious house

⭐ “I am sending you.” “I am sending you” (sholeach ani otkha) stands in this one verse, and the verb is the one earlier commissions use: Exodus 3:10 (“I will send you to Pharaoh”) and Isaiah 6:8 (“whom shall I send?”), both now on these pages. And then the people he is sent to: “rebellious nations” (goyim hamordim) stands in this one verse. Goyim is the plural that usually names the other nations, and here it names Israel. The shelf splits on the number: the KJV has “to a rebellious nation that hath rebelled against me”, the NIV “to a rebellious nation that has rebelled against me”, the ASV “to nations that are rebellious”, the Douay “to a rebellious people” and the NWT 2013 “to rebellious nations that have rebelled against me”. This translation keeps the plural.

⭐ Hard of face. “Hard of face” (qeshei panim) stands in this one verse. The law’s word for this people was stiff-necked, which Jehovah says to Moses at Exodus 32:9 (now on these pages); here the stiffness has moved to the face and the heart. The KJV has “impudent children and stiffhearted”, the NIV “obstinate and stubborn”, the Douay “children of a hard face, and of an obstinate heart” and the NWT 2013 “defiant and hardhearted”.

⭐ A rebellious house. “A rebellious house” (beit meri) stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible, and all seven are in Ezekiel: 2:5 and 2:6, 3:9, 3:26 and 3:27, and 12:2 and 12:3 (meri). Verse 8 has the form with the article, “the rebellious house”. On the shelf, the KJV has “a rebellious house” and the NIV “a rebellious people”. What the verse promises is a result and not a hearing: whether they hear or refuse, “they will know that a prophet was among them” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and Ezekiel 33:33 (not yet on these pages). The KJV has “yet shall know that there hath been a prophet among them”, the NIV “they will know that a prophet has been among them” and the NWT 2013 “they will certainly know that a prophet was among them”.

Verses 6–7 · Ezekiel 2:6–7 do not be afraid — briers and thorns, and scorpions — whether they hear or refuse

⭐ “Do not be afraid of them.” “Do not be afraid of them” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Joshua 10:8 (now on these pages), where Jehovah says it to Joshua before a battle, and here. A close parallel is Jeremiah’s commission, where Jehovah says not to be afraid of them (Jeremiah 1:8, now on these pages) and not to be dismayed at them, lest he dismay him before them (1:17, now on these pages); the same verb of dismay is in verse 6 here, said of their faces. The KJV has “nor be dismayed at their looks”, the NWT 1984 “at their faces do not you be struck with terror” and the NWT 2013 “do not be terrified by their faces”.

⚠ Briers and thorns, and scorpions. “Sarabim and sallonim” stands in this one verse, so nothing else in the Bible shows what the pair means. The shelf splits: the KJV has “briers and thorns be with thee, and thou dost dwell among scorpions”, the NWT 2013 “you are surrounded by briars and thorns”, the Geneva “rebels and thorns”, the Douay “thou art among unbelievers and destroyers” and the NWT 1984 “obstinate ones and things pricking you”. This translation prints “briers and thorns” and says that it is a guess. And “scorpions” (aqrabbim) stands in eight verses of the Hebrew Bible: three where it is the name of a mountain ascent, Numbers 34:4, Joshua 15:3 and Judges 1:36; four where it is Rehoboam’s threat, that where his father chastised with whips he will chastise with scorpions (1 Kings 12:11, now on these pages, and 12:14, with 2 Chronicles 10:11 and 10:14); and here (aqrab).

⭐ Whether they hear or refuse. “Whether they hear or whether they refuse” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: verses 5 and 7 of this chapter, and Ezekiel 3:11 (now on these pages). He is told to speak whether or not he is heard. The KJV has “for they are most rebellious”, the NIV “for they are rebellious”, the NWT 1984 “for they are a case of rebellion” and the TLB “for they are utter rebels”. The Hebrew is ki meri hemmah, literally ‘for they are rebellion’, and this translation says it that way.

Verses 8–10 · Ezekiel 2:8–10 do not be rebellious — open your mouth and eat — a hand, a scroll, written on front and back

⭐ “Open your mouth and eat.” The prophet is told not to be what he is sent to: “Do not be rebellious like the rebellious house.” “Open your mouth” (pitseh fikha) stands in this one verse, and what he is to put in it comes in the next verses and in chapter 3. The KJV has “Be not thou rebellious like that rebellious house: open thy mouth, and eat that I give thee” and the NIV “Do not rebel like that rebellious people; open your mouth and eat what I give you”. The words eaten are in Jeremiah’s mouth as well (Jeremiah 15:16, not yet on these pages), and the scroll that is taken and eaten returns in the New Testament, where an angel tells John to take the little scroll and eat it, bitter in the stomach and sweet as honey in the mouth (Revelation 10:9–10, now on these pages).

⭐ A hand, and a scroll. The prophet sees a hand sent to him, and in it “a roll of a book” (megillah, sefer) stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible: here; Psalm 40:8 (now on these pages); and Jeremiah 36:2 and 36:4 (not yet on these pages), the scroll Baruch writes at Jeremiah’s dictation. The KJV has “a roll of a book” and the NIV “a scroll”.

⚠ Front and back, and what is written. “Front and back” (panim ve’achor) stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible; the other two, 1 Chronicles 19:10 and 2 Chronicles 13:14 (neither yet on these pages), are a battle line with the enemy before and behind. The tablets of Moses were written on both sides too, with other words (Exodus 32:15, now on these pages), and in the New Testament the scroll in the hand of the one on the throne is written inside and on the back (Revelation 5:1, now on these pages). Then what it says: “dirges and moaning and woe” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “lamentations, and mourning, and woe”, the NIV “words of lament and mourning and woe”, the NWT 1984 “dirges and moaning and wailing”, the NWT 2013 “Dirges and mourning and wailing”, the Douay “lamentations, and canticles, and woe” and the TLB “warnings and sorrows and pronouncements of doom”. The scroll is full on both sides, and he is to eat it before he speaks (3:1, now on these pages).

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “rebellious house” (beit meri) kept as one phrase at verses 5, 6 and 8, so the seven-verse formula of the book is visible from its first use; “speak” and “send” kept as the two verbs of the commission; “whether they hear or whether they refuse” kept identical at verses 5 and 7; “do not be afraid” kept as one clause at verse 6, three times; and “spirit” (ruach) printed without a capital, as in chapter 1.

Echoes paid. The voice that chapter 1 ended on (“I heard a voice speaking”, 1:28) now speaks; Jeremiah 1:8 and 1:17, the commission of Jeremiah; and Exodus 32:9, the people who were stiff-necked at the foot of the mountain.

Echoes planted. The scroll that is eaten, in Ezekiel 3:1–3 (now on these pages), where it is sweet as honey; the spirit that sets him on his feet again at 3:24; the rebellious house that comes back at 3:9, 3:26–27 and 12:2–3; and the title ben adam, ninety-three verses of it, which an angel also uses of Daniel at Daniel 8:17 (not yet on these pages).

Ezekiel 3, now on these pages: the scroll is eaten and tastes sweet as honey, and the prophet is made a watchman, sits seven days among the exiles at Tel-abib, and is shut up in his house and made mute.
