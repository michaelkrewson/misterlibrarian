“As his name is, so is he”: Samuel dies and all Israel buries him in his house at Ramah, and David goes down to the wilderness. At the shearing in Carmel he sends ten young men to ask the rich Nabal of Maon for a share; Nabal answers “Who is David?”, and David girds on his sword with four hundred men and swears to leave no male of the house alive by morning. Nabal’s wife Abigail loads donkeys with bread, wine, raisins and cakes of figs, meets him on the road, falls at his feet and turns him back from bloodguilt. When she tells Nabal in the morning his heart dies within him; ten days later Jehovah strikes him, and Abigail becomes David’s wife, with Ahinoam of Jezreel, while Saul gives Michal to Palti.
Undated: in Saul’s reign, after the cave at En-gedi; Samuel’s death is placed here, before the second sparing of chapter 26.

And Samuel died; and all Israel gathered together and lamented him, and they buried him in his house at Ramah. And David arose and went down to the wilderness of Paran.note
Y murió Samuel; y todo Israel se juntó y lo lamentó, y lo sepultaron en su casa, en Ramá. Y David se levantó y bajó al desierto de Parán.
2
וְאִישׁ בְּמָעוֹן וּמַעֲשֵׂהוּ בַכַּרְמֶל, וְהָאִישׁ גָּדוֹל מְאֹד, וְלוֹ צֹאן שְׁלֹשֶׁת-אֲלָפִים, וְאֶלֶף עִזִּים; וַיְהִי בִּגְזֹז אֶת-צֹאנוֹ, בַּכַּרְמֶל.
And there was a man in Maon, and his business was in Carmel; and the man was very great, and he had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats. And he was shearing his sheep in Carmel.note
Y había un hombre en Maón, y su hacienda estaba en Carmel; y el hombre era muy grande, y tenía tres mil ovejas y mil cabras. Y estaba esquilando sus ovejas en Carmel.
3
וְשֵׁם הָאִישׁ נָבָל, וְשֵׁם אִשְׁתּוֹ אֲבִגָיִל; וְהָאִשָּׁה טוֹבַת-שֶׂכֶל וִיפַת תֹּאַר, וְהָאִישׁ קָשֶׁה וְרַע מַעֲלָלִים וְהוּא כלבו כָלִבִּי.
And the name of the man was Nabal, and the name of his wife Abigail. And the woman was good of understanding and beautiful of form; but the man was harsh and evil in his deeds, and he was a Calebite.note
Y el nombre del hombre era Nabal, y el nombre de su mujer, Abigail. Y la mujer era de buen entendimiento y hermosa de figura; pero el hombre era duro y malo en sus hechos, y era calebita.
4
וַיִּשְׁמַע דָּוִד, בַּמִּדְבָּר:  כִּי-גֹזֵז נָבָל, אֶת-צֹאנוֹ.
And David heard in the wilderness that Nabal was shearing his sheep.note
Y David oyó en el desierto que Nabal estaba esquilando sus ovejas.
5
וַיִּשְׁלַח דָּוִד, עֲשָׂרָה נְעָרִים; וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד לַנְּעָרִים, עֲלוּ כַרְמֶלָה וּבָאתֶם אֶל-נָבָל, וּשְׁאֶלְתֶּם-לוֹ בִשְׁמִי, לְשָׁלוֹם.
And David sent ten young men; and David said to the young men: ‘Go up to Carmel, and come to Nabal, and ask after his welfare in my name.note
Y David envió diez jóvenes; y David dijo a los jóvenes: «Suban a Carmel, y vayan a Nabal, y pregúntenle en mi nombre por su bienestar.
6
וַאֲמַרְתֶּם כֹּה, לֶחָי; וְאַתָּה שָׁלוֹם וּבֵיתְךָ שָׁלוֹם, וְכֹל אֲשֶׁר-לְךָ שָׁלוֹם.
And thus you shall say: “To life! And peace to you, and peace to your house, and peace to all that you have.note
Y así dirán: “¡A la vida! Y paz a ti, y paz a tu casa, y paz a todo lo que tienes.
7
וְעַתָּה שָׁמַעְתִּי, כִּי גֹזְזִים לָךְ; עַתָּה, הָרֹעִים אֲשֶׁר-לְךָ הָיוּ עִמָּנוּ--לֹא הֶכְלַמְנוּם וְלֹא-נִפְקַד לָהֶם מְאוּמָה, כָּל-יְמֵי הֱיוֹתָם בַּכַּרְמֶל.
And now I have heard that you have shearers. Now, your shepherds were with us; we did not humiliate them, and nothing of theirs went missing all the days they were in Carmel.note
Y ahora he oído que tienes esquiladores. Ahora bien, tus pastores estuvieron con nosotros; no los humillamos, y no les faltó nada en todos los días que estuvieron en Carmel.
8
שְׁאַל אֶת-נְעָרֶיךָ וְיַגִּידוּ לָךְ, וְיִמְצְאוּ הַנְּעָרִים חֵן בְּעֵינֶיךָ--כִּי-עַל-יוֹם טוֹב, בָּנוּ; תְּנָה-נָּא, אֵת אֲשֶׁר תִּמְצָא יָדְךָ לַעֲבָדֶיךָ, וּלְבִנְךָ, לְדָוִד.
Ask your young men, and they will tell you. So let the young men find favour in your eyes, for we have come on a good day. Give, please, whatever your hand finds to your servants and to your son David.”’note
Pregunta a tus jóvenes, y ellos te lo dirán. Hallen, pues, los jóvenes favor a tus ojos, porque hemos venido en un buen día. Da, por favor, lo que halle tu mano a tus siervos y a tu hijo David”».
9
וַיָּבֹאוּ נַעֲרֵי דָוִד, וַיְדַבְּרוּ אֶל-נָבָל כְּכָל-הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה בְּשֵׁם דָּוִד; וַיָּנוּחוּ.
And David’s young men came, and spoke to Nabal according to all these words in the name of David; and they waited.note
Y llegaron los jóvenes de David, y hablaron a Nabal conforme a todas estas palabras en nombre de David; y esperaron.
10
וַיַּעַן נָבָל אֶת-עַבְדֵי דָוִד, וַיֹּאמֶר, מִי דָוִד, וּמִי בֶן-יִשָׁי; הַיּוֹם, רַבּוּ עֲבָדִים, הַמִּתְפָּרְצִים, אִישׁ מִפְּנֵי אֲדֹנָיו.
And Nabal answered David’s servants and said: ‘Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse? Today there are many servants who are breaking away, each from his master.note
Y Nabal respondió a los siervos de David, y dijo: «¿Quién es David? ¿Y quién es el hijo de Isaí? Hoy son muchos los siervos que se escapan, cada uno de su señor.
11
וְלָקַחְתִּי אֶת-לַחְמִי, וְאֶת-מֵימַי, וְאֵת טִבְחָתִי, אֲשֶׁר טָבַחְתִּי לְגֹזְזָי; וְנָתַתִּי, לַאֲנָשִׁים, אֲשֶׁר לֹא יָדַעְתִּי, אֵי מִזֶּה הֵמָּה.
And shall I take my bread and my water, and my slaughtered meat that I have slaughtered for my shearers, and give it to men of whom I do not know where they are from?’note
¿Y he de tomar mi pan y mi agua, y mi carne degollada que he degollado para mis esquiladores, y darla a hombres que no sé de dónde son?».
12
וַיַּהַפְכוּ נַעֲרֵי-דָוִד, לְדַרְכָּם; וַיָּשֻׁבוּ, וַיָּבֹאוּ, וַיַּגִּדוּ לוֹ, כְּכֹל הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה.
And David’s young men turned on their way and went back, and came and told him according to all these words.note
Y los jóvenes de David dieron la vuelta por su camino y regresaron, y vinieron y le contaron conforme a todas estas palabras.
13
וַיֹּאמֶר דָּוִד לַאֲנָשָׁיו חִגְרוּ אִישׁ אֶת-חַרְבּוֹ, וַיַּחְגְּרוּ אִישׁ אֶת-חַרְבּוֹ, וַיַּחְגֹּר גַּם-דָּוִד, אֶת-חַרְבּוֹ; וַיַּעֲלוּ אַחֲרֵי דָוִד, כְּאַרְבַּע מֵאוֹת אִישׁ, וּמָאתַיִם, יָשְׁבוּ עַל-הַכֵּלִים.
And David said to his men: ‘Gird on, each man, his sword!’ And they girded on each man his sword, and David also girded on his sword; and there went up after David about four hundred men, and two hundred stayed by the baggage.note
Y David dijo a sus hombres: «¡Cíñase cada uno su espada!». Y se ciñeron cada uno su espada, y también David se ciñó su espada; y subieron tras David unos cuatrocientos hombres, y doscientos se quedaron junto al equipaje.
14
וְלַאֲבִיגַיִל אֵשֶׁת נָבָל, הִגִּיד נַעַר-אֶחָד מֵהַנְּעָרִים לֵאמֹר:  הִנֵּה שָׁלַח דָּוִד מַלְאָכִים מֵהַמִּדְבָּר לְבָרֵךְ אֶת-אֲדֹנֵינוּ, וַיָּעַט בָּהֶם.
And to Abigail, Nabal’s wife, one of the young men told it, saying: ‘Look — David sent messengers from the wilderness to bless our master, and he swooped on them.note
Y a Abigail, mujer de Nabal, se lo contó uno de los jóvenes, diciendo: «Mira, David envió mensajeros desde el desierto para bendecir a nuestro amo, y él se lanzó sobre ellos.
15
וְהָאֲנָשִׁים, טֹבִים לָנוּ מְאֹד; וְלֹא הָכְלַמְנוּ, וְלֹא-פָקַדְנוּ מְאוּמָה, כָּל-יְמֵי הִתְהַלַּכְנוּ אִתָּם, בִּהְיוֹתֵנוּ בַּשָּׂדֶה.
And the men were very good to us; we were not humiliated, and we missed nothing all the days we went about with them, when we were in the field.note
Y los hombres fueron muy buenos con nosotros; no fuimos humillados, y no echamos de menos nada en todos los días que anduvimos con ellos, cuando estábamos en el campo.
16
חוֹמָה הָיוּ עָלֵינוּ, גַּם-לַיְלָה גַּם-יוֹמָם, כָּל-יְמֵי הֱיוֹתֵנוּ עִמָּם, רֹעִים הַצֹּאן.
They were a wall around us, both by night and by day, all the days we were with them, tending the flock.note
Muro fueron alrededor de nosotros, tanto de noche como de día, todos los días que estuvimos con ellos apacentando el rebaño.
17
וְעַתָּה, דְּעִי וּרְאִי מַה-תַּעֲשִׂי--כִּי-כָלְתָה הָרָעָה אֶל-אֲדֹנֵינוּ, וְעַל כָּל-בֵּיתוֹ; וְהוּא, בֶּן-בְּלִיַּעַל, מִדַּבֵּר, אֵלָיו.
And now, know and see what you will do; for the evil is decided against our master and against all his house; and he is a son of worthlessness — one cannot speak to him.’note
Y ahora, sabe y mira lo que harás; porque el mal está decidido contra nuestro amo y contra toda su casa; y él es un hijo de la nada: no se le puede hablar».
18
וַתְּמַהֵר אבוגיל אֲבִיגַיִל וַתִּקַּח מָאתַיִם לֶחֶם וּשְׁנַיִם נִבְלֵי-יַיִן, וְחָמֵשׁ צֹאן עשוות עֲשׂוּיוֹת וְחָמֵשׁ סְאִים קָלִי, וּמֵאָה צִמֻּקִים, וּמָאתַיִם דְּבֵלִים; וַתָּשֶׂם, עַל-הַחֲמֹרִים.
And Abigail hurried, and took two hundred loaves of bread, and two skins of wine, and five sheep made ready, and five seahs of roasted grain, and a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and put them on the donkeys.note
Y Abigail se dio prisa, y tomó doscientos panes, y dos odres de vino, y cinco ovejas preparadas, y cinco seás de grano tostado, y cien racimos de pasas, y doscientas tortas de higos, y lo puso sobre los asnos.
19
וַתֹּאמֶר לִנְעָרֶיהָ עִבְרוּ לְפָנַי, הִנְנִי אַחֲרֵיכֶם בָּאָה; וּלְאִישָׁהּ נָבָל, לֹא הִגִּידָה.
And she said to her young men: ‘Go on ahead of me; look — I am coming after you.’ But to her husband Nabal she told nothing.note
Y dijo a sus jóvenes: «Pasen delante de mí; miren, yo voy tras ustedes». Pero a su marido Nabal no le dijo nada.
20
וְהָיָה הִיא רֹכֶבֶת עַל-הַחֲמוֹר, וְיֹרֶדֶת בְּסֵתֶר הָהָר, וְהִנֵּה דָוִד וַאֲנָשָׁיו, יֹרְדִים לִקְרָאתָהּ; וַתִּפְגֹשׁ, אֹתָם.
And so it was, as she was riding on the donkey and coming down under cover of the mountain, look — David and his men were coming down toward her; and she met them.note
Y sucedió que, mientras ella iba montada en el asno y bajaba al amparo del monte, mira, David y sus hombres bajaban hacia ella; y ella los encontró.
21
וְדָוִד אָמַר, אַךְ לַשֶּׁקֶר שָׁמַרְתִּי אֶת-כָּל-אֲשֶׁר לָזֶה בַּמִּדְבָּר, וְלֹא-נִפְקַד מִכָּל-אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ, מְאוּמָה; וַיָּשֶׁב-לִי רָעָה, תַּחַת טוֹבָה.
Now David had said: ‘Surely it was for a lie that I guarded all that this one has in the wilderness, and nothing went missing of all that is his; and he has returned me evil in place of good.note
Y David había dicho: «De cierto fue para una mentira que guardé todo lo que este tiene en el desierto, y nada faltó de todo lo que es suyo; y él me ha devuelto mal en lugar de bien.
22
כֹּה-יַעֲשֶׂה אֱלֹהִים לְאֹיְבֵי דָוִד, וְכֹה יֹסִיף:  אִם-אַשְׁאִיר מִכָּל-אֲשֶׁר-לוֹ עַד-הַבֹּקֶר, מַשְׁתִּין בְּקִיר.
Thus may God do to the enemies of David, and more also, if I leave of all that is his until the morning anyone who urinates against the wall.’note
Así haga Dios a los enemigos de David, y aun les añada, si de todo lo que es suyo dejo hasta la mañana a uno solo que orine contra la pared».
23
וַתֵּרֶא אֲבִיגַיִל, אֶת-דָּוִד, וַתְּמַהֵר, וַתֵּרֶד מֵעַל הַחֲמוֹר; וַתִּפֹּל לְאַפֵּי דָוִד, עַל-פָּנֶיהָ, וַתִּשְׁתַּחוּ, אָרֶץ.
And Abigail saw David, and she hurried and got down from the donkey, and fell before David on her face, and bowed down to the ground.note
Y Abigail vio a David, y se dio prisa y bajó del asno, y cayó delante de David sobre su rostro, y se inclinó a tierra.
24
וַתִּפֹּל, עַל-רַגְלָיו, וַתֹּאמֶר, בִּי-אֲנִי אֲדֹנִי הֶעָוֺן; וּתְדַבֶּר-נָא אֲמָתְךָ, בְּאָזְנֶיךָ, וּשְׁמַע, אֵת דִּבְרֵי אֲמָתֶךָ.
And she fell at his feet and said: ‘On me, on me, my lord, be the iniquity! And let your handmaid speak, please, in your ears, and hear the words of your handmaid.note
Y cayó a sus pies y dijo: «¡Sobre mí, sobre mí, señor mío, sea la iniquidad! Y deja que tu sierva hable, por favor, a tus oídos, y oye las palabras de tu sierva.
25
אַל-נָא יָשִׂים אֲדֹנִי אֶת-לִבּוֹ אֶל-אִישׁ הַבְּלִיַּעַל הַזֶּה עַל-נָבָל, כִּי כִשְׁמוֹ כֶּן-הוּא--נָבָל שְׁמוֹ, וּנְבָלָה עִמּוֹ; וַאֲנִי, אֲמָתְךָ--לֹא רָאִיתִי אֶת-נַעֲרֵי אֲדֹנִי, אֲשֶׁר שָׁלָחְתָּ.
Please let not my lord set his heart on this man of worthlessness, on Nabal; for as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him. But I, your handmaid, did not see the young men of my lord whom you sent.note
Por favor, no ponga mi señor su corazón en este hombre de la nada, en Nabal; porque como es su nombre, así es él: Nabal es su nombre, y la insensatez está con él. Pero yo, tu sierva, no vi a los jóvenes de mi señor que enviaste.
26
וְעַתָּה אֲדֹנִי, חַי-יְהוָה וְחֵי-נַפְשְׁךָ אֲשֶׁר מְנָעֲךָ יְהוָה מִבּוֹא בְדָמִים, וְהוֹשֵׁעַ יָדְךָ, לָךְ; וְעַתָּה, יִהְיוּ כְנָבָל אֹיְבֶיךָ, וְהַמְבַקְשִׁים אֶל-אֲדֹנִי, רָעָה.
And now, my lord, as Jehovah lives and as your soul lives, Jehovah has withheld you from coming into bloodguilt and from your own hand saving you. And now let your enemies, and those who seek evil against my lord, be like Nabal.note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verse 1 · The death of Samuel — buried in his house at Ramah, and David goes down to the wilderness of Paran

Samuel died. “And Samuel died; and all Israel gathered together and lamented him”: va-yamot Shemu’el. The man whose birth fills the book’s first chapter is gone in two words, and he is not seen dying. The book will say it a second time, almost word for word, as the opening of the night at En-dor: “Now Samuel had died, and all Israel had lamented him and buried him in Ramah, in his own city” (28:3, now on these pages). “Lamented him”, va-yispedu lo, from safad, the wailing of a funeral. The KJV has “and all the Israelites were gathered together, and lamented him”; the NWT 1984 “and all Israel proceeded to collect together and bewail him”; the NIV “and all Israel assembled and mourned for him”.

Buried in his house. “And they buried him in his house at Ramah”: be-veito ba-Ramah. Ramah was where Samuel lived and where his circuit ended: “And his return was to Ramah, for his house was there” (7:17, already on these pages). Whether “his house” is the dwelling itself or the family’s ground beside it the text does not say; 28:3 has “in Ramah, in his own city” instead. The TLB has “and buried him in his family plot at Ramah”; the Douay, following the Latin, “and buried him in his house in Ramatha”.

The wilderness of Paran. “And David arose and went down to the wilderness of Paran”: the great wilderness south of Judah where Ishmael grew up, “And he lived in the wilderness of Paran” (Genesis 21:21), and where Israel camped after Sinai (Numbers 12:16, both already on these pages) (Paran). It lies far to the south of Maon and Carmel, where the next verse sets the story, and the Greek reads “the wilderness of Maon” here (Maan). All seven versions on the English shelf follow the Masoretic “Paran”, and so does this translation. The NIV has “Then David moved down into the Desert of Paran”; the NWT 1984 “Then David rose up and went down to the wilderness”; the Douay “And David rose and went down into the wilderness of Pharan”.

Verses 2–3 · Nabal and Abigail — Maon and Carmel, the sheep-shearing, and “a Calebite” or “like his heart”

Maon and Carmel. “And there was a man in Maon, and his business was in Carmel”: u-ma’asehu ba-Karmel, his work, his holdings. The two towns stand side by side in Judah’s hill country, “Maon, Carmel, and Ziph” (Joshua 15:55, already on these pages) (Maon), and David has just been hiding in “the wilderness of Maon” (23:24–25, already on these pages). This Carmel is not the mountain by the sea; it is the town where Saul, on his way back from Amalek, was “setting up a monument for himself” (15:12, already on these pages) (Carmel). The KJV and ASV have “whose possessions were in Carmel”; the NWT 1984 “and his work was in”; the TLB gives him “a sheep ranch”: “A wealthy man from Maon owned a sheep ranch there”.

Very great, and shearing. “And the man was very great”: gadol me’od, great in wealth, with three thousand sheep and a thousand goats. “And he was shearing his sheep in Carmel”: bi-gzoz et-tsono. Shearing was the flock-owner’s harvest and his feast, and in Genesis it takes the men away from home: Laban “had gone to shear his flock” when Rachel took the teraphim (Genesis 31:19), and Judah “went up to his sheepshearers” on the road where he met Tamar (Genesis 38:12, both already on these pages). At another shearing Absalom will tell his young men, “Mark now, when Amnon’s heart is merry with wine”, and have his brother killed (2 Samuel 13:23–29). The NIV has “He had a thousand goats and three thousand sheep, which he was shearing in Carmel”; the Douay “and it happened that he was shearing his sheep in Carmel”.

Nabal and Abigail. “And the name of the man was Nabal, and the name of his wife Abigail”: Naval and Avigayil (Nabal; Abigail). Her name is spelled four ways in this one chapter: אבגיל here, אביגיל in most verses, אבוגיל in the written text of verse 18, and אביגל in verse 32. The narrator gives her two qualities: tovat-sekhel, “good of understanding”, and yefat to’ar, “beautiful of form”. Sekhel belongs to the verb sakal (sakal), the word the book used of David at Saul’s court: “And David was acting wisely in all his ways” (18:14, already on these pages). Yefat to’ar is the phrase of Rachel, “beautiful in form and lovely to look at” (Genesis 29:17, already on these pages). The man gets two qualities as well: qasheh, “harsh”, and ra ma’alalim, “evil in his deeds”. The KJV has “but the man was churlish and evil in his doings”; the NWT 1984 “but the husband was harsh and bad in his practices”; the NIV “but her husband was surly and mean in his dealings”; the TLB stacks up four words, “uncouth, churlish, stubborn, and ill-mannered”.

A Calebite, or “like his heart”. “And he was a Calebite”: the written text has ke-libbo, “like his heart”, and the reading tradition says kalibbi, “a Calebite” (ketiv and qere). Caleb’s share was Hebron and the hill country round it (Joshua 15:13, already on these pages) (Caleb), so the reading puts Nabal in an honoured family on its own land. The same consonants, k-l-b, spell kelev, a dog (kelev), and in the last chapter David called himself “a dead dog” before Saul (24:14, already on these pages). The Greek heard the dog: it calls Nabal kynikos, “doglike”. The KJV and ASV have “and he was of the house of Caleb”; the Geneva “and was of the family of Caleb”; the TLB “who was a descendant of Caleb”.

Verses 4–8 · David’s ten young men — “To life!”, peace three times, and “your son David”

Ask after his welfare. “And David sent ten young men … ‘Go up to Carmel, and come to Nabal, and ask after his welfare in my name’”: u-she’eltem lo vi-shmi le-shalom, literally “ask him in my name for peace” (shalom). It is the phrase of the three men Saul would meet at the oak of Tabor, “they will ask after your welfare” (10:4), and of David at the battle line, who “asked his brothers how they were” (17:22, both already on these pages). The ASV has “and greet him in my name”; the NWT 1984 “and ask him in my name about his welfare”; the Douay “and salute him in my name with peace”; the Geneva “and ask him in my name how he doeth”.

“To life!” “And thus you shall say: ‘To life! And peace to you, and peace to your house, and peace to all that you have’”: ko … le-chai. The first word of the greeting is two letters and a vowel, and the shelf cannot agree what it is. As the Masoretic text points it, le-chai is “to life” or “to the living one”. The KJV and ASV make it a description of Nabal: “to him that liveth in prosperity”; the NIV a toast, “Long life to you!”; the NWT 1984 has “to my brother”, the reading le-achi, one letter longer; the NWT 2013 goes back to the toast, “May you live long”; the Douay, following the Latin, “Peace be to my brethren”. The Greek has eis hōras, “for seasons”. Then shalom three times: peace to Nabal, to his house, and to everything he owns.

We did not humiliate them. “Your shepherds were with us; we did not humiliate them, and nothing of theirs went missing”: lo hekhlamnum, from kalam, to humiliate, to treat with contempt (kalam), the word of Jonathan’s anger at the new moon, “because his father had humiliated him” (20:34, already on these pages), and lo nifqad lahem me’umah, “nothing was missed by them”, the verb paqad used of an empty place, as when “David’s place was missed” at Saul’s table (20:27, already on these pages) (paqad). Readers have taken the message two ways: as a fair claim for a service already given, and as the polite demand of an armed band for its pay. Nabal’s own young man will confirm the service in verses 15–16, in the same words. The NWT 1984 has “We did not molest them”; the KJV “we hurt them not”; the NIV “we did not mistreat them”.

A good day, and “your son David”. “For we have come on a good day”: al-yom tov, a day of feasting, here the shearing feast; Esther uses the phrase of Purim, “a day of gladness and feasting, and a good day” (Esther 9:19). “Give, please, whatever your hand finds to your servants and to your son David”: David calls himself Nabal’s son, as Saul called him in the cave, “Is this your voice, my son David?” (24:16, already on these pages). The NIV has “since we come at a festive time”; the NWT 1984 “because it was upon a good day that we came”; the TLB “for we have come at a happy time of holiday”.

Verses 9–11 · “Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse?” — Nabal’s answer, and servants breaking away

And they waited. “And they spoke to Nabal according to all these words in the name of David; and they waited”: va-yanuchu, “and they rested”, stopped speaking and stood for the answer. The Greek gives the movement to Nabal instead: “and he sprang up”. The KJV and ASV have “and ceased”; the NWT 1984 “and then waited”; the Geneva “and held their peace”.

“Who is David? And who is the son of Jesse?” Mi David u-mi ven-Yishai. It is Gaal’s sneer at Shechem, “Who is Abimelech, and who is Shechem, that we should serve him?” (Judges 9:28, already on these pages). “The son of Jesse” is what Saul calls David when he will not say his name: “Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal?” (20:27); “Will the son of Jesse give to all of you also fields and vineyards?” (22:7, both already on these pages). The same pair of names will come back as a cry of revolt: “We have no portion in David, and no inheritance in the son of Jesse” (2 Samuel 20:1). The NIV has “Who is this David? Who is this son of Jesse?”; the TLB “Who does this son of Jesse think he is?”; the Douay, following the Latin, “and what is the son of Isai?”

Servants breaking away. “Today there are many servants who are breaking away, each from his master”: ha-mitpartsim, from parats, to break out, to breach. It is the root of Perez’s name, “How you have broken out — a breach upon you!” (Genesis 38:29, already on these pages) (perets), and David is of Perez’s line (Ruth 4:18–22, already on these pages). Nabal does not name the master David broke away from; he does not need to. The NWT 1984 has “Nowadays the servants that are breaking away, each one from before his master, have become many”; the KJV “there be many servants now a days that break away every man from his master”; the NIV “Many servants are breaking away from their masters these days.”

My bread, my water, my slaughter. “And shall I take my bread and my water, and my slaughtered meat that I have slaughtered for my shearers”: lachmi … meimai … tivchati asher tavachti … le-gozezai, “my” four times, and the noun and verb of slaughter side by side. The Greek has “my wine” for “my water”. The NWT 1984 keeps both words, “my slaughtered meat that I have butchered for my shearers”; the NIV has “men coming from who knows where”; the TLB “a gang who comes from God knows where”.

Verses 12–13 · “Gird on, each man, his sword” — four hundred go up, two hundred stay by the baggage

Gird on each man his sword. “Gird on, each man, his sword! And they girded on each man his sword, and David also girded on his sword”: chigru ish et-charbo, the verb three times in one verse: the order, the men, and the commander. The ASV keeps all three, “Gird ye on every man his sword. And they girded on every man his sword; and David also girded on his sword”; the NIV compresses them, “So they did, and David strapped his on as well”; the NWT 2013 has “Everyone strap on your sword!”

Four hundred and two hundred. “And there went up after David about four hundred men, and two hundred stayed by the baggage”: al-ha-kelim, the baggage David once left “in the hand of the keeper of the baggage” before he ran to the battle line (17:22, already on these pages). The four hundred who gathered at Adullam (22:2) had become “about six hundred men” at Keilah (23:13, both already on these pages), and the six hundred divide here as they will at the brook Besor, four hundred pursuing and two hundred left behind, where David rules that the one who stays by the baggage shares alike with the one who goes down to battle (30:10, 24, now on these pages). The KJV has “and two hundred abode by the stuff”; the Geneva “and two hundred abode by the carriage”; the TLB “two hundred remained behind to guard their gear”.

Verses 14–17 · “He swooped on them” — a wall by night and by day, and “a son of worthlessness”

He swooped on them. “Look — David sent messengers from the wilderness to bless our master, and he swooped on them”: va-ya’at bahem, from ayat, the verb of the bird of prey, ayit, “And birds of prey came down on the carcasses” (Genesis 15:11, already on these pages) (ayat). The verb stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in this book: “the people swooped on the spoil” (14:32), Samuel’s question to Saul, “Why then did you … swoop on the spoil?” (15:19, both already on these pages), and here. “To bless our master”, le-varekh, is to greet him; the same word names Abigail’s gift in verse 27 and David’s three blessings in verses 32–33. The KJV has “to salute our master; and he railed on them”; the NWT 1984 “to wish our master well, but he screamed rebukes at them”; the NIV “but he hurled insults at them”; the Douay “and he rejected them”.

A wall around us. “They were a wall around us, both by night and by day”: chomah, a city wall (chomah), the word of the sea at the crossing, “the waters were a wall for them on their right and on their left” (Exodus 14:22, already on these pages). The young man repeats David’s message almost word for word, “we were not humiliated, and we missed nothing”, and confirms it. The NWT 1984 has “A wall was what they proved to be around us both by night and by day”; the NWT 2013 “They were like a protective wall around us”; the TLB “they were like a wall of protection to us and the sheep”.

The evil is decided. “And now, know and see what you will do; for the evil is decided against our master and against all his house”: ki khaletah ha-ra’ah, Jonathan’s phrase at the new moon, “know that the evil is decided by him” (20:7, already on these pages). “And he is a son of worthlessness — one cannot speak to him”: ben-beliyya’al (beliyya’al), the label of Eli’s sons (2:12) and of the men who said of Saul, “How will this one save us?” (10:27, both already on these pages). Here a servant says it of his own master. The KJV has “for he is such a son of Belial, that a man cannot speak to him”; the ASV “for he is such a worthless fellow”; the NWT 1984 “as he is too much of a good-for-nothing fellow to speak to him”; the TLB “he’s such a stubborn lout”.

Verses 18–20 · Abigail’s provisions — two skins of wine, roasted grain, raisins and cakes of figs, and a ride down the mountain

Food that travels. “And Abigail hurried, and took two hundred loaves of bread, and two skins of wine, and five sheep made ready, and five seahs of roasted grain, and a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs”. Qali, roasted grain, is what Jesse sent with David to his brothers (17:17) and what Boaz held out to Ruth (Ruth 2:14, both already on these pages); raisins and a cake of figs bring the starving Egyptian back to life in 30:12 (now on these pages). The sheep are “made ready”, asuyot, dressed for eating; the written text spells the word asuvot. The NWT 1984 has “two hundred cakes of pressed figs”; the KJV “and five measures of parched corn, and an hundred clusters of raisins”; the TLB “two bushels of roasted grain”; the Geneva “an hundred frails of raisins”. The Greek has “five ephahs of fine flour” and “one homer” of raisins. A train of donkeys carrying two hundred loaves, a hundred clusters of raisins and a skin of wine will meet David once more on a road, brought by Ziba (2 Samuel 16:1).

Two skins of wine, and Nabal. “Two skins of wine”: nivlei-yayin. A nevel is a skin or a jar (nevel), written with the three letters of Nabal’s name, נבל. The singular, nevel yayin, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Hannah took “a skin of wine” up to Shiloh with the boy Samuel (1:24), one of the three men going up to Bethel carried “a skin of wine” (10:3, both already on these pages), and Ziba brings one to David (2 Samuel 16:1). Abigail loads two on a donkey to save the house of a man whose name is the jar’s. The ASV has “and two bottles of wine”; the NIV “two skins of wine”; the NWT 1984 “and two large jars of wine”.

She told her husband nothing. “But to her husband Nabal she told nothing”: u-le-ishah Naval lo higgidah, with the husband put first in the Hebrew sentence. It will be said again in verse 36, “she did not tell him a thing, small or great”, until the morning. The ASV has “But she told not her husband Nabal”; the NIV “But she did not tell her husband Nabal”; the TLB “But she didn’t tell her husband what she was doing.”

Under cover of the mountain. “As she was riding on the donkey and coming down under cover of the mountain, look — David and his men were coming down toward her”: be-seter ha-har, in the hiding place of the mountain. Both are coming down, out of sight of each other, until they meet. The ASV has “and came down by the covert of the mountain”; the NWT 1984 applies the hiding to her, “and secretly going down the mountain”; the NIV “As she came riding her donkey into a mountain ravine”; the Geneva “she came down by a secret place of the mountain”.

Verses 21–22 · “Anyone who urinates against the wall” — David’s oath against Nabal’s house, and “to the enemies of David”

For a lie. “Now David had said: ‘Surely it was for a lie that I guarded all that this one has in the wilderness’”: akh la-sheqer shamarti (sheqer). The narrator stops the meeting in mid-step to report what David had been saying on the way down. Sheqer is a lie, a falsehood: the guarding promised a return and brought none. “This one”, la-zeh, without his name. The KJV and ASV have “Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness”; the NWT 1984 “It was altogether for disappointment that I guarded everything that belongs to this fellow”; the TLB “A lot of good it did us to help this fellow.”

Evil in place of good. “And he has returned me evil in place of good”: va-yashev-li ra’ah tachat tovah. In the cave Saul wept and said, “you have repaid me with good, and I have repaid you with evil” (24:17, already on these pages). The king confessed it; the sheep-owner does it. The KJV has “and he hath requited me evil for good”; the NWT 1984 “and yet he repays me evil in return for good”.

“To the enemies of David.” “Thus may God do to the enemies of David, and more also”: the oath Jonathan swore against himself, “Thus may Jehovah do to Jonathan, and more also” (20:13, already on these pages). Here the curse does not fall on the one who swears but on “the enemies of David”, and the Greek has “to David”. The words “the enemies of” have been explained as a scribe’s softening, so that David is not left under a curse for an oath he does not carry out, and as what David said; the Masoretic text has them, and so does this translation. The KJV has “So and more also do God unto the enemies of David”; the NIV follows the Greek, “May God deal with David, be it ever so severely”; the TLB “May God curse me”.

Anyone who urinates against the wall. “If I leave of all that is his until the morning anyone who urinates against the wall”: mashtin be-qir, a male, said the way soldiers say it (mashtin be-qir). The phrase stands in six verses of the Hebrew Bible: twice in this chapter (verses 22 and 34), and four times in Kings, every one of them about wiping out a king’s house: Jeroboam’s (1 Kings 14:10), Baasha’s (1 Kings 16:11), and Ahab’s twice (1 Kings 21:21; 2 Kings 9:8). David speaks the language of the end of a dynasty about a sheep-farmer’s household. The KJV, the Geneva and the Douay keep it, “any that pisseth against the wall”; the NWT 1984 has “anyone of all who are his that urinates against the wall”; the ASV “so much as one man-child”; the NIV “one male of all who belong to him”. This translation keeps the soldiers’ phrase, because it comes back in Kings.

Verses 23–25 · “On me, my lord, be the iniquity” — Abigail at David’s feet, and “as his name is, so is he”

On her face, at his feet. “And Abigail saw David, and she hurried and got down from the donkey, and fell before David on her face, and bowed down to the ground. And she fell at his feet”: she falls twice, before him and at his feet. In the last chapter David “bowed with his face to the earth, and prostrated himself” to Saul (24:8, already on these pages); now he is bowed to. The ASV has “and fell before David on her face, and bowed herself to the ground”; the Douay, following the Latin, “and adored upon the ground”; the TLB “she quickly dismounted and bowed low before him”.

“On me, my lord, be the iniquity.” “On me, on me, my lord, be the iniquity!”: bi-ani adoni he-avon (avon). Bi is “on me”, and ani, “I”, repeats it. The same syllable opens Judah’s plea before Joseph, bi adoni, “Please, my lord — let your servant speak a word in my lord’s ears” (Genesis 44:18, already on these pages), and Abigail’s next words make the same request: “let your handmaid speak, please, in your ears”. Judah ended by asking to bear the punishment “instead of the boy” (44:33); Abigail begins by taking on a guilt that is not hers. Hannah opened with it to Eli, “Oh, my lord!” (1:26, already on these pages). Most of the shelf reads “on me” here rather than the polite “please” of Judah and Hannah. The ASV has “Upon me, my lord, upon me be the iniquity”; the NWT 1984 “Upon me myself, O my lord, be the error”; the Geneva “Oh, my lord, I have committed the iniquity”; the NIV “Pardon your servant, my lord”.

“As his name is, so is he.” “Please let not my lord set his heart on this man of worthlessness, on Nabal; for as his name is, so is he: Nabal is his name, and folly is with him.” Naval is the fool in the moral sense, the man who could know better and does not care (naval): Moses calls Israel “foolish and senseless people” (Deuteronomy 32:6, already on these pages), and “The fool says in his heart, ‘There is no God’” (Psalm 14:1). Nevalah, “folly”, is from the same root (nevalah). Elsewhere on these pages it names an outrage, a deed, Shechem’s and the men of Gibeah’s (Genesis 34:7; Judges 19:23, both already on these pages); here it names what a man is, and this translation gives it the English that keeps her pun audible: Nabal, folly. The text does not say whether anyone ever named a son “Fool”, or whether the name once meant something else; Abigail takes it at its word. The KJV has “Nabal is his name, and folly is with him”; the NWT 1984 “and senselessness is with him”; the NIV “and folly goes with him”; the Douay, following the Latin, translates the name: “for according to his name, he is a fool”.

“I did not see the young men.” “But I, your handmaid, did not see the young men of my lord whom you sent.” She does not defend her husband; she says only that his answer was not hers. The ASV has “but I thy handmaid saw not the young men of my lord, whom thou didst send”; the NIV “And as for me, your servant, I did not see the men my lord sent.”

Verses 26–28 · “As Jehovah lives” — bloodguilt, “your own hand saving you”, this blessing, and “a faithful house”

As Jehovah lives and as your soul lives. “And now, my lord, as Jehovah lives and as your soul lives”: chai-YHVH ve-chei-nafshekha, the double oath David swore to Jonathan, “as Jehovah lives and as your soul lives, there is but a step between me and death” (20:3), and the half of it Hannah said to Eli, “As your soul lives, my lord” (1:26, both already on these pages). Then she speaks as if it had already happened: “Jehovah has withheld you from coming into bloodguilt”, mena’akha … mi-bo ve-damim (damim), with the verb David will hand back to her in verse 34. The ASV has “as Jehovah liveth, and as thy soul liveth, seeing Jehovah hath withholden thee from bloodguiltiness”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah has held you back from entering into bloodguilt”.

Your own hand saving you. “And from your own hand saving you”: ve-hoshea yadekha lakh (yasha), the boast Jehovah would not allow Gideon’s army: “My own hand has saved me” (Judges 7:2, already on these pages). Abigail says it twice (verses 26 and 31), and David says it back to her (verse 33). Taking vengeance into one’s own hand is described with the verb of salvation. The NWT 1984 keeps the verb, “and having your own hand come to your salvation”; the Geneva “and that thine hand should not save thee”; the KJV has “and from avenging thyself with thine own hand”; the Douay “and hath saved thy hand to thee”.

Let your enemies be like Nabal. “And now let your enemies, and those who seek evil against my lord, be like Nabal”: a wish that will come true of Nabal himself in verse 38. The ASV has “now therefore let thine enemies, and them that seek evil to my lord, be as Nabal”; the TLB “that all your enemies shall be as cursed as Nabal is”.

This blessing. “And now this blessing that your maidservant has brought to my lord”: ha-berakhah, a gift, as Jacob pressed his on Esau, “Take, I pray, my blessing that is brought to you” (Genesis 33:11, already on these pages) (berakhah). David’s men came “to bless” Nabal (verse 14) and were swooped on; the blessing comes back from his wife. “Let it be given to the young men who walk at the feet of my lord”: ha-mithallekhim be-raglei adoni, his followers; her own maids will “walk at her feet” in verse 42. The KJV has “And now this blessing which thine handmaid hath brought unto my lord”; the ASV “And now this present which thy servant hath brought unto my lord”; the NWT 1984 “this gift blessing that your maidservant has brought to my lord”. The Greek has “this blessing” too.

Handmaid and maidservant. Abigail calls herself amatekha, “your handmaid”, five times between verses 24 and 31 (amah), and once shifchatekha, “your maidservant”, in verse 27 (shifchah); in verse 41 she uses the two together. The NWT 1984 keeps them apart, “your slave girl” and “your maidservant”; the KJV has “thine handmaid” for both.

Lift away, please, the transgression. “Lift away, please, the transgression of your handmaid”: sa na le-fesha amatekha (pesha), the plea Joseph’s brothers sent him, in the same words, “please lift away the transgression of the servants of the God of your father” (Genesis 50:17, already on these pages). The brothers had a transgression of their own to be forgiven; Abigail asks forgiveness for one she has taken on (verse 24). The ASV has “Forgive, I pray thee, the trespass of thy handmaid”; the NWT 1984 “Pardon, please, the transgression of your slave girl”; the NIV “Please forgive your servant’s presumption.”

A faithful house. “For Jehovah will surely make for my lord a faithful house”: bayit ne’eman, the promise made at Shiloh about the priest who would replace Eli’s line, “I will build him a faithful house” (2:35, already on these pages), and the promise Nathan will bring David, “And your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever” (2 Samuel 7:16), with the same word, ne’eman; Ahijah will promise Jeroboam “a faithful house, as I built for David” (1 Kings 11:38). Here it is said to David by a woman on the road. The KJV and ASV have “a sure house”; the NWT 1984 “a lasting house”; the NIV “a lasting dynasty”; the Douay “a faithful house”; the TLB “eternal royalty for your descendants”.

The battles of Jehovah. “Because my lord is fighting the battles of Jehovah, and evil shall not be found in you all your days”: milchamot YHVH, Saul’s words when he offered David his daughter Merab, “only be a valiant man for me, and fight the battles of Jehovah” (18:17, already on these pages), said then with a trap in mind. The Greek turns the phrase round: “for the Lord fights the battle of my lord”. The ASV has “because my lord fighteth the battles of Jehovah; and evil shall not be found in thee all thy days”; the KJV reads the last clause as a past, “and evil hath not been found in thee all thy days”; the Douay as a wish, “let not evil therefore be found in thee”.

Verses 29–31 · “The bundle of life” and the hollow of the sling — “leader over Israel”, and “remember your handmaid”

The bundle of life. “The soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with Jehovah your God”: tserurah bi-tseror ha-chayyim, the verb and the noun from one root, “bound in the binding” (tseror). A tseror is a pouch or bundle tied shut, a thing kept safe: Joseph’s brothers found “each man’s bundle of silver” in his sack (Genesis 42:35, already on these pages). “The bundle of life” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. Its words became the closing line of Jewish gravestones, abbreviated to five Hebrew letters: “May his soul be bound in the bundle of life”. The KJV and ASV have “shall be bound in the bundle of life”; the NWT 1984 “will certainly prove to be wrapped up in the bag of life”; the NIV “will be bound securely in the bundle of the living”; the TLB “just as though you were safe inside his purse!”

From the hollow of the sling. “And the soul of your enemies he will sling out from the hollow of the sling”: yeqalle’ennah be-tokh kaf ha-qala, verb and noun of the sling together (qela), with kaf, the palm of the hand, for the pouch the stone sits in. David killed Goliath “with the sling and with the stone” (17:50, already on these pages). One soul is tied up in a bundle and kept; the other is flung out of a pouch. The ASV has “them shall he sling out, as from the hollow of a sling”; the NWT 1984 “he will sling it forth as from inside the hollow of the sling”; the NIV “he will hurl away as from the pocket of a sling”; the Douay “shall be whirled, as with the violence and whirling of a sling”.

Leader over Israel. “When Jehovah does for my lord according to all the good that he has spoken concerning you, and commands you to be leader over Israel”: ve-tsivvekha le-nagid al-Yisra’el (nagid). These are Samuel’s words to Saul at Gilgal, “Jehovah has commanded him to be leader over his people” (13:14, already on these pages), with the same verb and the same title. The chapter opened with Samuel’s burial; twenty-nine verses later his sentence is spoken again, by Abigail. The ASV has “and shall have appointed thee prince over Israel”; the KJV “and shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel”; the NWT 1984 “he certainly will commission you as leader over Israel”.

A staggering, a stumbling of heart. “That this shall not be to you a staggering, or a stumbling of heart to my lord”: le-fuqah u-le-mikhshol lev. Puqah stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one (puqah), and its sense is inferred. “To have shed blood without cause”: lishpokh-dam chinnam (chinnam), the word of the Accuser’s question, “Is it for nothing that Job fears God?” (Job 1:9, already on these pages). The NWT 1984 has “a cause for staggering or a stumbling block to the heart of my lord”; the KJV “That this shall be no grief unto thee, nor offence of heart unto my lord”; the NIV “the staggering burden of needless bloodshed”. The Greek has bdelygmos, “an abomination”.

Remember your handmaid. “And when Jehovah does good to my lord, remember your handmaid”: ve-zakharta et-amatekha (zakhar). Hannah prayed at Shiloh with the same verb and the same noun, “and remember me, and not forget your servant” (1:11, already on these pages), where “your servant” is amatekha. Hannah asked it of Jehovah; Abigail asks it of David. The ASV has “then remember thy handmaid”; the NWT 1984 “and you must remember your slave girl”; the TLB “please remember me!”

Verses 32–35 · “Blessed be your good sense” — David’s three blessings, and “I have lifted up your face”

Three blessings. “Blessed be Jehovah, the God of Israel, who sent you this day to meet me; and blessed be your good sense, and blessed be you”: barukh … u-varukh … u-verukhah, three times in two verses. Abigail brought a blessing (verse 27), and David answers it three times: the God who sent her, her good sense, and her. Ta’mekh, “your good sense” (ta’am), is the word of David’s worst day: before Achish of Gath “he changed his good sense in their eyes, and played the madman” (21:14, already on these pages). The man who once disguised his own good sense to save himself blesses a woman for hers. The ASV has “and blessed be thy discretion”; the KJV “And blessed be thy advice”; the NWT 1984 “And blessed be your sensibleness”; the NIV “May you be blessed for your good judgment”; the Douay “and blessed be thy speech”.

Restrained, withheld, held back. “Who have restrained me this day from coming into bloodguilt”: kelitini, from kala, to shut in, to hold back. Then “as Jehovah the God of Israel lives, who has withheld me from harming you”: mena’ani, the verb Abigail used of Jehovah in verse 26. A third verb of restraint comes in verse 39, when Jehovah “has held back his servant from evil”: Abigail restrains him, and Jehovah withholds him and holds him back. The ASV has “that hast kept me this day from bloodguiltiness”; the NWT 1984 “who have restrained me this day from entering into bloodguilt”.

Had you not hurried. “Had you not hurried and come to meet me”: lulei miharte va-tavot; the written text has an older spelling of “you came”, va-tavoti. Mahar, to hurry, is Abigail’s verb: she hurried in verse 18, hurried down from the donkey in verse 23, and will hurry again in verse 42. The NIV has “if you had not come quickly to meet me”; the KJV “except thou hadst hasted and come to meet me”.

“I have lifted up your face.” “Go up in peace to your house. See — I have listened to your voice, and have lifted up your face”: va-essa panayikh. To lift a person’s face is to grant what they ask, as the angel said to Lot, “Look — I have granted you this favor too” (Genesis 19:21, already on these pages), where the Hebrew has the same idiom. She came with her face to the ground (verse 23); he sends her home with it lifted. “Go up in peace”, ali le-shalom: Eli sent Hannah home with “Go in peace” (1:17, already on these pages). The KJV and ASV have “and have accepted thy person”; the NWT 1984 “that I may have consideration for your person”; the NIV “I have heard your words and granted your request”; the Douay keeps the face, “and have honoured thy face”.

Verses 36–38 · Nabal’s feast — “his heart died within him, and he became as a stone”, and Jehovah struck Nabal

A feast like a king’s. “And look — he had a feast in his house like the feast of a king”: mishteh ke-mishteh ha-melekh (mishteh), a drinking-feast. The man who asked “Who is David?” is feasting like a king while the man who will be king turns back from his door. “And Nabal’s heart was merry within him, and he was very drunk”: shikkor ad-me’od. At Shiloh the priest took Hannah’s silent prayer for drunkenness, “so Eli thought she was drunk” (1:13, already on these pages); here the man is drunk indeed. The KJV has “and Nabal’s heart was merry within him, for he was very drunken”; the NWT 1984 “and he was as drunk as could be”; the TLB “He was roaring drunk”.

Small or great, until the morning light. “And she did not tell him a thing, small or great, until the light of the morning”: davar qaton ve-gadol, the phrase of Jonathan’s father, who “does nothing, great or small, without uncovering my ear” (20:2, already on these pages). The morning was the limit of David’s oath (verses 22 and 34): Nabal’s household lives to see it, and in the morning Abigail speaks. The ASV has “wherefore she told him nothing, less or more, until the morning light”; the NWT 1984 “and she did not tell him a thing, small or great, until the morning light”.

When the wine went out of Nabal. “And it happened in the morning, when the wine had gone out of Nabal”: be-tset ha-yayin mi-Naval. Eli told Hannah, “Put away your wine from you” (1:14, already on these pages). The wine goes out of Nabal as out of the skins Abigail loaded in verse 18, the nivlei-yayin whose name his shares. The KJV and ASV have “when the wine was gone out of Nabal”; the NIV “when Nabal was sober”; the Douay “when Nabal had digested his wine”.

His heart died, and he became as a stone. “And his heart died within him, and he became as a stone”: va-yamot libbo be-qirbo ve-hu hayah le-aven. The night before, his heart was “merry within him”; in the morning it dies within him. The stone is the figure of the Song at the Sea, where the nations are “still as a stone” (Exodus 15:16, already on these pages). What happened to his body the text does not name. Readers have suggested a stroke, and the TLB writes the diagnosis into the verse: “he had a stroke and lay paralyzed for about ten days”. The NWT 2013 has “and he lay paralyzed like a stone”; the NIV “and his heart failed him and he became like a stone”.

Jehovah struck Nabal. “And it happened about ten days later that Jehovah struck Nabal, and he died”: va-yiggof YHVH et-Naval, from nagaf, the verb of a blow from God: “And Jehovah struck the people, because they made the calf” (Exodus 32:35, already on these pages). David will say of Saul at the second sparing, “Jehovah will strike him” (26:10, now on these pages), with the same verb. The man David swore to kill is dead, and David has not touched him. The ASV has “that Jehovah smote Nabal, so that he died”.

Verses 39–42 · “Who has pleaded the case of my reproach” — David asks for Abigail, and she rides out again

He pleaded the case of my reproach. “Blessed be Jehovah, who has pleaded the case of my reproach from the hand of Nabal”: asher rav et-riv cherpati (riv; cherpah). In the cave David asked that Jehovah would “see, and plead my case, and judge me free of your hand” (24:15, already on these pages): the same verb and noun, and the same “hand”. He asked it about Saul; it is answered first about Nabal. “Reproach”, cherpah, is what David once asked to take away from Israel, “and takes away the reproach from upon Israel” (17:26, already on these pages). The KJV has “that hath pleaded the cause of my reproach from the hand of Nabal”; the NIV “who has upheld my cause against Nabal for treating me with contempt”.

Held back from evil. “And has held back his servant from evil; and the evil of Nabal Jehovah has returned on his own head”: chasakh, the verb God used to Abimelech, “it was I who held you back from sinning against me” (Genesis 20:6, already on these pages). Ra’ah, “evil”, has run through the chapter: the evil decided against Nabal’s house (verse 17), the evil he returned for good (verse 21), the evil sought against David (verse 26), the evil that will not be found in him (verse 28); now it goes back on Nabal’s head. The ASV has “and hath kept back his servant from evil: and the evil-doing of Nabal hath Jehovah returned upon his own head”; the NIV “He has kept his servant from doing wrong”.

David spoke for Abigail. “And David sent and spoke for Abigail, to take her to himself as a wife”: va-yedabber ba-Avigayil. To speak “for” a woman is to ask for her in marriage: “what shall we do for our sister in the day when she is spoken for?” (Song of Solomon 8:8). The KJV has “And David sent and communed with Abigail”; the NWT 1984 “And David proceeded to send and propose to”; the NIV “Then David sent word to Abigail, asking her to become his wife.”

To wash the feet of the servants. “Look — your handmaid is a maidservant, to wash the feet of the servants of my lord”: amatekha le-shifchah, both her words for a serving woman side by side. Water for washing the feet is the host’s service to guests: “Let a little water be brought — wash your feet” (Genesis 18:4; 24:32, both already on these pages). She offers it to David’s messengers, a step below his servants. The ASV has “Behold, thy handmaid is a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord”; the NWT 1984 “Here is your slave girl as a maidservant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord”; the TLB gives only “she readily agreed to his request”.

Five young women at her feet. “And Abigail hurried and arose, and rode on the donkey, with five of her young women who walked at her feet”: ha-holekhot le-raglah, her attendants, as David’s men were “the young men who walk at the feet of my lord” (verse 27). She rides out a second time, in haste again, this time behind David’s messengers. “And she became his wife.” She is still named by her first husband in four later verses, “Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite” (27:3 and 30:5, both now on these pages, and 2 Samuel 2:2, now on these pages; 2 Samuel 3:3), and the son she bears David is called Chileab in Samuel and Daniel in Chronicles (2 Samuel 3:3; 1 Chronicles 3:1). The KJV has “with five damsels of hers that went after her”; the NWT 1984 “with five maids of hers walking behind her”; the NIV “attended by her five female servants”.

Verses 43–44 · Ahinoam of Jezreel, and Michal given to Palti son of Laish of Gallim

Ahinoam of Jezreel. “And Ahinoam David had taken from Jezreel; and the two of them became his wives”. The verb can be read as a marriage before this story (“had taken”) or as the next event (“took”), and the shelf divides. This Jezreel is the town of Judah’s hill country listed with Maon, Carmel and Ziph (Joshua 15:55–56, already on these pages) (Jezreel), not the city of the northern valley. The name Ahinoam stands in seven verses of the Hebrew Bible: one of them names Saul’s wife, “Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz” (14:50, already on these pages), and the other six are this woman, the mother of David’s firstborn, Amnon (2 Samuel 3:2). Some readers identify the two women, taking Nathan’s “I gave you your master’s house, and your master’s wives into your bosom” (2 Samuel 12:8) as a hint; others take them as two women with one name. The text never joins them. The KJV has “David also took Ahinoam of Jezreel; and they were also both of them his wives”; the NWT 1984 “David had also taken”; the NIV “David had also married Ahinoam of Jezreel”.

Michal given to Palti. “And Saul had given Michal his daughter, David’s wife, to Palti son of Laish, who was from Gallim”. Saul gave her to David for the Philistines’ foreskins (18:27), and she let him down through the window when Saul’s messengers came for him (19:12, both already on these pages) (Michal). The verse does not say when Saul gave her away, or why. David will demand her back, and her husband, called Paltiel there, “went with her, weeping as he went” as far as Bahurim (2 Samuel 3:14–16) (Palti). Gallim appears with Laishah and Anathoth in Isaiah’s list of towns in the path of an army marching on Jerusalem (Isaiah 10:30) (Gallim). The Greek calls the town Romma and the father Amis. The KJV has “to Phalti the son of Laish, which was of Gallim”; the NIV uses the longer form of 2 Samuel 3:15, “to Paltiel son of Laish, who was from Gallim”; the TLB gives Saul a means the Hebrew does not, “had forced David’s wife Michal, Saul’s daughter, to marry a man from Gallim”.
