🕰
The Song of Moses itself, dictated one chapter ago and now spoken in full: heaven and earth summoned as witnesses, Jehovah as the Rock whose work is perfect against a generation crooked and warped, a history of the wilderness told in one long poem — the eagle stirring its nest, honey from the crag, Jeshurun growing fat and kicking into apostasy, the anger that nearly consumes and the enemy’s taunt that stays it, and Jehovah’s own final claim, ‘I — I am he, and there is no god besides me.’ Moses then teaches the whole song to the assembled nation, and God commands him, on that same day, up Mount Nebo to see the land he will not enter.
Still the plains of Moab in the fortieth year, and the whole of the weekly portion Ha’azinu — fifty-two verses, forty-three of them the Song itself (v1–43) and the remaining nine a narrative frame (v44–52). Hebrew and English verse numbers align throughout. Nearly every hemistich of the poem carries a Masoretic layout mark, stripped in this translation’s verse text and described in the chapter’s own closing note; v43 alone keeps its mark visible, the promised partner to 31:30’s. A petuchah closes v47 and again v52, ending both Moses’ own address and the chapter.

Deuteronomy 32 — The Mister Translation

1
הַאֲזִינוּ הַשָּׁמַיִם, וַאֲדַבֵּרָה; וְתִשְׁמַע הָאָרֶץ, אִמְרֵי-פִי.
Give ear, heavens, and let me speak; and let the earth hear the words of my mouth.note
Escuchen, cielos, y hablaré; y que la tierra oiga las palabras de mi boca.
2
יַעֲרֹף כַּמָּטָר לִקְחִי, תִּזַּל כַּטַּל אִמְרָתִי, כִּשְׂעִירִם עֲלֵי-דֶשֶׁא, וְכִרְבִיבִים עֲלֵי-עֵשֶׂב.
May my teaching fall like the rain, my speech settle like the dew — like showers on the grass, like abundant rain on the plants.note
Que caiga mi enseñanza como la lluvia, que se destile mi palabra como el rocío —como la llovizna sobre la hierba, como los aguaceros sobre las plantas.
3
כִּי שֵׁם יְהוָה, אֶקְרָא: הָבוּ גֹדֶל, לֵאלֹהֵינוּ.
For I proclaim the name of Jehovah — ascribe greatness to our God.note
Porque proclamo el nombre de Jehová —atribuyan grandeza a nuestro Dios.
4
הַצּוּר תָּמִים פָּעֳלוֹ, כִּי כָל-דְּרָכָיו מִשְׁפָּט: אֵל אֱמוּנָה וְאֵין עָוֶל, צַדִּיק וְיָשָׁר הוּא.
The Rock — his work is perfect, for all his ways are justice; a God of faithfulness, without iniquity — righteous and upright is he.note
La Roca —perfecta es su obra, porque todos sus caminos son justicia; un Dios de fidelidad, sin iniquidad —justo y recto es él.
5
שִׁחֵת לוֹ לֹא, בָּנָיו מוּמָם: דּוֹר עִקֵּשׁ, וּפְתַלְתֹּל.
They have dealt corruptly with him; they are no longer his children — the defect is their own: a generation crooked and warped.note
Ellos han obrado con corrupción hacia él; ya no son sus hijos —la mancha es de ellos: una generación torcida y perversa.
6
הַ לְיְהוָה, תִּגְמְלוּ-זֹאת-- עַם נָבָל, וְלֹא חָכָם: הֲלוֹא-הוּא אָבִיךָ קָּנֶךָ, הוּא עָשְׂךָ וַיְכֹנְנֶךָ.
Is this how you repay Jehovah, foolish and senseless people? Is he not your father, who acquired you? He made you, and he established you.note
¿Es así como pagan a Jehová, pueblo necio e insensato? ¿No es él tu padre, que te adquirió? Él te hizo, y te estableció.
7
זְכֹר יְמוֹת עוֹלָם, בִּינוּ שְׁנוֹת דֹּר-וָדֹר; שְׁאַל אָבִיךָ וְיַגֵּדְךָ, זְקֵנֶיךָ וְיֹאמְרוּ לָךְ.
Remember the days of old; consider the years of generation after generation. Ask your father, and he will tell you; your elders, and they will say it to you.note
Recuerda los días de antaño; consideren los años de generación tras generación. Pregunta a tu padre, y él te lo dirá; a tus ancianos, y ellos te lo contarán.
8
בְּהַנְחֵל עֶלְיוֹן גּוֹיִם, בְּהַפְרִידוֹ בְּנֵי אָדָם; יַצֵּב גְּבֻלֹת עַמִּים, לְמִסְפַּר בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל.
When the Most High gave the nations their inheritance, when he separated the sons of man, he fixed the borders of the peoples according to the number of the sons of Israel.note
Cuando el Altísimo dio a las naciones su herencia, cuando separó a los hijos del hombre, fijó los límites de los pueblos según el número de los hijos de Israel.
9
כִּי חֵלֶק יְהוָה, עַמּוֹ: יַעֲקֹב, חֶבֶל נַחֲלָתוֹ.
For Jehovah’s portion is his people; Jacob is the measured line of his inheritance.note
Porque la porción de Jehová es su pueblo; Jacob es el cordel medido de su herencia.
10
יִמְצָאֵהוּ בְּאֶרֶץ מִדְבָּר, וּבְתֹהוּ יְלֵל יְשִׁמֹן; יְסֹבְבֶנְהוּ, יְבוֹנְנֵהוּ-- יִצְּרֶנְהוּ, כְּאִישׁוֹן עֵינוֹ.
He found him in a desert land, in the waste of a howling wilderness; he surrounded him, he cared for him, he guarded him as the pupil of his eye.note
Lo encontró en tierra desierta, en un yermo de aullidos salvajes; lo rodeó, lo cuidó, lo guardó como a la niña de su ojo.
11
כְּנֶשֶׁר יָעִיר קִנּוֹ, עַל-גּוֹזָלָיו יְרַחֵף; יִפְרֹשׂ כְּנָפָיו יִקָּחֵהוּ, יִשָּׂאֵהוּ עַל-אֶבְרָתוֹ.
Like an eagle that stirs up its nest, that hovers over its young; it spreads its wings, takes them up, carries them on its pinions—note
Como el águila que agita su nido, que se cierne sobre sus crías; que extiende sus alas, los toma, los lleva sobre sus plumas—
cross-refs⤷ Exod 19:4
12
יְהוָה, בָּדָד יַנְחֶנּוּ; וְאֵין עִמּוֹ, אֵל נֵכָר.
Jehovah alone led him; there was no foreign god with him.note
Solo Jehová lo guió; no había con él dios extraño.
13
יַרְכִּבֵהוּ עַל-במותי בָּמֳתֵי אָרֶץ, וַיֹּאכַל תְּנוּבֹת שָׂדָי; וַיֵּנִקֵהוּ דְבַשׁ מִסֶּלַע, וְשֶׁמֶן מֵחַלְמִישׁ צוּר.
He made him ride on the heights of the earth, and he ate the produce of the field; he nursed him with honey from the crag, and oil from the flinty rock.note
Lo hizo cabalgar sobre las alturas de la tierra, y comió el fruto del campo; lo amamantó con miel de la peña, y aceite del pedernal de la roca.
14
חֶמְאַת בָּקָר וַחֲלֵב צֹאן, עִם-חֵלֶב כָּרִים וְאֵילִים בְּנֵי-בָשָׁן וְעַתּוּדִים, עִם-חֵלֶב, כִּלְיוֹת חִטָּה; וְדַם-עֵנָב, תִּשְׁתֶּה-חָמֶר.
Curds of the herd and milk of the flock, with the fat of lambs, rams of Bashan’s breed, and goats, with the richest kernels of wheat; and you drank the blood of the grape, foaming wine.note
Cuajada de vacas y leche de ovejas, con la grasa de corderos, carneros de la raza de Basán, y machos cabríos, con lo más selecto del trigo; y bebiste sangre de uva, vino espumoso.
15
וַיִּשְׁמַן יְשֻׁרוּן וַיִּבְעָט, שָׁמַנְתָּ עָבִיתָ כָּשִׂיתָ; וַיִּטֹּשׁ אֱלוֹהַּ עָשָׂהוּ, וַיְנַבֵּל צוּר יְשֻׁעָתוֹ.
But Jeshurun grew fat and kicked — you grew fat, you grew thick, you grew gorged — and he abandoned the God who made him, and scorned the Rock of his salvation.note
Pero Jesurún engordó, y dio patadas —engordaste, te espesaste, te hinchaste— y abandonó al Dios que lo hizo, y despreció a la Roca de su salvación.
16
יַקְנִאֻהוּ, בְּזָרִים; בְּתוֹעֵבֹת, יַכְעִיסֻהוּ.
They roused him to jealousy with strange gods; with detestable things they provoked him to anger.note
Lo hicieron sentir celos con dioses extraños; con cosas detestables lo provocaron a ira.
17
יִזְבְּחוּ, לַשֵּׁדִים לֹא אֱלֹהַּ-- אֱלֹהִים, לֹא יְדָעוּם; חֲדָשִׁים מִקָּרֹב בָּאוּ, לֹא שְׂעָרוּם אֲבֹתֵיכֶם.
They sacrificed to demons, no-gods — gods they had not known, new gods that came recently, that your fathers had not dreaded.note
Sacrificaron a demonios, a no-dioses —dioses que no habían conocido, dioses nuevos que llegaron hace poco, que sus padres no habían temido.
18
צוּר יְלָדְךָ, תֶּשִׁי; וַתִּשְׁכַּח, אֵל מְחֹלְלֶךָ.
You were unmindful of the Rock who fathered you, and you forgot the God who gave you birth.note
Te olvidaste de la Roca que te engendró, y no te acordaste del Dios que te dio a luz.
19
וַיַּרְא יְהוָה, וַיִּנְאָץ, מִכַּעַס בָּנָיו, וּבְנֹתָיו.
And Jehovah saw, and spurned them, because of the provocation of his sons and his daughters.note
Y Jehová vio, y los rechazó, por la provocación de sus hijos y sus hijas.
20
וַיֹּאמֶר, אַסְתִּירָה פָנַי מֵהֶם-- אֶרְאֶה, מָה אַחֲרִיתָם: כִּי דוֹר תַּהְפֻּכֹת הֵמָּה, בָּנִים לֹא-אֵמֻן בָּם.
And he said: ‘I will hide my face from them; I will see what their end will be — for they are a generation of perversity, sons in whom there is no faithfulness.note
Y dijo: ‘Esconderé mi rostro de ellos; veré cuál será su fin —porque son una generación de perversidad, hijos en quienes no hay fidelidad.
21
הֵם קִנְאוּנִי בְלֹא-אֵל, כִּעֲסוּנִי בְּהַבְלֵיהֶם; וַאֲנִי אַקְנִיאֵם בְּלֹא-עָם, בְּגוֹי נָבָל אַכְעִיסֵם.
They roused me to jealousy with a no-god; they provoked me to anger with their vapors. So I will rouse them to jealousy with a no-people; with a foolish nation I will provoke them to anger.note
Ellos me hicieron sentir celos con un no-dios; me provocaron a ira con sus vanidades. Así que yo los haré sentir celos con un no-pueblo; con una nación necia los provocaré a ira.
22
כִּי-אֵשׁ קָדְחָה בְאַפִּי, וַתִּיקַד עַד-שְׁאוֹל תַּחְתִּית; וַתֹּאכַל אֶרֶץ וִיבֻלָהּ, וַתְּלַהֵט מוֹסְדֵי הָרִים.
For a fire has been kindled in my anger, and it burns down to the depths of Sheol; it consumes the earth and its produce, and sets ablaze the foundations of the mountains.note
Porque un fuego se ha encendido en mi furor, y arde hasta las profundidades del Seol; consume la tierra y su fruto, y prende fuego a los cimientos de las montañas.
23
אַסְפֶּה עָלֵימוֹ, רָעוֹת; חִצַּי, אֲכַלֶּה-בָּם.
I will heap disasters upon them; I will spend my arrows against them.note
Amontonaré desastres sobre ellos; gastaré mis flechas contra ellos.
24
מְזֵי רָעָב וּלְחֻמֵי רֶשֶׁף, וְקֶטֶב מְרִירִי; וְשֶׁן-בְּהֵמֹת, אֲשַׁלַּח-בָּם, עִם-חֲמַת, זֹחֲלֵי עָפָר.
Wasting hunger, and the devouring of fever, and bitter destruction; and the teeth of beasts I will send against them, with the venom of things that crawl in the dust.note
Consunción de hambre, y devoración de fiebre, y destrucción amarga; y dientes de fieras enviaré contra ellos, con el veneno de lo que se arrastra por el polvo.
25
מִחוּץ, תְּשַׁכֶּל-חֶרֶב, וּמֵחֲדָרִים, אֵימָה; גַּם-בָּחוּר, גַּם-בְּתוּלָה--יוֹנֵק, עִם-אִישׁ שֵׂיבָה.
Outside, the sword will bereave; and inside, terror — young man and virgin alike, the nursing infant with the gray-haired man.note
Afuera, la espada dejará sin hijos; y adentro, el terror —al joven y a la virgen igual, al niño de pecho con el hombre de canas.
26
אָמַרְתִּי, אַפְאֵיהֶם; אַשְׁבִּיתָה מֵאֱנוֹשׁ, זִכְרָם.
I said: I will scatter them; I will make their memory cease from mankind—note
Dije: los dispersaré; haré cesar su memoria de entre los hombres—
27
לוּלֵי, כַּעַס אוֹיֵב אָגוּר-- פֶּן-יְנַכְּרוּ, צָרֵימוֹ: פֶּן-יֹאמְרוּ יָדֵנוּ רָמָה, וְלֹא יְהוָה פָּעַל כָּל-זֹאת.
were it not that I dreaded the enemy’s taunt — that their adversaries might misread it, that they might say: “Our hand has triumphed; it was not Jehovah who did all this.”’note
si no fuera que temo la burla del enemigo —que sus adversarios lo entiendan mal, que digan: «nuestra mano ha triunfado; no fue Jehová quien hizo todo esto».’
28
כִּי-גוֹי אֹבַד עֵצוֹת, הֵמָּה; וְאֵין בָּהֶם, תְּבוּנָה.
For they are a nation lacking counsel, and there is no understanding in them.note
Porque son una nación que ha perdido el consejo, y no hay entendimiento en ellos.
29
לוּ חָכְמוּ, יַשְׂכִּילוּ זֹאת; יָבִינוּ, לְאַחֲרִיתָם.
If only they were wise — they would understand this; they would discern their own end.note
¡Si fueran sabios! Entenderían esto; discernirían su propio fin.
30
אֵיכָה יִרְדֹּף אֶחָד, אֶלֶף, וּשְׁנַיִם, יָנִיסוּ רְבָבָה: אִם-לֹא כִּי-צוּרָם מְכָרָם, וַיהוָה הִסְגִּירָם.
How could one chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight, unless their rock had sold them, and Jehovah had given them up?note
¿Cómo podría uno perseguir a mil, y dos hacer huir a diez mil, si no fuera que su roca los vendió, y Jehová los entregó?
31
כִּי לֹא כְצוּרֵנוּ, צוּרָם; וְאֹיְבֵינוּ, פְּלִילִים.
For their rock is not like our Rock — even our enemies concede it.note
Porque su roca no es como nuestra Roca —hasta nuestros enemigos lo reconocen.
32
כִּי-מִגֶּפֶן סְדֹם גַּפְנָם, וּמִשַּׁדְמֹת עֲמֹרָה: עֲנָבֵמוֹ, עִנְּבֵי-רוֹשׁ-- אַשְׁכְּלֹת מְרֹרֹת, לָמוֹ.
For their vine is from the vine of Sodom, and from the fields of Gomorrah; their grapes are grapes of poison, their clusters are bitter.note
Porque su vid es de la vid de Sodoma, y de los campos de Gomorra; sus uvas son uvas de veneno, sus racimos son amargos.
33
חֲמַת תַּנִּינִם, יֵינָם; וְרֹאשׁ פְּתָנִים, אַכְזָר.
Their wine is the venom of serpents, the cruel poison of cobras.note
Su vino es veneno de serpientes, el cruel veneno de las cobras.
34
הֲלֹא-הוּא, כָּמֻס עִמָּדִי; חָתוּם, בְּאוֹצְרֹתָי.
Is this not laid up in store with me, sealed in my treasuries?note
¿No está esto guardado conmigo, sellado en mis tesoros?
35
לִי נָקָם וְשִׁלֵּם, לְעֵת תָּמוּט רַגְלָם: כִּי קָרוֹב יוֹם אֵידָם, וְחָשׁ עֲתִדֹת לָמוֹ.
Vengeance is mine, and recompense, for the time when their foot will slip; for the day of their disaster is near, and what is coming for them hastens.note
Mía es la venganza, y la retribución, para el tiempo en que su pie resbale; porque cercano está el día de su desastre, y lo que les espera se apresura.
36
כִּי-יָדִין יְהוָה עַמּוֹ, וְעַל-עֲבָדָיו יִתְנֶחָם: כִּי יִרְאֶה כִּי-אָזְלַת יָד, וְאֶפֶס עָצוּר וְעָזוּב.
For Jehovah will judge his people, and he will relent concerning his servants, when he sees that their strength is gone, and there is no one left, whether shut up or set free.note
Porque Jehová juzgará a su pueblo, y se apiadará de sus siervos, cuando vea que su fuerza se ha ido, y que no queda nadie, ni encerrado ni libre.
37
וְאָמַר, אֵי אֱלֹהֵימוֹ-- צוּר, חָסָיוּ בוֹ.
And he will say: ‘Where are their gods, the rock in whom they took refuge—note
Y dirá: ‘¿Dónde están sus dioses, la roca en la que se refugiaban—
38
אֲשֶׁר חֵלֶב זְבָחֵימוֹ יֹאכֵלוּ, יִשְׁתּוּ יֵין נְסִיכָם; יָקוּמוּ, וְיַעְזְרֻכֶם-- יְהִי עֲלֵיכֶם, סִתְרָה.
who ate the fat of their sacrifices, who drank the wine of their libations? Let them rise up and help you; let them be your protection!’note
que comían la grasa de sus sacrificios, que bebían el vino de sus libaciones? ¡Que se levanten y los ayuden! ¡Que sean su protección!’

Verses 1–6 · Heaven and earth take the stand, and the Rock’s first accuser is a crux no version reads alike

★★ The song opens by calling the exact two witnesses 31:28 and 30:19 (both already on these pages) already summoned — heaven and earth — and now puts them on the stand. Ha’azinu ha-shamayim, ‘give ear, heavens’, and tishma ha-aretz, ‘let the earth hear’, are the same legal witnesses named three times already in this book, now finally addressed rather than merely invoked. The form is a rib, a covenant lawsuit — the same shape Isaiah 1 (already on these pages) borrows centuries later, reversing the order (‘hear, heavens, and give ear, earth’) while keeping the function.

★ V2’s two verbs for the song itself are both agricultural, and the shelf keeps them apart. Ya’arof, ‘drop, distil’, and tizzal, ‘trickle, settle’, describe rain and dew the way a farmer would, not a preacher: the KJV reads ‘my doctrine shall drop as the rain’, the ASV ‘my doctrine shall drop as the rain’, and the NWT 1984 ‘my instruction will drip as the rain’. Leqach, rendered here ‘teaching’, is elsewhere in Proverbs the thing a wise son takes FROM his father (Proverbs 4:2) — the same root as ‘take’ — so the image is speech a hearer receives the way soil receives water, not doctrine handed down as a verdict.

★★ V4 gives the Rock its first word in this chapter, and two versions on the English shelf lose the noun outright. Douay-Rheims, translating the Vulgate rather than the Hebrew (this project’s standing note on that version), reads ‘the works of GOD are perfect’ — no rock anywhere — and Geneva 1599 does the same, ‘perfect is the work of the mighty GOD’. KJV, ASV, NIV, NWT 1984 and TLB all keep ‘the Rock’, as does the whole Spanish shelf — RV 1909, RV60 and NVI all read «la Roca». Tzur will recur at vv15, 18, 30 and 31 (below) — this chapter is the title’s home address in the Hebrew Bible — so losing it at the very first mention costs a reader the whole refrain the two Latin-descended versions never restore.

★★★ V5 is one of the harder cruxes in the whole Torah, and four shelf versions solve it four different ways. The bare Hebrew reads, word by word: shichet (he/they dealt corruptly) lo (to/for him — or, on a different vocalization, ‘not’) lo (not) banav (his sons) mumam (their blemish). KJV reads ‘they have corrupted THEMSELVES, their spot is not the spot of his children’; ASV ‘they have dealt corruptly WITH HIM, they are not his children, it is their blemish’; the NWT 1984 ‘they have acted ruinously ON THEIR OWN PART; they are not his children, the defect is their own’; NIV drops the pronoun question entirely, ‘they are corrupt and not his children’. This translation follows ASV/NWT’s reading — a broken relationship stated first, then the reason — because it keeps both halves of the wordplay the Hebrew builds: shichet, corrupted, is the identical verb Genesis uses of the whole earth before the flood (Genesis 6:11-12, already on these pages), so the charge against this ‘crooked and warped generation’ is scaled to the charge against the pre-flood world.

⚠ V6’s verb for what God did to Israel divides the shelf between a purchase and a creation. Qanah, rendered here ‘acquired’, is the same root Genesis 4:1 uses when Eve says she has ‘acquired’ (or ‘created’) a man from Jehovah — a verb that covers both BUYING and BRINGING INTO BEING, and never fully separates them in Hebrew. KJV and ASV both read ‘hath BOUGHT thee’; Douay-Rheims ‘that hath POSSESSED thee’; Geneva 1599 the same, ‘that hath BOUGHT thee’; the NWT 1984 interprets rather than translates, ‘who has PRODUCED you’; NIV goes further still, ‘your CREATOR’. This translation keeps ‘acquired’ because the next clause already supplies ‘he made you’ as its own separate verb — collapsing qanah into ‘created’ makes the verse say the same thing twice.

Verses 7–14 · The crux of v8 — ‘sons of Israel’ in the Masoretic Text, ‘sons of God’ in a Dead Sea fragment and the Greek — and the eagle that lends Genesis 1 its verb

★★★ V8’s closing phrase is a genuine textual fork, and this translation follows the Masoretic Text while showing the reader the other branch. The printed Hebrew this project works from reads le-mispar benei Yisrael, ‘according to the number of the SONS OF ISRAEL’ — the borders of every nation on earth fixed to match a people who, at the moment the poem imagines (Babel, before Abraham is even born), do not yet exist. A fragment of Deuteronomy found at Qumran (4QDeutq) and the Greek Septuagint both read instead benei Elohim / angelōn Theou, ‘SONS OF GOD’ / ‘ANGELS OF GOD’ — the nations apportioned among the members of the divine council this project’s reader already met at Genesis 6:2 (the ‘sons of God’ who took human wives) and again presenting themselves before Jehovah at Job 1:6 (both already on these pages). Read that way, v9’s very next line is the point of the whole passage: every OTHER nation was handed to a lesser member of the council, and Jehovah reserved exactly one people for himself. This project’s own standing rule is to show a genuine textual split rather than resolve it by vote — and one shelf version has already made the choice openly: TLB, a paraphrase rather than a translation, reads v8 ‘when God divided up the world among the nations, He gave each of them a SUPERVISING ANGEL’, adopting the Qumran/Greek reading outright while every other shelf version keeps the Masoretic ‘sons of Israel’. The two readings differ by exactly one letter cluster in the consonantal text (bene yisrael against bene elohim), and a scribe copying either from dictation could produce the other.

★ V9’s two nouns for what Israel IS to Jehovah are both drawn from land law. Cheleq, ‘portion’, is an inheritance-share; chevel nachalato, ‘the measured line of his inheritance’, is more literal still — chevel is the actual CORD a surveyor stretches to mark out a plot. Jehovah does not merely value Israel; the poem pictures him surveying and staking a claim, the identical procedure Joshua will use to divide Canaan among the twelve tribes a generation later.

★ V10’s ‘pupil of his eye’ uses the RARER of two Hebrew idioms for the same body part, and this translation follows it rather than the familiar English figure. Ishon, literally ‘the little man’, names the pupil from the tiny reflection a person sees of themselves in another’s eye — the same picture behind Latin’s pupilla and English ‘pupil’. It is a DIFFERENT Hebrew word from bavat ayin (Zechariah 2:12, already on these pages), the more disputed idiom this project has already flagged; both are usually flattened in English translation to the borrowed, unrelated figure ‘the apple of his eye’ — apples appear in neither Hebrew phrase. KJV and ASV both read ‘apple of his eye’; the NWT 1984 already reads ‘pupil of his eye’, the reading this translation follows.

★★ V11’s eagle hovers with the exact verb the Spirit of God uses over the waters at the world’s first verses. Yerachef, ‘hovers, flutters’, is the same root as merachefet at Genesis 1:2 (already on these pages) — the image this translation’s own opening note already promised would land here: ‘a living presence poised over’ something helpless, not a description of weather. The Bible’s first and its most sustained pictures of protective hovering share one Hebrew verb, six words apart in subject — the uncreated world in one, a nation just found in a wilderness in the other.

⚠ V13’s ‘heights’ carries a written/read pair, a ketiv/qere: the consonants on the page spell bamotai, ‘MY heights’, an archaic first-person form; the vowels supplied for reading give bamotei, ‘the heights OF’, expecting aretz, ‘earth’, to follow — which it does, one word later. Mechon-Mamre’s own printed text keeps both forms side by side in the Hebrew column, the manuscript tradition’s own honesty about a word it read differently than it was written. None of the thirteen shelf versions checked prints the ketiv’s first person (‘MY heights’); most follow the qere explicitly, ‘the heights of the earth’ (KJV, ASV and NWT among them), and the remaining two (Douay-Rheims ‘high land’, TLB ‘fertile hilltops’) paraphrase without committing to either form.

Verses 15–18 · Jeshurun, the upright one’s crooked turn, and demons the fathers never dreaded

★★★ V15 names Israel Jeshurun, and the name is a pun against the whole poem it stands inside. The name stands in four verses of the Hebrew Bible — this one, 33:5 and 33:26 below, and Isaiah 44:2 — and this, its first appearance, is where the pun is made. Yeshurun is built on yashar, ‘upright, straight’ — the identical root v4 has just used of the Rock himself (‘righteous and UPRIGHT is he’) and v5 has just used, negated, of the people (‘a generation CROOKED and warped’, iqqesh u-fetaltol, letter-for-letter’s opposite of yashar). The Upright One grows fat and kicks: the name itself is the accusation. KJV, ASV and the NWT 1984 all transliterate rather than translate, ‘Jeshurun’; NIV keeps it too; only TLB drops the name outright and reads simply ‘Israel’, losing the pun with it.

★ V17’s ‘demons’ is one of only two places in the whole Hebrew Bible where the bare word stands. Searched consonantally, shedim stands in exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible — this one and Psalm 106:37, which quotes this very verse back almost word for word centuries later (‘they sacrificed their sons and their daughters TO DEMONS’). (The bare consonants also spell ‘Chaldeans’, kasdim, and ‘breasts’, shadayim, which is why the count has to be read rather than matched — both are unrelated words caught only by shared letters.) The shelf splits on how frightening to make the word: Douay-Rheims and Geneva 1599 both read ‘devils’; the NWT 1984 reads ‘demons’; NIV alone softens it to ‘false gods’, which erases the specifically demonic sense the Hebrew word carries and that every other shelf version keeps.

★★ V18 pictures Jehovah in labor, and one shelf version alone lets the reader see it. Mecholeleka, ‘who gave you birth’, is built on the verb chul, to writhe — specifically the writhing of CHILDBIRTH; the same root gives Isaiah’s ‘as a woman in labor’ and the ordinary Hebrew word for labor pains. the KJV reads ‘that formed thee’ and the ASV ‘that gave thee birth’, both without the image; the NWT 1984 alone spells it out, ‘the One bringing you forth WITH CHILDBIRTH PAINS’. The same verse opens with God as father (‘the Rock who fathered you’, yeladekha) and closes with God in labor — two images of begetting, one masculine and one specifically, physically maternal, inside a single Hebrew line.

Verses 19–27 · A chiasm Paul later quotes, and the enemy’s taunt that stays God’s hand

★★ V21 is built as a mirror, and the mirror is the whole argument. ‘THEY roused me to jealousy with a NO-GOD (lo-el); I will rouse THEM to jealousy with a NO-PEOPLE (lo-am)’ — the identical grammatical shape, lo plus a noun, used first of a false god and then of a nation with no standing as Jehovah’s own. Every shelf version keeps some form of the mirror (‘that which is not God’ / ‘those that are not a people’, KJV/ASV; ‘what is no god’ / ‘what is not a people’, NWT). Centuries later Paul quotes this verse almost verbatim from the Greek to explain why the gospel reached non-Jews: ‘I will make you jealous by those who are not a nation’ (Romans 10:19, not yet on these pages) — the poem’s own threatened punishment read, in hindsight, as a description of exactly what happened.

★ V22’s fire reaches further down than any other fire in the Torah. Most Torah judgment-fire burns what is above ground (Sodom, Korah’s company); this one burns ‘down to the depths of Sheol’ — the one place in the Pentateuch where a fire is said to reach the realm of the dead itself, not merely kill the living.

★★ V27 gives the ONE reason in the whole poem that God holds back — and it is not mercy. Every earlier threat in this chapter runs to its conclusion; this one alone is interrupted mid-sentence, lulei, ‘were it not that’, and what stops it is a concern about being MISREAD — that Israel’s enemies would credit their own hand rather than Jehovah’s for the destruction. The logic runs the opposite direction from Abraham’s bargaining at Sodom (Genesis 18, already on these pages) or Moses’s own intercession after the golden calf (Exodus 32:14, already on these pages): those arguments appeal to God’s justice or reputation FOR RIGHTEOUSNESS; this one is a purely reputational calculation about who gets the credit for a catastrophe. All thirteen versions checked on both shelves locate ka’as here with the enemy, not with God — ASV ‘provocation’, KJV ‘wrath’, NIV ‘taunt’, NWT 1984 ‘vexation’ — though the emotion named is the enemy’s own anger or taunt, not God’s, which the same word names three verses earlier (v19) — the identical noun doing two different jobs six verses apart.

Verses 28–35 · A wine from Sodom’s vineyard, a serpent’s venom, and the verse Paul forbids Christians to act on

★ V30’s ‘rock’ is lowercase in this translation on purpose — the ONLY other nation’s god named ‘rock’ anywhere in this chapter. Tzuram, ‘THEIR rock’, borrows the exact title vv4, 15, 18, 31 and 37 (below) reserve for Jehovah and hands it, for one clause, to whatever power Israel’s enemies credit with victory — before v31 immediately takes it back: ‘their rock is not like OUR Rock’. The capital letter in this translation follows that argument rather than the bare noun.

★★ V33’s ‘serpents’ is the SAME Hebrew word Genesis 1 uses for the great sea-beasts of day five, and here it means something much smaller and much closer. Tannin, already noted on these pages as ‘sea-beast; elsewhere serpent or dragon’, delivers on that second sense here: ‘the venom of TANNINIM is their wine’, an ordinary poisonous snake rather than a chaos-monster of the deep. Two shelf versions keep the older, more monstrous English word regardless: Douay-Rheims reads ‘the gall of DRAGONS’, and Geneva 1599 the same, ‘the poison of DRAGONS’ — both following the Vulgate’s draconum rather than reading the word down to size.

★★ V35’s ‘vengeance is mine’ is the verse Paul quotes to forbid Christians the very thing it claims. Li naqam ve-shillem, ‘mine is vengeance and recompense’, is God reserving naqam — the identical root already flagged at Numbers 31 (already on these pages) as a word this book never once lets a human wield on his own account — as his own exclusive office. Paul quotes it almost verbatim in the Greek at Romans 12:19 (not yet on these pages): ‘never avenge yourselves… for it is written, “Vengeance is mine, I will repay,” says the Lord.’ The verse that describes what God alone will do becomes, eleven centuries later, an instruction for what everyone else must not.

Verses 36–42 · ‘I am he’ — and the one place in the Torah the root for relenting is used to AFFIRM it

★★★ V36’s ‘relent’ is the positive twin of a denial Balaam already made, and the two verses share one rare Hebrew form. The reflexive stem yitnecham stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Numbers 23:19 (already on these pages), Psalm 135:14, and this one. Numbers 23:19 uses it to DENY the possibility — ‘God is not a man, that he should lie, nor a son of man, that he should relent’ — spoken by a pagan seer hired to curse Israel. This verse and Psalm 135:14 use the identical form to AFFIRM the opposite: ‘Jehovah will judge his people, and concerning his servants he WILL relent’. The same rare stem, in the same book’s own poetry twice over, says both that God does not change his mind and that he does — and this translation leaves both standing rather than harmonizing either into the other, the same discipline already kept at Numbers 23.

★★★ V39’s ‘I — I am he’ doubles the pronoun for an emphasis Hebrew grammar does not require. Searched against the whole archived Hebrew Bible, the doubled form stands in exactly one verse, this one. Ani, ani hu — literally ‘I, I, he’ — repeats the first-person pronoun before adding a demonstrative that itself functions as a verb, ‘I am’. The single form, ani hu, becomes Isaiah’s own repeated signature for the identical claim (41:4; 43:10, 13; 46:4; 48:12; 52:6 — none yet on these pages), but only this verse doubles it. Isaiah 43:13 comes closest to a full echo: it pairs its own single ani hu with the identical closing line this verse ends on, ‘there is no one who can rescue out of my hand’ — the two verses sharing both the claim and its last four words, a century or more apart.

⚠ V41’s ‘flashing sword’ and ‘my hand takes hold of judgment’ are read by this project’s established doctrine as courtroom language rather than a mere threat of violence — the same neutral note already struck for naqam above: this is a legal verdict being carried out, not an outburst.

Verse 43 · The Song of Moses closes with its own layout mark — the one promised at the end of the last chapter — and a Greek line the New Testament applies to Christ

★★★ This verse carries the exact layout mark Deuteronomy 31’s own closing note already promised would fall here. The printed Hebrew closes v43 with {ש} where its own English column prints a petuchah — the identical substitute mark that already closed Exodus 14:31 and 15:19 (bracketing the Song of the Sea) and Judges 4:24 and 5:31 (bracketing the Song of Deborah), and that 31:30 already carried as this song’s own opening bracket. What began there is finished here: at forty-three verses, this is the LONGEST of the three songs the mark brackets — longer than the Song of the Sea (nineteen, Exodus 15:1–19) and the Song of Deborah (Judges 5’s own thirty-one).

★★★ The Masoretic Text prints a shorter verse here than the Greek Septuagint does, and the missing lines are the ones a New Testament letter quotes about Christ. The Hebrew this project translates has four short clauses: sing for joy, nations, with his people — he avenges his servants’ blood — he repays his adversaries — he makes atonement for his land, his people. The Septuagint’s Greek, supported in part by the same Qumran fragment already discussed at v8 (4QDeutq), is roughly twice as long, opening instead: ‘Rejoice, O heavens, together with him, and let all the SONS OF GOD worship him; rejoice, O nations, with his people, and let all the ANGELS OF GOD be strong for him.’ Hebrews 1:6 (already on these pages) quotes the second of those two added lines — ‘let all God’s angels bow down before him’ — and applies it to the Son, language originally addressed to Jehovah alone now directed at Christ, one more instance of the ‘sons of God’/‘angels of God’ textual fork already met at v8. This translation follows the Masoretic Text’s shorter reading, as its doctrine requires, and notes the Greek expansion rather than adopting it.

Verses 44–47 · Moses hands the song to a man the text suddenly calls by his old name

★★★ V44 calls Joshua ‘Hoshea son of Nun’ — the name he carried before Moses renamed him, one of only two places in the whole Hebrew Bible the bare old name still stands. Searched consonantally, the phrase ‘Hoshea son of Nun’ without the divine-name prefix that makes it ‘Yehoshua’ stands in exactly two verses of the Hebrew Bible: Numbers 13:8, before Moses renames him at Numbers 13:16 (both already on these pages), and this one — every other occurrence among the 28 raw hits for the bare consonants is the LONGER name, ‘Yehoshua’, which simply contains the shorter one as its last four letters. At the very moment Moses finishes the last words he will ever teach Israel, before the narrator, the text quietly drops back to the name Joshua had before Moses gave him his own. No version on either shelf comments on it; none reads it as anything but the ordinary name. Whether it is a deliberate echo of the man Joshua once was, an old textual layer, or simple variation, this translation shows the reader the name the Hebrew actually prints rather than silently harmonizing it to ‘Joshua’ the way every shelf version does.

★ V47’s ‘it is your life’ answers a question this book has been building toward since its most famous line. Ki hu chayeikhem, ‘for it is your life’, echoes 30:19’s ‘choose life’ (already on these pages) by naming the instruction itself, not merely obedience to it, as the thing chosen. The Masoretic Text marks a petuchah after this verse, closing Moses’s address to the nation before the narrative turns, in the next three verses, to God addressing Moses alone.

Verses 48–52 · Nebo, named at last, and the sentence that sends Moses up to see a land he will not enter

★★ V49 finally names the mountain Abarim only pointed toward before. Numbers 27:12 (already on these pages) sent Moses up ‘this mountain of Abarim’ without naming a peak; this verse supplies it, ‘Mount NEBO’, in the land of Moab facing Jericho — the same instruction repeated a second time, now with its geography complete, and Deuteronomy 34 (not yet on these pages) is where it is finally carried out.

★ V50 sends Moses to the same fate as Aaron, in the same idiom, on a DIFFERENT mountain — and the text is careful to say so. ‘Be gathered to your people’ is the Torah’s standing idiom for dying, and (reading both the third-person and second-person forms) this verse’s first half is its sixth and final verse in the Hebrew Bible — already met of Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob (each ‘gathered to HIS people’) and now, for the only time, of Moses himself (‘gathered to YOUR people’); the verse’s second half immediately repeats the third-person form of Aaron, a seventh instance of the phrase but not a new verse. ⚠ Numbers 20:26 is commonly cited as a sixth instance already, but its actual Hebrew reads only ‘Aaron shall be gathered, and shall die there’ — no ‘to his people’ in that verse at all; the fuller phrase there is supplied by English translation from its parallel a verse earlier, not by the Hebrew itself. The comparison to Aaron is explicit — ‘as Aaron your brother died on Mount Hor’ — the same brief, unmourned death Numbers 20 (already on these pages) already gave the reader, offered here as the precedent for what is about to happen to Moses.

★★ V51 states the reason Moses is barred from the land as a plural charge against two men, though only one of them is standing here. ‘Because YOU BOTH broke faith with me… at the waters of Meribah of Kadesh’ is grammatically plural throughout — Aaron, already dead by this point in the story, is charged in the same breath as Moses, exactly as Numbers 20 (already on these pages) charged them together at the rock. The verb ma’al, ‘broke faith’, is a covenant-betrayal word, sharper than ordinary disobedience — the same root Leviticus and Joshua use of Achan’s theft from what belonged to God alone.

★ The chapter’s last verse is the sentence and the mercy in one breath. ‘You will see the land only from a distance; you will not go there’ delivers the verdict and the concession in the same clause — sight without possession — the identical structure already found at Numbers 27’s parallel instruction (already on these pages): see it, then be gathered to your people. The Masoretic Text marks a petuchah after this verse, the same mark that closed v47, closing both Moses’s own book and the week’s reading, Ha’azinu, together.

Echoes paid. Tzur, flagged on these pages since Habakkuk 1:12, finally reaches its own chapter of origin: five occurrences in one poem (vv4, 15, 18, 30, 31, 37). Tohu, defined at Genesis 1:2, pays its own forward promise at v10’s ‘howling waste’. Naqam, flagged at Numbers 31 as a vengeance this Torah never lets a human claim for himself, reaches the verse (v35) that makes the reservation explicit — and that Paul will later quote to bind Christians to the same restraint. Nacham’s reflexive stem, denied at Numbers 23:19, is affirmed at v36. And the verb of Genesis 1:2’s hovering Spirit lands, promised in that chapter’s own opening note, on this chapter’s eagle (v11).

Echoes planted. Yeshurun, Israel’s poetic name, recurs at 33:5 and 33:26 (not yet on these pages), both inside the very next chapter. And v39’s closing line, ‘there is no one who can rescue out of my hand’, is picked up almost verbatim — alongside its own single ani hu — at Isaiah 43:13 (not yet on these pages): ‘also from today I am he, and there is no one who can rescue from my hand’.

Decisions. Hebrew and English verse numbers align through this whole chapter. This translation strips the Masoretic layout marks ({ר}/{ס}) that mark nearly every hemistich of the poem’s 43 verses — the ‘brick and half-brick’ column layout already explained at Exodus 14:31 and Deuteronomy 31:30 (both already on these pages) — except at v43, where Mechon-Mamre’s own substitute mark is kept visible as the promised partner to 31:30’s. The Masoretic Text also marks a petuchah after v47 and again after v52, closing Moses’s address to the people and then the whole chapter. ‘The Rock’ for tzur, ‘Jehovah’ for the tetragrammaton, and ‘gathered to your people’ for the death-idiom already established at Genesis 25 and Numbers 20 all continue this project’s established renderings. Where the Masoretic Text and the Qumran/Greek witnesses genuinely diverge (vv8 and 43), this translation follows the Masoretic Text and states the other reading rather than adopting or silently omitting it.

Library. Two new dictionary terms in both languages — yeshurun and shedim. Nine existing dictionary entries extended: tzur (its own chapter of origin, five occurrences), tannin (the ‘elsewhere serpent’ sense paid off at v33), chalaq (the Masoretic/Dead-Sea-fragment fork at v8–9 spelled out), naqam (its Deuteronomy 32:35 forward citation now a working link), nacham (its v36 citation now linked), tohu and bavat ayin (their Deuteronomy 32:10 citations now linked), and evrah and avel (their Deuteronomy 32:4 and 32:11 citations now linked). One encyclopedia entry extended in both languages — Abarim (Mount Nebo finally named).
