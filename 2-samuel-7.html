Jerusalem: with the king settled in his house and given rest from his enemies, David tells Nathan he lives in a house of cedar while the ark dwells within the curtain, and Nathan says to do what is in his heart; that night the word of Jehovah comes to Nathan: Jehovah has walked in a tent and a tabernacle and never asked for a house, he took David from the pasture to be leader, and he will make David a house — a seed from his own body who will build a house for his name, a father to him as a son, with a kindness not taken away as from Saul, and a house and kingdom made sure forever; David goes in and sits before Jehovah and prays: who am I, none is like you, Israel your people established forever, make the word stand, bless the house of your servant.
After the ark is set in its tent (2 Samuel 6:17) and the Philistine victories (5:17–25); the chapter says only that it was the night after Nathan’s first answer, and that Jehovah had given the king rest round about (7:1). It does not date itself within David’s forty years (5:4).

Por eso eres grande, Jehová Dios; porque no hay como tú, ni hay Dios fuera de ti, conforme a todo lo que hemos oído con nuestros oídos.
23

¿Y quién como tu pueblo, como Israel, una sola nación en la tierra, a quien Dios fue a redimir para sí como pueblo, y a ponerse nombre, y a hacer por ustedes las cosas grandes y temibles, para tu tierra, delante de tu pueblo, al que redimiste para ti de Egipto — naciones y sus dioses?

Y ahora, Jehová Dios, la palabra que has hablado acerca de tu siervo y acerca de su casa, hazla permanecer para siempre; y haz como has hablado.

Y sea engrandecido tu nombre para siempre, diciendo: “Jehová de los ejércitos es Dios sobre Israel”; y que la casa de tu siervo David sea firme delante de ti.
27

28

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–3 · A house of cedar and an ark within the curtain — David’s wish, rest from all his enemies, and Nathan’s quick answer

Rest all around. “And it happened, when the king dwelt in his house, and Jehovah had given him rest from all his enemies round about”: “had given him rest round about” stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible. The rest is the one the law promised for the day Israel crosses the Jordan, “he will give you rest from all your enemies around you”, which is also the day the chosen place is named, “the place that Jehovah your God chooses to make his name dwell” (Deuteronomy 12:10–11); Joshua’s days had the same words, “Jehovah gave them rest all around” and “had given rest to Israel from all their enemies round about” (Joshua 21:44; 23:1, all already on these pages). The house is the one built for David in Jerusalem (2 Samuel 5:11, already on these pages). The ASV has “given him rest from all his enemies round about”; the KJV “given him rest round about from all his enemies”; the NIV “settled in his palace and the Lord had given him rest from all his enemies around him”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah himself had given him rest from all his enemies round about”; the Douay “rest on every side from all his enemies”. The Greek has “the Lord gave him an inheritance round about”.

A house of cedar. “See now, I dwell in a house of cedar, but the ark of God dwells within the curtain”: “a house of cedar” stands in three verses. They are this one, verse 7 and 1 Chronicles 17:6. The cedar came from Tyre (2 Samuel 5:11, already on these pages). “The curtain” is yeri’ah, the tent-cloth, in the singular; the tabernacle’s own were “ten curtains” (Exodus 26:1, already on these pages); the ark stands “in the midst of the tent that David had pitched for it” (2 Samuel 6:17, already on these pages). The ASV has “the ark of God dwelleth within curtains”; the KJV “the ark of God dwelleth within curtains”; the NIV “the ark of God remains in a tent”; the NWT 1984 “in the middle of tent cloths”; the Douay “is lodged within skins”; the TLB “out in a tent”. The Chronicles’ account says “the ark of the covenant of Jehovah” (1 Chronicles 17:1).

Nathan’s first answer. “Go, do all that is in your heart; for Jehovah is with you”: the answer comes before any word of Jehovah, and it is the courtier’s answer; the words “Jehovah is with you” are what the narrator has said of David (2 Samuel 5:10, already on these pages). That night the word of Jehovah comes to Nathan with a different answer (verse 4). Solomon will say that Jehovah told David “it was in your heart” and that David “did well” (1 Kings 8:18). The ASV has “Go, do all that is in thy heart; for Jehovah is with thee”; the NIV “Whatever you have in mind, go ahead and do it, for the Lord is with you”; the NWT 1984 “Everything that is in your heart—go, do, because Jehovah is with you”; the Douay “Go, do all that is in thy heart: because the Lord is with thee”; the TLB “Go ahead with what you have in mind”.

Verses 4–7 · “Will you build me a house?” — the word to Nathan that night, a God who has been walking about in a tent and a tabernacle

Will you build me a house? “Go and say to my servant David: Thus says Jehovah: Will you build me a house for my dwelling?”: the question has the pronoun “you” stressed, ha’attah. Whether it is a refusal or an open question is not settled in the Hebrew, and the Chronicles state it, “You shall not build me a house to dwell in” (1 Chronicles 17:4). “My servant” is the title of 2 Samuel 3:18 (already on these pages). The ASV has “Shalt thou build me a house for me to dwell in?”; the KJV “Shalt thou build me an house for me to dwell in?”; the NIV “Are you the one to build me a house to dwell in?”; the NWT 1984 “Should you yourself build me a house for me to dwell in?”; the Geneva “Shalt thou build me an house for my dwelling?”; the TLB “Tell my servant David not to do it!”.

Walking about in a tent. “For I have not dwelt in a house from the day I brought up the sons of Israel from Egypt until this day; but I have been walking about in a tent and in a tabernacle”: “walking about in a tent” stands in one verse. The participle is that of Jehovah God “walking about in the garden” (Genesis 3:8), of “I will walk among you” (Leviticus 26:12) and of the God who “walks about in the midst of your camp” (Deuteronomy 23:15, all already on these pages). “Tabernacle” is mishkan, the dwelling Moses was shown (Exodus 25:9, already on these pages). The ASV has “have walked in a tent and in a tabernacle”; the KJV “but have walked in a tent and in a tabernacle”; the NIV “moving from place to place with a tent as my dwelling”; the NWT 1984 “I was continually walking about in a tent and in a tabernacle”; the Douay “walked in a tabernacle, and in a tent”; the TLB “My home has been a tent”. The Greek has “in a lodging and in a tent”.

Not a word about a house. “Did I speak a word with one of the tribes of Israel whom I commanded to shepherd my people Israel, saying: ‘Why have you not built me a house of cedar?’”: “shepherd” is ra’ah, the word of the tribes at Hebron, “You shall shepherd my people Israel” (2 Samuel 5:2, already on these pages). The Chronicles have “judges of Israel” where this verse has “tribes” (1 Chronicles 17:6), and the Greek has “one tribe”. The ASV has “any of the tribes of Israel, whom I commanded to be shepherd of my people Israel”; the NIV “any of their rulers whom I commanded to shepherd my people Israel”; the NWT 1984 “one of the tribes of Israel that I commanded to shepherd my people Israel”; the TLB “Israel’s leaders, the shepherds of my people”; the Geneva “when I commanded the Judges to feed my people Israel”; the Douay “any one of the tribes of Israel”.

Verses 8–11 · “I took you from the pasture” — a great name, a place planted for Israel, rest, and “Jehovah will make you a house”

From the pasture. “I took you from the pasture, from following the flock, to be leader over my people, over Israel”: “from the pasture” (min-hanaveh) stands in two verses. They are this one and 1 Chronicles 17:7. The youngest son “shepherding the flock” (1 Samuel 16:11), the one sent for “with the flock” (16:19) and the shepherd who struck the lion and the bear (17:34, all already on these pages) are all behind this verse; see naveh. “Leader” is nagid, the third time in this book (2 Samuel 5:2; 6:21, already on these pages). The ASV has “I took thee from the sheepcote, from following the sheep, that thou shouldest be prince over my people”; the KJV “to be ruler over my people”; the NIV “I took you from the pasture, from tending the flock, and appointed you ruler over my people Israel”; the NWT 1984 “from the pasture ground from following the flock to become a leader”; the TLB “when you were a mere shepherd”; the Douay “I took thee out of the pastures from following the sheep to be ruler”.

A great name. “And I was with you wherever you went, and I cut off all your enemies from before you; and I will make for you a great name, like the name of the great ones who are in the earth”: the verb karat, “cut off”, is the verb of “cut a covenant” (2 Samuel 5:3, already on these pages); see karat. “I will make your name great” is Jehovah’s word to Abram (Genesis 12:2), against the builders of Babel who said “let us make a name for ourselves” (Genesis 11:4, both already on these pages). The Hebrew verb form can be read as past or future. The ASV has “I will make thee a great name”; the KJV “have made thee a great name”; the NIV “Now I will make your name great”; the NWT 1984 “I shall certainly make for you a great name”; the Douay “I have made thee a great man”; the TLB “I will make your name greater yet”. The Greek has “I made you famous”.

A place planted. “And I will appoint a place for my people, for Israel, and will plant them, and they shall dwell in their own place and be disturbed no more; and the sons of injustice shall not afflict them again, as at the first”: “I will appoint a place for my people” stands in two verses, this one and 1 Chronicles 17:9. To plant a people is the song of the sea’s “you will bring them in and plant them on the mountain of your inheritance” (Exodus 15:17, already on these pages). “Sons of injustice” stands in three verses. They are David’s lament for Abner, “as one falls before sons of injustice” (2 Samuel 3:34, already on these pages), this verse and the Chronicles’. The ASV has “the children of wickedness afflict them any more”; the KJV “the children of wickedness”; the NIV “Wicked people will not oppress them anymore”; the NWT 1984 “the sons of unrighteousness”; the Douay “the children of iniquity”; the Geneva “neither shall wicked people trouble them anymore”.

Jehovah will make you a house. “Even from the day that I commanded judges over my people Israel; and I will give you rest from all your enemies. And Jehovah tells you that Jehovah will make you a house”: the sentence of verse 10 runs on into verse 11, back to the judges (Judges 2:16, already on these pages). David had proposed to build a house; Jehovah says he will make one, and the word bayit carries both a building and a family. The ASV has “Moreover Jehovah telleth thee that Jehovah will make thee a house”; the KJV “telleth thee that he will make thee an house”; the NIV “The Lord declares to you that the Lord himself will establish a house for you”; the NWT 1984 “Jehovah has told you that a house is what Jehovah will make for you”; the Douay “the Lord foretelleth to thee, that the Lord will make thee a house”. The Greek reverses the sense: “that you will build a house for him”.

Verses 12–17 · The promise — a seed from your own body, a throne established, “I will be to him a father”, and a house made sure forever

A seed from your own body. “When your days are fulfilled and you lie down with your fathers, I will raise up your seed after you, who shall come out of your own body; and I will establish his kingdom”: “from your own body” (mime’ekha) stands in three verses, Jehovah’s word to Abram about his heir (Genesis 15:4), the word to Rebekah about the two peoples within her (Genesis 25:23, both already on these pages), and this verse. “Lie down with your fathers” is the word to Moses (Deuteronomy 31:16, already on these pages); see zera, “seed”. The ASV has “that shall proceed out of thy bowels”; the KJV “which shall proceed out of thy bowels”; the Geneva “which shall proceed out of thy body”; the NIV “your own flesh and blood”; the NWT 1984 “which will come out of your inward parts”; the TLB “I will put one of your sons upon your throne”; the Douay “which shall proceed out of thy bowels”.

Established. The root kun runs through the promise: “I will establish his kingdom” (verse 12), “I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever” (verse 13), “your throne shall be firm forever” (verse 16), and in David’s prayer “you established for yourself your people Israel” (verse 24) and “let the house of your servant David be firm” (verse 26); it is the root of nakhon, and of the verb that told David “Jehovah had established him king” (2 Samuel 5:12, already on these pages). The ASV has “I will establish his kingdom”; the NWT 1984 “I shall indeed firmly establish his kingdom”; the TLB “I will make his kingdom strong”; the NIV “I will establish his kingdom”. The Greek has “I will prepare his kingdom”.

A house for my name. “He shall build a house for my name”: the one who builds is the seed of verse 12, and he is not named here. “My name” answers the place “to make his name dwell” (Deuteronomy 12:11, already on these pages). This chapter gives no reason why David may not build; the Chronicles give one, “you have shed blood abundantly” (1 Chronicles 22:8), and Solomon tells it as a son who was to build (1 Kings 8:19). The ASV has “He shall build a house for my name”; the NIV “He is the one who will build a house for my Name”; the NWT 1984 “He is the one that will build a house for my name”; the TLB “He is the one who shall build me a temple”; the Douay “He shall build a house to my name”.

A father and a son. “I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son; when he does wrong, I will reprove him with the rod of men and with the strokes of the sons of men”: “I will be to him a father” stands in three verses. They are this one and two in the Chronicles (1 Chronicles 17:13; 28:6). “With the rod of men” stands in one verse. Israel is “my firstborn son” (Exodus 4:22, already on these pages), and the first half of this verse is quoted of the Son in Hebrews 1:5 (already on these pages); the second half, “when he does wrong”, is not quoted there. Psalm 89 repeats the rod and the strokes (Psalm 89:33). The ASV has “if he commit iniquity, I will chasten him with the rod of men, and with the stripes of the children of men”; the NIV “When he does wrong, I will punish him with a rod wielded by men, with floggings inflicted by human hands”; the NWT 1984 “I will also reprove him with the rod of men and with the strokes of the sons of Adam”; the TLB “If he sins, I will use other nations to punish him”; the Douay “if he commit any iniquity, I will correct him with the rod of men”; the Geneva “with the plagues of the children of men”.

Kindness, as I removed it from Saul. “But my kindness shall not depart from him, as I removed it from Saul, whom I removed from before you”: chesed, the “kindness of Jehovah” (1 Samuel 20:14, already on these pages). Saul’s kingdom was torn from him (1 Samuel 15:28), and the spirit of Jehovah “had turned away from Saul” (16:14), with the verb of this verse, sur (both already on these pages). The ASV has “my lovingkindness shall not depart from him, as I took it from Saul”; the KJV “my mercy shall not depart away from him”; the NIV “my love will never be taken away from him”; the NWT 1984 “As for my loving-kindness, it will not depart from him”; the TLB “my love and kindness shall not leave him”; the Douay “my mercy I will not take away from him”.

Made sure forever. “And your house and your kingdom shall be made sure forever before you; your throne shall be firm forever”: the verb is ne’eman, “faithful”, and “a faithful house” stands in three verses. They are Jehovah’s “I will build him a faithful house” (1 Samuel 2:35), Abigail’s “Jehovah will surely make for my lord a faithful house” (1 Samuel 25:28, both already on these pages) and Ahijah’s promise to Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:38). Here the words are in the other order, “faithful your house”, and Abigail’s word is Jehovah’s own. The Gospel speaks of “the throne of David his father” and a kingdom without end (Luke 1:32–33, already on these pages). The ASV has “thy house and thy kingdom shall be made sure for ever before thee”; the KJV “shall be established for ever before thee”; the NIV “Your house and your kingdom will endure forever before me”; the NWT 1984 “will certainly be steadfast to time indefinite before you”; the TLB “Your family shall rule my kingdom forever”; the Douay “thy house shall be faithful”. The Greek has “before me”, and “his house” for “your house”.

Verses 18–21 · David sits before Jehovah — “Who am I, Lord Jehovah?”, the law of the human, and “according to your own heart”

He sat before Jehovah. “And King David went in and sat before Jehovah”: “and sat before Jehovah” stands in two verses. They are this one and 1 Chronicles 17:16. The tent is where the ark is (2 Samuel 6:17, already on these pages), though the text says only “before Jehovah”; Israel at Bethel “sat there before Jehovah” in weeping and fasting (Judges 20:26, already on these pages). The NIV has “went in and sat before the Lord”; the ASV “went in, and sat before Jehovah”; the NWT 1984 “came in and sat down before Jehovah”; the TLB “went into the Tabernacle and sat before the Lord and prayed”.

Who am I? “Who am I, Lord Jehovah, and what is my house, that you have brought me this far?”: the question is the one David asked of Saul, “Who am I, and what is my life … that I should be son-in-law to the king?” (1 Samuel 18:18, already on these pages). “Lord Jehovah” is Adonai Yehovih, the title of verses 18, 19, 20, 28 and 29. The ASV has “Who am I, O Lord Jehovah, and what is my house”; the NIV “Who am I, Sovereign Lord, and what is my family”; the NWT 1984 “Who am I, O Sovereign Lord Jehovah? And what is my house”; the KJV “Who am I, O Lord GOD? and what is my house”; the TLB “why have you showered your blessings on such an insignificant person as I am?”.

The law of the human. “And this was yet a small thing in your eyes, Lord Jehovah; and you have spoken also to the house of your servant for a distant time; and this is the law of the human, Lord Jehovah”: “the law of the human” (torat ha’adam) stands in one verse. Torah is “instruction”, and adam is the human, not a man. The readings differ. The NIV has “this decree, Sovereign Lord, is for a mere human!”; the ASV “this too after the manner of men”; the KJV “And is this the manner of man, O Lord GOD?”; the NWT 1984 “this is the law given for mankind”; the Douay “for this is the law of Adam”; the TLB “Such generosity is far beyond any human standard!”. The Greek has “this is the law of man”.

According to your own heart. “What more can David say to you? For you know your servant … For the sake of your word, and according to your own heart, you have done all this greatness”: the heart is David’s in verse 3 (“all that is in your heart”), Jehovah’s here, and David’s again in verse 27 (“has found his heart”); “a man after his own heart” is Samuel’s word of David (1 Samuel 13:14, already on these pages). The ASV has “according to thine own heart, hast thou wrought all this greatness”; the NIV “according to your will, you have done this great thing”; the NWT 1984 “in agreement with your own heart”; the TLB “just because you promised to and because you want to!”; the Douay “according to thy own heart”.

Verses 22–24 · “There is none like you” — the nation God went to redeem, “nations and their gods”, and a people established for ever

None like you. “Therefore you are great, Jehovah God; for there is none like you, and there is no God beside you, in all that we have heard with our ears”: Hannah’s “there is none holy like Jehovah, for there is none besides you” (1 Samuel 2:2) and the song of the sea’s “Who is like you among the gods, Jehovah?” (Exodus 15:11), with Moses’ “there is no other besides him” (Deuteronomy 4:35, all already on these pages). The ASV has “thou art great, O Jehovah God: for there is none like thee”; the NIV “How great you are, Sovereign Lord!”; the NWT 1984 “there is no other like you, and there is no God except you”; the Douay “thou art magnified, O Lord God”; the TLB “How great you are, Lord God!”.

Whom God went to redeem. “And who is like your people, like Israel, one nation in the earth, whom God went to redeem for himself as a people, and to set himself a name, and to do for you the great things and the fearful, for your land, before your people whom you redeemed for yourself from Egypt — nations and their gods?”: “whom God went” (asher halekhu Elohim) stands in one verse, and its verb is plural though its subject is Elohim; Abraham’s “when God caused me to wander” (Genesis 20:13) and Jacob’s “there God had revealed himself to him” (Genesis 35:7, both already on these pages) have the same plural verb. The verse turns from “your people” to “for you” and ends in a fragment, “nations and their gods”, with no verb of its own. The Chronicles have “to make yourself a name by great and fearful things, in driving out nations” (1 Chronicles 17:21), and the Greek ends “nations and tents”. The ASV has “to do great things for you, and terrible things for thy land”; the KJV “to do for you great things and terrible”; the NIV “by driving out nations and their gods from before your people”; the NWT 1984 “to drive out because of your people … the nations and their gods”; the Douay “to do for them great and terrible things”; the TLB “You have done great miracles to destroy Egypt and its gods”.

Established for ever. “And you established for yourself your people Israel to be a people to you forever; and you, Jehovah, became their God”: the formula is Jehovah’s promise at the exodus, “I will take you to myself for a people, and I will be to you a God” (Exodus 6:7) and “you shall be my people” (Leviticus 26:12, both already on these pages), and “established” is the root kun again. The ASV has “thou didst establish to thyself thy people Israel”; the KJV “thou hast confirmed to thyself thy people Israel”; the NIV “You have established your people Israel as your very own forever”; the NWT 1984 “you proceeded to establish your people Israel firmly for yourself”; the Geneva “thou hast ordained to thyself thy people Israel”; the TLB “You chose Israel to be your people forever”.

Verses 25–29 · “Make it stand forever” — the uncovered ear, the heart David found, “your words are truth”, and a house to be blessed

Make it stand. “The word that you have spoken concerning your servant and concerning his house, make it stand forever; and do as you have spoken”: haqem is the verb of verse 12, “I will raise up your seed”, and David asks that the word be raised as the seed is. The ASV has “confirm thou it for ever”; the KJV “establish it for ever”; the NIV “keep forever the promise you have made”; the NWT 1984 “carry out to time indefinite”; the Douay “raise up for ever the word”; the Geneva “confirm forever the word”; the TLB “do as you have promised”. The Greek has “make sure”.

The uncovered ear. “You have uncovered the ear of your servant, saying: ‘A house I will build for you’; therefore your servant has found his heart to pray this prayer to you”: “you have uncovered the ear” (galita et ozen) stands in one verse; the idiom of “Jehovah had uncovered Samuel’s ear” (1 Samuel 9:15) and of Jonathan’s father doing nothing “without uncovering my ear” (1 Samuel 20:2, both already on these pages). In David’s report Jehovah says “I will build”, where verse 11 had “make”. The ASV has “hast revealed to thy servant, saying, I will build thee a house”; the NIV “you have revealed this to your servant … So your servant has found courage to pray this prayer to you”; the NWT 1984 “have made a revelation to your servant’s ear”; the TLB “I have been bold enough to pray this prayer of acceptance”; the Douay “hast revealed to the ear of thy servant”.

Your words are truth. “And now, Lord Jehovah, you are the God, and your words are truth; and you have spoken to your servant this good thing”: the Hebrew has “your words”, and the word “covenant” is not in this chapter. The NIV has “Your covenant is trustworthy”, a rendering that the Hebrew does not carry. The covenant is named elsewhere, “an everlasting covenant he has made with me” (2 Samuel 23:5) and “I have made a covenant with my chosen” (Psalm 89:4). The ASV has “thou art God, and thy words are truth”; the KJV “thou art that God, and thy words be true”; the NWT 1984 “you are the [true] God; and as for your words, let them prove to be truth”; the Douay “thou art God, and thy words shall be true”; the TLB “your words are truth”.

Bless the house. “And now, be pleased to bless the house of your servant, that it may be forever before you; for you, Lord Jehovah, have spoken, and from your blessing the house of your servant shall be blessed forever”: David had blessed his own house (2 Samuel 6:20), and Jehovah had blessed the house of Obed-edom (6:11–12, all already on these pages). The ASV has “let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant”; the NIV “Now be pleased to bless the house of your servant”; the NWT 1984 “take it upon yourself and bless the house of your servant”; the Douay “begin, and bless the house of thy servant”; the TLB “Bless me and my family forever!”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “rest round about”, as in Deuteronomy 12:10 and Joshua 21:44; “a house of cedar”; “the curtain” for yeri’ah; “walking about”, as in Genesis 3:8; “a tent and a tabernacle”; “shepherd”, as in 2 Samuel 5:2; “leader” for nagid; “cut off” for karat; “sons of injustice”, as in 2 Samuel 3:34; “I will raise up your seed” and “make it stand” for qum; “from your own body”, as in Genesis 15:4; “establish” for kun, as in 2 Samuel 5:12; “kindness” for chesed, as in 1 Samuel 20:14; “made sure” for ne’eman; “Lord Jehovah” for Adonai Yehovih; “the law of the human”; “uncovered the ear”, as in 1 Samuel 9:15; and “your words are truth”.

Echoes paid. The rest and the chosen place (Deuteronomy 12:10–11; Joshua 21:44; 23:1); the house built in Jerusalem and the cedar of Tyre (2 Samuel 5:11); the ark in the tent (2 Samuel 6:17; Exodus 26:1); Jehovah with David (2 Samuel 5:10); the God who walks about (Genesis 3:8; Leviticus 26:12; Deuteronomy 23:15); the tabernacle (Exodus 25:9); the shepherd of the flock (1 Samuel 16:11, 19; 17:34); “leader” (2 Samuel 5:2; 6:21); the name made great and the name made by builders (Genesis 12:2; 11:4); the judges (Judges 2:16); the sons of injustice (2 Samuel 3:34); the people planted (Exodus 15:17); the heir from the body and the fathers laid down with (Genesis 15:4; 25:23; Deuteronomy 31:16); the firstborn son (Exodus 4:22) and the Son in Hebrews (Hebrews 1:5); Jehovah’s kindness (1 Samuel 20:14), and Saul’s kingdom and spirit removed (1 Samuel 15:28; 16:14); the faithful house (1 Samuel 2:35; 25:28) and the throne of David in Luke (Luke 1:32–33); the sitting before Jehovah (Judges 20:26); “Who am I” (1 Samuel 18:18); “a man after his own heart” (1 Samuel 13:14); none like Jehovah (1 Samuel 2:2; Exodus 15:11; Deuteronomy 4:35); the plural verb with God (Genesis 20:13; 35:7); the people for a God (Exodus 6:7); the uncovered ear (1 Samuel 9:15; 20:2); and the blessed houses (2 Samuel 6:11–12, 20).

Echoes planted. David “got a name” when he returned from the Valley of Salt (2 Samuel 8:13); Solomon on what Jehovah told David, “it was in your heart” (1 Kings 8:17–19); David’s blood and the builder-son in the Chronicles (1 Chronicles 22:8–10; 28:6) and the Chronicles’ own telling of this chapter (1 Chronicles 17:1–27); Ahijah’s faithful house for Jeroboam (1 Kings 11:38); “an everlasting covenant” in David’s last words (2 Samuel 23:5); and the rod and the strokes, and the covenant with the chosen, in Psalm 89 (Psalm 89:4, 33). None of these is translated here yet.
