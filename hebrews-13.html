Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–6 · Hospitality — “entertained angels without knowing it,” marriage, money, and “I will never let go of you”

“Let brotherly love abide. Do not forget hospitality, for through it some have entertained angels without knowing it.” Philadelphia, “brotherly love,” stands in five verses, among them Romans 12:10 (dictionary). Philoxenia, “hospitality,” literally love of strangers, stands in two verses, here and Romans 12:13 (dictionary); the two words sit side by side, love of brothers and love of strangers. “Without knowing it” is lanthanō, “to escape notice”: the woman at Luke 8:47 saw “that she had not gone unnoticed” (dictionary). Abraham ran to meet “three men” at Genesis 18:2, and Lot rose to meet “the two angels” at Genesis 19:1. “Abide” is menō, the verb of the “abiding” city of verse 14. The KJV reads “for thereby some have entertained angels unawares”; the NWT 1984 reads “unknown to themselves, entertained angels”; the Douay reads “Let the charity of the brotherhood abide in you.”

“Remember the prisoners, as though bound with them, and those who are ill-treated, since you yourselves also are in a body.” The prisoners are those of Hebrews 10:34, “you sympathized with the prisoners” (dictionary). Syndeō, “bound with,” stands in one verse. Kakoucheō, “ill-treat,” stands in two verses, here and the faithful “oppressed, ill-treated” of Hebrews 11:37 (dictionary). “In a body” has no article in the Greek; it is read as the body you live in, able to suffer as theirs does, or as the body of the congregation. The KJV reads “as being yourselves also in the body”; the NWT 1984 reads “since YOU yourselves also are still in a body”; the NIV reads “as if you yourselves were suffering.”

“Let marriage be held in honor among all, and let the marriage bed be undefiled; for God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers.” The Greek has no verb: it can be a statement, “Marriage is honorable,” or, among the commands around it, a command; this page prints the command and reports the statement. “Among all” can also be “in all things.” Amiantos, “undefiled,” stands in four verses; the first is the high priest “holy, innocent, undefiled” of Hebrews 7:26 (dictionary). “Sexually immoral” is pornos, as at Hebrews 12:16. The noun moichos, “adulterer,” stands in three verses, among them 1 Corinthians 6:9 (dictionary). Robinson–Pierpont reads “but” for “for” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled”; the ASV reads “Let marriage be had in honor among all”; the NIV reads “for God will judge the adulterer and all the sexually immoral.”

⚠ “Let your way of life be free from the love of money … for he himself has said, ‘I will never let go of you, nor will I ever forsake you.’ So we say with good courage, ‘Jehovah is my helper; I will not be afraid. What will man do to me?’” Aphilargyros, “free from the love of money,” stands in two verses; the other is the overseer “not a lover of money” at 1 Timothy 3:3 (dictionary). “Content” is the verb of “be content with your wages” at Luke 3:14. The promise is the language of Deuteronomy 31:6, “He will not let go of you and he will not forsake you,” said of Jehovah, and of Joshua 1:5, where Jehovah says it himself: “I will not let you go and I will not forsake you.” Hebrews has the first person and a doubled “never”; its exact form is not the Greek of Deuteronomy 31:6, Joshua 1:5 or Genesis 28:15 (all checked), and the same form is reported in Philo (reported, not checked here). The second quotation is Psalm 118:6, in the Greek Psalter 117:6, “Kyrios is my helper, and I will not fear what man will do to me”; the Hebrew has “Jehovah is for me; I will not fear; what can man do to me?” (both checked). The Psalm has the divine name, so this page prints “Jehovah.” Tregelles, Nestle–Aland 28 and Robinson–Pierpont have the Psalter’s “and,” and Westcott–Hort omit it (the apparatus); with it the line can be read as the Psalter reads, “I will not fear what man will do to me,” and without it as a question. Boēthos, “helper,” stands in one verse; its verb is the “help” of Hebrews 2:18 (dictionary). The KJV reads “and I will not fear what man shall do unto me”; the ASV reads “I will not fear: What shall man do unto me?”; the NWT 1984 reads “Jehovah is my helper.”

Verses 7–9 · “Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today, and forever” — leaders, their faith, and strange teachings

“Remember your leaders, who spoke the word of God to you; considering the outcome of their conduct, imitate their faith.” “Leaders” is hēgoumenoi, the participle of the “leading men” of Acts 15:22, here and at verses 17 and 24 (dictionary). They “spoke” in the past tense, and “the outcome” is read as the end of their lives or as the result of how they lived. Ekbasis, “outcome,” stands in two verses; the other is “the way out” of 1 Corinthians 10:13 (dictionary). “Conduct” is anastrophē, Paul’s “former way of life” at Galatians 1:13. The verb mimeomai, “imitate,” stands in three verses, two of them at 2 Thessalonians 3:7 and 3:9; its noun is the “imitators of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises” at Hebrews 6:12. The ASV reads “Remember them that had the rule over you”; the NWT 1984 reads “Remember those who are taking the lead among YOU”; the Douay reads “Remember your prelates.”

“Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today, and forever.” There is no verb in the Greek; “is” is supplied. “The same” is ho autos, the words of Psalm 102 at Hebrews 1:12, “but you are the same, and your years will not fail.” Echthes, “yesterday,” stands in three verses; the others are “Yesterday at the seventh hour” at John 4:52 and “the way you killed the Egyptian yesterday” at Acts 7:28 (dictionary). “Forever” is eis tous aiōnas, “to the ages.” The KJV reads “Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever”; the Geneva reads “Jesus Christ yesterday, and today, the same also is forever”; the Douay reads “Jesus Christ, yesterday, and to day; and the same for ever.”

“Do not be carried away by various and strange teachings; for it is good for the heart to be made firm by grace, not by foods, from which those who walk in them have not benefited.” “Various” is the word of the “various acts of power” at Hebrews 2:4; “strange” is xenos, the “strangers” of Hebrews 11:13 (dictionary). Parapherō, “carry away,” stands in two verses; the other is the clouds “carried along by winds” at Jude 12. “Made firm” is the verb of “confirmed to us” at Hebrews 2:3. “Foods” are those of Hebrews 9:10, “foods and drinks and various washings”; which foods are meant, the letter does not say, and readings differ. “Benefited” is the verb of Hebrews 4:2, “the word they heard did not benefit them.” Robinson–Pierpont reads “those who walked” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “Be not carried about with divers and strange doctrines”; the NWT 1984 reads “not by eatables”; the NIV reads “not by eating ceremonial foods.”

Verses 10–16 · ⚠ “We have an altar” — outside the camp, outside the gate, a sacrifice of praise and “the fruit of lips”

⚠ “We have an altar from which those who serve the tent have no right to eat.” Thysiastērion, “altar,” is the word of Hebrews 7:13, “from which no one has attended to the altar” (dictionary). The letter does not say what the altar is: it has been read as the cross, as the Lord’s table, and as the heavenly altar of Christ’s own sacrifice, and this page does not choose. “Those who serve” is the verb of Hebrews 8:5, “They serve a copy and shadow.” The KJV reads “whereof they have no right to eat which serve the tabernacle”; the NWT 1984 reads “those who do sacred service at the tent have no authority to eat”; the TLB reads “the cross where Christ was sacrificed,” an interpretation the Greek does not state.

“For the bodies of those animals whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin are burned outside the camp. Therefore Jesus also … suffered outside the gate. So let us go out to him outside the camp, bearing his reproach.” The law is Leviticus 16:27, the Day of Atonement: the bull and the goat “whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place, shall be carried outside the camp.” “The sanctuary” is ta hagia, as at Hebrews 9:12. “Outside the gate” agrees with John 19:20, “the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city.” Parembolē, “camp,” stands in ten verses; six are the Roman “barracks” in Jerusalem, as at Acts 21:34, and one is the “armies” of Hebrews 11:34 (dictionary). Oneidismos, “reproach,” stands in five verses, among them the “insults” of Hebrews 10:33 and “the reproach of the Anointed” at Hebrews 11:26 (dictionary). The NIV reads “bearing the disgrace he bore”; the NWT 1984 reads “bearing the reproach he bore”; the TLB reads “beyond the city walls.”

“For here we have no abiding city, but we are looking for the one that is to come.” “Abiding” is the participle of menō, as in “a better possession, and an abiding one” at Hebrews 10:34. “Looking for” is the verb of Hebrews 11:14, “looking for a homeland.” The city is the one of Hebrews 11:10, 11:16 and 12:22. The KJV reads “For here have we no continuing city”; the ASV reads “but we seek after the city which is to come”; the TLB reads “For this world is not our home.”

⚠ “Through him, then, let us offer up a sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of lips that confess his name. And do not forget doing good and sharing, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased.” “A sacrifice of praise” is the Greek of Leviticus 7:12, where the Hebrew has “the sacrifice of thanksgiving”, and of Psalm 50:14 and 50:23, 49:14 and 49:23 in the Greek Psalter (all checked). Ainesis, “praise,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). “The fruit of lips” is the Greek of Hosea 14:3, 14:2 in English Bibles: “we will repay the fruit of our lips,” where the Hebrew has “we will repay bulls, our lips” (both checked); the Hebrew word for “bulls,” parim, begins with the letters of peri, “fruit.” “Confess” is homologeō, the root of the “confession” of Hebrews 4:14 and 10:23 (dictionary). Westcott–Hort omit “then” (the apparatus). Eupoiia, “doing good,” stands in one verse (dictionary). “Sharing” is koinōnia, the “contribution” for the poor at Romans 15:26 (dictionary). “Is well pleased” is the verb of Enoch, who “pleased God,” at Hebrews 11:5. The KJV reads “the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name”; the ASV reads “the fruit of lips which make confession to his name”; the Douay reads “for by such sacrifices God's favour is obtained.”

Verses 17–19 · “They keep watch over your souls” — obey, be persuaded, and “restored to you sooner”

“Obey your leaders and yield to them, for they keep watch over your souls as those who will give an account, so that they may do this with joy and not with groaning, for that would be of no profit to you.” “Obey” is peithesthe, from peithō, whose passive means “be persuaded”: the same verb is “we are persuaded” in verse 18. Hypeikō, “yield,” stands in one verse (dictionary). The verb agrypneō, “keep watch,” literally stay awake, stands in four verses, among them “Watch! Keep alert!” at Mark 13:33 (dictionary). “Give an account” is the phrase of Matthew 12:36, “they will give an account of it on the day of judgment.” The verb stenazō, “groan,” stands in six verses, among them “we ourselves groan within ourselves” at Romans 8:23. Alysitelēs, “of no profit,” stands in one verse (dictionary). The KJV reads “Obey them that have the rule over you, and submit yourselves”; the NWT 1984 reads “not with sighing, for this would be damaging to YOU”; the NIV reads “Have confidence in your leaders.”

“Pray for us; for we are persuaded that we have a good conscience, wishing to conduct ourselves well in all things. And I exhort you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you sooner.” Robinson–Pierpont reads “we trust” for “we are persuaded” (the apparatus). “Conscience” is the word of Hebrews 9:9, 9:14 and 10:22. “Conduct ourselves” is the verb of the “conduct” of verse 7. The writer turns from “us” to “I,” as at Hebrews 11:32, “Time will fail me.” “Restored” is apokathistēmi, the verb of “restoring the kingdom to Israel” at Acts 1:6 (dictionary); what keeps the writer away, the letter does not say. The KJV reads “for we trust we have a good conscience”; the ASV reads “for we are persuaded that we have a good conscience”; the NWT 1984 reads “that I may be restored to YOU the sooner.”

Verses 20–21 · “The God of peace” — the great shepherd of the sheep, and “the blood of an eternal covenant”

⚠ “Now may the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep by the blood of an eternal covenant, our Lord Jesus …” The phrase ho theos tēs eirēnēs, “the God of peace,” stands in five verses; the other four are Paul’s, as at Romans 16:20 and 1 Thessalonians 5:23. “Brought up” is anagō, as in “to bring Christ up from the dead” at Romans 10:7. The line echoes the Greek of Isaiah 63:11, “Where is the one who brought up from the sea the shepherd of the sheep?”, where the Hebrew has “the shepherds of his flock,” plural, of Moses’ time (both checked). “Shepherd” is poimēn, the “good shepherd” of John 10:11 (dictionary). “The blood of the covenant” is the phrase of Hebrews 9:20 and 10:29; Zechariah 9:11 has “by the blood of your covenant I have sent out your prisoners from the pit,” and Isaiah 55:3 “an everlasting covenant” (both checked in Hebrew and Greek). “Eternal” is the word of the “eternal redemption” of Hebrews 9:12 and the “eternal inheritance” of 9:15. “By the blood” can go with “brought up” or with “the great shepherd”; readings differ. The KJV reads “through the blood of the everlasting covenant”; the ASV reads “with the blood of an eternal covenant”; the Douay reads “in the blood of the everlasting testament.”

“… equip you in every good thing to do his will, working in us what is pleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory forever. Amen.” “Equip” is katartizō, the “prepared” of Hebrews 10:5 and the “set in order” of Hebrews 11:3 (dictionary). “Pleasing” is euarestos, the root of “acceptably” at Hebrews 12:28 and of “well pleased” in verse 16 (dictionary). Three places differ: Tregelles and Robinson–Pierpont have “every good work” and “in you”; Westcott–Hort and Nestle–Aland 28 have “in us”; and all four have “forever and ever,” where the printed text has “forever” (the apparatus). “To whom” can be God, the subject of the sentence, or Jesus Christ, the nearest name; readings differ. The KJV reads “Make you perfect in every good work”; the NWT 1984 reads “equip YOU with every good thing to do his will”; the NIV reads “and may he work in us what is pleasing to him.”

Verses 22–25 · “The word of exhortation” — “in few words,” Timothy released, and “those from Italy”

“And I exhort you, brothers, bear with the word of exhortation, for indeed I have written to you in few words.” The phrase logos tēs paraklēseōs stands in two verses: here, and at Acts 13:15, where the rulers of the synagogue ask Paul and Barnabas for a “word of encouragement for the people.” “Exhortation” is the word of Hebrews 12:5. “Bear with” is anechō, the “put up with sound teaching” of 2 Timothy 4:3 (dictionary). “In few words” is dia bracheōn; the adjective is the “for a little while” of Hebrews 2:7 and 2:9. A letter that runs to thirteen chapters in modern Bibles calls itself short. “I have written” is epistellō, the verb of “we write to them” at Acts 15:20. The KJV reads “suffer the word of exhortation: for I have written a letter unto you in few words”; the NWT 1984 reads “to bear with this word of encouragement”; the Douay reads “that you suffer this word of consolation.”

⚠ “Know that our brother Timothy has been released; with him, if he comes soon, I will see you. Greet all your leaders and all the saints. Those from Italy greet you.” This is the one mention of Timothy in Hebrews. “Released” is apolyō, the verb of “Release those men” at Acts 16:35 (dictionary); whether Timothy has been let out of prison or sent off on a journey, the letter does not say. Robinson–Pierpont omits “our” (the apparatus). “The saints” are the hagioi of Hebrews 6:10. Italia, “Italy,” stands in four verses; the other three are in Acts, among them Aquila “lately come from Italy” at Acts 18:2, the same words “from Italy” (encyclopedia). “Those from Italy” can be Italians with the writer outside Italy sending greetings home, or people in Italy greeting a congregation elsewhere; readings differ, and this page does not choose. The Douay reads “The brethren from Italy salute you”; the NWT 1984 reads “Those in Italy send YOU their greetings”; the NIV reads “Those from Italy send you their greetings.”

“Grace be with you all.” The same Greek closes Titus 3:15, “Grace be with you all.” Tregelles and Robinson–Pierpont add “Amen” (the apparatus). After the last verse the Geneva prints “Written to the Hebrews from Italy, and sent by Timothy”: a later note on the letter, not part of the Greek text this translation reads, nor of Robinson–Pierpont (checked). The letter itself names no writer. The KJV reads “Grace be with you all. Amen.”; the NWT 1984 reads “The undeserved kindness be with all of YOU.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “brotherly love” and “hospitality,” side by side; “abide” and “abiding,” one verb; “the prisoners,” as at 10:34; “ill-treated,” as at 11:37; “sexually immoral,” as at 12:16; “let go of,” the verb of Deuteronomy 31:6 and Joshua 1:5 on these pages; “Jehovah is my helper”; “leaders”; “the same,” as at 1:12; “the sanctuary”; “the camp”; “reproach,” as at 11:26; “a sacrifice of praise”; “confess”; “obey” and “persuaded,” one verb; “conduct”; “the God of peace”; “the blood of an eternal covenant”; “pleasing”; “the word of exhortation”; and “those from Italy.”

Echoes paid. The prisoners of Hebrews 10:34; ill-treated from Hebrews 11:37; the sexually immoral of Hebrews 12:16; help from Hebrews 2:18; the imitators of Hebrews 6:12; the same from Hebrews 1:12; the foods of Hebrews 9:10; the altar of Hebrews 7:13; the sanctuary of Hebrews 9:12; the reproach of Hebrews 11:26; the abiding possession of Hebrews 10:34; the city of Hebrews 12:22; confession from Hebrews 10:23; pleased God from Hebrews 11:5; conscience from Hebrews 10:22; the blood of the covenant from Hebrews 10:29; eternal from Hebrews 9:15; acceptably from Hebrews 12:28; and the exhortation of Hebrews 12:5.

The letter as a whole. Hebrews opens with God “having spoken to the fathers by the prophets” (Hebrews 1:1) and ends with “the word of exhortation.” It names no writer and no place of writing; the only companion of the writer it names is Timothy. Its recurring words (“make complete,” “covenant,” “the living God,” “sanctuary,” “once for all”) are printed the same way in every chapter of this translation.

Next in this book: none; Hebrews ends here.
