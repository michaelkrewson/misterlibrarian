A people that seeks God every day and asks why its fasting is not seen; the answer, that on the fast day they find their own pleasure and drive their workers; the fast that Jehovah chooses (loosen the yoke, break your bread for the hungry, do not hide from your own flesh); the light that breaks out like the dawn, “Here I am”, the repairer of the breach; and the sabbath called a delight.
Undated. The chapter names no king, no city and no occasion, and it sits in the second half of Isaiah, which this site files with the exile; the ruins to be built (verse 12) and the paths to be restored are what the chapter itself says. ⚠ The book’s own place for it is a cry to a people that already fasts and keeps the sabbath, and nothing in it fixes the year.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–2 · Isaiah 58:1–2 “Call out with the throat” — a people that seeks God daily

⭐ The throat and the horn. “Call out with the throat” stands in this one verse, the full voice, and “raise your voice like a ram’s horn” stands in this one verse, the shofar. The one who is told to cry out is not named; what he is to tell is “my people” their transgression and “the house of Jacob” their sins. The KJV has “Cry aloud, spare not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet”, the NIV “Raise your voice like a trumpet”, the NWT 1984 “Call out full-throated; do not hold back. Raise your voice just like a horn”, the TLB “Shout with the voice of a trumpet blast” and the Douay “Cry, cease not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet”.

⭐ Day by day they seek. “Day by day they seek” stands in this one verse: the people are devout. They seek God, delight to know his ways, ask for righteous judgments and delight in drawing near. The word for delight here is chefets, and it will turn up again in verse 3, “you find your own pleasure”, and twice in verse 13, so that one root carries both the sincere delight of verse 2 and the self-interest the prophet charges them with. The comparison “like a nation that did righteousness” is left standing, and the versions lean different ways. The KJV has “as a nation that did righteousness”, the NIV “as if they were a nation that does what is right”, the NWT 1984 “like a nation that carried on righteousness itself” and the TLB “just as though they would obey them”. ⚠ Whether the verse describes them in earnest or in show is the question of the whole chapter, and this translation keeps the Hebrew “like a nation that did righteousness” without answering it.

Verses 3–5 · Isaiah 58:3–5 “Why have we fasted?” — your pleasure and your workers — is this the fast?

⭐ The complaint. “Why have we fasted, and you did not see” stands in this one verse, and “we afflicted our soul” stands in this one verse; to afflict the soul (anah) is the wording of the day of atonement (Leviticus 16:29, now on these pages), and the fast is tsom. The KJV has “Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and thou seest not?”, the NIV “Why have we humbled ourselves, and you have not noticed?” and the NWT 1984 “and did we afflict our soul and you would take no note?”.

⭐ Your pleasure, and your workers. The answer is in the same word as verse 2: on the day of the fast “you find chefets”, your own pleasure or business. The word for “your workers”, atsvekhem, stands in this one verse, and the verb for what is done to them, tigghesu, comes from nagas, the verb of the taskmasters (noges) of Egypt (Exodus 5:6, now on these pages). The KJV has “exact all your labours”, the NIV “you do as you please and exploit all your workers”, the TLB “you keep right on oppressing your workers”, the Geneva “and require all your debts” and the Douay “you exact of all your debtors”. ⚠ The Hebrew word is a rare one and the versions split between labour, workers and debts.

⭐ Strife and a fist. “With a fist of wickedness” stands in this one verse. The fast ends in a fight. The KJV has “ye fast for strife and debate, and to smite with the fist of wickedness”, the NIV “Your fasting ends in quarreling and strife, and in striking each other with wicked fists”, the TLB “what good is fasting when you keep on fighting and quarreling” and the Douay “you fast for debates and strife”.

⭐ The head like a rush. “Like a rush his head” stands in this one verse, agmon, the reed that bends, and “sackcloth and ashes he spreads” stands in this one verse, the saq and the efer. The question is whether this is what Jehovah calls a fast, and a day acceptable to him (“a day of favour to Jehovah” stands in this one verse). Jesus speaks of the fast without the disfigured face at Matthew 6:16 (now on these pages). The KJV has “to bow down his head as a bulrush”, the NIV “like a reed”, the NWT 1984 “bowing down his head just like a rush”, the TLB “bowing like reeds in the wind” and the Douay “to wind his head about like a circle”.

Verses 6–7 · Isaiah 58:6–7 the fast I choose — loosen the yoke, break your bread, do not hide from your own flesh

⭐ The fast I choose. “This is the fast I choose” stands in this one verse, answering the question of verse 5, in four lines: “to loosen the bonds of wickedness” stands in this one verse, with chartsubbot, a word found in two verses, here and Psalm 73:4; “to untie the cords of the yoke” stands in this one verse; “to send the crushed away free” stands in this one verse; and “break every yoke”. Luke 4:18 has the line “to set at liberty them that are bruised” inside the reading of Isaiah 61 (Luke 4:18 and Isaiah 61:1, both now on these pages), which is this verse’s “send the crushed free” in the Greek. The KJV has “to loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free”, the NIV “to loose the chains of injustice and untie the cords of the yoke”, the NWT 1984 “To loosen the fetters of wickedness, to release the bands of the yoke bar” and “send away the crushed ones free”, the Douay “let them that are broken go free” and the TLB “you stop oppressing those who work for you and treat them fairly”.

⭐ Bread, a house, and your own flesh. “To break your bread for the hungry” stands in this one verse, and the afflicted and homeless (“the afflicted, the homeless” stands in this one verse) are brought into the house; and the naked are covered. “And from your own flesh do not hide yourself” stands in this one verse: the one in need is flesh of your flesh. The list is the one of Matthew 25:35–36 (now on these pages) and the cry of Isaiah 1:17 (now on these pages). The KJV has “Is it not to deal thy bread to the hungry” and “hide not thyself from thine own flesh”, the NIV “to share your food with the hungry and to provide the poor wanderer with shelter”, the NWT 1984 “the dividing of your bread out to the hungry one”, the TLB “I want you to share your food with the hungry” and the Douay “bring the needy and the harbourless into thy house”.

Verses 8–9 · Isaiah 58:8–9 your light like the dawn — “Here I am” — the pointing finger

⭐ Light, healing, righteousness and glory. “Your light will break out like the dawn” stands in this one verse, the shachar that is the first light; then a healing that springs up, righteousness that walks ahead, and “the glory of Jehovah will gather you up” stands in this one verse, the verb being asaf, to gather. Isaiah 52:12 uses the same verb of him as the rear guard of the people. The KJV has “the glory of the Lord shall be thy reward”, the NIV “the glory of the Lord will be your rear guard”, the NWT 1984 “the very glory of Jehovah would be your rear guard”, the Douay “the glory of the Lord shall gather thee up”, the Geneva “the glory of the Lord shall embrace thee” and the TLB “the glory of the Lord will protect you from behind”. ⚠ Four readings of one verb; this translation keeps “gather you up”, the plain sense of the root. The NIV has “your healing will quickly appear” and the KJV “thine health shall spring forth speedily”.

⭐ “Here I am.” “Then you will call, and Jehovah will answer” stands in this one verse, and what he answers is hineni, “Here I am”, the word of Abraham and of Isaiah’s own answer at Isaiah 6:8 (now on these pages), now in the mouth of God. The KJV has “he shall say, Here I am”, the NIV “he will say: Here am I” and the TLB ““Yes, I am here,” he will quickly reply”.

⭐ The finger and the word. “The sending out of the finger” stands in this one verse, the pointing finger, with the speaking of aven, wickedness or trouble. The KJV has “the putting forth of the finger, and speaking vanity”, the NIV “the pointing finger and malicious talk”, the NWT 1984 “the poking out of the finger and the speaking of what is hurtful”, the Douay “cease to stretch out the finger, and to speak that which profiteth not” and the TLB “stop making false accusations and spreading vicious rumors”.

Verses 10–12 · Isaiah 58:10–12 pour out your soul — light in the darkness — a watered garden — the repairer of the breach

⭐ Pour out your soul. “And you pour out your soul for the hungry” stands in this one verse. In verse 7 it was bread; here it is the self (nefesh) that is given, and the afflicted soul is satisfied in return. The KJV has “if thou draw out thy soul to the hungry”, the NIV “if you spend yourselves in behalf of the hungry”, the Geneva “If thou pour out thy soul to the hungry” and the Douay “When thou shalt pour out thy soul to the hungry”.

⭐ Light in the darkness. “Then your light will rise in the darkness” stands in this one verse, with the gloom as noon; compare the great light of Isaiah 9:1 (now on these pages). The KJV has “then shall thy light rise in obscurity, and thy darkness be as the noon day”, the NIV “then your light will rise in the darkness, and your night will become like the noonday”, the NWT 1984 “your light also will certainly flash up even in the darkness” and the TLB “the darkness around you shall be as bright as day”.

⭐ A watered garden. “And Jehovah will lead you always” stands in this one verse, “in parched places” (tsachtsachot) stands in this one verse; “like a watered garden” stands in two verses, here and Jeremiah 31:12 (now on these pages); and “like a spring of water whose waters do not fail” stands in this one verse, the verb kazav being “to lie”. The NWT 1984 keeps it, “the waters of which do not lie”; the KJV has “whose waters fail not” and the NIV “whose waters never fail”. For the parched places the KJV has “satisfy thy soul in drought”, the NIV “in a sun-scorched land”, the TLB “satisfy you with all good things” and the Douay “will fill thy soul with brightness”.

⭐ The repairer of the breach. “The repairer of the breach” stands in this one verse, goder perets (perets), and “the restorer of paths” stands in this one verse. “And from you they will build the ruins of long ago” stands in this one verse; Isaiah 61:4 has the same words without “from you” (now on these pages). The KJV has “The repairer of the breach, The restorer of paths to dwell in”, the NIV “Repairer of Broken Walls, Restorer of Streets with Dwellings”, the NWT 1984 “the restorer of roadways by which to dwell”, the Douay “the repairer of the fences, turning the paths into rest” and the TLB ““The People Who Rebuild Their Walls and Cities.””.

Verses 13–14 · Isaiah 58:13–14 the sabbath a delight — you will delight yourself in Jehovah

⭐ The sabbath a delight. “If you turn back your foot from the sabbath” stands in this one verse and “and call the sabbath a delight” stands in this one verse, the word being oneg, with the holy day of Jehovah “honored” (kaved). The command is the one of Exodus 20:8 and Exodus 31:13 (both now on these pages). The word of verse 2 comes back twice: “doing your pleasure” and “finding your pleasure” (chefets), the same root that said they delighted in God and that said they found their own gain on the fast day. The KJV has “call the sabbath a delight”, the NIV “if you call the Sabbath a delight”, the NWT 1984 “will actually call the sabbath an exquisite delight”, the Douay “call the sabbath delightful” and the TLB “enjoying the Sabbath”.

⚠ “Speaking a word.” The verse ends with vedaber davar, “and speaking a word”, and the versions fill the gap three ways: the KJV “nor speaking thine own words”, the Geneva “nor speaking a vain word”, the TLB “nor talking idly” and the NWT 1984 “and speaking a word”. This translation keeps the bare “a word” and leaves its meaning to the verse before it.

⭐ Delight yourself in Jehovah. “Then you will delight yourself in Jehovah” stands in this one verse, the verb of Psalm 37:4 (now on these pages). “And I will feed you with the inheritance of Jacob your father” stands in this one verse, and the ride on the high places is the one of Deuteronomy 32:13 (now on these pages); the Hebrew of this verse has a written and read pair here, written bamoti, read bamatei, kept in the Hebrew column (ketiv/qere). The KJV has “I will cause thee to ride upon the high places of the earth”, the NIV “I will cause you to ride in triumph on the heights of the land”, the NWT 1984 “I will make you ride upon the high places of the earth”, the TLB “I will see to it that you ride high” and the Douay “I will lift thee up above the high places of the earth”. For the first line the NIV has “then you will find your joy in the Lord”, the NWT 1984 “you will in that case find your exquisite delight in Jehovah” and the TLB “then the Lord will be your delight”.

⭐ “For the mouth of Jehovah has spoken.” “For the mouth of Jehovah has spoken” stands in three verses, all in Isaiah: Isaiah 1:20 and Isaiah 40:5 (both now on these pages) and this one.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “fast” for tsom; “afflict the soul” for anah nefesh; “delight” and “pleasure” both for chefets, so that the repeated root shows; “yoke” for motah in verses 6 and 9; “Here I am” for hineni; “the repairer of the breach” for goder perets; “delight” for oneg and for the verb hit‘anneg; “Jehovah” for the Name, written seven times in the chapter.

Echoes paid. Leviticus 16:29; Exodus 5:6, 20:8 and 31:13; Isaiah 1:17, 1:20, 6:8, 9:1, 40:5, 61:1 and 61:4; Luke 4:18; Matthew 6:16 and 25:35–36; Jeremiah 31:12; Deuteronomy 32:13; Psalm 37:4.

Echoes planted. Psalm 73:4 (the other chartsubbot) and Isaiah 52:12 (the same verb of the rear guard). Neither is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Isaiah stands on these pages at chapters 1, 6, 7, 9, 11, 14, 26, 40, 41, 43, 53, 54, 55, 58 and 61, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Isaiah 2.
