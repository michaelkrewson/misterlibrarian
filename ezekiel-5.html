The hair: Ezekiel shaves with a sword, divides the hair into thirds to be burned, struck and scattered, ties a few in his garment and throws some of those into the fire. Then the judgment is spoken: Jerusalem, set in the middle of the nations, has done worse than they; fathers will eat sons and sons fathers; and famine, beasts, pestilence and sword are sent.
Follows chapter 4 in the early years of the exile of King Jehoiachin. The chapter gives no date.

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–4 · Ezekiel 5:1–4 a sword for a razor — the hair weighed into thirds — a few tied in the skirts

⭐ A priest shaves. He is to take a sword and use it as a barber’s razor on his head and his beard; “a barber’s razor” (ta’ar haggallavim) stands in this one verse (ta’ar). For a priest this is the thing the law forbids: priests are not to make their heads bald or shave off the corners of their beard (Leviticus 21:5, now on these pages), a rule given to all Israel at Leviticus 19:27, and a Nazirite’s head is not to be touched by a razor (Numbers 6:5, both now on these pages). Isaiah had already pictured Jehovah shaving with a razor hired beyond the River, the king of Assyria (Isaiah 7:20, now on these pages). The KJV has “take thee a sharp knife”, the ASV “take thee a sharp sword”, the Douay “take thee a sharp knife that shaveth the hair” and the NWT 2013 “take a sharp sword for yourself”.

⭐ Three thirds. One third is burned in the middle of the city, one struck with the sword around it, and one scattered to the wind, with a sword drawn after it. “I will draw [the sword] after them” stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: verses 2 and 12 here, and Ezekiel 12:14. The same verb is in the covenant warning of Leviticus 26:33 (now on these pages), where Jehovah says he will scatter them among the nations and draw out the sword after them.

⚠ A few in the skirts. A small number are tied in the fold of his garment, the kanaf (kanaf). “In your skirts” stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: here and Jeremiah 2:34 (now on these pages). It is not a promise that all of them are safe: verse 4 takes some of these as well and throws them into the fire, and from it a fire goes out to all the house of Israel. The KJV has “bind them in thy skirts”, the NIV “tuck them away in the folds of your garment” and the NWT 2013 “wrap them up in the folds of your garment”.

Verses 5–7 · Ezekiel 5:5–7 Jerusalem in the middle of the nations — more turbulent than they — the “not”

⭐ This is Jerusalem. The sign is explained at once: the brick, the hair, the city are one. “I have set her in the middle of the nations” stands in this one verse. Her place among them is the measure of what follows, because verse 6 says she has done wickedness more than the nations around her. The KJV has “I have set it in the midst of the nations” and the NIV “which I have set in the center of the nations”.

⭐ Turbulent. The word of verse 7 (hamonkhem) stands in this one verse, and the shelf splits on it: the KJV has “ye multiplied more than the nations”, the ASV “ye are turbulent more than the nations”, the NIV “You have been more unruly than the nations”, the Douay “you have surpassed the Gentiles” and the NWT 2013 “more troublesome than the nations”.

⚠ The “not”. The Hebrew ends verse 7 with lo asitem, ‘you have not done’, said of the judgments of the nations around you: they did not do Jehovah’s and did not do the others either. Verse 7’s sentence returns at Ezekiel 11:12 without the ‘not’: there they have done according to the judgments of the nations. The shelf follows the Hebrew of this verse in part: the KJV has “neither have done according to the judgments of the nations that are round about you” and the NIV “You have not even conformed to the standards of the nations around you”, while the NWT 2013 has “you followed the judicial decisions of the nations all around you”. This translation keeps what the verse here says (mishpat).

Verses 8–10 · Ezekiel 5:8–10 “I am against you” — judgments in your midst — fathers and sons

⭐ “Look, I am against you.” The same words are said later of Tyre, of Sidon and of Pharaoh (Ezekiel 26:3, 28:22 and 29:3), and of Gog with a different preposition (38:3). Here they are said to Jerusalem.

⭐ Done to them. Verse 7 says they did not do (asah) his judgments (mishpatim); verse 8 says he will do (asah) judgments in their midst. The same two words turn from the people’s duty to Jehovah’s act. The KJV has “will execute judgments in the midst of thee” and the NIV “I will inflict punishment on you”. Verses 10 and 15 use the shorter noun shefatim, ‘acts of judgment’.

⚠ Fathers and sons. The curse of the siege is the one Leviticus 26:29 and Deuteronomy 28:53 (both now on these pages) had named, parents eating their children; Jeremiah says it too (Jeremiah 19:9, now on these pages), and Lamentations reports it (4:10, now on these pages). The second half here is new: “sons will eat their fathers” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “the fathers shall eat the sons in the midst of thee, and the sons shall eat their fathers”.

Verses 11–13 · Ezekiel 5:11–13 the oath — “my eye will not spare” — the thirds explained — appeased

⭐ “As I live.” An oath, with the reason given: the sanctuary defiled with detestable things and abominations (shiqquts, toevah). “My eye will not spare” stands in five verses of the Hebrew Bible, all in Ezekiel: 5:11, 7:4 and 7:9 and 8:18 (now on these pages), and 9:10. Deuteronomy gives the same refusal as a judge’s rule: your eye shall not spare (Deuteronomy 19:21, now on these pages).

⚠ Diminish, or shave. The verb is gara, ‘to diminish, to cut down’: the KJV has “will I also diminish thee”, the Douay “I will also break thee in pieces”, the Geneva “will I also destroy thee” and the NIV “I myself will shave you”. The NIV hears the razor of verse 1; the same root is used of a beard cut off at Isaiah 15:2.

⭐ The thirds, said. The hair is now explained: a third by pestilence and famine, a third by the sword, a third scattered with the sword after it. Then the anger is spent, and a verb in the middle of verse 13 has been read three ways: the KJV has “I will be comforted”, the NIV “I will be avenged” and the NWT 2013 “I will be satisfied”. Where this translation has ‘zeal’, the NWT 2013 has “insistence on exclusive devotion” (qanna). “I, Jehovah, have spoken” stands in fourteen verses of Ezekiel, three of them here: 13, 15 and 17.

Verses 14–17 · Ezekiel 5:14–17 a ruin and a reproach — arrows of famine — the staff of bread — famine, beasts, pestilence, sword

⭐ A reproach. “A ruin and a reproach” stands in this one verse. Verse 15 shifts from ‘you’ to ‘it’ (‘it will be a reproach’): the KJV keeps it, “So it shall be a reproach and a taunt”, where the NIV has “You will be a reproach and a taunt”. This translation keeps the third person.

⭐ Arrows of famine. “The arrows of famine” stands in this one verse (chets). The picture is the Song of Moses’, where Jehovah spends his arrows and they are wasted by hunger (Deuteronomy 32:23–24, now on these pages). The staff of bread is the one broken in 4:16 and in Leviticus 26:26 (both now on these pages). The KJV has “the evil arrows of famine” and the NIV “my deadly and destructive arrows of famine”.

⭐ Four things. Famine, evil beasts that bereave, pestilence and blood, and the sword: the beasts that rob of children are the ones of Leviticus 26:22 (now on these pages). ⚠ The person changes as the chapter ends, from ‘them’ to a plural ‘you’ and then to a singular ‘you’; this translation keeps them, and the Spanish shows the difference.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact / distinct words: “sword” for the one noun at verses 1, 2, 12 and 17; “judgments” (mishpatim) at verses 6, 7 and 8 and “acts of judgment” (shefatim) at verses 10 and 15; “fury” for chemah at verses 13 and 15; and “abominations” at verses 9 and 11.

Echoes paid. The siege and the staff of bread of chapter 4; and the covenant warnings of Leviticus 26: the beasts, the flesh of the children, the scattering and the sword drawn after.

Echoes planted. The sword after them again at 12:14 and the judgments of the nations at 11:12; and “my eye will not spare” at 7:4 and 7:9 and 8:18 (now on these pages), and at 9:10.

Ezekiel 6, now on these pages: the prophet sets his face against the mountains of Israel and the high places where altars stand.
