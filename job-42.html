Job answers Jehovah: I know you can do all things; I spoke what I did not understand; I had heard of you, and now my eye has seen you; therefore I reject, and I repent, upon dust and ashes. Jehovah tells Eliphaz that he and his two friends have not spoken to him what is right, as his servant Job has, and sends them with seven bulls and seven rams for Job to pray over; Jehovah turns the captivity of Job and adds to all he had, to double; his family comes and eats with him; and Job has seven sons and three named daughters, an inheritance given to the daughters among their brothers, a hundred and forty years more, four generations, and dies old and full of days.
Undated. The book gives no date and names no king; the epilogue is the last chapter of the book and the only place it speaks of what happened after the suffering. ⚠ Job speaks for the last time in verses 1–6; the rest is told about him.

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–4 · Job 42:1–4 “I know that you can do all things” — Job quotes what he heard, and changes it

⭐ A written word and a read word. The first word of Job’s answer is a ketiv/qere pair: the consonants written in the text say yada’ta, “you know”, and the reading tradition says yada’ti, “I know”. The Hebrew column keeps both as printed, and each of the seven English versions on the shelf follows the reading. The other two pairs in this chapter are in verses 10 and 16. Then the confession itself: “and no purpose is beyond you” stands in this one verse, and the verb is the one of the tower: “yibbatser mi-, is beyond, is cut off from” with this preposition stands in two verses, here and Genesis 11:6 (now on these pages), where “nothing they scheme to do will be beyond them”. The word for a purpose is mezimmah, from the root of the scheming there. What was true of Babel’s plan, that nothing was beyond it, Job says of God’s. The KJV has “no thought can be withholden from thee”, the ASV “no purpose of thine can be restrained”, the NIV “no purpose of yours can be thwarted”, the NWT 1984 “no idea that is unattainable for you”, the Douay “no thought is hid from thee” and the TLB “you can do anything”, with no purpose in the second half.

⭐ Job quotes God, and changes the verb. “Who is this that hides counsel” stands in this one verse. At the storm God said machshikh etsah bemillin bli da’at, “darkens counsel with words without knowledge” (Job 38:2, now on these pages). Job’s version has ma’lim, “hides”, in place of “darkens”, and leaves out “with words”; the rest, bli da’at, “without knowledge”, is the same. The NIV and the NWT 1984 print “obscures” and “obscuring” here, the same verb they use at 38:2, so their readers do not see the change. The KJV has “Who is he that hideth counsel without knowledge?”, the NIV “You asked, ‘Who is this that obscures my plans without knowledge?’”, the NWT 1984 “Who is this that is obscuring counsel without knowledge?”, the Douay “Who is this that hideth counsel without knowledge?” and the TLB “You ask who it is who has so foolishly denied your providence. It is I.”

⭐ “Therefore I declared”. “I declared, and I did not understand” stands in this one verse, and “things too wonderful for me” stands in two verses, here and Psalm 131:1. The KJV has “therefore have I uttered that I understood not”, the NIV “Surely I spoke of things I did not understand”, the NWT 1984 “Therefore I talked, but I was not understanding” and the Douay “Therefore I have spoken unwisely”.

⚠ “Hear, please, and I will speak.” “I will question you, and you make me know” stands in three verses, Job 38:3 (now on these pages), Job 40:7 and here, and in the first two it is God who says it. But “Hear, please, and I will speak” stands in this one verse: nothing in the speech from the storm has those words, so Job is either quoting what he took God to mean or adding to it, and the text does not say which. The NIV has “You said, ‘Listen now, and I will speak;” and the TLB “You said, ‘Listen and I will speak!”, which print it as a saying of God; the KJV has “Hear, I beseech thee, and I will speak: I will demand of thee, and declare thou unto me.”, with no “you said”; the Douay has “Hear, and I will speak: I will ask thee, and do thou tell me.”

Verses 5–6 · Job 42:5–6 “Now my eye has seen you” — and the last words Job speaks

⭐ Heard, and seen. “By the hearing of the ear I had heard you” stands in this one verse; the first three words, “by the hearing of the ear”, stand in two verses, here and Psalm 18:45 (now on these pages). The verb has a direct object, shema’tikha, “I heard you”, and the second half is “my eye has seen you”, which stands in this one verse. The Hebrew does not say what Job saw. It says only that the organ has changed, from the ear to the eye, and that Jehovah had said that no man can see him and live (Exodus 33:20, now on these pages). The KJV has “I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee.”, the ASV “I had heard of thee by the hearing of the ear”, the NIV “My ears had heard of you but now my eyes have seen you.”, the NWT 1984 “In hearsay I have heard about you, But now my own eye does see you.”, the Douay “With the hearing of the ear, I have heard thee, but now my eye seeth thee.” and the TLB “I had heard about you before, but now I have seen you”. Of these, only the Douay keeps “heard thee”.

⚠ The verb with no object. “Therefore I reject, and I repent”, in these words, stands in this one verse, and the first verb, em’as (ma’as), has nothing after it: no “myself”, no “my words”. In Job 9:21 the same verb takes an object, “my life”. The second, nichamti, is the niphal of nacham, which can mean to be sorry, to relent or to be comforted; it is the verb of Genesis 6:6, where Jehovah “regretted” (now on these pages), and in verse 11 below it comes back in the friends’ comforting. Then al afar va’efer, “dust and ashes”, which stands in three verses: Genesis 18:27, where Abraham says “I am dust and ashes” (now on these pages), Job 30:19 and this one; and the preposition is al, “upon”, not “in”. Six of the seven English versions supply an object. The KJV has “Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes.”, the ASV “Wherefore I abhor myself”, the Geneva “Therefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes.”, the NIV “Therefore I despise myself and repent in dust and ashes.”, the TLB “and I loathe myself and repent in dust and ashes.” and the Douay “Therefore I reprehend myself, and do penance in dust and ashes.”; the NWT 1984 has “That is why I make a retraction, And I do repent in dust and ashes.”, with no “myself”. These are the last words Job speaks in the book.

Verses 7–9 · Job 42:7–9 “You have not spoken to me what is right, as my servant Job has” — seven bulls and seven rams

⭐ Jehovah speaks to Eliphaz. “And Jehovah said to Eliphaz” stands in this one verse, and “my anger is hot against you” stands in this one verse; the “you” is singular, and the other two are “your two friends”. Bildad and Zophar are named only in verse 9. Elihu, who speaks in Job 32–37, is not named in this chapter.

⭐ To me, not about me. “Spoken to me what is right” stands in two verses, this verse and the next, both here. The word is elai, “to me”; the shelf nearly all prints “of me” or “about me”. The KJV has “ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right, as my servant Job hath”, the NIV “you have not spoken the truth about me, as my servant Job has”, the NWT 1984 “have not spoken concerning me what is truthful as has my servant Job”, the Geneva “ye have not spoken of me the thing that is right”, the TLB “you have not been right in what you have said about me” and the Douay “you have not spoken the thing that is right before me”, the nearest. The friends spoke about God to Job; Job, whatever else he did, spoke to God. ⚠ The verse gives no reason, and it does not sort Job’s sayings, including the retraction of verse 6, into right and wrong. “My servant Job” stands in four verses: Job 1:8 (now on these pages), 2:3 (not yet), and here twice. Jehovah’s first words about Job, and his last, use the same two words.

⭐ Seven bulls and seven rams. “Seven bulls and seven rams”, in this spelling, stands in three verses: Numbers 23:1, where Balaam asks for them at seven altars (now on these pages; at 23:29 the second word is spelled differently), 1 Chronicles 15:26 and here. The offering is an olah, a burnt offering, the kind Job himself offered for his children (Job 1:5, now on these pages). The KJV has “a burnt offering”, the NWT 1984 “a burnt sacrifice” and the Douay “a holocaust”. And the offenders must go to the man they wronged: “and Job my servant will pray for you” is in this one verse, and the pattern is older: Abimelech is told that Abraham “will pray for you, and you shall live” (Genesis 20:7, now on these pages).

⭐ “His face only will I lift up.” “his face I will lift up” stands in this one verse, the idiom for accepting someone (panim, nasa), and verse 9 says it was done: “and Jehovah lifted up Job’s face” stands in this one verse. The KJV has “for him will I accept”, the NIV “I will accept his prayer”, the NWT 1984 “His face only I shall accept” and the Douay “his face I will accept”; at verse 9 the KJV has “the Lord also accepted Job”, the NIV “the Lord accepted Job’s prayer”, the TLB “accepted Job’s prayer on their behalf” and the Douay “accepted the face of Job”. The reason given for sparing them is “so as not to do folly with you”, which stands in this one verse, with nevalah: the KJV has “lest I deal with you after your folly”, the NIV “not deal with you according to your folly”, the NWT 1984 “so as not to commit disgraceful folly with YOU”, the Douay “that folly be not imputed to you”, the Geneva “lest I should put you to shame” and the TLB “won’t destroy you as I should because of your sin”.

Verses 10–11 · Job 42:10–11 Jehovah turned the captivity of Job — and the family comes to dinner

⭐ Turned, and added. The first clause is the idiom for a restoration, shuv shevut, “turn the captivity”, as in Deuteronomy 30:3 (now on these pages), and here it is a ketiv/qere pair, written shevit and read shevut. It happens “when he prayed for”, in this form, which stands in this one verse; and the object is re’ehu, “his friend”, in the singular, where all seven English versions on the shelf print the plural, “friends” or “companions”. ⚠ The form can be a collective, or a plural written short; this translation prints it as it stands. “And Jehovah added to all that Job had” stands in this one verse: the verb is vayosef, “added”, not “gave back”, and the sum is lemishneh, “to double”. The KJV has “turned the captivity of Job, when he prayed for his friends”, the NIV “restored his fortunes and gave him twice as much as he had before”, the NWT 1984 “began to give in addition all that had been Job’s, in double amount”, the TLB “restored his wealth and happiness! In fact, the Lord gave him twice as much as before!” and the Douay “The Lord also was turned at the penance of Job”.

⭐ They grieved with him, and comforted. “And they grieved with him”, vayanudu lo, stands in this one verse, with “and comforted him”, which stands in this one verse. The pair is the one the three friends came with: “to grieve with him” stands in this one verse, Job 2:11. The KJV has “they bemoaned him, and comforted him over all the evil that the Lord had brought upon him”, the NWT 1984 “to sympathize with him and to comfort him”, the Douay “bemoaned him, and comforted him upon all the evil that God had brought upon him”, and the TLB “all the trials the Lord had brought upon him”. “Over all the evil that Jehovah had brought upon him” stands in this one verse: it is the narrator who says it here.

⭐ A kesitah and a ring. Each guest gives a kesitah, which stands in three verses: Genesis 33:19 and Joshua 24:32 (now on these pages) and here (kesitah); the KJV has “a piece of money”, the NIV “a piece of silver” and the Douay “one ewe”. And “a ring of gold” stands in four verses: Genesis 24:22 (now on these pages, where it is a nose-ring), here and Proverbs 11:22 and 25:12. Nezem is a nose ring or an earring; the KJV has “an earring of gold”, the ASV “a ring of gold”, the NIV “a gold ring” and the Douay “one earring of gold”.

Verses 12–15 · Job 42:12–15 Jehovah blessed the latter end of Job — twice as many sheep, the same seven sons, and three named daughters

⭐ The latter end. “blessed the latter end” stands in this one verse, with acharit, the end that comes after; and the verb is barakh, the one Job used when everything was taken: “blessed be the name of Jehovah” (Job 1:21, now on these pages). The KJV has “So the Lord blessed the latter end of Job more than his beginning”, the NIV “The Lord blessed the latter part of Job’s life more than the former part”, the NWT 1984 “he blessed the end of Job afterward more than his beginning”, the Geneva “the last days of Job more than the first” and the TLB “So the Lord blessed Job at the end of his life more than at the beginning”.

⭐ Four numbers, doubled. “fourteen thousand sheep” stands in this one verse, and “a thousand she-donkeys” stands in this one verse, and the list sets each number from Job 1:3 (now on these pages) beside its double: 7,000 sheep and 14,000, 3,000 camels and 6,000, 500 yoke of oxen and 1,000, 500 she-donkeys and 1,000. “A thousand yoke” stands in this one verse; a yoke is a pair. The KJV has “a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she asses”, the NIV “a thousand yoke of oxen and a thousand donkeys”, the NWT 1984 “a thousand spans of cattle and a thousand she-asses” and the TLB “1,000 teams of oxen, and 1,000 female donkeys”.

⭐ Seven sons and three daughters, again. “and three daughters” stands in two verses, the first count of the book (Job 1:2, now on these pages) and the last; and the seven is spelled differently: “seven sons”, shiv’anah banim, stands in this one verse, where 1:2 has shiv’ah. Everything else was doubled and the children were not; Job 1 says it already. The TLB prints “seven more sons and three more daughters”, with “more”, and runs verses 12–14 together under verse 12.

⚠ Three names, and no sons’ names. “And he called the name of the first” stands in this one verse, and the three are Yemimah, Qetsi’ah and Qeren-happukh. The second, qetsi’ah, stands in this one verse, and in the plural, qetsi’ot, it is a spice in Psalm 45:9: the KJV has “cassia” there (dictionary). “Keren-happuch” stands in this one verse: keren is a horn, and puk is eye-paint, kohl: “in kohl” stands in three verses, 2 Kings 9:30 (now on these pages), Jeremiah 4:30 and Isaiah 54:11, where Jehovah lays the stones “in kohl” (now on these pages) (puk). The meaning of Jemimah is not given in the text. The KJV has “Jemima”, “Kezia” and “Kerenhappuch”; the NIV “Jemimah”, “Keziah” and “Keren-Happuch”; the Douay translates the names, “one Dies, and the name of the second Cassia”. The book never names one of the seven sons, in 1:2 or here.

⭐ An inheritance among their brothers. “And their father gave them an inheritance” stands in this one verse; the daughters of Zelophehad had to ask Moses for a holding because they had no brother (Numbers 27:7, “among their father’s brothers”, now on these pages; nachalah), and Job’s daughters have seven. The KJV has “their father gave them inheritance among their brethren”, the NIV “their father granted them an inheritance along with their brothers”, the NWT 1984 “their father proceeded to give them an inheritance in among their brothers” and the TLB “their father put them into his will along with their brothers”. For the beauty, the KJV has “no women found so fair as the daughters of Job”, the NWT 1984 “no women were found as pretty as Job’s daughters”, the TLB “no other girls as lovely as the daughters of Job” and the Douay “women so beautiful as the daughters of Job”.

Verses 16–17 · Job 42:16–17 A hundred and forty years — old and full of days

⭐ Four generations. “And Job lived after this” stands in this one verse, and “four generations”, spelled dorot without the vav, stands in this one verse. The verb “saw” is the third ketiv/qere pair of the chapter, written vayar and read vayire. The KJV has “saw his sons, and his sons’ sons, even four generations”, the NIV “he saw his children and their children to the fourth generation”, the NWT 1984 “came to see his sons and his grandsons—four generations”, the Douay “he saw his children, and his children’s children, unto the fourth generation” and the TLB “living to see his grandchildren and great-grandchildren too”.

⭐ Old and full of days. “Old and full of days” stands in three verses: Genesis 35:29, where Isaac dies (now on these pages), 1 Chronicles 23:1, of David, and here; Abraham has the same two words without “days” (Genesis 25:8, “an old man and full of years” in this translation, now on these pages). The last word of the Hebrew book is yamim, “days”. The KJV has “So Job died, being old and full of days”, the NIV “an old man and full of years”, the NWT 1984 “old and satisfied with days”, the Douay “he died an old man, and full of days” and the TLB “an old, old man, after living a long, good life”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “purpose” for mezimmah at verse 2; “beyond you” for yibbatser mimkha, the verb of Genesis 11:6; “to me” for elai at verses 7 and 8; “lift up his face” for nasa panim; “burnt offering” for olah; “folly” for nevalah; “the captivity” for shevut; “to double” for lemishneh; “latter end” for acharit; “kesitah” left as it is; “Jehovah” for the Name, which stands nine times in this chapter: verses 1, 7 (twice), 9 (twice), 10 (twice), 11 and 12. The Hebrew has three written and read pairs in this chapter, at verses 2, 10 and 16, kept as it is printed.

Echoes paid. Job 38:2 and 38:3; Job 1:2, 1:3, 1:5, 1:8 and 1:21; Genesis 11:6, 6:6, 18:27, 20:7, 24:22, 25:8, 33:19 and 35:29; Numbers 23:1 and 27:7; Joshua 24:32; Deuteronomy 30:3; Exodus 33:20; 2 Kings 9:30; Isaiah 54:11.

Echoes planted. Job 2:3, 2:11, 9:21, 30:19, 40:7 and the speeches of Elihu (Job 32–37); Psalm 18:45 (now on these pages), 45:9 and 131:1; 1 Chronicles 15:26 and 23:1; Jeremiah 4:30; Proverbs 11:22 and 25:12. None of these, except Psalm 18:45, is translated here yet.

⚠ A note on order: Job stands on these pages at chapters 1, 38 and 42, chosen by how often a passage is read rather than in sequence. The sequential thread is unchanged: next in sequence is Job 2.
