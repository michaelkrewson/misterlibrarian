Jehovah appears to Solomon a second time, as at Gibeon, and answers the prayer: he has heard it and hallowed the house, and the throne stands if Solomon walks as David walked; if Israel turns away, the house will be a warning and every passer-by will ask why. Then twenty years end: Solomon gives Hiram of Tyre twenty cities in Galilee, which Hiram calls Cabul; the forced labor builds the house, the Millo, the wall of Jerusalem, Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer, which Pharaoh had burned and given to his daughter; the nations left in the land become servants of forced labor, but no Israelite is made a slave; Pharaoh’s daughter goes up to her own house; Solomon offers three times a year; and a fleet at Ezion-geber brings four hundred and twenty talents of gold from Ophir.
At the end of twenty years of building: seven for the house of Jehovah (1 Kings 6:38) and thirteen for the king’s house (1 Kings 7:1).

1
וַיְהִי כְּכַלּוֹת שְׁלֹמֹה, לִבְנוֹת אֶת-בֵּית-יְהוָה וְאֶת-בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ, וְאֵת כָּל-חֵשֶׁק שְׁלֹמֹה, אֲשֶׁר חָפֵץ לַעֲשׂוֹת.
And when Solomon had finished building the house of Jehovah and the king’s house, and all Solomon’s desire that he was pleased to do,note
Y cuando Salomón terminó de edificar la casa de Jehová y la casa del rey, y todo el deseo de Salomón que se complació en hacer,
2
וַיֵּרָא יְהוָה אֶל-שְׁלֹמֹה, שֵׁנִית, כַּאֲשֶׁר נִרְאָה אֵלָיו, בְּגִבְעוֹן.
Jehovah appeared to Solomon a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon.note
Jehová se apareció a Salomón por segunda vez, como se le había aparecido en Gabaón.
3
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֵלָיו, שָׁמַעְתִּי אֶת-תְּפִלָּתְךָ וְאֶת-תְּחִנָּתְךָ אֲשֶׁר הִתְחַנַּנְתָּה לְפָנַי--הִקְדַּשְׁתִּי אֶת-הַבַּיִת הַזֶּה אֲשֶׁר בָּנִתָה, לָשׂוּם-שְׁמִי שָׁם עַד-עוֹלָם; וְהָיוּ עֵינַי וְלִבִּי שָׁם, כָּל-הַיָּמִים.
And Jehovah said to him: “I have heard your prayer and your supplication that you made before me. I have hallowed this house that you have built, to put my name there forever; and my eyes and my heart will be there all the days.note
Y Jehová le dijo: «He oído tu oración y tu súplica que hiciste delante de mí. He santificado esta casa que has edificado, para poner allí mi nombre para siempre; y mis ojos y mi corazón estarán allí todos los días.
4
וְאַתָּה אִם-תֵּלֵךְ לְפָנַי, כַּאֲשֶׁר הָלַךְ דָּוִד אָבִיךָ בְּתָם-לֵבָב וּבְיֹשֶׁר, לַעֲשׂוֹת, כְּכֹל אֲשֶׁר צִוִּיתִיךָ--חֻקַּי וּמִשְׁפָּטַי, תִּשְׁמֹר.
And as for you, if you walk before me as David your father walked, in integrity of heart and in uprightness, doing according to all that I have commanded you, and you keep my statutes and my judgments,note
Y en cuanto a ti, si andas delante de mí como anduvo David tu padre, con integridad de corazón y con rectitud, haciendo conforme a todo lo que te he mandado, y guardas mis estatutos y mis juicios,
5
וַהֲקִמֹתִי אֶת-כִּסֵּא מַמְלַכְתְּךָ, עַל-יִשְׂרָאֵל--לְעֹלָם:  כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבַּרְתִּי, עַל-דָּוִד אָבִיךָ לֵאמֹר, לֹא-יִכָּרֵת לְךָ אִישׁ, מֵעַל כִּסֵּא יִשְׂרָאֵל.
then I will establish the throne of your kingdom over Israel forever, as I spoke concerning David your father, saying: ‘There shall not be cut off for you a man from the throne of Israel.’note
entonces yo estableceré el trono de tu reino sobre Israel para siempre, como hablé acerca de David tu padre, diciendo: “No te será cortado un varón del trono de Israel”.
6
אִם-שׁוֹב תְּשֻׁבוּן אַתֶּם וּבְנֵיכֶם, מֵאַחֲרַי, וְלֹא תִשְׁמְרוּ מִצְוֺתַי חֻקֹּתַי, אֲשֶׁר נָתַתִּי לִפְנֵיכֶם; וַהֲלַכְתֶּם, וַעֲבַדְתֶּם אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים, וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוִיתֶם, לָהֶם.
If you turn away from following me — you and your sons — and do not keep my commandments and my statutes that I have set before you, and you go and serve other gods and bow down to them,note
Si ustedes se apartan de seguirme — ustedes y sus hijos — y no guardan mis mandamientos y mis estatutos que he puesto delante de ustedes, y van y sirven a otros dioses y se postran ante ellos,
7
וְהִכְרַתִּי אֶת-יִשְׂרָאֵל, מֵעַל פְּנֵי הָאֲדָמָה אֲשֶׁר נָתַתִּי לָהֶם, וְאֶת-הַבַּיִת אֲשֶׁר הִקְדַּשְׁתִּי לִשְׁמִי, אֲשַׁלַּח מֵעַל פָּנָי; וְהָיָה יִשְׂרָאֵל לְמָשָׁל וְלִשְׁנִינָה, בְּכָל-הָעַמִּים.
then I will cut off Israel from upon the face of the ground that I have given them; and the house that I have hallowed for my name I will send away from my face; and Israel will become a byword and a taunt among all the peoples.note
entonces cortaré a Israel de sobre la faz del suelo que les he dado; y la casa que he santificado a mi nombre la echaré de delante de mí; e Israel será refrán y burla entre todos los pueblos.
8
וְהַבַּיִת הַזֶּה יִהְיֶה עֶלְיוֹן, כָּל-עֹבֵר עָלָיו יִשֹּׁם וְשָׁרָק; וְאָמְרוּ, עַל-מֶה עָשָׂה יְהוָה כָּכָה, לָאָרֶץ הַזֹּאת, וְלַבַּיִת הַזֶּה.
And this house will be high — everyone passing by it will be appalled and will whistle; and they will say: ‘Why has Jehovah done this to this land and to this house?’note
Y esta casa será alta — todo el que pase junto a ella quedará asombrado y silbará; y dirán: “¿Por qué hizo Jehová esto a esta tierra y a esta casa?”.
9
וְאָמְרוּ, עַל אֲשֶׁר עָזְבוּ אֶת-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיהֶם אֲשֶׁר הוֹצִיא אֶת-אֲבֹתָם מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם, וַיַּחֲזִקוּ בֵּאלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים, וישתחו וַיִּשְׁתַּחֲווּ לָהֶם וַיַּעַבְדֻם; עַל-כֵּן, הֵבִיא יְהוָה עֲלֵיהֶם, אֵת כָּל-הָרָעָה, הַזֹּאת.
And they will say: ‘Because they forsook Jehovah their God, who brought their fathers out of the land of Egypt, and laid hold of other gods and bowed down to them and served them; therefore Jehovah has brought upon them all this evil.’”note
Y dirán: “Porque abandonaron a Jehová su Dios, que sacó a sus padres de la tierra de Egipto, y se aferraron a otros dioses y se postraron ante ellos y les sirvieron; por eso Jehová ha traído sobre ellos todo este mal”».
10
וַיְהִי, מִקְצֵה עֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה, אֲשֶׁר-בָּנָה שְׁלֹמֹה, אֶת-שְׁנֵי הַבָּתִּים--אֶת-בֵּית יְהוָה, וְאֶת-בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ.
And at the end of twenty years in which Solomon had built the two houses, the house of Jehovah and the king’s house —note
Y al cabo de veinte años en que Salomón había edificado las dos casas, la casa de Jehová y la casa del rey —
11
חִירָם מֶלֶךְ-צֹר נִשָּׂא אֶת-שְׁלֹמֹה בַּעֲצֵי אֲרָזִים וּבַעֲצֵי בְרוֹשִׁים, וּבַזָּהָב--לְכָל-חֶפְצוֹ; אָז יִתֵּן הַמֶּלֶךְ שְׁלֹמֹה לְחִירָם, עֶשְׂרִים עִיר, בְּאֶרֶץ, הַגָּלִיל.
Hiram king of Tyre had supplied Solomon with cedar trees and cypress trees and with gold, according to all his desire — then King Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities in the land of Galilee.note
Hiram rey de Tiro había abastecido a Salomón de maderas de cedro y de ciprés y de oro, conforme a todo su deseo — entonces el rey Salomón dio a Hiram veinte ciudades en la tierra de Galilea.
12
וַיֵּצֵא חִירָם, מִצֹּר, לִרְאוֹת אֶת-הֶעָרִים, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן-לוֹ שְׁלֹמֹה; וְלֹא יָשְׁרוּ, בְּעֵינָיו.
And Hiram went out from Tyre to see the cities that Solomon had given him; and they were not right in his eyes.note
Y salió Hiram de Tiro para ver las ciudades que Salomón le había dado; y no le parecieron bien a sus ojos.
13
וַיֹּאמֶר--מָה הֶעָרִים הָאֵלֶּה, אֲשֶׁר-נָתַתָּה לִּי אָחִי; וַיִּקְרָא לָהֶם אֶרֶץ כָּבוּל, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
And he said: “What are these cities that you have given me, my brother?” And he called them the land of Cabul, to this day.note
Y dijo: «¿Qué son estas ciudades que me has dado, hermano mío?». Y las llamó tierra de Cabul, hasta hoy.
14
וַיִּשְׁלַח חִירָם, לַמֶּלֶךְ, מֵאָה וְעֶשְׂרִים, כִּכַּר זָהָב.
And Hiram sent to the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold.note
Y Hiram envió al rey ciento veinte talentos de oro.
15
וְזֶה דְבַר-הַמַּס אֲשֶׁר-הֶעֱלָה הַמֶּלֶךְ שְׁלֹמֹה, לִבְנוֹת אֶת-בֵּית יְהוָה וְאֶת-בֵּיתוֹ וְאֶת-הַמִּלּוֹא, וְאֵת, חוֹמַת יְרוּשָׁלִָם; וְאֶת-חָצֹר וְאֶת-מְגִדּוֹ, וְאֶת-גָּזֶר.
And this is the account of the forced labor that King Solomon raised to build the house of Jehovah and his own house, and the Millo and the wall of Jerusalem, and Hazor and Megiddo and Gezer.note
Y esta es la relación del trabajo forzado que levantó el rey Salomón para edificar la casa de Jehová y su propia casa, y el Milo y el muro de Jerusalén, y Hazor y Meguido y Guézer.
16
פַּרְעֹה מֶלֶךְ-מִצְרַיִם עָלָה, וַיִּלְכֹּד אֶת-גֶּזֶר וַיִּשְׂרְפָהּ בָּאֵשׁ, וְאֶת-הַכְּנַעֲנִי הַיֹּשֵׁב בָּעִיר, הָרָג; וַיִּתְּנָהּ, שִׁלֻּחִים, לְבִתּוֹ, אֵשֶׁת שְׁלֹמֹה.
Pharaoh king of Egypt had gone up and captured Gezer and burned it with fire, and the Canaanites who lived in the city he killed; and he gave it as a parting gift to his daughter, Solomon’s wife.note
El faraón, rey de Egipto, había subido y tomado Guézer y la había quemado con fuego, y a los cananeos que habitaban en la ciudad los mató; y la dio como regalo de despedida a su hija, la mujer de Salomón.
17
וַיִּבֶן שְׁלֹמֹה אֶת-גָּזֶר, וְאֶת-בֵּית חֹרֹן תַּחְתּוֹן.
And Solomon built Gezer and Lower Beth-horon,note
Y Salomón edificó Guézer y Bet-horón la de abajo,
18
וְאֶת-בַּעֲלָת וְאֶת-תמר תַּדְמֹר בַּמִּדְבָּר, בָּאָרֶץ.
and Baalath and Tadmor in the wilderness, in the land,note
y Baalat y Tadmor en el desierto, en la tierra,
19
וְאֵת כָּל-עָרֵי הַמִּסְכְּנוֹת, אֲשֶׁר הָיוּ לִשְׁלֹמֹה, וְאֵת עָרֵי הָרֶכֶב, וְאֵת עָרֵי הַפָּרָשִׁים; וְאֵת חֵשֶׁק שְׁלֹמֹה, אֲשֶׁר חָשַׁק לִבְנוֹת בִּירוּשָׁלִַם וּבַלְּבָנוֹן, וּבְכֹל, אֶרֶץ מֶמְשַׁלְתּוֹ.
and all the store cities that Solomon had, and the cities for the chariots and the cities for the horsemen, and what Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem and in Lebanon and in all the land of his dominion.note
y todas las ciudades de almacenaje que tenía Salomón, y las ciudades para los carros y las ciudades para los jinetes, y lo que Salomón deseó edificar en Jerusalén y en el Líbano y en toda la tierra de su dominio.
20
כָּל-הָעָם הַנּוֹתָר מִן-הָאֱמֹרִי הַחִתִּי הַפְּרִזִּי, הַחִוִּי וְהַיְבוּסִי, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-מִבְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, הֵמָּה.
All the people who were left of the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites, who were not of the children of Israel —note
Todo el pueblo que quedaba de los amorreos, los hititas, los ferezeos, los heveos y los jebuseos, que no eran de los hijos de Israel —
21
בְּנֵיהֶם, אֲשֶׁר נֹתְרוּ אַחֲרֵיהֶם בָּאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר לֹא-יָכְלוּ בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, לְהַחֲרִימָם; וַיַּעֲלֵם שְׁלֹמֹה לְמַס-עֹבֵד, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
their children who were left after them in the land, whom the children of Israel were not able to devote to destruction — Solomon raised them as servants of forced labor, to this day.note
sus hijos que quedaron después de ellos en la tierra, a quienes los hijos de Israel no pudieron consagrar a la destrucción — Salomón los levantó como siervos de trabajo forzado, hasta hoy.
22
וּמִבְּנֵי, יִשְׂרָאֵל, לֹא-נָתַן שְׁלֹמֹה, עָבֶד:  כִּי-הֵם אַנְשֵׁי הַמִּלְחָמָה, וַעֲבָדָיו וְשָׂרָיו וְשָׁלִישָׁיו, וְשָׂרֵי רִכְבּוֹ, וּפָרָשָׁיו.
But of the children of Israel Solomon made no slave; for they were the men of war, and his servants and his princes and his adjutants, and the commanders of his chariots and his horsemen.note
Pero de los hijos de Israel Salomón no hizo esclavo a nadie; porque ellos eran los hombres de guerra, y sus siervos y sus príncipes y sus ayudantes, y los comandantes de sus carros y de sus jinetes.
23
אֵלֶּה שָׂרֵי הַנִּצָּבִים, אֲשֶׁר עַל-הַמְּלָאכָה לִשְׁלֹמֹה, חֲמִשִּׁים, וַחֲמֵשׁ מֵאוֹת--הָרֹדִים בָּעָם, הָעֹשִׂים בַּמְּלָאכָה.
These were the chiefs of the officers who were over Solomon’s work: five hundred and fifty, who ruled over the people who did the work.note
Estos eran los jefes de los oficiales que estaban sobre la obra de Salomón: quinientos cincuenta, los que dominaban sobre el pueblo que hacía la obra.
24
אַךְ בַּת-פַּרְעֹה, עָלְתָה מֵעִיר דָּוִד, אֶל-בֵּיתָהּ, אֲשֶׁר בָּנָה-לָהּ; אָז, בָּנָה אֶת-הַמִּלּוֹא.
But Pharaoh’s daughter went up from the city of David to her house that he had built for her; then he built the Millo.note
Pero la hija del faraón subió de la ciudad de David a su casa que él le había edificado; entonces edificó el Milo.
25
וְהֶעֱלָה שְׁלֹמֹה שָׁלֹשׁ פְּעָמִים בַּשָּׁנָה עֹלוֹת וּשְׁלָמִים, עַל-הַמִּזְבֵּחַ אֲשֶׁר בָּנָה לַיהוָה, וְהַקְטֵיר אִתּוֹ, אֲשֶׁר לִפְנֵי יְהוָה; וְשִׁלַּם, אֶת-הַבָּיִת.
And three times a year Solomon offered burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar that he had built to Jehovah, and he burned incense with it, the one that was before Jehovah; and he finished the house.note
Y tres veces al año ofrecía Salomón holocaustos y ofrendas de paz sobre el altar que había edificado a Jehová, y quemaba incienso con él, el que estaba delante de Jehová; y terminó la casa.
26
וָאֳנִי עָשָׂה הַמֶּלֶךְ שְׁלֹמֹה בְּעֶצְיוֹן-גֶּבֶר אֲשֶׁר אֶת-אֵלוֹת, עַל-שְׂפַת יַם-סוּף--בְּאֶרֶץ אֱדוֹם.
And King Solomon made a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, which is beside Eloth, on the shore of the Sea of Reeds, in the land of Edom.note
Y el rey Salomón hizo una flota de naves en Ezión-guéber, que está junto a Elot, a la orilla del mar de los Juncos, en la tierra de Edom.
27
וַיִּשְׁלַח חִירָם בָּאֳנִי, אֶת-עֲבָדָיו, אַנְשֵׁי אֳנִיּוֹת, יֹדְעֵי הַיָּם--עִם, עַבְדֵי שְׁלֹמֹה.
And Hiram sent in the fleet his servants, seamen who knew the sea, with the servants of Solomon.note

Translator's Notes — verse by verse

Verses 1–5 · 1 Kings 9 Jehovah appears to Solomon a second time — “I have heard your prayer and your supplication”, a hallowed house, and a promise with an “if”

A second time. “Jehovah appeared to Solomon a second time, as he had appeared to him at Gibeon”: the first time was a dream in the night, “Ask what I shall give you” (1 Kings 3:5, now on these pages), and Solomon “woke — and look, it was a dream” (1 Kings 3:15). This verse does not say how the second appearance came. The parallel in Chronicles says it was “by night” (2 Chronicles 7:12), and the narrator will later count the two, Jehovah “who had appeared to him twice” (1 Kings 11:9). The ASV has “the second time, as he had appeared unto him at Gibeon”, the NWT 1984 “the second time, the same as he had appeared to him in Gibeon”, the NIV “a second time”, and the TLB adds a parenthesis the Hebrew does not have, “(the first time had been at Gibeon)”.

Prayer and supplication. “I have heard your prayer and your supplication that you made before me”: this is the answer to the chapter before. Solomon asked Jehovah to “turn to the prayer of your servant and to his supplication” (1 Kings 8:28, now on these pages), that his eyes “may be open toward this house night and day”, toward the place of which Jehovah said, “My name shall be there” (1 Kings 8:29). Here Jehovah gives the same two things back in the first person, the name and the eyes, and adds a third, the heart. The pair “your prayer and your supplication”, addressed to the one who prayed, stands in one verse of the Hebrew Bible, this one. The ASV has “I have heard thy prayer and thy supplication”, the NWT 1984 “your prayer and your request for favor”, the NIV “the prayer and plea you have made before me”, and the TLB drops the pair, “I have heard your prayer”. “I have hallowed this house”: the ASV and the KJV have “hallowed”, the NIV “consecrated”, the NWT 1984 and the Douay “sanctified”.

An “if” that was already there. “If you walk before me as David your father walked, in integrity of heart and in uprightness”: the condition is the one David gave Solomon on his deathbed, “If your sons keep their way, to walk before me in truth with all their heart and with all their soul … there shall not be cut off for you a man from upon the throne of Israel” (1 Kings 2:4, now on these pages), and the one Solomon repeated in his own prayer, “only if your sons keep their way” (1 Kings 8:25). Jehovah had said it to Solomon at Gibeon, “if you walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as David your father walked” (1 Kings 3:14), and while the house was building, “if you walk in my statutes” (1 Kings 6:12, both now on these pages). What is new in this verse is only that Jehovah says it now to the man who has finished. The word for “integrity” is tom, and the ASV keeps it, “in integrity of heart”, the NWT 1984 has “with integrity of heart”, the NIV has “faithfully with integrity of heart”, the Douay “in simplicity of heart”, which follows the Latin, and the TLB “in honesty and truth”. Here the Hebrew has “my statutes and my judgments”, as in 1 Kings 8:58; the Douay has “my ordinances and my judgments”.

Verses 6–9 · 1 Kings 9:6–9 The warning — a house “high” or in ruins, a byword and a taunt, and the question every passer-by asks

“You” becomes “you all”. Through verse 5 Jehovah speaks to one man, “you”, “your kingdom”. In verse 6 the Hebrew turns plural: “If you turn away … you and your sons, and do not keep my commandments, my statutes that I have set before you, and you go and serve other gods”. The verbs and the pronouns are plural, and the sons are named in the sentence. The warning is no longer about the throne; it is about the people the house stands among, and the sentence that follows is about Israel: “then I will cut off Israel from upon the face of the ground that I have given them”.

A byword and a taunt. “And Israel will become a byword and a taunt among all the peoples”: the pair mashal ve-shninah, “a byword and a taunt”, stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: Deuteronomy 28:37 (now on these pages), where it is the curse itself, “an object of dismay, a byword and a taunt among all the peoples where Jehovah will drive you”, this verse, and 2 Chronicles 7:20. The prayer of the chapter before had imagined the day a people is carried off, “and forgive your people who have sinned against you” (1 Kings 8:50, now on these pages); the answer here takes the word of the covenant curse and fastens it to the house. The ASV and the KJV have “a proverb and a byword”, the NWT 1984 “a proverbial saying and a taunt”, the NIV “a byword and an object of ridicule”, and the Geneva “a proverb, and a common talk”.

High, or in ruins? “And this house will be high”: the Hebrew word is elyon, “high”, the word that is also a name of God, “Most High”, and the sentence does not finish; it goes straight on to what the passer-by does. This is a famous difficulty, and the shelf splits on it. The ASV has “And though this house is so high, yet shall every one that passeth by it be astonished”, the KJV “And at this house, which is high”, the Geneva “Even this high house shall be so”, and the Douay “this house shall be made an example of”. The NWT 1984 has “this house itself will become heaps of ruins”, the NIV “This temple will become a heap of rubble”, and the TLB “This Temple will become a heap of ruins”: these three print ruin, which is what the verse goes on to describe and is not the word the Hebrew has. The parallel in Chronicles has the same Hebrew word, elyon, in its version of the speech (2 Chronicles 7:21). This page keeps “high” and lets the sentence break off, as the Hebrew does. Everyone passing by “will be appalled and will whistle”: Jeremiah has the same two verbs of the city, “everyone who passes by it will be appalled, and will hiss” (Jeremiah 19:8, now on these pages).

The question, and its answer. “Why has Jehovah done this to this land and to this house?”: the question comes from the end of Deuteronomy, where all the nations ask, “Why has Jehovah done this to this land? What is the heat of this great anger?” and the answer is, “Because they forsook the covenant of Jehovah the God of their fathers, which he made with them when he brought them out of the land of Egypt, and they went and served other gods and bowed down to them” (Deuteronomy 29:23–25, which is 29:24–26 in English Bibles; now on these pages). Solomon’s version adds “and to this house”, and the answer shortens the forsaking: not “the covenant” but “Jehovah their God, who brought their fathers out of the land of Egypt”. In verse 9 the Hebrew text prints one word twice, written vayishtachu and read vayyishtachavu, “and they bowed down”, a difference of one letter that does not change the meaning. The ASV has “laid hold on other gods”, the NWT 1984 “take hold of other gods”, the NIV “embraced other gods”, and the Douay “followed strange gods”.

Verses 10–14 · 1 Kings 9:10–14 Twenty cities in Galilee — Hiram, “my brother”, and the land of Cabul

Twenty years. “At the end of twenty years in which Solomon had built the two houses”: the house of Jehovah took seven years (1 Kings 6:38) and the king’s house thirteen (1 Kings 7:1, both now on these pages), and the two numbers make the twenty. Verses 10 and 11 are one long sentence, broken by a parenthesis, and the main clause, “then King Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities”, comes at the end of verse 11. The ASV and the KJV print the middle as a parenthesis, “(now Hiram the king of Tyre had furnished Solomon …)”, and so does the NWT 1984, “had himself assisted”.

Payment, or gift? “Hiram king of Tyre had supplied Solomon with cedar trees and cypress trees and with gold, according to all his desire — then King Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities”: the Hebrew says “then” and does not say “because” or “in payment”. The NIV turns it round and supplies the reason, “King Solomon gave twenty towns in Galilee to Hiram king of Tyre, because Hiram had supplied him”, and the TLB has “as payment for all the cedar and cypress lumber and gold”. In the earlier chapter Hiram gave “timbers of cedar and timbers of cypress, all his desire”, and Solomon paid year by year, “twenty thousand kor of wheat, food for his household, and twenty kor of beaten oil” (1 Kings 5:24–25, now on these pages); “all his desire” is the phrase of verse 11 again, and the payment is now cities. Hiram is the one who “had always loved David” (1 Kings 5:15). The Chronicles tell it the other way round: “the cities which Huram had given to Solomon, Solomon built them, and caused the children of Israel to dwell there” (2 Chronicles 8:2). Neither book reconciles the two.

“My brother”, and the land of Cabul. “They were not right in his eyes”, and “what are these cities that you have given me, my brother?” “Brother” is what Hiram calls the man who has just given him twenty cities he does not like. “And he called them the land of Cabul, to this day”: the verse does not say what the name means. The word Kabul stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible, here and at the boundary of Asher, “and it went out to Cabul on the left hand” (Joshua 19:27, now on these pages), so it is a place name before this verse and after it. The NIV has “What kind of towns are these you have given me, my brother?”, the NWT 1984 “What sort of cities are these”, and the Douay “Are these the cities which thou hast given me, brother?”, with “Chabul”. The TLB gives the name a meaning: “These cities are a wasteland!”, “The Wasteland”, a meaning the Hebrew does not give.

A hundred and twenty talents. “And Hiram sent to the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold”: the NWT 1984 has “In the meantime Hiram sent”, the NIV “Now Hiram had sent”, a past before the past, which the Hebrew verb does not mark; this page prints a plain past and leaves the order to the reader. The word for “talent” is kikkar.

Verses 15–23 · 1 Kings 9:15–23 The forced labor — Gezer, Pharaoh’s gift, and a levy of “servants” who are not “slaves”

The account of the levy. “This is the account of the forced labor that King Solomon raised”: the word is mas, “forced labor”, the word of the levy in the chapters of the building: “King Solomon raised forced labor out of all Israel; and the forced labor was thirty thousand men … and Adoniram was over the forced labor” (1 Kings 5:27–28, now on these pages), and David’s own roll had “Adoram … over the forced labour” (2 Samuel 20:24). The list of what it built is the list of the king’s works: the house of Jehovah and his own house, the Millo and the wall of Jerusalem, and three cities, Hazor, Megiddo and Gezer. The ASV and the KJV have “the levy”, the NWT 1984 “those conscripted for forced labor”, the NIV “the forced labor King Solomon conscripted”, and the Douay “the sum of the expenses”, a different word.

Gezer. “Pharaoh king of Egypt had gone up and captured Gezer and burned it with fire, and the Canaanites who lived in the city he killed”: the city that Ephraim could not take was taken by Egypt. “They did not drive out the Canaanites who lived in Gezer; and the Canaanite has lived in the midst of Ephraim to this day, and became a servant for forced labour” (Joshua 16:10, now on these pages; Judges 1:29 says the same of Ephraim). Pharaoh is not named, and the verse gives no date; it says he gave the burned city “as a parting gift to his daughter”, whom Solomon had taken in the first chapters (1 Kings 3:1, now on these pages), and Solomon then built it (verse 17). For the word, the shelf chooses among five: the ASV has “a portion”, the KJV and the Geneva “a present”, the NWT 1984 “a parting gift”, the NIV “a wedding gift”, and the Douay and the TLB “a dowry”.

Tadmor, written Tamar. “And Baalath and Tadmor in the wilderness”: the Hebrew text prints two spellings of the last name, Tamar as written and Tadmor as read. The ASV and the NWT 1984 print “Tamar”, the KJV, the NIV and the Geneva “Tadmor”, and the Douay “Palmira”. Chronicles has “And he built Tadmor in the wilderness” (2 Chronicles 8:4). This page prints the name as it is read.

Servants of forced labor, and no slave. “All the people who were left of the Amorites, the Hittites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites”: five of the seven nations of Deuteronomy 7:1 (now on these pages), whom Israel “was not able to devote to destruction”, that is, to the ban (cherem). Solomon “raised them as servants of forced labor, to this day”. The phrase le-mas oved stands in three verses of the Hebrew Bible: the blessing of Issachar, who “became a servant of forced labor” (Genesis 49:15), the Canaanites of Gezer who were made to do forced labor (Joshua 16:10, now on these pages), and this verse. Then verse 22: “But of the children of Israel Solomon made no slave; for they were the men of war, and his servants”. The Hebrew uses one root twice in the sentence: eved, “slave”, and avadav, “his servants”, so Israelites are not avadim in the first sense but are in the second. The levy of Israel in the early chapters is called mas too (1 Kings 5:27), and this verse does not say whether it is the same thing. The ASV has “a levy of bondservants” for the first, and “no bondservants” for the second; the KJV “a tribute of bondservice” and “no bondmen”; the NWT 1984 “slavish forced labor” and “slaves”; the NIV “to serve as slave labor” and “slaves”; the Douay “tributary”.

Five hundred and fifty. “These were the chiefs of the officers who were over Solomon’s work: five hundred and fifty”: all thirteen versions on the two shelves print the number. The earlier chapter has the same office, in the same Hebrew words, with a different count, “besides the chiefs of the officers of Solomon who were over the work, three thousand three hundred” (1 Kings 5:30, now on these pages), and Chronicles says “two hundred and fifty” (2 Chronicles 8:10). Each book gives its own number, and this page prints each as it stands.

Verses 24–28 · 1 Kings 9:24–28 Pharaoh’s daughter in her own house, three feasts a year, and a fleet at Ezion-geber — four hundred and twenty talents from Ophir

Her own house. “Pharaoh’s daughter went up from the city of David to her house that he had built for her; then he built the Millo”: Solomon had brought her “to the city of David, until he had finished building his house” (1 Kings 3:1, now on these pages), and here the waiting is over. Kings gives no reason for the move. Chronicles gives one, in Solomon’s own mouth: “No wife of mine shall dwell in the house of David king of Israel, because the places are holy, whereunto the ark of Jehovah hath come” (2 Chronicles 8:11). The TLB adds that she was moved “to the new quarters he had built for her in the palace”, which the Hebrew does not say.

Three times a year. “Three times a year Solomon offered burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar that he had built to Jehovah”: the three times are the three feasts of Deuteronomy 16:16 (now on these pages), and Chronicles names them, “even in the feast of unleavened bread, and in the feast of weeks, and in the feast of tabernacles” (2 Chronicles 8:13). “And he burned incense with it, the one that was before Jehovah”: the Hebrew has the verb and the word “with it” and a clause “that was before Jehovah”, and no noun for what is burned on. The versions supply one. The ASV has “burning incense therewith, upon the altar that was before Jehovah”, the KJV “he burnt incense upon the altar that was before the LORD”, the NWT 1984 “a making of sacrificial smoke on the [altar], which was before Jehovah”, with the noun in brackets, the NIV “burning incense before the Lord along with them”, and the Douay “burnt incense before the Lord”. The last clause is the one on which the versions part: “and he finished the house”. The ASV and the KJV have “So he finished the house”, the NWT 1984 “and he completed the house”, the Douay “the temple was finished”, and the NIV “and so fulfilled the temple obligations”, which reads the verb as paying a debt.

A fleet at Ezion-geber. “King Solomon made a fleet of ships at Ezion-geber, which is beside Eloth, on the shore of the Sea of Reeds, in the land of Edom”: Ezion-geber was a camp of the wilderness (Numbers 33:35, now on these pages), and Eloth is Elath. This page keeps its established “Sea of Reeds” for yam suf, and all eight English versions on the shelf print “Red Sea”. Hiram sends “his servants, seamen who knew the sea, with the servants of Solomon”: the Tyrians are the sailors. Chronicles has Huram send “ships, and servants that had knowledge of the sea” (2 Chronicles 8:18), and the TLB adds that they “used to sail back and forth from Ophir”, which the verse does not say.

Ophir. “And they came to Ophir and took from there gold, four hundred and twenty talents, and brought it to King Solomon”: “to Ophir”, in this form, stands in two verses of the Hebrew Bible: this one and 2 Chronicles 8:18, where the amount is “four hundred and fifty talents”. Neither says where Ophir was. Hiram’s fleet and Ophir come back in the next chapter, “the navy also of Hiram, that brought gold from Ophir, brought in from Ophir great plenty of sandal-wood and precious stones”, and “a navy of Tarshish with the navy of Hiram” (1 Kings 10:11, 22). The ASV and the KJV have “fetched from thence gold, four hundred and twenty talents”, the NWT 1984 “take from there four hundred and twenty talents of gold”, and the NIV “420 talents of gold”.

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the words exact and distinct: “hallowed” for hiqdashti (verses 3 and 7, as in Jehovah’s own word of the house); “my statutes and my judgments”, as in 1 Kings 8:58; “the ground” for adamah; “a byword and a taunt” for mashal ve-shninah, as in Deuteronomy 28:37; “high” for elyon in verse 8, kept, with the difficulty named; “forced labor” for mas and “servants of forced labor” for mas oved; “slave” for eved and “servants” for avadim in verse 22, where the root is one; “devote to destruction” for lehacharim; “a parting gift” for shilluchim; “the Sea of Reeds”, as in Deuteronomy 11:4; and “the Millo”, with its article.

Echoes paid. The dream at Gibeon and the second appearance (1 Kings 3:5, 15); the condition given on David’s deathbed and repeated in the prayer (1 Kings 2:4; 3:14; 6:12; 8:25); the prayer, the supplication and the open eyes (1 Kings 8:28–29); the curse of the proverb and the byword and the question of the nations (Deuteronomy 28:37; 29:23–25); the hissing of Jeremiah’s city (Jeremiah 19:8); the seven years and the thirteen (1 Kings 6:38; 7:1); the covenant of Hiram and Solomon, the timbers, the wheat and the oil, and the love of David (1 Kings 5:15, 24–26); the forced labor, Adoniram, and the chiefs of the officers (1 Kings 5:27–30; 2 Samuel 20:24); Gezer, which Ephraim did not take (Joshua 16:10; Judges 1:29); Pharaoh’s daughter in the city of David (1 Kings 3:1); the nations left in the land (Deuteronomy 7:1); the three feasts (Deuteronomy 16:16); Cabul on the boundary of Asher (Joshua 19:27); and Ezion-geber, a camp of the wilderness (Numbers 33:35).

Echoes planted. The narrator’s count of the two appearances and the heart “not whole” (1 Kings 11:4, 9); the Chronicler’s own telling of this chapter, with the cities Huram gave to Solomon, the two hundred and fifty officers, the reason for Pharaoh’s daughter’s house, the three feasts and the four hundred and fifty talents (2 Chronicles 7:12–22; 8:1–18); the fleet of Hiram, the gold of Ophir and the navy of Tarshish (1 Kings 10:11, 22); and the Millo, which comes up again as the cause of Jeroboam’s lifting his hand against the king (1 Kings 11:27). None is translated here yet.
