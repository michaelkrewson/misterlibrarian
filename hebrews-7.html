Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, read from Genesis 14 and Psalm 110: without father, without mother, without genealogy, receiving a tenth from Abraham and blessing him, so that Levi 'paid tithes' through Abraham. A change of priesthood brings a change of law; a priest from Judah 'by the power of an indestructible life'; the oath 'Jehovah has sworn'; Jesus the guarantor of a better covenant, who always lives to plead, and who offered himself once for all.
Before AD 70, most likely c. AD 60-69, continuing the same letter as chapter 1 (see that note).

Translator’s Notes

Verses 1–3 · Melchizedek, king of Salem — “without father, without mother, without genealogy”

“This Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of God Most High, who met Abraham as he returned from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him, to whom also Abraham apportioned a tenth of everything.” The verses retell Genesis 14:18–20 (encyclopedia). “Priest of God Most High” is word for word the Greek of Genesis 14:18 (checked), for the Hebrew El Elyon; “the slaughter” is the Greek of Genesis 14:17, where this translation’s Genesis reads “striking down Chedorlaomer” (checked); and “a tenth of everything” is the Greek of Genesis 14:20, with one change: Genesis says Abram “gave,” and Hebrews says he “apportioned” (checked). The writer then reads the two names. Malki-tsedeq is “king of righteousness,” and Salem is heard as shalom, “peace” (encyclopedia). The KJV reads “priest of the most high God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings”; the ASV reads “priest of God Most High”; the NWT 1984 reads “and to whom Abraham apportioned a tenth from all things.”

⚠ “Without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life.” Apatōr, “without father,” stands in one verse of the New Testament (dictionary). Amētōr, “without mother,” also stands in one verse (dictionary). Agenealogētos, “without genealogy,” likewise stands in one verse (dictionary). Genesis gives Melchizedek no parents, no line, no birth and no death, and traditions read the three words differently: as an argument from what Scripture leaves unsaid, so that the record itself presents him as a figure without beginning or end; as a sign that he was a heavenly being, as the Qumran scroll known as 11QMelchizedek presents him (reported, not checked here); as an appearance of the Son himself before his birth; or, in a later Jewish tradition, as Shem the son of Noah under another name (reported, not checked here). This page does not choose. The KJV reads “Without father, without mother, without descent”; the NWT 1984 reads “In being fatherless, motherless, without genealogy”; the TLB reads “He was never born and he never died,” which turns the silence of Genesis into a statement about his life.

“But made like the Son of God, remains a priest for all time.” The likeness runs one way: Melchizedek is made like the Son, not the Son like Melchizedek. Aphōmoioō, “to make like,” stands in one verse (dictionary). “For all time” is eis to diēnekēs; the word diēnekēs stands in four verses, all in Hebrews (here and 10:1, 10:12 and 10:14) (dictionary). It is a different phrase from the “forever” of Psalm 110, eis ton aiōna, in verses 17, 21, 24 and 28, and this translation keeps the two apart. The Geneva reads “but is likened unto the Son of God, and continueth a Priest forever”; the ASV reads “abideth a priest continually”; the NWT 1984 reads “he remains a priest perpetually.”

Verses 4–10 · A tenth of the spoils — Levi “paid tithes” through Abraham, “so to speak”

“See how great this man was, to whom Abraham the patriarch gave a tenth from the best of the spoils.” Pēlikos, “how great,” stands in two verses: Paul’s “See with what large letters” at Galatians 6:11, and here. Akrothinia, “the best of the spoils,” the top of the heap, stands in one verse (dictionary). “The patriarch” is the title of David at Acts 2:29 (dictionary), placed last in the Greek for weight. The KJV reads “unto whom even the patriarch Abraham gave the tenth of the spoils”; the ASV reads “gave a tenth out of the chief spoils”; the NWT 1984 reads “to whom Abraham, the family head, gave a tenth.”

“Those of the sons of Levi who receive the priestly office have a commandment, according to the law, to collect tithes from the people … even though these too have come out of the loins of Abraham.” Hierateia, “priestly office,” stands in two verses, Luke 1:9 and here (dictionary); it is a different word from hierōsynē, “priesthood,” in verses 11, 12 and 24, and this translation keeps them apart. In the Law the people’s tithe goes to the Levites (Numbers 18:21), who lift out “a tithe of the tithe” (18:26) and give it “to Aaron the priest” (18:28); Hebrews speaks of the line of Levi as a whole. “Come out of the loins” is the phrase of Genesis 35:11, “kings shall come out of your loins.” The KJV reads “have a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law”; the NWT 1984 reads “even if these have issued from the loins of Abraham”; the NIV reads “even though they also are descended from Abraham.”

“But the one whose genealogy is not traced from them has collected tithes from Abraham … And beyond all dispute, the lesser is blessed by the better.” “Dispute” is antilogia, the word of Hebrews 6:16, where the oath is “an end of every dispute.” “Better” is kreittōn, the word of Hebrews 6:9, “better things,” and it returns in verses 19 and 22: a better hope, a better covenant. “The one who had the promises” is Abraham, blessed at Genesis 14:19. Verse 8 sets “men who die” against “one of whom it is testified that he lives”: readers take the testimony as the silence of Genesis about his death, or as the “forever” of Psalm 110:4, quoted in verse 17 with the same verb, “it is testified.” The KJV reads “And without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better”; the NWT 1984 reads “the less is blessed by the greater”; the NIV reads “by him who is declared to be living.”

“And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who receives tithes, has paid tithes, for he was still in the loins of his father.” Hōs epos eipein, “so to speak,” stands in one verse of the New Testament: the writer marks the argument as a figure. The verb is passive, “has been tithed”; dekatoō, “to take a tenth,” stands in two verses, verses 6 and 9 (dictionary). Levi is Abraham’s great-grandson, through Isaac and Jacob, so “his father” here is his forefather. The KJV reads “And as I may so say, Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham”; the ASV reads “And, so to say, through Abraham even Levi”; the NIV reads “Levi was still in the body of his ancestor.”

Verses 11–19 · “A change of law” — a priest from Judah, “the power of an indestructible life,” and a better hope

“Now if completion had been through the Levitical priesthood … what further need was there for another priest to arise according to the order of Melchizedek?” Teleiōsis, “completion,” stands in two verses: “a fulfilment of the things spoken to her” at Luke 1:45, and here (dictionary). It is the noun of the verb this translation prints as “make complete” at Hebrews 2:10 and 5:9, and the verb returns in verses 19 and 28. Hierōsynē, “priesthood,” stands in three verses, all in this chapter (dictionary). “Order” is taxis (dictionary). Robinson–Pierpont has the pronoun in the dative and the verb in the pluperfect (the apparatus). The KJV reads “If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood”; the ASV reads “and not be reckoned after the order of Aaron”; the NIV reads “and indeed the law given to the people established that priesthood.”

“For when the priesthood is changed, of necessity a change of law also takes place.” Metathesis, “change,” stands in three verses: here; at Hebrews 11:5, where Enoch is “taken up” and the noun is his “being taken”; and at 12:27 (dictionary). Its verb is the “changed” of this verse and the “deserting” of Galatians 1:6, and its negative is the “unchangeableness” of Hebrews 6:17. The KJV reads “For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law”; the NIV reads “For when the priesthood is changed, the law must be changed also”; the Douay reads “For the priesthood being translated, it is necessary that a translation also be made of the law,” an English “translated” in the old sense of “moved.”

“For it is plain that our Lord has sprung from Judah, a tribe about which Moses said nothing concerning priests.” “Plain” is prodēlos (dictionary). Anatellō, “to spring up, to rise,” stands in six verses; it is used of the sun at Matthew 5:45, “he makes his sun rise,” and of a light at Matthew 4:16, “a light dawned,” and the Greek of Numbers 24:17 has it for the star that “will rise out of Jacob” (checked), where this translation’s Numbers reads “A star steps forth from Jacob.” Judah is the tribe of “the Lion of the tribe of Judah” at Revelation 5:5. Robinson–Pierpont has “nothing concerning priesthood” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood”; the ASV reads “Moses spake nothing concerning priests”; the NWT 1984 reads “our Lord has sprung up out of Judah.”

“Another priest arises in the likeness of Melchizedek, who has become one not according to the law of a fleshly commandment, but according to the power of an indestructible life.” Katadēlos, “evident,” stands in one verse. “In the likeness” is the phrase of Hebrews 4:15, “tested in every respect in the same way.” “Fleshly” is sarkinos, the word of Romans 7:14 and 1 Corinthians 3:1; Robinson–Pierpont has sarkikos (the apparatus) (dictionary). Akatalytos, “indestructible,” stands in one verse (dictionary); its root is the verb of Mark 14:58, “I will destroy this sanctuary.” Verse 17 quotes Psalm 110:4 again, as at Hebrews 5:6; Robinson–Pierpont has “he testifies” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “but after the power of an endless life”; the Douay reads “according to the power of an indissoluble life”; the NIV reads “not on the basis of a regulation as to his ancestry.”

“A setting aside of a former commandment, because of its weakness and unprofitableness (for the law made nothing complete), and on the other the bringing in of a better hope, through which we come near to God.” Athetēsis, “setting aside,” stands in two verses, here and 9:26 (dictionary); its verb is the one Paul uses of a covenant once confirmed at Galatians 3:15. “Unprofitable” is anōphelēs, as at Titus 3:9 (dictionary). Epeisagōgē, “bringing in,” stands in one verse. “Come near” is engizō, a different verb from the “draw near” of Hebrews 4:16, which returns in verse 25. The KJV reads “For the law made nothing perfect, but the bringing in of a better hope did,” with “did” supplied, so that the hope becomes the subject that makes perfect; the ASV reads “(for the law made nothing perfect), and a bringing in thereupon of a better hope”; the NWT 1984 reads “a setting aside of the preceding commandment on account of its weakness and ineffectiveness.”

Verses 20–25 · “Jehovah has sworn” — the guarantor of a better covenant, and a priest who always lives to plead

“But this one with a sworn oath, through the one who says to him, ‘Jehovah has sworn and will not change his mind: You are a priest forever.’” Horkōmosia, “sworn oath,” stands in three verses, all in this chapter (dictionary); it is a different word from the horkos, “oath,” of Hebrews 6:16 and 6:17. The Hebrew of Psalm 110:4 opens nishba YHVH, “Jehovah has sworn” (checked), and the Greek reads kyrios; this translation prints “Jehovah” for the God of Israel in a quotation (Psalm 110 yet to come in this translation). “Will not change his mind” is metamelomai, the word of the Greek Psalter (checked) and of Paul’s “I do not regret it” at 2 Corinthians 7:8. Robinson–Pierpont adds “according to the order of Melchizedek” (the apparatus), and the KJV follows it. The KJV reads “The Lord sware and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec”; the NWT 1984 reads “Jehovah has sworn (and he will feel no regret)”; the NIV reads “will not change his mind.”

“By so much Jesus has become the guarantor of a better covenant.” Engyos, “guarantor,” the one who stands surety, stands in one verse (dictionary). Diathēkē, “covenant,” appears here for the first time in the letter, and chapters 8 and 9 turn on it (dictionary); the cup of Luke 22:20 is “the new covenant in my blood.” Westcott–Hort and Nestle–Aland 28 have “of a better covenant also” (the apparatus). The KJV reads “By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament”; the ASV reads “the surety of a better covenant”; the NWT 1984 reads “the one given in pledge of a better covenant.”

“Those others have become priests many in number, because death prevents them from continuing; but he, because he remains forever, has a permanent priesthood.” Aaron himself died on Mount Hor, after Moses “stripped Aaron of his garments and clothed Eleazar his son with them” (Numbers 20:28). Aparabatos, “permanent,” stands in one verse (dictionary), and it is read two ways: “unchangeable, inviolable,” or “not passing to another,” a priesthood with no successor; this translation prints “permanent” and leaves the range here. The KJV reads “hath an unchangeable priesthood”; the Geneva reads “hath a Priesthood, which cannot pass from one to another”; the NWT 1984 reads “has his priesthood without any successors.”

“Hence he is also able to save completely those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to plead for them.” Pantelēs stands in two verses: the woman who “could not straighten up at all” at Luke 13:11, and here (dictionary). Readers take eis to pantelēs as completeness, “completely,” or as time, “for all time”; the shelf divides. “Draw near” is the verb of Hebrews 4:16. “Plead” is entynchanō, as at Romans 8:34, “who also pleads for us” (dictionary). The KJV reads “Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost”; the Douay reads “to save for ever them that come to God by him”; the NWT 1984 reads “because he is always alive to plead for them.”

Verses 26–28 · “Once for all, when he offered up himself” — holy, innocent, undefiled, and a Son made complete forever

“For such a high priest was fitting for us: holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and become higher than the heavens.” “Was fitting” is the verb of Hebrews 2:10, “it was fitting for him.” “Holy” is hosios, a different word from hagios: “your holy one” of Acts 2:27 (dictionary). Akakos, “innocent,” stands in two verses, Romans 16:18 and here (dictionary). Amiantos, “undefiled,” stands in four verses, among them “religion pure and undefiled” at James 1:27 (dictionary). “Higher than the heavens” follows “who has passed through the heavens” of Hebrews 4:14. The KJV reads “holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners”; the NWT 1984 reads “loyal, guileless, undefiled”; the NIV reads “Such a high priest truly meets our need.”

“Who has no need, as those high priests do, to offer up sacrifices day by day, first for his own sins and then for those of the people.” On the Day of Atonement, once a year, Aaron makes atonement “for himself and for his household” (Leviticus 16:6) and then for the people (16:15); at his first sacrifice he is told to “make atonement for yourself and for the people” (Leviticus 9:7); and Hebrews 5:3 has already said it. “Day by day” is read three ways: loosely, of the whole round of sacrifice; of the anointed priest’s grain offering, made “continually — half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening” (Leviticus 6:13, 6:20 in most English Bibles); or as the yearly rite drawn together with the daily one. This page does not choose. The KJV reads “Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice”; the NIV reads “he does not need to offer sacrifices day after day”; the TLB reads “He never needs the daily blood of animal sacrifices.”

“For this he did once for all when he offered up himself.” Ephapax, “once for all,” stands in five verses: Romans 6:10, “he died to sin once for all”; 1 Corinthians 15:6, “at one time”; here; 9:12; and 10:10 (dictionary). For the first time the letter says what the high priest offered: himself. The KJV reads “for this he did once, when he offered up himself”; the ASV reads “for this he did once for all”; the NWT 1984 reads “for this he did once for all time when he offered himself up.”

“For the law appoints as high priests men who have weakness, but the word of the sworn oath, which came after the law, appoints a Son, made complete forever.” “Weakness” is the word of Hebrews 5:2, “surrounded by weakness”; “appoints” is the verb of 5:1. The oath of Psalm 110 comes later than the Law of Moses, and the writer reads the later word as the one that stands. “A Son,” without the article, as in “spoke to us by a Son” at Hebrews 1:2; “made complete” is the verb of Hebrews 2:10 and 5:9. The KJV reads “maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore”; the ASV reads “appointeth a Son, perfected for evermore”; the NIV reads “appointed the Son, who has been made perfect forever.”

Patterns worth carrying forward

Where this translation keeps the exact words: “God Most High,” as at Genesis 14:18; “apportioned,” where Genesis says “gave”; “without father, without mother, without genealogy”; “for all time,” kept apart from “forever”; “the best of the spoils”; “priestly office” kept apart from “priesthood”; “dispute,” as at 6:16; “better” in verses 7, 19 and 22; “so to speak”; “completion” and “made complete,” as at 2:10 and 5:9; “sprung,” the verb of the rising sun; “indestructible”; “unprofitable,” as at Titus 3:9; “sworn oath” kept apart from “oath”; “Jehovah has sworn”; “guarantor”; “covenant,” never “testament”; “plead,” as at Romans 8:34; and “once for all,” as at Romans 6:10.

Echoes paid. Melchizedek, priest of God Most High, from Genesis 14:18 and from the promise of Hebrews 6:20; a tenth of everything from Genesis 14:20; the patriarch of Acts 2:29; the tithe of Numbers 18:21; out of the loins from Genesis 35:11; dispute and better from Hebrews 6:16 and Hebrews 6:9; made complete from Hebrews 2:10 and Hebrews 5:9; Enoch taken up at Hebrews 11:5; the rising star of Numbers 24:17; in the same way from Hebrews 4:15; unprofitable from Titus 3:9; draw near from Hebrews 4:16; pleads for us from Romans 8:34; it was fitting from Hebrews 2:10; once for all from Romans 6:10; and surrounded by weakness from Hebrews 5:2.

Echoes planted. “A better covenant” at 8:6; “once for all” at 9:12 and “setting aside” at 9:26; “for all time” at 10:1, 10:12 and 10:14, “once for all” again at 10:10; and the “change” of what can be shaken at 12:27.

Next in this book: Hebrews 8, already on these pages. “The main point”: a high priest seated at the right hand of the throne of the Majesty, a minister of the true tent; the earthly sanctuary a copy and shadow, made “according to the pattern” shown on the mountain; and a better covenant, with Jeremiah’s promise of a new covenant written on the heart.
