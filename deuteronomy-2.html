Moses continues the historical review, picking up where chapter 1 left off: the thirty-eight years spent circling Edom's territory, the peaceful passage granted by Edom and Moab (each a kin-nation with its own God-given possession), the death of the last fighting men of the old generation at the brook Zered, and the refusal and destruction of Sihon king of Heshbon, whose land becomes Israel's first Transjordan conquest.
Same setting as chapter 1 — the plains of Moab, 'in the fortieth year' (1:3) — recounting events that themselves span roughly Israel's second through fortieth years after the exodus, on the early-date chronology this project foregrounds (c. 1446-1406 BC).

Deuteronomy 2 — The Mister Translation

1
וַנֵּפֶן וַנִּסַּע הַמִּדְבָּרָה, דֶּרֶךְ יַם-סוּף, כַּאֲשֶׁר דִּבֶּר יְהוָה, אֵלָי; וַנָּסָב אֶת-הַר-שֵׂעִיר, יָמִים רַבִּים.
Then we turned and set out into the wilderness by the way to the Red Sea, as Jehovah had spoken to me, and we went around Mount Seir for many days.note
Entonces nos volvimos y partimos hacia el desierto, camino del mar Rojo, tal como Jehová me había dicho, y anduvimos rodeando el monte Seír por muchos días.
2
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, אֵלַי לֵאמֹר.
And Jehovah said to me,note
Y Jehová me habló, diciendo:
3
רַב-לָכֶם, סֹב אֶת-הָהָר הַזֶּה; פְּנוּ לָכֶם, צָפֹנָה.
'You have gone around this mountain long enough. Turn northward.note
«Ya han rodeado bastante este monte; vuélvanse hacia el norte.
4
וְאֶת-הָעָם, צַו לֵאמֹר, אַתֶּם עֹבְרִים בִּגְבוּל אֲחֵיכֶם בְּנֵי-עֵשָׂו, הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּשֵׂעִיר; וְיִירְאוּ מִכֶּם, וְנִשְׁמַרְתֶּם מְאֹד.
And command the people, saying: You are about to pass through the territory of your brothers, the sons of Esau, who live in Seir. They will be afraid of you, so be very careful.note
Y manda al pueblo, diciendo: Están a punto de pasar por el territorio de sus hermanos, los hijos de Esaú, que habitan en Seír. Ellos tendrán miedo de ustedes, así que tengan mucho cuidado.
5
אַל-תִּתְגָּרוּ בָם--כִּי לֹא-אֶתֵּן לָכֶם מֵאַרְצָם, עַד מִדְרַךְ כַּף-רָגֶל: כִּי-יְרֻשָּׁה לְעֵשָׂו, נָתַתִּי אֶת-הַר שֵׂעִיר.
Do not provoke them, for I will not give you any of their land — not so much as a foot's breadth — because I have given Mount Seir to Esau as a possession.note
No los provoquen, porque no les daré nada de su tierra — ni siquiera lo que cubre la planta de un pie — porque yo le he dado a Esaú el monte Seír como posesión.
6
אֹכֶל תִּשְׁבְּרוּ מֵאִתָּם בַּכֶּסֶף, וַאֲכַלְתֶּם; וְגַם-מַיִם תִּכְרוּ מֵאִתָּם, בַּכֶּסֶף--וּשְׁתִיתֶם.
You shall buy food from them for silver, that you may eat, and you shall also buy water from them for silver, that you may drink.note
Ustedes les comprarán alimento por dinero, para comer, y también les comprarán agua por dinero, para beber.
7
כִּי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ בֵּרַכְךָ, בְּכֹל מַעֲשֵׂה יָדֶךָ--יָדַע לֶכְתְּךָ, אֶת-הַמִּדְבָּר הַגָּדֹל הַזֶּה: זֶה אַרְבָּעִים שָׁנָה, יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ עִמָּךְ--לֹא חָסַרְתָּ, דָּבָר.
For Jehovah your God has blessed you in all the work of your hand. He has known your walking through this great wilderness. These forty years Jehovah your God has been with you; you have lacked nothing.'note
Porque Jehová su Dios los ha bendecido en toda obra de sus manos. Él ha conocido su andar por este gran desierto. Estos cuarenta años Jehová su Dios ha estado con ustedes; no les ha faltado nada».
8
וַנַּעֲבֹר מֵאֵת אַחֵינוּ בְנֵי-עֵשָׂו, הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּשֵׂעִיר, מִדֶּרֶךְ הָעֲרָבָה, מֵאֵילַת וּמֵעֶצְיֹן גָּבֶר; וַנֵּפֶן, וַנַּעֲבֹר, דֶּרֶךְ, מִדְבַּר מוֹאָב.
So we passed by our brothers, the sons of Esau, who live in Seir — away from the road of the Arabah, from Elath and from Ezion-geber — and we turned and passed by the way of the wilderness of Moab.note
Así pasamos de largo de nuestros hermanos, los hijos de Esaú, que habitan en Seír — lejos del camino del Arabá, desde Elat y desde Ezión-guéber — y nos volvimos, y pasamos por el camino del desierto de Moab.
9
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֵלַי, אַל-תָּצַר אֶת-מוֹאָב, וְאַל-תִּתְגָּר בָּם, מִלְחָמָה: כִּי לֹא-אֶתֵּן לְךָ מֵאַרְצוֹ, יְרֻשָּׁה--כִּי לִבְנֵי-לוֹט, נָתַתִּי אֶת-עָר יְרֻשָּׁה.
And Jehovah said to me, 'Do not harass Moab, and do not provoke them to battle, for I will not give you any of their land as a possession, because I have given Ar to the sons of Lot as a possession.note
Y Jehová me dijo: «No molestes a Moab, ni los provoques a la guerra, porque no te daré nada de su tierra como posesión, porque a los hijos de Lot les he dado Ar como posesión».
10
הָאֵמִים לְפָנִים, יָשְׁבוּ בָהּ--עַם גָּדוֹל וְרַב וָרָם, כָּעֲנָקִים.
(The Emim used to live there, a people great and many and tall as the Anakim.note
(Los emitas habitaban allí antes, un pueblo grande y numeroso y alto como los anaquitas.
11
רְפָאִים יֵחָשְׁבוּ אַף-הֵם, כָּעֲנָקִים; וְהַמֹּאָבִים, יִקְרְאוּ לָהֶם אֵמִים.
They too are counted as Rephaim, like the Anakim, but the Moabites call them Emim.note
También a ellos se les cuenta como refaítas, igual que a los anaquitas, pero los moabitas los llaman emitas.
12
וּבְשֵׂעִיר יָשְׁבוּ הַחֹרִים, לְפָנִים, וּבְנֵי עֵשָׂו יִירָשׁוּם וַיַּשְׁמִידוּם מִפְּנֵיהֶם, וַיֵּשְׁבוּ תַּחְתָּם: כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה יִשְׂרָאֵל, לְאֶרֶץ יְרֻשָּׁתוֹ, אֲשֶׁר-נָתַן יְהוָה, לָהֶם.
And the Horites used to live in Seir, but the sons of Esau dispossessed them and destroyed them from before them and lived in their place — just as Israel did to the land of its possession, which Jehovah gave them.)note
Y los horeos habitaban antes en Seír, pero los hijos de Esaú los desposeyeron y los destruyeron de delante de sí, y habitaron en su lugar — tal como hizo Israel con la tierra de su posesión, que Jehová les dio.)
13
עַתָּה, קֻמוּ וְעִבְרוּ לָכֶם--אֶת-נַחַל זָרֶד; וַנַּעֲבֹר, אֶת-נַחַל זָרֶד.
Now rise up and cross the brook Zered.' So we crossed the brook Zered.note
Ahora levántense y crucen el arroyo Zered». Y cruzamos el arroyo Zered.
14
וְהַיָּמִים אֲשֶׁר-הָלַכְנוּ מִקָּדֵשׁ בַּרְנֵעַ, עַד אֲשֶׁר-עָבַרְנוּ אֶת-נַחַל זֶרֶד, שְׁלֹשִׁים וּשְׁמֹנֶה, שָׁנָה--עַד-תֹּם כָּל-הַדּוֹר אַנְשֵׁי הַמִּלְחָמָה, מִקֶּרֶב הַמַּחֲנֶה, כַּאֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּע יְהוָה, לָהֶם.
And the days that we walked from Kadesh-barnea until we crossed the brook Zered were thirty-eight years, until the whole generation of the men of war had perished from the midst of the camp, just as Jehovah had sworn to them.note
Y los días que anduvimos desde Cades-barnea hasta que cruzamos el arroyo Zered fueron treinta y ocho años, hasta que toda la generación de los hombres de guerra hubo perecido de en medio del campamento, tal como Jehová les había jurado.
15
וְגַם יַד-יְהוָה הָיְתָה בָּם, לְהֻמָּם מִקֶּרֶב הַמַּחֲנֶה, עַד, תֻּמָּם.
Moreover, the hand of Jehovah was against them, to destroy them from the midst of the camp, until they had perished.note
Además, la mano de Jehová estuvo contra ellos, para destruirlos de en medio del campamento, hasta que perecieron.
16
וַיְהִי כַאֲשֶׁר-תַּמּוּ כָּל-אַנְשֵׁי הַמִּלְחָמָה, לָמוּת--מִקֶּרֶב הָעָם.
So it was, when all the men of war had died off from among the people,note
Y sucedió que, cuando todos los hombres de guerra hubieron muerto de entre el pueblo,
17
וַיְדַבֵּר יְהוָה, אֵלַי לֵאמֹר.
that Jehovah spoke to me, saying:note
Jehová me habló, diciendo:
18
אַתָּה עֹבֵר הַיּוֹם אֶת-גְּבוּל מוֹאָב, אֶת-עָר.
'You are this day crossing the border of Moab, at Ar.note
«Hoy vas a cruzar el territorio de Moab, en Ar.
19
וְקָרַבְתָּ, מוּל בְּנֵי עַמּוֹן--אַל-תְּצֻרֵם, וְאַל-תִּתְגָּר בָּם: כִּי לֹא-אֶתֵּן מֵאֶרֶץ בְּנֵי-עַמּוֹן לְךָ, יְרֻשָּׁה--כִּי לִבְנֵי-לוֹט, נְתַתִּיהָ יְרֻשָּׁה.
And when you come near the sons of Ammon, do not harass them, and do not provoke them, for I will not give you any of the land of the sons of Ammon as a possession, because I have given it to the sons of Lot as a possession.note
Y cuando te acerques a los hijos de Amón, no los molestes, ni los provoques, porque no te daré nada de la tierra de los hijos de Amón como posesión, porque a los hijos de Lot se la he dado como posesión.
20
אֶרֶץ-רְפָאִים תֵּחָשֵׁב, אַף-הִוא: רְפָאִים יָשְׁבוּ-בָהּ, לְפָנִים, וְהָעַמֹּנִים, יִקְרְאוּ לָהֶם זַמְזֻמִּים.
(That too is counted a land of Rephaim. Rephaim used to live there, but the Ammonites call them Zamzummim —note
(Esa tierra también se cuenta como tierra de refaítas. Los refaítas habitaban allí antes, pero los amonitas los llaman zamzumitas —
21
עַם גָּדוֹל וְרַב וָרָם, כָּעֲנָקִים; וַיַּשְׁמִידֵם יְהוָה מִפְּנֵיהֶם, וַיִּירָשֻׁם וַיֵּשְׁבוּ תַחְתָּם.
a people great and many and tall as the Anakim. But Jehovah destroyed them from before them, and they dispossessed them and lived in their place,note
un pueblo grande y numeroso y alto como los anaquitas. Pero Jehová los destruyó de delante de ellos, y ellos los desposeyeron y habitaron en su lugar,
22
כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה לִבְנֵי עֵשָׂו, הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּשֵׂעִיר--אֲשֶׁר הִשְׁמִיד אֶת-הַחֹרִי, מִפְּנֵיהֶם, וַיִּירָשֻׁם וַיֵּשְׁבוּ תַחְתָּם, עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
just as he did for the sons of Esau who live in Seir, when he destroyed the Horites from before them, and they dispossessed them and lived in their place, to this day.note
tal como hizo con los hijos de Esaú que habitan en Seír, cuando destruyó a los horeos de delante de ellos, y ellos los desposeyeron y habitaron en su lugar, hasta hoy.
23
וְהָעַוִּים הַיֹּשְׁבִים בַּחֲצֵרִים, עַד-עַזָּה--כַּפְתֹּרִים הַיֹּצְאִים מִכַּפְתֹּר, הִשְׁמִידֻם וַיֵּשְׁבוּ תַחְתָּם.
And the Avvim, who lived in villages as far as Gaza — the Caphtorim, who came from Caphtor, destroyed them and lived in their place.)note
Y a los aveos, que habitaban en aldeas hasta Gaza — los caftoritas, que salieron de Caftor, los destruyeron y habitaron en su lugar.)
24
קוּמוּ סְּעוּ, וְעִבְרוּ אֶת-נַחַל אַרְנֹן--רְאֵה נָתַתִּי בְיָדְךָ אֶת-סִיחֹן מֶלֶךְ-חֶשְׁבּוֹן הָאֱמֹרִי וְאֶת-אַרְצוֹ, הָחֵל רָשׁ; וְהִתְגָּר בּוֹ, מִלְחָמָה.
Rise up, set out, and cross the brook Arnon. Look — I have given into your hand Sihon the Amorite, king of Heshbon, and his land. Begin to possess it, and provoke him to battle.note
Levántense, pónganse en marcha, y crucen el arroyo Arnón. Miren — he entregado en tu mano a Sehón el amorreo, rey de Hesbón, y su tierra. Comienza a poseerla, y provócalo a la guerra.
25
הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, אָחֵל תֵּת פַּחְדְּךָ וְיִרְאָתְךָ, עַל-פְּנֵי הָעַמִּים, תַּחַת כָּל-הַשָּׁמָיִם--אֲשֶׁר יִשְׁמְעוּן שִׁמְעֲךָ, וְרָגְזוּ וְחָלוּ מִפָּנֶיךָ.
This day I will begin to put the dread of you and the fear of you on the peoples under the whole heaven, who will hear the report of you, and will tremble and be in anguish because of you.'note
Hoy mismo comenzaré a poner tu terror y tu temor sobre los pueblos debajo de todo el cielo, quienes oirán el informe acerca de ti, y temblarán y se angustiarán a causa de ti».
26
וָאֶשְׁלַח מַלְאָכִים מִמִּדְבַּר קְדֵמוֹת, אֶל-סִיחוֹן מֶלֶךְ חֶשְׁבּוֹן, דִּבְרֵי שָׁלוֹם, לֵאמֹר.
So I sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon king of Heshbon, with words of peace, saying:note
Entonces envié mensajeros desde el desierto de Cademot a Sehón, rey de Hesbón, con palabras de paz, diciendo:
27
אֶעְבְּרָה בְאַרְצֶךָ, בַּדֶּרֶךְ בַּדֶּרֶךְ אֵלֵךְ: לֹא אָסוּר, יָמִין וּשְׂמֹאול.
'Let me pass through your land. I will keep to the road; I will turn aside neither to the right nor to the left.note
«Déjame pasar por tu tierra. Me mantendré en el camino; no me apartaré ni a la derecha ni a la izquierda.
28
אֹכֶל בַּכֶּסֶף תַּשְׁבִּרֵנִי וְאָכַלְתִּי, וּמַיִם בַּכֶּסֶף תִּתֶּן-לִי וְשָׁתִיתִי; רַק, אֶעְבְּרָה בְרַגְלָי.
You shall sell me food for silver, that I may eat, and give me water for silver, that I may drink. Only let me pass through on foot —note
Me venderás alimento por dinero, para que coma, y me darás agua por dinero, para que beba. Solo déjame pasar a pie —
29
כַּאֲשֶׁר עָשׂוּ-לִי בְּנֵי עֵשָׂו, הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּשֵׂעִיר, וְהַמּוֹאָבִים, הַיֹּשְׁבִים בְּעָר--עַד אֲשֶׁר-אֶעֱבֹר, אֶת-הַיַּרְדֵּן, אֶל-הָאָרֶץ, אֲשֶׁר-יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ נֹתֵן לָנוּ.
just as the sons of Esau who live in Seir did for me, and the Moabites who live in Ar — until I cross the Jordan into the land that Jehovah our God is giving us.'note
tal como hicieron por mí los hijos de Esaú que habitan en Seír, y los moabitas que habitan en Ar — hasta que cruce el Jordán a la tierra que Jehová nuestro Dios nos da».
30
וְלֹא אָבָה, סִיחֹן מֶלֶךְ חֶשְׁבּוֹן, הַעֲבִרֵנוּ, בּוֹ: כִּי-הִקְשָׁה יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ אֶת-רוּחוֹ, וְאִמֵּץ אֶת-לְבָבוֹ, לְמַעַן תִּתּוֹ בְיָדְךָ, כַּיּוֹם הַזֶּה.
But Sihon king of Heshbon was not willing to let us pass by him, for Jehovah your God had made his spirit stubborn and strengthened his heart, so that he might give him into your hand, as he has done this day.note
Pero Sehón, rey de Hesbón, no quiso dejarnos pasar por él, porque Jehová tu Dios había endurecido su espíritu y fortalecido su corazón, para entregarlo en tu mano, como lo ha hecho hoy.
31
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה, אֵלַי, רְאֵה הַחִלֹּתִי תֵּת לְפָנֶיךָ, אֶת-סִיחֹן וְאֶת-אַרְצוֹ; הָחֵל רָשׁ, לָרֶשֶׁת אֶת-אַרְצוֹ.
And Jehovah said to me, 'Look — I have begun to give Sihon and his land over to you. Begin to possess it, that you may inherit his land.'note
Y Jehová me dijo: «Mira — he comenzado a entregarte a Sehón y su tierra. Comienza a poseerla, para que heredes su tierra».

Entonces Sehón salió a nuestro encuentro, él y todo su pueblo, para pelear en Jahaza.

And Jehovah our God gave him over to us, and we struck him down, and his sons, and all his people.note
Y Jehová nuestro Dios lo entregó delante de nosotros, y lo derrotamos a él, y a sus hijos, y a todo su pueblo.

Y tomamos todas sus ciudades en aquel tiempo, y destruimos por completo cada ciudad — hombres, mujeres y niños. No dejamos sobreviviente alguno.

Translator's Notes

Verses 1–3 · a command from thirty-eight years ago, finally obeyed

⭐ Deuteronomy 1 ended with a defeat and a long silence — and this chapter opens by quoting the very order Israel broke to earn the first and finally kept to end the second. The defeat (1:41-45, already on these pages) came from ignoring this exact command and attacking anyway; the thirty-eight years of silence came from eventually obeying it. "We turned and set out into the wilderness by the way to the Red Sea, as Jehovah had spoken to me." That is not a vague gesture at divine guidance. It is a word-for-word citation: Numbers 14:25 (already on these pages) has God telling Moses, the morning after the scouts' report, "tomorrow, turn and set out for the wilderness, by the way of the Red Sea" — penu u-se'u lachem ha-midbarah derekh yam-suf. Deuteronomy 2:1 repeats the same verb, the same noun, and the same route: vannefen vannissa ha-midbarah derekh yam-suf. Thirty-eight chapters and thirty-eight years separate the command from this sentence reporting it kept — the whole intervening wilderness (Numbers 15 through 26 — a plague, the rock, the bronze serpent, Balaam, the second census) is compressed into three verses and a verb of circling, sabab, "we went around." The scribes agree that a section ends here: the Masoretic text marks a setumah break after v1, the first of three in this chapter.

"You have gone around this mountain long enough." Same verb, now as command rather than report — sov, from the same root as v1's vannasav. Thirty-eight years of circling is dismissed in five Hebrew words. KJV ‘Ye have compassed this mountain long enough’; NWT 1984 ‘YOU have gone around this mountain long enough’; NIV ‘You have made your way around this hill country long enough.’ The instruction to turn north is the same instruction given at Kadesh thirty-eight years earlier, except this time it is obeyed the same day.

Verses 4–8 · "your brothers" — and a war Numbers 20 already fought without a shot

⚠️ Compare this passage to Numbers 20:14-21 (already on these pages) and a real tension sits on the surface. There, Edom refuses Israel passage outright — "you shall not pass through," said twice, backed by an army — and Israel turns away without a fight, "the one time in Numbers it takes no for an answer" (that chapter's own note). Here, Israel is simply told it is "about to pass through the territory of your brothers, the sons of Esau" and to buy food and water from them, which it then does. The honest options: this is the SAME episode read from two angles, God's command to keep the peace regardless of Edom's earlier refusal; or it is a genuinely different leg of the journey — the route named in v8, "away from the road of the Arabah, from Elath and from Ezion-geber," runs along Edom's southern and eastern edge toward the Gulf of Aqaba, not up the King's Highway through Edom's heartland that Numbers 20 was refused. Both readings are old and neither is forced by the text; this translation reports the seam rather than closing it.

The kinship language runs both directions. Numbers 20:14 has Israel call Edom "your brother Israel"; here God calls Edom "your brothers, the sons of Esau" — the same claim, made by each side of the family to the other. ⚠️ The explicit word "brothers" (achim) is used only twice, at v4 and v8 — the two verses that frame the actual passage through Edom's territory; at v22 and v29 the text drops back to the plainer "the sons of Esau," kinship implied rather than restated. Even so, "Esau" is never once paired with a hostile verb in this chapter, even when the passage is tense enough that "they will be afraid of you, so be very careful."

"Not so much as a foot's breadth." Ad midrakh kaf-ragel — literally the tread of the sole of a foot, the same idiom Joshua 1:3 (already on these pages) will use in the OPPOSITE direction, promising Israel "every place the sole of your foot treads." What Israel is refused here against Edom, it will be granted everywhere else. ⭐ And the word behind the refusal, yerushah, "possession," is the chapter's own refrain: it lands for Edom (v5), twice for Moab (v9), twice for Ammon (v19), and once more describing Israel's own coming inheritance (v12) — the same word, applied without distinction to four nations. Whatever Israel is about to receive, this chapter insists three neighboring peoples already received the same kind of thing from the same God.

"He has known your walking through this great wilderness." Yada lechtekha is kept literal rather than smoothed to "watched over your journey" — yada is the same intimate verb Genesis uses of a person knowing another, not the language of distant observation. The strangeness is deliberate, and this is where it earns its keep: the same wilderness this verb calls known is the one vv14-15 will say the hand of Jehovah moved through to destroy a whole generation — provision and destruction, in the same stretch of ground, held together rather than resolved.

"These forty years… you have lacked nothing." Lo chasarta davar — a flatly positive verdict on the very years Deuteronomy 1 spent narrating as a sentence for unbelief. Nothing here is walked back; the two readings of the wilderness — punishment, and provision — sit in the same book without being reconciled, the way the pardon and the "but" of Numbers 14:20-21 (already on these pages) already did.

Verses 9–12 · Lot's sons, and a giant-clan the Torah has met before

"The sons of Lot." Moab and Ammon are named here as Lot's own descendants without a word of explanation, because the explanation was already given: Genesis 19:36-38 (already on these pages) names Lot's daughters' two sons Moab and Ben-Ammi, "father of Moab to this day" and "father of the children of Ammon to this day" — the identical closing formula, "to this day," that this chapter itself will reuse twice more (vv22, 23). A story that began in a cave above Zoar, with no man on earth left as Lot's daughters believed, ends up naming two nations Israel is now commanded to respect as kin.

⭐ The Emim-Rephaim-Anakim identification is not new information in the Torah — it is a callback across the widest possible gap. Genesis 14:5 (already on these pages), Chedorlaomer's coalition sweeping the Transjordan highway in Abram's own lifetime, already names the Rephaim, the Zuzim, and the Emim among the peoples it strikes — legendary giant-clans old enough that Abram's generation only knew them as history. That chapter's own note flags exactly this payoff: "the Rephaim and Emim resurface in Deuteronomy 2-3 as the tall aboriginal peoples the later conquest displaced." Here is the resurfacing. ⚠️ The likeliest identification, reported and not forced: Genesis 14's Zuzim and this chapter's Zamzummim (v20) are probably the same people under two names — a coalition-record's foreign label against the Ammonites' own word for their predecessors, separated by the better part of five centuries of narrated time.

Three nations, three names, one people. The Moabites call them Emim (v11); the Ammonites will call the same category Zamzummim (v20); the umbrella term the text itself reaches for is Rephaim, "reckoned" the same as the Anakim of the south (the giant clan the scouts feared at Numbers 13, already on these pages). One extinct people, remembered under a different name by every nation that displaced them — which is the chapter's own quiet lesson about how memory works. ⚠️ The shelf splits on whether to translate the word at all. ASV and the NWT 1984 keep ‘Rephaim’ transliterated, as does this translation; KJV resolves it to ‘giants’ at v11 — ‘which also were accounted GIANTS, as the Anakims’ — and Geneva (‘they also were taken for giants as the Anakims’) and the Douay-Rheims (‘they were esteemed as giants, and were like the sons of the Enacims’) do the same, each in its own wording. Keeping the name rather than the gloss preserves the fact that this is an ethnonym, not a physical description standing alone; the text's own comparative "tall as the Anakim" already carries the height.

⭐ V12's closing clause is spoken from the far side of the conquest. "The sons of Esau dispossessed them… just as Israel did to the land of its possession, which Jehovah gave them" — a past-tense verb describing what ISRAEL did, inside a speech Moses is giving before Israel has crossed the Jordan at all. Genesis 36:20-30 (already on these pages) already anticipated this exact sentence by name, quoting it almost verbatim before this chapter existed on these pages: "the Horites lived in Seir formerly, but the sons of Esau dispossessed them and dwelt in their place." Read as Moses' own words, the clause is a confident forward glance from a man about to die outside the land. Read as a scribal aside, it is one of the honest seams this project does not sand smooth: a narrator's voice, later than the events it narrates, briefly steps forward inside what is framed as direct speech. Both readings are old; this translation adopts neither and reports the sentence exactly as it stands.

Verses 13–16 · a brook, and an arithmetic that closes the book

Zered is where the count comes due. V14 states outright what Deuteronomy 1 only implied: thirty-eight years from Kadesh-barnea to this brook — add the roughly two years already spent reaching Kadesh in the first place (Deuteronomy 1:2, already on these pages, "eleven days from Horeb… to Kadesh-barnea"), and the number lands on the forty years this book keeps naming (1:3; 2:7). The sentence is not new theology; it is the bill for Numbers 14:29-34's "a day for a year," now paid out to the last coin — "until the whole generation of the men of war had perished from the midst of the camp, just as Jehovah had sworn to them."

⭐ The command itself is worth noticing as a pair. "Rise up… and cross the brook" — qumu… ve'ivru et-nachal — is the shared skeleton of both of this chapter's river-crossings: Zered here, marking the end of the wandering, and the Arnon at v24, marking the start of the conquest. ⚠️ Not quite identical: v13 adds "for yourselves" (lachem) that v24 lacks, and v24 inserts "set out" (se'u) that v13 lacks — the same three-word core, each verse carrying one word of its own. Close enough that the pairing is real, not close enough to call it a fixed formula.

Nothing here is softened. The very next verse repeats the idea for emphasis rather than moving on — "the hand of Jehovah was against them, to destroy them… until they had perished" — the same flat double statement of cause that Numbers 14's notes already found this book unwilling to soften. The second of the chapter's three setumah breaks falls right after v16, at the exact hinge between the dying-off finishing and God speaking again — the scribes pausing the text at the one moment nothing is left of the generation the census counted.

Verses 17–23 · Ammon, Zamzummim, and a fourth case that breaks the pattern

The Ammon paragraph is Moab's own formula, run a second time — and Edom's, a plainer one — the same refusal-to-harass, the doubled "possession" that Moab's own paragraph already used (v9), the same parenthetical aside naming who lived there first. Structurally it is not new material so much as the chapter proving, by repetition, that this is a rule rather than an exception: every nation bordering Israel's road has its own yerushah, and every one of them displaced somebody to get it.

⚠️ The fourth case, the Avvim and Caphtorim (v23), is shaped differently on purpose — no "reckoned as Rephaim," no height comparison, just a village people wiped out by newcomers "as far as Gaza." Genesis 10:14 (already on these pages) already listed the Caphtorim among the descendants of Mizraim, alongside the ancestors of the Philistines, without saying where Caphtor was. The shelf disagrees sharply about it: the NVI resolves the guess outright — «los caftoritas procedentes de Creta» — while the Douay-Rheims, following the Vulgate, reads an entirely different geography: ‘The Hevites… were expelled by the Cappadocians: who came out of Cappadocia’ — Crete and Cappadocia are on opposite sides of the Aegean and Anatolia, roughly a thousand miles apart, and the disagreement is old rather than careless. This translation keeps the Hebrew names transliterated and leaves the identification to the note.

"To this day" (v22) is this chapter's own refrain, not a fresh observation. It closed Genesis 19's naming of Moab and Ammon (see n9 above), and it will close v23's Avvim notice in substance if not in the exact words. A book delivered forty years after the exodus keeps reaching for the phrase that means: this is still true as I stand here telling you.

Verses 24–31 · the embassy Numbers 21 already promised, and a heart made strong for the wrong reason

⭐ This is the payoff Numbers 21 named by chapter and verse before this page existed. That chapter's own closing note reads: "Deuteronomy 2-3 (not yet), which retells Sihon and Og at length." Here is the retelling — and it is close enough to be checked word for word. Numbers 21:21-22 (already on these pages) already flagged that Israel's embassy to Sihon is "the embassy to Edom, one chapter later and one degree colder" — the same request, the same refusal. This chapter adds a THIRD telling of that request, in Moses' own retelling voice rather than Numbers' narration, and it is close enough to set beside the other two directly: "let me pass through your land… I will turn aside neither to the right nor to the left… only let me pass through on my feet" repeats Numbers 20:17's appeal to Edom clause for clause — except Edom is family and lives, and Sihon is not and does not. ⚠️ One phrase does NOT repeat: Numbers 20:17 and 21:22 both name "the king's road" (derekh ha-melekh), the specific highway; this chapter's own version of the request drops the king and simply doubles the plain word for road, ba-derekh ba-derekh elekh, "on the road, on the road I will go" — emphatic, but not the same idiom.

"The dread of you and the fear of you… under the whole heaven." The first noun, pachad, is exactly the word Exodus 15:16 (already on these pages) put in the Song of the Sea forty years earlier: "terror and dread fall upon them." The second noun differs — yir'ah, fear or awe, where the Song used eimah, terror — so the echo is real but not exact, a promise substantially kept rather than quoted verbatim. Three shelf versions independently reach for the same pairing this translation does: Geneva ‘thy fear and thy dread’; Douay-Rheims ‘the dread and fear of thee’; TLB ‘tremble with fear… and dread your arrival.’

⭐ V30 uses two verbs for Sihon's refusal, and the second one is new to this project's dictionary — because of where it goes next. "Jehovah your God had made his spirit stubborn" uses qashah, one of the three verbs already catalogued for Pharaoh's own hardening (Exodus 4:21, already on these pages — this project's chazak entry documents all three: chazak, kaved, and qashah together). But "and strengthened his heart" is a different root entirely: amats, to be strong or bold — the same word, doubled with chazak, that will become Joshua's own charge three times over in the very next book (Joshua 1:6-9, already on these pages, repeated a fourth time by the people at v18: "be strong and resolute," chazak ve-ematz). The identical root that steadies a leader for the crossing is the root that seals a king in his refusal to allow one. Courage and obstinacy are not two different Hebrew ideas here; they are one word, and which side of God's purpose you stand on decides which English word it becomes.

"I have BEGUN to give Sihon and his land." Hachiloti, from chalal, "to begin" — the same root opens v24's "begin to possess it" and v31's "begin to possess it, that you may inherit." Three "begins" in eight verses, framing the first Transjordan conquest as a first installment rather than a completed grant — precisely the posture this book will carry into Deuteronomy 3's Og and, much later, into Joshua's own book-length series of beginnings.

Verses 32–37 · total war on one king, and a line not crossed

V34's "devoted every city to destruction" is the same cherem already catalogued from Israel's first cities-taken chapter (Numbers 21:1-3, already on these pages, naming Hormah). Nothing about the vocabulary is new; what is worth noticing is the SHAPE of the destruction against the shape of the restraint one verse later, v37: every city of Sihon's kingdom, and not one foot of Ammon's, in the same short paragraph. The chapter that opened by refusing Edom's land closes by refusing Ammon's — the total-war momentum of vv34-36 stops exactly at the line Jehovah drew back in v19, and the text states plainly that it stopped there because it was commanded to: "everywhere that Jehovah our God had forbidden us."

Aroer to Gilead, in one breath. The border formula names the southern edge (Aroer, on the Arnon) and the northern limit (Gilead) of everything just taken — the same two place-names Numbers 32 (already on these pages) used to describe where Reuben and Gad asked to settle. This chapter hands over the land whose allotment that one already narrated.

Notes on this translation

Decisions. Hebrew and English agree on 37 verses. The rendering to flag is "Rephaim" kept transliterated, against KJV, Geneva and the Douay-Rheims' "giants" — see n9 above. "Strengthened" for amats (v30) is chosen specifically to let an English reader feel the same root Joshua 1 will use for courage; most of the shelf reaches for "obstinate" or "hard" a second time, flattening two different Hebrew verbs into one English idea. Elsewhere the established renderings hold: Jehovah for the divine name, possession for yerushah, and cherem "devoted to destruction." There are three Masoretic paragraph breaks (all setumot, after v1, mid-v8, and after v16) — the first isolates the bare fact of the turn, the second falls between leaving Edom and turning toward Moab, and the third sits at the exact moment the dying-off generation finishes.

Library. Two new dictionary entries in both languages: yerushah, the chapter's own refrain-word for a granted possession; and amats, the strength-root Sihon's hardened heart shares with Joshua's charge. Eight new encyclopedia entries in both languages: the giant-clans Rephaim, Emim and Zamzummim; the displaced Avvim and their conquerors the Caphtorim; and the places Aroer, Zered and Elath. Two existing entries gained their first Spanish edition here, touched by this chapter but not previously translated: Anakim and Horites.

Echoes paid. Numbers 14:25's "turn… by the way of the Red Sea" — quoted almost verbatim at v1, thirty-eight years later. Genesis 19:36-38's naming of Moab and Ben-Ammi. Genesis 14:5's promise that "the Rephaim and Emim resurface in Deuteronomy 2-3" — paid at vv10-11, 20. Genesis 36:20-30's advance quotation of this chapter's own v12. Numbers 21's own "Deuteronomy 2-3 (not yet), which retells Sihon and Og at length" — paid for Sihon here, with Og still to come. And Exodus 15:16's "terror and dread," substantially answered at v25.

Patterns worth carrying forward. First: this chapter runs the SAME formula four times — a nation refused or granted a yerushah, a parenthetical aside naming who lived there before them — and repetition is the argument. What Israel is about to do to Canaan, Edom, Moab, Ammon, and even Caphtor's own immigrants have each already done to somebody. Second: the chapter keeps its most theologically loaded sentences — v12's "as Israel did," v30's hardened heart — inside otherwise plain narrative prose, without a single verse pausing to flag either one; the seams are structural, not announced. Third, and worth watching in Deuteronomy 3: the three "begins" of vv24-31 (see n24) frame Sihon as the FIRST Transjordan king taken, not the last — Og is next, and his outsized bed is the detail Numbers 21 chose not to tell.

Echoes planted. Og, named nowhere in this chapter yet due next, whose iron bedstead Numbers 21 pointedly withheld; the "begin" formula (chalal), now three-for-three and due a fourth use; and the amats root, planted here in a king's obstinate heart and due to return, doubled with chazak, in the mouth of the man about to succeed Moses.

Next installment in this book: Deuteronomy 3 — Og of Bashan, the last king before the Jordan, and a bed nine cubits long still on show at Rabbah of the Ammonites.
